## ② INVERSION — what would have to be true for the item to stay open

***The corpus's best case for keeping it, stated in full:*** *the gate dissolving is not the same as the
question being answered. Frontier 1 was answered in the sense that **there is no worldline crossing to
compute** — a comoving worldline reaches the branch point at finite proper time and terminates, and what
continues is the analytic continuation. **That is an answer about the crossing, not about the Kerr-inner and
RN interiors.** If the interior reassignments carry structure that the branch-point answer does not reach,
then the gate's removal left a live question standing without its gate, and closing it is the eighth face —
stamping the untouched as unneeded.*

***Why it does not hold, and this is the whole of the case:*** *what remains of the item after the gate is
removed is, in P7's own words,* **"ordinary interior analysis rather than a frontier of this construction."**
*The Kerr-inner and RN interiors are objects of general relativity, not of the slicing operator's range —
P9 established the range as the symmetry-reducible sector and these interiors sit outside it. **So the
residue is not unowned work; it is work that belongs to a different theory.** An item may leave a frontier
list either by being answered or by ceasing to be a frontier **of this construction**, and this is the second
kind. ***The distinction is recorded in `CONSOLIDATE` §16 entry 1 as what the closure taught:*** *a gate can
vanish without its item being answered, and that is a different closure from being solved.*
