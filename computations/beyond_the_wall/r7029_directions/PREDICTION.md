## ⛔⛔ AND ONE THING ABOUT `ⓑ` THAT I AM SAYING BEFORE I RUN IT, NOT AFTER

**`ⓑ` is proportional to one of its inputs by construction, so its AMPLITUDE is not an independent
measurement.** *A one-coefficient regression's fitted part is a scalar multiple of one of the two vectors.
Whichever orientation is taken:*

* *regressing the **channel on the arm** (`cc66.64`'s orientation, the one the 45% was measured in):
  fitted part $=\beta\,e_{\rm arm}$, so its comb amplitude is $\beta \times 7.30$ — **combed by arithmetic,
  whatever the physics.***
* *regressing the **arm on the channel** (the orientation "the part of the arm's excess the term mix explains"
  reads most naturally): fitted part $=\gamma\,e_{k}$, so its comb amplitude is $\gamma \times$ `ⓐ` — carrying
  nothing beyond `ⓐ`.*

⇒ *** SO `ⓑ` CANNOT BE REPORTED AS A FINDING IN ITS OWN RIGHT, AND A "`ⓑ` COMBED" READING TAKEN FROM ITS
AMPLITUDE ALONE WOULD BE THE ARITHMETIC TALKING. ***

⛔ **What IS informative, and what this file therefore reads:**
* **`ⓐ`** — is the channel's own cost combed at all. *A genuine measurement.*
* **`ⓒ`** — the residual. *Not proportional to anything; a genuinely new vector, and the sharp one.*
* **the PHASES** — `ⓑ`'s phase against the arm's is fixed by construction, but **`ⓐ`'s and `ⓒ`'s are not**,
  and the guard below is why they decide the reading rather than the amplitudes.
* **the SPLIT** — how the arm's 7.30 divides between the part a channel explains and the part it does not.

⌗ ***AND BOTH ORIENTATIONS ARE RUN AND BOTH REPORTED.*** *They answer neighbouring questions and the order's
