# Why the rule sits above even a re-read ledger — and above the seat whose threshold it matches

**Offered, not ordered** (`r7317` item ③). The numbers it is about: two blind appliers put owedness near
`67`–`72`%; `60`'s era re-read, on a matched denominator, lands at `30.5`%; this seat's pooled reading is
`60.7`% over `122` cells, and the rule's calibration matches it.

**My answer: most of the `37` points is a MIX and not a LEVEL. The three numbers are averages over
different proportions of two strata, and the strata have genuinely different owed rates.** The rule does not
disagree with a re-read ledger about keys; it is weighted towards the keys where owedness is high.

---

## 1. The two strata are already measured, and they are construction and meaning

`r7313` ran the stratification and `r7317` named its grounds. Put side by side they are the same split
twice:

| | stratum | owed |
|---|---|---|
| `70` | **text alone** | `95.8`% |
| `70` | judgement | `53.3`% |
| `71` | **text alone** | `83.3`% |
| `71` | judgement | `48.1`% |

And `60`'s `27` withdrawals: **Test 1 takes `11`, Test 5 takes `11`.** Both are **construction** tests — a
span cut inside its own sentence, and one label over several arms with nothing joining them. `r7317` put it
exactly: *"what takes the rows is where the check was CUT, and not what the quoted words say."*

⇒ **"Text alone" is the construction stratum. "Judgement" is the meaning stratum.** Nearly every test in the
rule is a construction test: Test 1 (what is outside the span), Test 3 (does the check run the tool), Test 4
(count and locus), Test 5 (are the pieces joined), Test 6 (is an arm dead), Test 7 (does the guard read
anything). **Only Test 2 asks what a word means, and even it asks it by a word list.** A rule made of
construction tests is decisive on construction and quiet on meaning — and it is decisive *because*
construction defects are visible, not because its threshold is loose.

## 2. So the three numbers average over different mixes

- **The appliers' `67`–`72`%** is measured over a held set drawn at a salt from the whole pool, so it
  carries whatever construction/meaning mix the corpus has — and construction keys, being the ones the text
  decides, dominate the DECIDED subset, because the undecidable ones were filed `UNDECIDED` and left out.
- **`60`'s `30.5`%** is an era re-read: an early stretch of the ledger, re-judged row by row. A re-reader
  passes over construction and meaning alike and reports one rate across both.
- **This seat's `60.7`%** is measured on `122` **pooled double-read cells** — and those cells are not a
  random draw. **Three of the four accidental double-reads in the programme's history are mine, and a key
  gets read twice because an assignment collided, not because it was representative.**

⇒ *** **The rule can sit above me without either of us having a different threshold, because its denominator
is construction-heavy and mine is whatever two colliding assignments happened to overlap on.** *** That is a
mix effect, and it predicts the sign of the gap without anyone reading loosely.

## 3. The one measurement that would settle it, and it needs no re-read

**Compare the construction/meaning mix across the three populations.** `70` already holds the stratum label
for every applier key — that is where the `95.8`/`53.3` split came from. The question is only:

> Of this seat's `122` pooled cells, and of `60`'s `40` sampled era rows, what fraction falls in the
> stratum where the text decides?

**If the appliers' decided keys are construction-heavy and the other two populations are not, the `37`
points are a mix and the line is not where any of the three numbers puts it.** If all three carry the same
mix, the mix explanation is dead and the gap is a real level difference — in which case the rule is simply
above every reader, including the one it was calibrated to, and that is a drafting fact about the tests.

## 4. A mechanism I predicted and measured, which did NOT hold

**I expected the ledger to show owedness rising with the number of keys a receipt carries.** The reasoning:
a construction defect owes **every** piece — Test 5 says so in its own words — and a check that pins several
unjoined pieces has, by construction, more pinned literals than one pinning a complete clause. So keys
should concentrate in the checks the rule condemns, and the key-weighted rate should climb with size.

**Measured over all 2,823 ledger rows, it does not climb:**

| keys per receipt | receipts | keys | `REPAIR-OWED` | rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 43 | 43 | 2 | **4.7%** |
| 2–3 | 71 | 175 | 32 | 18.3% |
| 4–7 | 146 | 814 | 97 | 11.9% |
| 8–15 | 120 | 1,256 | 172 | 13.7% |
| 16+ | 25 | 535 | 63 | 11.8% |

**Flat from 4 upward.** There is a single-key/multi-key step (`4.7`% against `13.1`%) in the predicted
direction, but it rests on `43` keys and is not the size gradient the mechanism requires. **So the multi-key
mechanism is refuted in the form I predicted, and I am not keeping the half of it that survives: a step on
43 keys is a sample, which is the eightieth member and I have been on the wrong side of it before.**

⌗ That is the second mechanism of mine refuted in three cycles — the first was the over-call hypothesis at
`r7313`. **Both were refutable by a measurement stated in advance, and both were run.** The mix argument in
§2 is offered on the same terms: §3 is how to kill it.

## 5. What I am not claiming

- **Not that the rule is wrong.** Nothing here says a construction test mis-decides a key. The argument is
  about which keys get counted, not about how any key is decided.
- **Not that `30.5`% is the true rate.** It is an era, re-read by one seat.
- **Not that my own `60.7`% is a reference.** It is the number most likely to be mix-biased of the three,
  because its denominator was assembled by collisions rather than drawn — and the rule having adopted my
  threshold makes it evidence about the rule's drafting, not about the threshold being right.
