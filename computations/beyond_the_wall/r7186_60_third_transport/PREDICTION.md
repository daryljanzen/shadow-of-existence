## The question, stated so that it can come back either way

`r7172` established that the layer's transport is **not** the four-route's — the four-route carries a
Weyl coupling the layer's cross-section problem has no counterpart for. Nobody has asked whether the
layer's transport is the **branch-point crossing's**. `r7180` showed the odd-ladder prediction hangs
on exactly that identification and declined to assume it.

- **If the two share a measure**, the layer's Hopf-charge parity governs what the expansion leg
  inherits, and the construction predicts **no odd-degree anisotropic content at any degree** — a
  parameter-free statement about the sky's odd multipoles.
- **If they do not**, the parity governs the layer alone and the floor at `L = 2` rests on the tensor
  tower's statement.

## What I expect, and it is the less attractive outcome

**P1 — I expect a measured NEGATIVE: the two measures are not shared.** My reason is already in print
and is why I am predicting against the prediction I would rather have. `P16`'s own result is that
**every mode is already super-horizon at the branch point, so the crossing's species filter has
nothing to act on and the passage is lossless for every species** — and the interior-to-observed map
is the **identity on `L`**, acting on the radial factor at fixed harmonic index. A transport that
filters nothing and preserves the index has **no selection on degree at all**, so it has no parity to
share. The layer's parity is a statement about which `(L,m)` *exist* on the squashed layer; the
crossing is a map on amplitudes at fixed `L`. Those are different kinds of object, and I expect the
measure test to say so.

**P2 — and I expect the negative to be clean rather than marginal.** If the crossing is lossless and
index-preserving, the mismatch should show up as a structural statement (the two act on different

test.

route by which the parity could still bound what arrives: not by sharing a measure, but by
**composition** — if the layer is necessarily in the path, then the parity bounds the arriving content

DIFFERENT question.** If I end up delivering the odd-ladder bound by composition, that is not a
positive answer to order ⓵ and must not be reported as one.

**P4 — what would change my mind.** A shared measure would show as the crossing's kernel acting on
the `(L,m)` label rather than on the radial factor — i.e. the crossing distinguishing modes the
layer's decomposition also distinguishes, by the same charge. If the crossing turns out to carry a
Hopf-charge dependence anywhere, P1 is wrong and I will say so plainly.
