## ⛭ The claim this revision makes, and it is a proof rather than a measurement

**Under the row's own equivalence rule, an added axis can only split classes. It can never merge
them.**

- A fourth binary axis maps every existing cell into at most two cells. So the occupied-cell count
  after the axis is added is at least the count before it, which is 7.
- **The clause asks for one class**, and the census is already at 7 under three axes. So **no fourth
  axis can meet the clause, and nor can any fifth.** The clause can only move toward "met" if axes
  are *removed*.
- **Removing axes does not work either**, because 60's Ⓐ② measured every one of the three axes as
  taking both values. So any non-empty subset of the axes still gives at least two classes.
- The only instrument under which the forty-five are one class is the empty instrument. **That would
  not show the family is one mechanism. It would only show that nothing was measured.**

⇒ **The predicted verdict is a REFUSAL. 66's reachable negative holds, and it holds for a reason that
does not depend on the object column.** The family is a CLASS under the instrument the row chose.
The second termination clause cannot be met by any extension of that instrument, and the row lives
or dies on its backlogs alone.

⌈ **Where this disagrees with 60, routed rather than edited:** 60's `S15` Ⓓ④ says that with a fourth
axis naming the object, *"the question becomes answerable rather than merely open."* **The question
is already answered at three axes, and the answer is no.** A fourth axis refines that answer. It
cannot reverse it.
