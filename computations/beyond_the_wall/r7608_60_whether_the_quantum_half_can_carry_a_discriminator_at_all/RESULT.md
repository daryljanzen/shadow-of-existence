## The answer, in one line

**The quantum half can carry a test, and it is not the gauge half's test: the gauge half's asks the face
to carry the sector's own STRUCTURE, while the quantum claim is only that the face carries its SCALE.**
The right test is therefore a *selection* test — the face is an actual quantum sector rather than a rhyme
iff its data remove a freedom of the quantum theory that no other principle removes. **There are exactly
three slots where it could do that, and all three are empty:** no value (Ⓓ), an extension that the
quadratic form singles out on its own (Ⓔ, measured here), and the one freedom the construction cannot
close internally, which the face provably cannot reach (Ⓕ). So the quantum half now has a named test and
it is **unmet** — and what would meet it is named with it.

### Ⓔ The extension slot — the measured core, and the arm the verdict turns on

The face **does** remove one freedom: the deficiency-$(1,1)$ family of the scale-factor Hamiltonian, by

$-\psi''+\gamma\psi/x^{2}=E\psi$ on $(0,1)$ with Dirichlet at $1$, at `P10`'s ordering-family maximum

## What this does to the row, and what would discharge the half

The quantum half's live clause can now be written, and it is not the clause the gauge half carries:

* **WHAT WOULD DISCHARGE IT:** a **dimensionless** selection the compact face makes and no other

* **OR TERMINATES IF:** it is shown that every freedom the face could close is one some other principle

**This is not a refutation of the placement.** That $\hbar$'s register is the compact face is not

gauge half, is an identification at the level of the structure with the level-of-the-world test open.
