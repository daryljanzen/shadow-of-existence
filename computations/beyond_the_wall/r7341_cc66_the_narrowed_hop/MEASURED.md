## ③ ⛭ AND THE SECOND REFUSAL SAYS WHAT THE FIRST COULD NOT: I AM FILTERING THE WRONG OBJECT

*`r7339`: filter the literal, `58 %`. `r7341`: filter the literal harder, `85 %`.* **Two narrowings of
the same kind, each derived from the previous measurement, each refused — and the residue is the same
shape every time: things that are not prose but look like it to a shape test.**

⇒ ⛔ ***The literal's SHAPE cannot decide this, because what makes a pin genuine is not what the string
looks like but WHAT IT IS READ AGAINST.*** `'r3142, \`L-261\`'` is a legitimate cross-reference into a
register; `'Hphys'` is an identifier in a receipt's own namespace; **they are indistinguishable as
strings and trivially distinguishable by container.** *The instrument already computes the container's
trace — `_reads` returns it — and `82` of my `117` are `SOURCE`, where the container may be a register,
a ledger, another receipt's source or a data file, and only the first two are text a seat owns as
wording.*

⇒ ***RECOMMENDED for the next revision, and it is a change of kind rather than another turn of the same
screw: gate the hop on the CONTAINER — follow it only where the trace reaches a prose body (a
`corpus/*.tex` paper or a register/ledger `.md`), and never where it reaches a receipt source, a `.py`,
a `.json`, a `.npz` or a data file.*** **Predicted to reduce R and to raise precision, and I am NOT
registering a point for it here: two points derived from the thing they predict have now both been
uninformative where it mattered, and the honest registration is the one I write after reading what the
containers actually are.** ⌗ *That reading is the next revision's first step and its cost is one pass of
the instrument.*
