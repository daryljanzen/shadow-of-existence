## The order and the row

`r7829` (69): *"`PO-93`. Its gate is data: a baryonic mass, gas plus stars, measured within Virgo's
turnaround radius of `7.0` to `7.8` Mpc."* ① state what exists, with references and **the radius each

`PO-93`'s live clause: *"A baryonic mass measured out to the kinematic turnaround radius on such a
cluster, so that the dark fraction at that radius is a number rather than a bound."* `60` established
the standing position: four kinematic turnaround determinations exceed the baryon-only bound by
`19`–`33` per cent and sit inside the dynamical-mass bound, **so the radius tracks the dynamical mass
unless the baryons inside the turnaround radius are `1.70`–`2.35` times those measured inside the
smaller aperture** — `33`–`56` per cent of the measured total, which would make Virgo
baryon-dominated inside its own turnaround radius.

⌗ *So this task is a LITERATURE QUESTION, not a computation on a bank. What it can deliver is either
a number with an error (②) or a precisely located gap (③). I register which I expect, and why, so
that the expectation can be wrong in public.*

## The predictions

- **① ⚠ THE ONE I EXPECT TO CARRY IT: the pieces do NOT reach `7.0` Mpc, and the GAS is what stops.**
  Virgo's hot-gas mass comes from X-ray emission, whose surface brightness falls as roughly the
  square of the gas density, so it is measured where the gas is dense — near the core and out to of
  order the virial radius. I predict **no published hot-gas mass profile for Virgo reaches beyond
  about `2` Mpc**, against a turnaround radius of `7.0`–`7.8`. ⇒ *Registered negative: if a
  published gas-mass profile DOES reach `7.0` Mpc, this prediction is wrong, ② applies instead of
  ③, and I build the mass and compare.*
- **② the stellar piece reaches further than the gas, and is the smaller term.** Galaxy catalogues
  of Virgo and its infall region cover a wider aperture than the X-ray, so I expect **stellar mass
  to be constructible further out than gas mass** — but to be the minority of the baryon budget at
  cluster scales, so reaching further with it does not close the gate.
- **③ the binding gap is a DIFFUSE-PHASE measurement, not a deeper exposure.** If ① holds, the
  missing quantity is the baryon content between roughly the virial radius and `7`–`7.8` Mpc, which
  is where the warm-hot phase is expected to sit — too cool and too rarefied for the X-ray emission
  that measures the hot core. I predict the closing measurement is an **absorption or
  Sunyaev–Zel'dovich-type** probe along that path rather than more of the same emission.
- **④ `PO-93` therefore does NOT discharge on this pass, and does NOT terminate either.** Its
  terminate arm needs the dark fraction at the turnaround scale *settled elsewhere well enough that
  the ratio discriminates nothing*; `60`'s own band is `1.70`–`2.35`, and I predict the literature
  does not pin the turnaround-scale baryon fraction to anything near that precision. ⇒ *So the
  honest outcome is ③: the row stays open with its gap located.*
- **⑤ what I will NOT do.** I will not build a baryonic mass at `7.0`–`7.8` Mpc by **extrapolating**
  a profile measured at `1`–`2` Mpc and present it as a measurement. ⇒ *The row's clause asks for a
  mass MEASURED out to the turnaround radius; an extrapolation is exactly the bound `60` already
  has, dressed as a number. If I give any extrapolated figure it is labelled as such and is not
  offered as a discharge.*
- **⑥ every figure I report carries the radius it was measured to.** The order asks for this
  explicitly, and it is the whole discriminator here: a baryonic mass is not a number but a number
  **and an aperture**, and the row turns on the aperture being `7.0`–`7.8` Mpc rather than the
  smaller one the measured masses use.

## What would make this pass worthless

Reporting a baryon budget without its aperture, or quoting a virial-radius mass as though it
answered a turnaround-radius question. That is the exact confusion `PO-93` exists to prevent, and
`60`'s `1.70`–`2.35` band is the measure of how much room the aperture mismatch leaves.
