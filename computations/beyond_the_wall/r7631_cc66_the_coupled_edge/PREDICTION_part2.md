## The test's logic, written down before it is applied, because it is asymmetric

| what the search finds | what follows |
|---|---|
| the **lowest** use lies **above** `8.345` eV | every use is above it, so the cutoff is **certainly** above it → the exit **fires** and `PO-89` closes as a stated limit |
| the lowest use lies **below** `8.345` eV | **nothing follows about the cutoff either way** — a low-energy use does not cap the cutoff, it merely fails to establish a floor for it. ⇒ Then the deliverable is to say so and name what **would** bound it |

⚠ **The trap in the second row, and it is the one I expect to be in.** *"The corpus uses the tower below
`8.345` eV"* is **not** *"the cutoff lies below `8.345` eV"*. A construction can use a tower at the Hubble
scale and still carry a cutoff at the Planck scale; the mode sums run over **all** `n`, so the regulator
is invoked at arbitrarily high mode number whatever the lowest use is. ⇒ **So I must not report a
low answer as evidence against the exit.** It is evidence of *nothing* about the cutoff, and saying
otherwise would be the flattering direction for a row that has already closed one exit.

## ⚠ WHERE I EXPECT TO LAND

**I expect the lowest use to be the de Sitter scale and to sit roughly `33` orders BELOW `8.345` eV, so
the exit does NOT fire on this route.** The reason is structural: the corpus's tower is the closed-`S³`
layer's transverse-traceless graviton tower on a de Sitter background of radius `α`, and every frequency
in it is set by `1/α`. The surface gravity is `κ = 1/α` and the regularity period `β = 2πα`, so the
construction's own scale at the bottom of the tower is the Hubble/de Sitter scale — of order `H₀`, which
`r7629` already carried as `1.44e−42` GeV `= 1.44e−33` eV.

⇒ **So I expect to report the second row of the table: the corpus uses the tower far below `8.345` eV,
the exit is not established by this route, and the deliverable becomes naming what would bound the
cutoff.** I register that as a **miss** of the order's firing condition rather than a result.

## What I expect to have to name, and the distinction I must keep

If the answer is the one above, then *what would bound the cutoff* is the question, and I expect the
candidates to be:

1. **The regulator's own scale** — the corpus says the ultraviolet definition of the mode sums is
   *"delivered by the construction's own regulator"*. If that regulator carries a scale, **that** is the
   cutoff, and it is the only candidate internal to the construction.
2. **The counterterm scales** — the divergences are named as quartic (a leading constant fixed by the
   propagating-component count), logarithmic (a coefficient the tower's spectrum settles exactly), and
   one further Weyl-squared counterterm. A counterterm's **coefficient** is not a scale, so I expect
   these to bound nothing by themselves, and I will say so rather than dress a coefficient as a cutoff.
3. **The single-scale ledger** — the corpus has the fundamental constants entering as unit gauges over
   one scale. If the ledger forces the cutoff onto that scale, that is a bound; if it only *permits* it,
   that is not.

⚠ **The thing I would most easily get wrong here.** `r7627` established that **the construction fixes no
absolute scale**, and `r7629` reported its threshold as an inequality for exactly that reason. ⇒ So the
honest answer to *"what would bound the cutoff"* may well be **"nothing the construction currently
contains"** — and if that is what the search shows, I must report it as a *gap* rather than reach for the
Planck scale, which the construction does not supply either. **Naming a scale the corpus does not carry
would be inventing the datum the exit needs.**
