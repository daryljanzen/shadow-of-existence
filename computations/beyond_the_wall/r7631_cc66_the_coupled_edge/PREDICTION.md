## ⚠ WHERE I EXPECT TO LAND, AND IT IS THE OUTCOME THAT DOES *NOT* HELP THE QUANTUM HALF

The order's **reachable negative** is: nothing without Euclidean content selects it, *or* the form
energy selects a different member — and then the face alone closes a freedom and the quantum half

⚠ **I expect to miss that, and to land the other outcome.** The reason is structural rather than
numerical: among the self-adjoint extensions of a lower-semibounded symmetric operator there is a
**distinguished** one, the **Friedrichs extension** — the one whose domain lies inside the closure of
the quadratic form. *"Finite quadratic-form energy"* is exactly the condition that names it. On the
inverse-square half-line problem the Friedrichs member is the branch `x^{1/2+ν}`, because the other
branch `x^{1/2−ν}` is the one whose form integral diverges. ⇒ **That is the same branch Hartle–Hawking
regularity imposes**, fibre by fibre.

**So I expect the form energy to select the same member, which is the order's second outcome: the
coupled edge closes as the free sector does, and the face is NOT the only closer here.** I register
that before computing because it is the answer that costs the corpus something, and I will not let it
arrive as a surprise or be softened into the first.

⌗ **And the one way the expectation could fail, which is the part worth computing rather than
asserting.** The deficiency being a **subspace** rather than a line means the extensions are
parametrised by the **unitaries on it**, `U(n)`, not by a single angle. The Friedrichs member is one
point of that family — but the coupling could in principle make the form-finite member a *fibre-mixing*
unitary rather than the diagonal "regular branch on every fibre" that Hartle–Hawking picks. ⇒ **Then
the two would differ and the reachable negative would fire.** So the computation is: parametrise, find
the form-finite member, and check whether it is diagonal in the fibre decomposition.
