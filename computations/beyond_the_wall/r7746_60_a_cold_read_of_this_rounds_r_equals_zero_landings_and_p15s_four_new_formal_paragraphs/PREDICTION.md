## ⓞ WHAT IS DERIVABLE BEFORE ANYTHING IS READ

* The horizon cubic is `r³ − r + 2M`, its value at `r = 0` is `2M`, so **`r = 0` is a root iff
  `2M = 0`** (`r7744 ⓞ`), and a root multiset of that cubic **sums to `0`**, the cubic carrying
  no `r²` term. At the Nariai member `2M = 2/(3√3) ≠ 0` and the multiset is `{r₀, r₀, −2r₀}` with
  `r₀ = 1/√3`. `r7744`'s first site turned on exactly the sum test and it is reusable here.
* `f = 1 − 2M/r − r²` in the gauge `α = 1`. Near a **simple** root `f ≈ f′(r₀)(r − r₀)`; near a
  **double** root `f′(r₀) = 0` and the leading behaviour is quadratic. The tortoise coordinate
  `r* = ∫dr/f` therefore behaves **logarithmically** at a simple root and like a **reciprocal** at
  a double one — the two are different divergence rates, and which root a theorem is about decides
  which it gets. ⇒ *`cm_tortoise_linear` and `cm_tortoise_double` are a pair about two different
  objects, and the arm establishes which each is about from its own statement before comparing.*
* **A divergence claim carries three separable things** — the variable, the direction, and the
  sign — and `r7744 FINDING 3` was that a fourth is separable too: **the SUBJECT**. A theorem may
  state the limit of an *expression* that equals the quantity only under hypotheses, in which case
  the prose's *"it diverges"* is not what was proved. The order names this risk for ⓵ in its own
  words (*"the leg's identification of `aH` with the expression"*), so the arm is written to
  decide it rather than to look for it.
