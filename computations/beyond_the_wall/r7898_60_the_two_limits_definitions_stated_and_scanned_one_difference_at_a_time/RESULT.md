# r7898 — the definitional part of the gap is the PARAMETERS, not the ruler, and it is about half

Order `r7895` (69) item ①, `Ⓡ⓶`. The registration stated both limit definitions point for point, fixed
the reproduction tolerance and the four outcomes, and named `Ⓡ⓵` **with the ruler** as its expectation
on grounds it called the weaker kind. The scan then ran one substitution at a time — and the grounds
turned out to be worse than weak: they rested on a figure the cited revision had already superseded,
which is reported below rather than smoothed over.

## THE CONTROL

Reading the limit with no substitution, through the unmodified path, reproduced the banked record
**byte for byte** — calibrated `193.545304` meV, minimised `310.147692` meV, a deviation of exactly
zero against the registered `0.001` meV. ⌗ *The test for that is `git diff` on the record, not the
figure the run printed: the module rewrites that record on every run, so reading the number back out
of it would have proved nothing.*

All three arms are monotonic above their own minimum, so all three crossings are readable — unlike the

### ⇒ `Ⓡ⓵`: DEFINITIONAL, CARRIED BY ONE AXIS — AND IT IS THE PARAMETERS

Holding `omega_b` and `n_s` as the reference's calibrated arm does moves this instrument's limit
`193.545` → `103.917` meV and closes `0.550` of the gap. ⌗ *It clears the bar, and it clears it
narrowly: `0.5499` against `0.5`. A bar fixed before the scan is what makes "narrowly" a fact rather
than an argument — had it been set afterwards this number would have been worthless either way.*

## ⛔ THE REGISTERED EXPECTATION IS REFUTED, AND THE REFUTATION WAS ALREADY IN THE RECORD

The registration expected the **ruler** to carry the gap, citing `r7862` as having measured the ruler
substitution moving the calibrated limit `146.13` → `89.91` meV, about half of that column's gap.
**That citation was a misreading, and `r7862`'s own record says so.** Its two calibrated columns are:

| `r7862`'s calibrated column | limit |
|---|---|
| on the **instrument's** own ruler | `78.3308` meV |
| on the **reference's** own `rdrag` | `89.9076` meV |

So that revision measured the ruler substitution moving the limit **`+11.5767` meV AWAY** from the
reference, and banked exactly that number under the name *price of the neutrino background*. `146.13`
meV is a **superseded** figure, which `r7862`'s own RESULT states in as many words: *"`146.13` meV is
superseded: the calibrated case on the reference's terms is `89.91` meV."*

⇒ *The ruler axis was already measured, it already pointed the wrong way, and the registration's
expectation was not a weak inference from the record — it was a misreading of it. The number that
refutes the expectation was sitting in the cited revision under a different name.* ⌗ **This is the
standing guard again: a sentence is a fact about the object it is about, and which object a headline
figure belongs to has to be established before it is carried forward.** I carried a superseded figure
into a registration as a measured column.

### ⛭ AND THE SUBSTITUTION REPRODUCES, WHICH NOTHING ASKED FOR

| | the ruler substitution's move |
|---|---|
| `r7862`, on a column at `78.3308` meV | `+11.5767` |
| this revision, on a column at `193.5453` meV | `+11.7229` |

The same substitution, the same direction, and the same size to about a seventh of a meV, on columns
differing by a factor of two and a half. The registration declared a negative share reportable in
advance and this is why it was right to: the ruler's effect is not small, not favourable, and **not
column-dependent**. It is a near-constant offset the wrong way, now measured twice on different
physics.

## ⛔ AND THE REGISTRATION'S OWN "ALREADY SETTLED" WAS WRONG ABOUT THIS AXIS

The registration listed the held-versus-profiled parameters among the three axes **already settled and
not to be re-run**, citing `cc66`'s measurement that freeing `omega_b` and `n_s` moves the reference's
limit `54.28` → `54.000` meV, half a per cent. That figure is correct and it settled nothing here:

| the same axis, on | move |
|---|---|
| the reference (`cc66`'s `r7853+cc66.12`) | `−0.280` meV |
| this instrument (`Ⓢ③` here) | `−89.6286` meV |

**The same definitional axis is worth about `320` times more on this instrument than on the
reference.** ⌗ *By the standing guard, a figure measured on one object is a fact about that object: a
sentence about the reference's sensitivity to freeing two parameters is not a sentence about the
instrument's. The registration read it as one, and had the scan honoured that listing it would have
skipped the only axis that carries anything.* The axis was re-run because the registration put it in
the scan anyway, which is the one thing that saved it.

## THE TWO AXES DO NOT COMPOSE, AND ONLY THE COMBINED ARM SHOWS IT

| | move |
|---|---|
| the ruler alone | `+11.7229` |
| the ruler **on top of** held parameters | `−1.9748` |

The ruler pushes the limit away from the reference when the four parameters are free to re-fit, and
pulls it slightly toward the reference when two of them are pinned — a sign reversal of the same
substitution, worth `13.70` meV. ⌗ *One difference at a time was the order's instruction and it is the
right instrument, but on its own it would have reported the ruler as a small push the wrong way and
missed that its direction is conditional. The combined arm was what made the interference visible,
and it is the same shape of finding as `r7896`'s: what a freedom does depends on which other freedoms
are open.*

## WHAT IS STILL NOT EXPLAINED, WHICH IS ABOUT HALF

With **both** substitutions applied the instrument reads `101.9419` meV against the reference's
`30.560`: still a factor of `3.34`, or `1.89` against the `54.000` arm that `cc66` calls the
like-for-like partner for this instrument's minimised ruler.

⇒ **So `Ⓡ⓶` is half right, and the half it gets right is the smaller claim.** About `0.55` of the gap
is definitional and is now located in one named place. The rest is not definitional on any axis this
scan enumerated — it is either physical or sits in a difference neither seat has written down. ⌗ *The
order's reading was that the disagreement "sits somewhere the instrument and the reference treat
differently while computing what should be the same thing". That is now confirmed for half of it and
refuted as a complete account.*

## WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED

- Not that holding `omega_b` and `n_s` is the **right** treatment. It is the reference's treatment,
  and the measurement is of what adopting it does. Which treatment is correct is not asked here.
- Not that the remaining factor of `3.34` is physical. It is unexplained on the enumerated axes, which
  is a different statement; the registration enumerated five and three were already measured.
- Not a new limit. `193.545` meV stands as this seat's single-fitter reading and `r7888`'s record is
  untouched; these are substituted readings made to size a difference, and none of them is offered as
  a limit of record.
- Not a statement about the minimised column, which stays unreadable as `r7888` established. No
  substitution here made it readable, and none was expected to.
- Nothing about item ② of the order, the late-ISW term computed directly. That is a separate revision
  and is not begun.
