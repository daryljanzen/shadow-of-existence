## WHAT THE FOUR FALSIFICATIONS TAUGHT, EACH IN ONE LINE

**⛔ ③ and ④ are one failure.** I reasoned that a fraction of the slope is more stable than the slope,
because the slope sits in both numerator and denominator. **It does not, because the numerator is not a
fraction of the slope**: it is a separate null measurement whose carrier — the two arms' *source* combs
drifting apart — grew by **five times** on the arm of record (`0.042` → `0.199` rad) while the slope only
doubled. So `36`–`39` per cent became `115`–`108`, and "about two thirds is retention" became *none of
it is*. ⇒ **A ratio is stable under a repoint only if BOTH its parts are the same measurement on the two
banks.** *That is `r7677+cc66.3`'s seventh lesson — a repoint is like-for-like only if the thing on the
other side of the comparison moves with it — arriving in a quantity rather than in a figure.*

`cc66_r185_verify_cr_leafrec1`. It is **`+0.41`**, because *that* bank family's two legs differ by four
multipoles at the fourth peak where **this** grid family's differ by half of one. ⇒ **A fourth-peak
displacement is a property of the bank the sentence is read on, not of `LEAFREC`** — and registering a
figure measured on a different bank family is registering against the wrong thing.
