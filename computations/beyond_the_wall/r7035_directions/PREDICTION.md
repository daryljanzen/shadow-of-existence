### ⛔⛔ THE ONE THING I AM FIXING BEFORE THE RUN, BECAUSE IT IS WHERE AN ACCOUNTING CAN LIE

***A "share" of a modulation is only additive if the removal is done as a VECTOR SUBTRACTION AT THE MEASURED
PHASE, not as a ratio of amplitudes.*** *Two combs at the same period with a phase offset $\Delta$ combine as
$A^2 = A_1^2 + A_2^2 + 2A_1A_2\cos\Delta$ — so amplitudes do **not** add, and a "share" computed as
$A_{\rm channel}/A_{\rm arm}$ would be a number that closes to the whole only when $\Delta = 0$.*

⇒ **So the accounting is done twice and both are reported:**
* **PRIMARY — complex/vector**: project each onto the same $(\cos, \sin)$ basis, subtract the channel's fitted
  contribution as a **vector**, and report the residue's amplitude. *These close exactly by construction, which
  is what makes it an accounting.*
* **ALONGSIDE — the naive amplitude ratio**, precisely so the gap between them is visible. *If they differ
  materially, the difference is the phase offset doing work and it is reported as that.*

⚠ ***THIS IS THE ROW'S OWN STANDING GUARD APPLIED ONCE MORE — a total is not a match — and this time to the
word "share" itself.*** *I would rather name the trap in advance than discover it in the numbers.*

### AND THE SCALE FACTOR, WHICH IS A CHOICE AND IS DECLARED NOW

*The channel's "contribution to the arm's modulation" needs a scale. Two are available and neither is obviously
right: the **spectrum-level ratio** (what the window's accounting used) and the **least-squares projection of
the arm's modulation onto the channel's** in the same $(\cos,\sin)$ plane.* ⇒ **Both are computed and both
reported. Neither is chosen.** *If they disagree about which outcome row is reached, no outcome is reported.*
