# `r7250` — PRE-REGISTRATION: how many distinct mechanisms do the forty-five reduce to?

*Written and committed BEFORE any member is classified. `re r7247`: the order is to run `70`'s three
axes over all forty-five members of the row and report how many distinct mechanisms they reduce to,
with the count pre-registered first and — the order's own addition — **the direction of this seat's
interest named in advance.***

## ⚠ WHAT WAS READ BEFORE THIS FILE WAS WRITTEN, AND IT IS ONLY THE INSTRUMENT

*The three axes, taken from the row's own four applications of them, which state them identically:*

1. ***does anybody CHOOSE the stand-in***, or is the substituted thing incidental;
2. ***is it repairable by a better CONDITION***;
3. ***does it destroy a TRUE VERDICT, or only a detection.***

*Two members are the same mechanism when they do not separate on ANY of the three. The row has
applied this four times: one pair merged, one pair split, one filing folded in as a sharpened form,
and one case where the applying seat got it wrong and said so.* ⌗ *No member's text has been
classified yet, and the extraction of the forty-five from the row has not been run.*

## ⛔ THE DIRECTION OF THIS SEAT'S INTEREST, NAMED BEFORE THE MEASUREMENT

***I have an interest in a SMALL number and the order is right to make me say so.*** *I half-authored
this family. A collapse to a handful of mechanisms would make thirty revisions of my own arc read as
one discovery repeatedly instantiated rather than as forty-five separate findings, and it would close
a row — which is the outcome that reads as achievement.*

⇒ ***So the defence is structural rather than a promise: the classification will publish the FULL
CELL CENSUS — every member's verdict on each of the three axes — so the mechanism count is DERIVABLE
from the table by anyone who disagrees with my merges, and a reader who rejects one merge can
recompute the number without re-reading the row.*** ⌗ *Any merge or split I think the axes do not
support is routed to `70`, whose test it is, rather than forced — and the count is reported with and
without the routed ones.*

## ⛭⛭ THE THIRD OUTCOME, NAMED BEFORE IT CAN BE PRESENTED AS A DISCOVERY

*The order names two: a measured collapse to a handful, or a measured refusal to collapse.* **There
is a third, and it is the one this family's own history makes most likely:**

⇒ ***THREE BINARY AXES CANNOT SEPARATE MORE THAN EIGHT THINGS. So a small mechanism count may be
measuring THE INSTRUMENT'S RESOLUTION rather than the corpus's structure*** — *and this row's whole
subject is a reading that turns out to be a property of the instrument rather than of the object. ⌗
**If the census shows many members crowded into few cells while differing in something no axis
mentions — what the stand-in is a stand-in FOR: a paper's sentence, another instrument's output, a
ledger row, a count over a live population — then the honest report is `the axes do not resolve this
family` and not `the family is N mechanisms`.***

⌈ *A fourth, less likely and sharper: **the axes separate members that the row has already treated as
one**, which would mean an earlier merge was wrong and the count goes UP rather than down. The row
records one case of its own applying seat getting the axes wrong, so this is not hypothetical.*

## ⌗ WHAT WILL BE REPORTED WHICHEVER WAY IT COMES OUT

*The order says: report whichever you get and do not reach for the one that sounds better.* ⇒ **The
cell census is published in full, the band is scored, the direction of interest above is restated
against the result, and if the number lands inside the band in the direction I have just admitted
wanting, that coincidence is stated rather than left for a reader to notice.**

⌗ *And the one thing this revision will NOT claim: that a mechanism count closes the row. `70` holds
the test, the eighteen retired-line sites were just re-assigned to it by a standing rule, and the
second termination clause is the gate's to call, not this seat's to declare met.*
