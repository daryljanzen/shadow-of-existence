## ⌗ L8.0 — THE DISCRIMINATOR (the first deliverable; built r1145)

### The question it answers

Given two corpus quantities that **agree in value**: are they **one number**, or **two that agree**?

### ⊢ The calibration — the corpus's own worked YES and worked NO

**⊢ THE WORKED YES (`ONTOLOGY_FOUNDATION_INDEX` §0 "the double root", r1103; receipt
`storyboard_receipts/one_to_two.py`).** A monic depressed cubic with a double root ρ factors exactly one

root ratio (**120:240**). ⟹ **Four of the corpus's numbers are ONE factorisation.** *"Nariai is not an
extra input — it is what the double root **means**."*

**⊢ THE WORKED NO (same entry; P7 `lem:twoturnings`).** The lift's 1:2 (`|Im τ̃| = πα/3` against the
period `2πα/3`) is **not** the double root's 2. It is *"sin² attains its extremum at half its own

it**. And the two cubics are not the same object: the turnaround is `1−f=0`, the horizons are `f=0` —
**affinely inequivalent** — no AFFINE change of variable identifies their root sets (obstruction at fixed cubic; the two are the E=1 and E=0 ends of one turning-point family, stage 5). ⟹ **Two 2s, not one and not three.**

### ⊢ THE RESPONSE TEST — the constructive form of a NO (added r1153, from L8.1-g)

**Two quantities that respond to DIFFERENT INPUTS are two objects, whatever their value.** Vary the input
each supposedly derives from: **if one moves and the other does not, no derivation can join them, and no
amount of numerical agreement repairs it.** Worked instance: `k=2/√3` is fixed by the harmonic-killing for
**every** hinge distance `d`; `X₀=√3α` holds **only** at `d=2α`. Same radical, different responses ⟹ two.
**Unlike a search, it EXHIBITS the split** — which is what the discriminator demands of an earned NO.

### ⊢ The discriminator, read off that pair

> **A structural identity requires a DERIVATION: one object, from whose own equation both quantities are
> produced with no new input.**
> **A shared abstract type is established by a PROOF OF INEQUIVALENCE: a positive demonstration that the
> objects producing them admit no canonical map.**
> **Neither is the default. A pair with neither is UNDECIDED, and undecided is an honest verdict.**

**The asymmetry that makes it a law and not a sieve: a NO must be EARNED exactly as a YES must.**
`lem:twoturnings` does not say *"we looked for a link and found none"* — it **proves affine
inequivalence**. **A failure to find a derivation is not a proof of inequivalence; it is the UNDECIDED
state.** A ledger that reads *"no link found"* as *"two numbers"* rigs its question in the sceptical
direction exactly as *"it's all one"* rigs it in the credulous one. **Both are the same failure.**

### ★ THE INDEXING RULE — and it is the sharpest thing here, and it is the corpus's own

**The verdict is not a property of the PAIR. It is a property of the CLAIM.** The same two objects return
different, equally honest verdicts to different questions — and **the corpus already demonstrates this on
the very pair `lem:twoturnings` governs:**

- **"The two ℤ₃ symmetries are one symmetry"** → **NO**, proved (affine inequivalence of the two cubics).
- **"There is a metric ratio between the two turnings"** → **OPEN**: *"`A = ∛2·ρ` is a metric relation
  between the two turnings — it ties the comoving turnaround's **scale** to a horizon **root**.
  `lem:twoturnings` denies a canonical identification of the two **ℤ₃ symmetries**; it says nothing
  against a **ratio between two specific radii**. Well-posed, unattempted."* (§0, r1103)

**Same pair. Different claims. NO and OPEN, both honest, simultaneously.** ⟹ **Every ledger entry must
state the claim it adjudicates, not merely the pair.** An entry reading *"the 2s: NO"* is malformed; the
well-formed entry is *"the two ℤ₃'s are one symmetry: NO (proved); a metric ratio between the radii: OPEN
(named, unrun)."*

**This is what stops the rigging, and it is why the discriminator had to be built before any merge:** a
ledger indexed by pairs collapses the claims, and a collapsed claim can only be answered one way.
