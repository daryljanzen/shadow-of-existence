## ⛭ 1 · THE OPENING QUESTION — and it comes from `Q3`

**In a Cayley–Klein geometry the metric is CONSTRUCTED from the absolute.** *So any transformation preserving the
absolute preserves the cross-ratio, and therefore preserves the metric.* ***Conformal and isometric coincide:
there is no conformal freedom over and above the isometries.***

> **THE QUESTION: does that hold for the CR substrate — is its conformal group its isometry group — and if so,
> is that WHY $\alpha$ cannot be gauged away?**

*The corpus says $\alpha$ is **"the manifold's one scale"**, a length and not a mass, with $c$ and $\Lambda$ read
as unit gauges. **A conformal geometry is one in which scale is precisely what is not fixed.** So if the
substrate's conformal group is its isometry group, **$\alpha$'s invariance is not a separate fact but the same
one**, and the homothety's status as a map *between* substrates rather than *of* one is forced rather than
incidental.*

***And the negative is real and available: if the substrate admits genuine conformal transformations that move
the metric while fixing the absolute, then $\alpha$ IS gaugeable within one substrate, and the corpus's
one-scale reading needs the qualification that it holds only up to conformal frame.***
