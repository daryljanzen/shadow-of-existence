## SA-6 · THE CLASH SWEEP — is the programme stepping on its own toes? (opened r1635, Daryl)
**Daryl's formulation, and it is the test:** *"Is this a thing that if left could create confusion and ambiguity
through clashing or otherwise? If so what would be the fix that removes that, and does the proposed fix actually
work. Then implement."*
**Why it is the lowest-hanging fruit in the programme:** *"when the source can unambiguously settle a thing that
currently creates a bunch of confusion we should leverage that and take the win"* — **these are the defects that
cannot be fixed any other way and that all take one general class of fix.** A clash is not a judgement call: the
corpus's own usage settles it, and the only work is seeing it and sweeping it.
**① Standing — four steps, in order, and the third is the one that gets skipped:**
1. **Spot the clash.** One symbol or term carrying two referents; a name whose meaning depends on which document
   you are in; a convention stated two ways.
2. **Do NOT reassign a symbol that is already effectively in use.** *Invent a non-clashing one* — and check
   whether the corpus has already coined it, because twice now it had.
3. **Verify the invention does not itself clash.** Count its existing uses corpus-wide **before** adopting it.
   *Skipping this is how a clash becomes two clashes.*
4. **Sweep to exhaustion, define the new symbol at first use with the reason, and record the canon in the
   glossary** — never only in the paper that prompted it.
**② Sweep:** rides the full-corpus read-through, and enters the resolved canon in the glossary (`§0`) with a
pointer from `PHYSICAL_VALUES_LEDGER` §A. **The instance, its pattern and its history go to ARC 9, and the scrap itself goes to
`THE_WISDOM_LEDGER` — into the compartment for the moment it applies (A–J), with its discharge condition —
never into the document that was wrong.**
**⛭ And write the discharge condition when you PLACE a scrap, not when you meet it later** — at placing, the
author still knows what would make it spent; afterwards it can only be guessed. *A problem area that carries the record of its own wrongness is unreadable and
is a grep landmine: a node searching a glossary for a definition should not meet "simply wrong" attached to
something correct.*
**Instances so far (r1635):** the star subscript — four star-adjacent symbols, two rendering identically, two in
one paragraph of P15; resolved with `r_N` (**which P12 had already coined and nobody propagated**) and `r_HE`
(new, verified free), retiring `r_\star` corpus-wide across 47 sites. And the seam names — where **the glossary
was right and the usage was wrong**, qualified at seven sites to terms the corpus already had.
**⛔ THE TRAP, and it caught this action twice on its own first run — read it before step 1.**
**A term can have THREE definition levels: the general definition · the positional or species name · the
geometry-specific VALUE.** *Seam* has all three: a turning point of the slicing curve (general) · front /
back / equatorial / Nariai (which one) · $r=2M$ in Schwarzschild, $\alpha/\sqrt3$ at Nariai, $M$-dependent
undercritical (the values). **Two different values are NOT two competing definitions, and a term is not
clashing merely because it resolves differently in different geometries.**
*What went wrong: a node read a Level-3 value as a Level-1 definition, tested the $\Lambda=0$ value in the SdS
metric, called the glossary "simply wrong" (r1634), then "fixed" the phantom clash by qualifying seven
correct general usages into geometry-specific ones (r1635) — **losing information and reverted r1636**.*
**So step 0 of this action: identify which LEVEL the two uses sit at. If they are values at Level 3, there is
no clash — there is a levels statement owed in the glossary.** A real clash is one symbol or term carrying two
referents *at the same level*.
