## ⚠ §3 · THE THREE LIVE CONFLICT CONDITIONS, worked

### ⚠ F1 · Does CR forbid what the mechanism requires?

*The Higgs mechanism needs **a scalar in a definite gauge representation**. And `PO-4`/P13 establish that
**CR's gauge group does not arise as a continuous isometry of the substrate** — the index obstruction, with the
one geometric opening being the **discrete orientation parity** (`U3`).*
⇒ ***The question is therefore sharp: if CR cannot host a gauge group geometrically, can it host a scalar charged
under one?***
**⌗ THE ANSWER IS THAT THE QUESTION IS ONE LEVEL TOO LOW, and P13 says so in its own voice:** *what is proved is
**not** that the representation content is unreachable but that **one route to it is closed** — the route through
a connected-group isometry. **The gauge sector "stays the ordinary route"** (P7 front matter).* ⇒ ***So CR does not
forbid the Higgs sector's ingredients; it declines to supply them, which is a different thing.*** **F1 does not
fire** — *but it fires **if** a future CR result claimed the gauge group as forced, because then the scalar's
representation would be forced too and could disagree.* ⌗ ***RECORDED AS A STANDING TRIP-WIRE: any CR result that
promotes the gauge group from "ordinary route" to "forced" re-opens F1.***

### ⚠ F3 · The exact $S_3$ — and this is where the two sectors genuinely MEET

*P14 delivers an **exact** global $S_3$. **The observed world has no exact family symmetry**, and the corpus knows
it:* ***"an exact $S_3$ delivered here must be broken by whatever supplies the masses, and the manner of that
breaking is a constraint this sector inherits rather than a freedom. We record that as an obligation, not a
result."***
⇒ ***THIS IS THE REAL POINT OF CONTACT, AND IT RUNS IN THE DIRECTION OPPOSITE TO THE ONE A READER EXPECTS.***
**It is not that CR constrains the Higgs sector. It is that the Higgs sector constrains CR** — *the manner in
which the Yukawa couplings break $S_3$ is a **constraint CR inherits**.*
⌗ *And P14 already checked it against a literature rather than leaving it standing, **because the check could have
gone badly**: $S_3$ is among the most studied discrete flavour symmetries, the symmetric limit gives **mass
matrices of a universal two-term form** (identity plus democracy matrix), and the breaking programme is
established work.* ***So F3 does not fire either — but only because someone did the check.***

### ⚠ F4 · Is the hypercharge derivation circular?

*The premise used is **the existence** of Yukawa couplings — that each fermion has a gauge-invariant mass term
available — **not their values**.* ⇒ ***That is a REPRESENTATION-THEORETIC constraint, not a magnitude***: it says
which hypercharge assignments admit invariant $\\bar\\psi\\phi\\psi$ terms at all. *The values remain entirely the
ordinary route.* **F4 does not fire** — ⚠ *but the distinction is thin enough to be worth stating in the paper,
because* ***a reader who slides "existence of Yukawas" into "Yukawa physics" will read the hypercharges as
imported.***
