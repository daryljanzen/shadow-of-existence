## I. The construction

**Start with the hole.** A circle of radius α, centred on the axis. That is the throat of the
substrate, seen from above, and α is not a number — it is the gauge. Everything below is α re-read.

**The Thales circle sets itself.** Ask where a hinge could go, and the geometry answers before you
do. Take the circle centred on the edge of the hole nearest the hinge, running from the hole's own
centre outward. There is only one such circle: centre (α, 0), radius α. It is *the hole, translated
onto its own edge*.

**The hinge is where that circle ends** — at 2α. Not placed there. That is simply the far end of the
circle the hole drew. The one thing that looked like a free choice in the whole construction is an
output.

And now everything follows, and nothing is chosen:

- **The hinge's height.** The substrate satisfies −X₀² + |X|² = α². At |X| = 2α this gives
  X₀ = √3 α. The hinge sits √3 α above the equator.
- **The hinge's power.** The power of a point with respect to a circle is |X|² − α² — Euclid's
  quantity, defined by his tangent–secant theorem. At the hinge it is 4α² − α² = **3α²**.
- **The tangent length.** √(power) = √3 α. *The tangent from the hinge is exactly as long as the
  hinge is high.* Hold that; it becomes the keystone.
- **The angle.** 60° = arctan(√3) = X₀/α = L_tangent/α = √(power)/α. Four statements, one number.
- **The triangle.** Three hinges, 120° apart, at radius 2α. Their triangle's sides are tangent to
  the hole at their midpoints — and that is not a second fact. An equilateral triangle's inradius is
  half its circumradius, so tangency happens *if and only if* the circumradius is 2α. **The
  tangency, the 2:1, the 60°, and the hinge's placement are one fact.**

Fourteen objects. One input.

## II. The keystone — the optics *is* the causal structure

The power of a point is |X|² − α². The substrate says |X|² − α² = X₀².

**So the power of any point is the square of its height.**

Now put those together. The tangent from a point has length √(power) = |X₀|. A null line in the
embedding is one whose spatial length equals its time drop. The tangent runs |X₀| across, and the
point sits |X₀| up.

> **The tangent from any point to the throat is a null line of the embedding.**
> **The power of a point and the null condition are the same equation.**

Two lines of proof, and both are classical: `tangent² = |X|² − α²` is Euclid III; `X₀² = |X|² − α²`
is the hyperboloid. **The only modern ingredient is the minus sign in the metric.**

Verified at 600 random heights: max |ds²| = 5.7 × 10⁻¹⁴.

So *"it's all optics"* was never a metaphor. The sightlines that touch the hole are light rays. The
construction's tangency conditions are null conditions. And the hinge stops being a stipulation: its
tangent length **is** its height **is** the root of its power — three statements, one fact. Placing
the hinge at 2α was never a choice about optics. It was a choice about height, and the optics
followed.

**The bound, and it sharpens rather than weakens.** The figure is not all tangents. The chords, the
pin, the triangle's edges are **secants**, and a secant is not null. The sightlines that *touch* are
light rays; the ones that *cut* are not. Which is the interesting part: Euclid's power theorem is
about secants; the null condition is the degenerate case where the secant's two points **merge** —
and that merge point is exactly where the light cone is.

**And the rulings cannot rest.** A null line cannot stand still in time: if dX₀ = 0 then |dX| = 0
and it is a point, not a line. So no ruling stays on one level. One null line runs from (−60°,
below) through (0°, the equator) to (+60°, above), collinear to machine zero. The twelve hinges
above and the twelve below are not a mirror pair — **they are the two ends of the same rulings.**
You cannot draw the top alone; the lines that make it leave it.

## III. The physics — the Thales circle is the dial

This is where the geometry becomes Schwarzschild–de Sitter.

**The dial.** Swing a slicing plane about the hinge. Let its perpendicular distance from the axis be
d. A plane through a hinge at azimuth χ, with normal at angle θ, has **d = 2α cos(θ − χ)**.

**And d alone fixes the physics.** Nothing else about the plane enters:

| d | the plane vs the hole | the geometry |
|---|---|---|
| d > α | **misses** | overcritical — no horizon on the forward branch |
| d = α | **tangent** | **Nariai** — the double root |
| d < α | **cuts** | undercritical — three distinct horizons |

**That is P3's trichotomy, and it is the conic trichotomy.** The dial is the plane, turning.

**And the Thales circle is the dial's track** — the locus of every cut's closest approach to the
axis. 400 sightlines, max error 2 × 10⁻¹⁶. Not a construction aid:

- **Overcritical:** the plane cuts the substrate in X₀² − s² = d² − α², a two-sheeted hyperbola with
  vertices directly above the Thales point. **At the dial's end (d = 2α) that vertex *is the
  hinge*.** The hinge is the vertex of the extreme overcritical cut.
- **Undercritical:** the Thales point is the secant's midpoint — because a chord's midpoint is
  always the foot of the perpendicular.
- **One circle, one locus.** Vertex on one arc, midpoint on the other — the same point, wearing what
  the hole does to it.

**And Nariai is where the two readings meet, and it is the best part.** At d = α the plane is
tangent and the cut degenerates: X₀² − s² = 0, so s = ±X₀ — **two straight lines, and they are
null.**

> **The cut at Nariai *is* the two rulings.**

## IV. Why Nariai is not a coincidence

P3's own dial: the slicing parameter is r₀ = (2/√3) sin w, and the mass relation is the pure
triple-angle 2M = (2/3√3) sin 3w.

The hinge at 2α makes the hole subtend a half-angle of arcsin(α/2α) = arcsin(½) = **30°**. So
**sin w = ½**.

Put that into the triple-angle identity, which is a schoolbook identity and nobody's convention:

> sin 3w = 3 sin w − 4 sin³w = 3(½) − 4(½)³ = 3/2 − 1/2 = **1**

**sin 3w = 1 is the maximum. The maximum is Nariai.**

The tangent from the hinge is Nariai — not because two 30°s happen to coincide, but because
3(½) − 4(½)³ = 1. The hinge's placement sets sin w = ½; the triple angle then peaks there, of its
own accord.

**And the 4 is inside the sine's own peak.** sin 3w = 1 means 3w = 90° = 360/4 — *a sine peaks a
quarter turn from its zero*. So w = 360/(3 × 4) = **30**.

> **hinge at 2α ⟹ sin w = ½ ⟹ sin 3w = 1 ⟹ 3w = 360/4 ⟹ w = 360/(3×4) = 30**

The kaleidoscope's 30°, P3's Nariai dial angle, and the hole's half-angle at the hinge are **one 30°,
by one route**. And the two 4s are one 4: the dial's {axis, perpendicular} **is** {the sine's zero,
the sine's peak}.

**12 = 3 × 4, both factors forced.** The 3 by the triple angle. The 4 by the sine's own quarter.

## V. The isometry — and why there are four of everything

**The dial is a ray, not a line.** A line has 180°; a ray has 360°, and the extra 180° **is the
side** — which a line does not have. That is where the chirality lives.

**The physics depends only on (θ − χ).** The hinge's azimuth and the dial are not two coordinates.
**They are one.**

> **Moving the hinge by +δ is turning the dial by −δ.** Verified exactly across 300 random triples.
> And the map that realises it is the azimuthal rotation — a genuine isometry of the substrate,
> SO(2) inside SO(5,1).

**A hinge transpose *is* a dial turn.**

And then: φ_Schwarzschild = χ + 180. **Four observers, four zeros, 90° apart, one hole.** Right calls
180 "zero", top calls 270, left calls 0, bottom calls 90. The dial's four quadrants are not four
regions of one map — **they are four vantages disagreeing about where zero is.** That is the corpus's
perspectival thesis, drawn.

**Why exactly four, with no principle involved.** The physics is invariant under swapping front and
back (ψ → ψ+180 is the same line with its rays exchanged) and under the mirror (ψ → −ψ), and under
no bare quarter. So the dial's 360° quotients by ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ — **the fundamental domain is exactly 90°.**

And 0–90 is the whole story, once: the hinge's own vertex (|d| = 2α) → Nariai (|d| = α, the two null
rulings) → Schwarzschild (|d| = 0). Every regime, exactly once. The other 270° is those same cuts

**So n = 8 is not excluded by a principle. It is excluded by a fact.** The dial's physical period is

## VI. What a triple is

P3 says it: the three roots of the horizon cubic are the three preimages of 3w under the sine —
w, π/3 − w, −π/3 − w.

Drawn, that means: **a triple is three lines in the overhead with the same mass.** One geometry,
three designations. And their three r₀ values *are* the three roots — each one is the others'
horizon.

- **Their feet lie on the Thales circle.** That is why the circle is in the figure at all: it is the

- **w = 0 and w = 60 are the same triple** — roots {−1, 0, +1} both. De Sitter, twice, under two
  designations.
- **At w = 30 two of the three preimages merge** (30, 30, −90). **The double root is two sky angles
