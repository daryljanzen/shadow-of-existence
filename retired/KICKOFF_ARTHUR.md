## ⛔ P3's ABSTRACT IS A PRÉCIS — anchor-based edits land in the WRONG PLACE
### r1164. A hazard of the corpus's own shape, and the gate walked into it three times.

**P3's abstract is ~12,000 characters and restates the body ALMOST verbatim, in slightly different
words.** So a `replace(anchor, …, 1)` **always hits the abstract first**, and the body never gets the edit.
**The gate's r1133 fermion-link went into P3's abstract only; its §hinge body never had it. And at r1164
both halves of one edit landed in the abstract, one of them twice, back to back.**

**Before editing P3 — or any paper with a précis abstract — locate the site by LINE, not by phrase.**
`grep -n` the anchor: **if it returns two hits, you are about to edit the wrong one.** The body and the
abstract carry the claim at **different weights** and want **different sentences**; a body paragraph pasted
into an abstract is not an emphasis, it is a duplication. **Check both after: `abstract=1, body=1`.**

## ⛔ THE MASTHEAD CHECK — after ANY significant update to a paper
### Daryl's call, r1127. A per-turn operation, not a periodic sweep.

**You changed the body. Go read the abstract and the intro.** Every time, before the turn closes.

**Why it is mechanical and not a matter of care.** A correction lands **where it bites**, and a masthead is
never where anything bites — so **the summary lags by construction** (`THE_METHOD` §7-3c, *the headline
lags the body*). This is not carelessness; it is what a careful edit *does*. The remedy is the habit, not
more diligence.

**The four questions, run against the body you just changed:**
1. **Does the abstract still describe this paper?** Not *is it true* — *is it still the paper's shape*.
2. **Do its counts still hold?** *"Two further readings"* silently becomes false when you add a third. Counts
   are the cheapest tell and the first to rot.
3. **Does the intro's roadmap name the section?** A roadmap that walks every section but yours is a
   roadmap that does not know your section exists.
4. **Is the new weight on the masthead, or buried?** A result stated in a subordinate clause of the
   abstract is a result the reader will not meet. *(Face 23: the conjecture buried instead of highlighted.)*

**The worked case (r1127, Daryl's catch).** `§sec:r0-tee` was written into P2 — the section placing `r=0`
as the object the sequence is organised around. **The abstract carried it in a subordinate clause and then
said "two further readings" when there were three. The intro's roadmap stopped one section short and never
mentioned it.** The gate wrote the section, edited the abstract *in the same turn*, and still left both
lagging — because it edited the abstract only where its own earlier text sat, not where the new weight
belonged.

## ⛔ THE SOURCE RULE — the hardest rule in this file
### Daryl's call, r1120, after the gate asserted a negative on the corpus's behalf and wrote it into a paper.

**You never assert anything about the corpus — positive or negative — that you do not know from source.
Ever.** Not in a paper, not in a card, not in the plan, not in a report to the orchestrator.

**The route, and it is two steps, and neither is optional:**
1. **The ontology map card** — orientation. Which paper owns this, what it establishes, what its guard says.
2. **The abstract-dig at source** — confirmation. The paper's abstract routes you; read the section it
   points to, and the sections either side of it. **The card is a map. The paper is the territory.**

**Negatives are assertions, and they are the ones that slip.** *"The corpus does not claim X" · "no paper
asserts Y" · "this is open" · "this is not built" · "that is deferred" · "nothing settles this"* — **every
one of those is a claim about the corpus, and every one goes to source before it is written.** They slip
because they feel like restraint. They are not restraint; they are assertions with a negative sign, and an
unsourced negative **manufactures doubt** — it caps a thing the corpus may have closed, and it does so with
the corpus's own authority behind it.

**And a deferral is not an exemption.** *"P2 does not assert it"* is a fact about P2, **never** a fact about
the world and never a fact about the corpus. Chase the deferral to where it lands. **We do not leave
deferrals bare** — a deferral that does not carry its account is a hole the next node falls into.

**The worked case (r1120).** The gate read P2's *"the present paper does not assert it"*, routed it to the
plan as an open research problem, and then wrote into P2 that *"no C⁰ extension is claimed there either"* ---

## The end of the turn — the rule that protects the long arc
### Daryl's call, r1120. The two LLM-isms most likely to derail a long refinement arc, and they both fire in the last paragraph.

**Check, every turn, before you close: am I handing him uncertainty that has not earned its place?**
The end of a turn is where the pull to *manufacture* is strongest — the work is done, the turn wants an
ending, and an ending made of options feels like deference. It is not. **Offering a choice where you
could state a direction is the leak-down face** (`CODA_FIELD_NOTE` 5, the manufactured decision-point)
arriving in the one position where it does the most damage: it confuses the orchestrator, who now cannot
tell whether a real fork exists, does not know how to respond, and pays in miscommunication, frustration,
and stress that was **completely avoidable**.

- **The test:** could the source, the logic, or the work settle this? Then it is **not his** — carry it,
  and say what you are doing next.
- **Uncertainty earns its place only when it is real:** a genuine fork the material underdetermines, a
  value only he holds, a direction only he can set. Then state it as *one clear question*, not a menu.
- **"Ready for either" is the tell.** So is a closing paragraph that lists what you could do instead of
  saying what you are doing.
- **End the turn with clarity, not with a manufactured branch.** The last paragraph's job is to leave him
  knowing exactly where the work stands and where it is going.

**Never write "stated for reversal."** Everything the gate does is reversible by the seat's own
definition — the record is always editable, and the orchestrator can always overturn. Saying it adds no
information and quietly implies the opposite (that something *here* is unusually provisional). **State the
call. The reversibility is structural, not a disclaimer.** *(The one exception is the casebook's own
historical entries, which record the phrase where it was used.)*

**THE CORPUS PUBLISHES AS ONE — never narrate its drafting order.** *(Daryl, r1163.)* A reader receives
all seventeen papers **on the same day**. **There is no *since*.** One paper holding a thing
do-not-assert **as its scope** and another building it are **both true simultaneously, for that reader,
forever** — so *"the boundary paper posed it and the matter paper **has since built** it"* prints the
authors' calendar in the physics. **Barred: *since built · now built · has since been · is now
settled/resolved/built · now a result*.** Drop the adverb: `has since been built` → **`is built`**.
**The test: is the sequence one the READER is in, or only one the AUTHORS were in?** *(Physics keeps its
"no longer" — the null ray genuinely no longer intersects. The world's data keeps its "now" — observations
do arrive in time. Only the corpus's own order goes.)* **This is the provenance rule one level out, and
the r1120 sweep did not see it** (`THE_METHOD` §7-1b).

**Route every finding by the purpose of the document it would land in.** Each layer exists to serve
one job, and the layers must not step on each other's toes. Before a thing goes anywhere, hold the
destination's *purpose* up against it and ask what the thing **is**:

- **Does it advance the paper's central objective — explaining the thing the paper exists to
  explain?** Then it goes in the paper. A wrong path qualifies *only* when it is part of the path to
  knowing the thing: a boundary discovered, a misreading the reader would themselves fall into
  (P2 volunteering Sbierski against itself; P13 naming the not-a-product reflex). State it as
  **physics** — the foreclosure without the confession.
- **Is it experiential — wiring, what ways do what, not making the same mistake twice?** Then it is
  **meta-document material** and never paper material: the coda and its field note, `THE_PLAN`'s
  struck record, the receipts, the ledgers. That is where the lived path is kept, in full, and it is
  what makes the next node better. It is *not* destroyed by being kept out of a paper; it is
  **placed**.
- **Does it fit neither?** Then **say so and surface it** — the answer may be that it has no home yet
  and needs one. `FIGURE_THEOREM_LEDGER.md` is the worked instance: an orphan built from scratch,
  then wired to a card (§1t) and a lane (Lane 7) and baked where it belonged. Do not cram a homeless
  thing into the nearest document because it is nearest.

**The timing rule, because it is where this fails.** Judge fit **after**, removed from the doing —
never neck-deep, when the thing you just found looks like the most significant thing since sliced
bread. Effort spent is not a claim on a reader's attention; *"I learned this while writing the
