# ⛭⛭ THE GATES — the standing tests an entry must pass before it is staged

## ⌗ `RG-1` · THE PLACEMENT GATE — *where a worked-through thing finally belongs*

*Set r2377 by Daryl, correcting this line's own misreading; **generalised r2378 by Daryl, and the
generalisation is the point.** It was written as a rule about papers. **It is not.** Asked whether
`CONSOLIDATE`'s own spent sections should be homed in the same pass that builds its receiver, Daryl's answer
was that the same rule decides it:*

> ***Things are not "left lying around all over the place", but when worked should be homed appropriately,
> at weight, with an eye to having everything eventually in the final places where they should be if you
> look at them and ask where each should be.***

***So `RG-1` is a placement gate on every document in the programme, and the paper case is its first
application rather than its scope.*** *Three clauses, and each is doing work:*
- ***homed appropriately*** *— not deleted, not left where it fell. **Every worked-through thing has a right
  place**, and the gate is what finds it.*
- ***at weight*** *— it lands as what it now is. A spent plan section lands as **record**; a live result
  lands as **result**. Landing a spent thing at live weight is how a document acquires a stale front, and
  landing a live thing at record weight is how a result gets lost.*
- ***with an eye to the final places*** *— **the test is not "where does this go now" but "where should this
  be when the programme is finished"**, asked of each thing on its own. That is what makes the pass converge
  instead of shuffling.*

**⌗ THE CONSEQUENCE FOR SEQUENCING, and it answers the question that produced the generalisation.** *A pass
that builds a home and a pass that fills it are **one pass**, not two — because the placement question is
asked of the thing, and you cannot ask it without deciding where the thing goes. **Splitting them leaves the
worked thing lying around for exactly the interval between them**, which is the condition the rule names.*

> ***A paper does not report on all the things it did not have figured out at some stale point in the past
> and has since worked through.*** *What is owed is that the content be **fully incorporated into the
> positive syntheses across the papers**, and that the stale state be **struck and moved to the appropriate
> document** — `CONSOLIDATE` above all, which is the document that exists to build the historical status of
> the programme and how it developed. **The final papers present the physics as the finished product, as
> clearly and coherently as they can.***

**⛭ THE GATE ITSELF, and it is a gate rather than a ban.** *If an account of **how** a problem was worked —
including the actual historical sequencing, and how it actually came together — is **particularly
pedagogical** and worth a paragraph or a section of the paper itself, **then it belongs there and we should
do it**. What is refused is grandfathering as a matter of course.*

***The question the gate asks, and it must be answered in one sentence per site:***

> **How does including this in the FINAL PRESENTATION add something — in a way that COMPLEMENTS
> `CONSOLIDATE`'s description of the same thing in its own framing?**

**⌗ Read the two halves.** *`add` rules out a passage that merely records. **`complements` rules out a
passage that duplicates** — if the paper says what `CONSOLIDATE` already says, in the same framing, the
paper is carrying the consolidation's job and doing it worse, because the paper's framing is the physics
and not the development.*

**⌗ THE THREE DISPOSITIONS**, and every site gets exactly one:
- **FOLD** — *the content is real and the staleness is the wrapper.* **Incorporate into the positive
  synthesis, strike the wrapper.** *This is the default and is expected to take most sites.*
- **MOVE** — *the account is the programme's record and has no pedagogical job in the paper.* **Strike from
  the paper; land in `CONSOLIDATE` in its framing, not the paper's.**
- **KEEP** — *the gate is passed, with the one-sentence answer written into the staged entry* **and, when
  it lands, into the section as its own justification** — *so the next node meets the reason rather than
  re-litigating it.*

**⚠ WHAT `RG-1` IS NOT.** *It is **not** licence to delete. **Nothing is struck from a paper until its
content has a home**, and a MOVE that has not landed in `CONSOLIDATE` is not a MOVE, it is a loss. The
negatives-are-the-map rule is untouched — **it governs the programme's record, and `CONSOLIDATE` is where
that record lives.** The error `RG-1` corrects was reading a rule about the **record** as a rule about the
**publication**.*

## ⌗ `RG-2` · THE COMPLEMENT GATE — *no fact lives in two framings by accident*

*The mirror of `RG-1`, and it fires in the other direction: when a passage is MOVED to `CONSOLIDATE`, the
paper must be checked for what it now **fails** to say. A history removed from a paper sometimes took a
result with it.* ***Done-test for every MOVE: name the sentence in the paper that now carries the result,
or the MOVE is not complete.***
