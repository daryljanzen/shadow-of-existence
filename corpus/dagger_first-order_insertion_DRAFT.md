## Why the r154 beat is superseded (a sweep finding)

The r154 beat split the two ends on "how hard each curves" (line-vs-bowl: a flat floor and a
steep bowl meeting at a point). That correctly illustrates the *general* "meet to first order,
split at second" truth — but it **misrepresents this geometry**. The two turning points bend
**identically**: $|r''|=M$ at both (one a peak, one a trough, same steepness). They do *not*
split on the profile's bend. The lopsided curvature is the **sweep** dividing that identical
bend by distance-to-axis — which is exactly the dagger's own thesis, "the curve is even-handed,
the map is not," and exactly the double-dagger's off-axis pivot. A beat that splits them on
curving contradicts both essays. So: **the dagger needs no insertion** (its hinge is resolved
through the Mercator/sweep, and it rightly defers the lopsidedness), and the beat's true content
— first-order identity, the asymmetry all in the sweep — already lives in the double-dagger,
better expressed as the off-axis pivot.

## Optional sharpening for the double-dagger (drafted for register, NOT inserted)

The double-dagger already answers the lopsidedness (§79–85: the black-hole chair is forced to
pivot its sweep about $r=0$, off the surface's true axis, and the distortion piles up at the
pivot). The **cone tip** is a cleaner image of *why* a forced off-axis pivot spikes. It would

**Two-axis caution (so it does not read as a contradiction).** In the double-dagger "the axis"
means the *surface's true symmetry axis* (the cosmic sweep's), and $r=0$ is **off** it. In the
cone image "the axis" means *the black-hole sweep's own rotation axis*, which passes **through**
$r=0$. Both are true and consistent: $r=0$ is on the black-hole sweep's axis (distance zero from
it $\Rightarrow$ spike) and off the cosmic surface's symmetry axis (the wrong pivot). Any
inserted wording must say *whose* axis each time.
