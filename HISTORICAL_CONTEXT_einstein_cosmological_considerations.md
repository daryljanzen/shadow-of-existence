## ★ 1. THE DIAGNOSIS
> *"The standard model accurately **describes** the cosmological data, yet the nature of what we're measuring
> remains **unexplained**: we lack good reasons why observations should be as they are; why we should
> **expect** to see what we do."*

*And the crisis is of an unfamiliar kind: **"we now lack the guidance of discrepancy between model and
observation that science is fit to resolve, and have only discontent with an impossibly fine-tuned description
of the facts."*** Unlike Ptolemy→Kepler, there is no residual to chase. **The failure is explanatory, not
observational** — which is exactly why it can be dropped for sixty years without anything breaking.

## ★★★ 2. THE MECHANISM — Einstein's *inaction* is the load-bearing part
*In the 1920s the $\lambda$-term was understood as **the driver of expansion** — de Sitter: "a measure of the
inherent expanding force of the universe". **Then Einstein removed it.***

*`Einstein1931a` is a three-page paper. It rejects $\lambda$ **without argument**: he calls it "anyhow
theoretically unsatisfactory" and* ***gives no reason at all*** *— "merely expressed his distaste for the term
adjectivally, i.e. as a matter of fact." He offers **no argument against cosmic repulsion**; he leaves it
unmentioned.*

**⇒ THE PARADIGM-FORMING MOVE WAS A SILENCE.** *The driver of expansion was removed **without a replacement
being proposed or the need for one being acknowledged**. The 1931 letter to Tolman confirms he knew what he was
rejecting ("the somewhat adventurous way of interpreting Hubble's line shifts") — but that reasoning **stayed in
private correspondence**. *This is the paper's central historical claim, and it is what makes the resulting
gap invisible: **nothing was ever argued, so there was nothing to rebut.***

## ★★ 3. THE AD HOC LESSON — and that modern cosmology is repeating it
> *"it is simply bad practice to invent new hypotheses of an ad hoc nature, just to 'save the appearances'&hellip;
> the hardcore empiricist stance — that accurately describing the facts is the **only** thing that matters, so
> long as the appearances are 'saved' — is **against** progress."*

*The $\lambda$ record is a **strike-out**: Einstein's static universe, Lemaître's age problem, the 1967
"$z=1.95$ phenomenon" — **each an ad hoc invocation, each killed by better data.** And the standard model now
carries **inflaton, dark matter, dark energy**: *"there's no good reason why the type of inflation that's
allowed by the data should actually have taken place, why there should be any dark matter or dark energy, **or
why the universe, as described, should ever have come to expand at all**."*

## ★★★★★ 4. **WHY QUANTUM GRAVITY CANNOT SUPPLY THE ANSWER** — the argument that forces a structural resolution
> *"there's no reason to expect that any modification to theory there should explain why a universe filled
> with, and for billions of years primarily influenced by, stuff that **would work to keep it from expanding**,
> should ever come to expand rather than remain tightly bound or contract. Given that the decelerative force
> increases exponentially towards $R(t)=0$, in all likelihood the quantum-gravitational modification of that
> point won't describe an enormously repulsive Planck-scale moment."*

*And Einstein 1945 is quoted making exactly the overlooked move — arguing the singularity is not to be taken
mathematically seriously. **The paper's reply is decisive:*** *"The problem has little to do with the
description of the initial point **as** a singularity: it's that the first instant&hellip; must explain why there
**should** subsequently be an expanding universe, **which all the well-defined physics actually opposes**."*

**⇒ THIS IS THE ARGUMENT THAT MAKES ENTRY 18 NECESSARY RATHER THAN OPTIONAL.** *If no Planck-scale story can
supply the initial rate, **the answer must be structural** — and CR's is: the lift carries $\dd r/\dd s$ from
zero to divergent over **no cosmic time**, and the divergences are **effective**, perspectival consequences of
parametric motion in $r$. ***Not a repulsive moment. A geometry.***
