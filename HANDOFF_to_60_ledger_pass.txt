THE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST

Everything that reads as unfinished ends the turn in exactly one of three states:
closable now, so close it; not closable in a turn, so it gets a PO row; or generic
scientific practice, so REMOVE it. There is no fourth state. Nothing may be swept
into obscurity, softened, or retitled -- an item that cannot be found again is worse
than one left open.

And a PO row means all three of: the row in THE_REGISTER.md, and ORDER, GROUP and
EST entries in scripts/regen_frontier.py. The generator throws if any is missing.

THE METHOD, IN ONE LINE EACH

  1. Read the whole passage, never the row. The row is a pointer.
  2. Follow every pointer to source -- read the kill file, RUN the receipt.
     When a register and a paper disagree, the paper governs, but read both.
     Read the row's own NOTE too. Rows carry the history of their disputes, and
     one of them caught an error of mine three revisions old.
  3. When a claim changes, sweep the phrase corpus-wide IN THE SAME TURN.
  4. Use word-boundary and wrapped-form searches. Exact-string matching failed five
     times in one session because LaTeX wraps across lines.
  5. Finish the three dependency grains: the P07 table row, the figure edge label,
     and the HTML companion via scripts/gen_matrix_html_tbody.py run from corpus/.

WHAT EACH TURN MUST PRODUCE

  the item worked to one of the three classes; all 17 papers compiling; the sweep
  green; the open ledger settled (rewrites change hashes, so retire dead rows and
  verdict new ones); a commit saying what was found INCLUDING what was found wrong in
  your own earlier work; and the tally above, updated.

WHAT KEEPS GOING WRONG

  Triaging instead of working. Reporting "the rest are only comments" as if that
  settled it -- a comment misleads a node exactly as much as live prose, because a
  node arrives by grep. Renaming a mess instead of clearing it. Calling a handover a
  debt. Leaving your own fix stale two revisions later. Stopping at the first
  plausible answer instead of computing the right object. Opening a PO row on a
  premise I had not checked against the paper -- a new row needs its premise verified
  exactly as a closure does. Reading a fix as a bug, because a comment warned about
  the thing it had already fixed. Breaking my own rules in the REPLACEMENT text --
  writing revision-history into the very sentence that was fixing a one-state
  violation. And accepting a paper's own claim about what its evidence shows: P12 said
  a computation "bears on" an attribution, and checked, it discriminates nothing.

  All six of those are mine, from today. They are in the instrument document so they
  do not get repeated.
