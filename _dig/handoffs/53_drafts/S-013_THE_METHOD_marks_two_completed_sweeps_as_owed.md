## ⚠ AND THE DEFERRAL AUDIT'S RESULT IS LOAD-BEARING ELSEWHERE, WHICH IS HOW I FOUND IT

*The audit did not merely close — its **null** became one of the corpus's standing priors:*

> **`THE_WISDOM_LEDGER`, LIVE:** *"⚑ **THE BASE RATE**: in a corpus this comprehensive, 'unstated /
> open / missing / contradictory' is **OVERWHELMINGLY LIKELY TO BE A STALE LINK**. Not a mood of
> deference — the honest posterior. **Evidence: the deferral audit closed 33 deferrals across 5 items
> and found not one real frontier.**"*

⇒ ***The corpus quotes this sweep's completion as the evidence for one of its most-used disciplines,
while the method document that owns the sweep still lists it as work to be done.*** *A node reading
`THE_METHOD` to find what is left would pick up a closed sweep and re-run 33 deferrals whose verdicts
are recorded.*

---

## ⇒ THE CAUSE IS NAMED IN THE DOCUMENT'S OWN HEADER, AND IT IS THE INTERESTING PART

> *"**DATED r2389 — `r1624`; no c54 marker, so untouched across the whole fork span.** It is a
> `METHOD` document and its subject is procedure, **so it does not go stale the way a position
> does**."*

**That reasoning is right about most of the file and wrong about §7.** *`THE_METHOD`'s procedure —
dig-don't-grep, ducks, restraint, the perimeter class — genuinely is timeless.* **But §7 is a
register**, and its rows carry **`RUN (r1120)` / `OWED`** — which are **positions**, not procedure.
⇒ ***A METHOD document containing a status register is a mixed-kind document, and the "methods do not
go stale" exemption was applied to the whole file.***

⌗ **This is exactly the failure `THE_OPERATING_MANUAL` §3 warns about for `INDEX.md`** — *"KIND BEFORE
CONTENT … the section carries the document's **kind**, and the kind tells you how to read it, whether
its staleness matters."* **Here the kind is declared at the top of the file and one section does not
obey it.**

## ⇒ WHAT TO CHANGE

1. **Mark §7's rows 2 and 3e `RUN`, with their revisions and their verdicts** — *r1144 and r1140, and
   the deferral audit's five verdicts, which are worth carrying because the null is the evidence for
   `THE_BASE_RATE`.*
2. **⛭ And add the currency caveat to the header**, since the general exemption is what let this
   stand: *"the procedure does not go stale; **§7's per-instance statuses are positions and do**."*
   **That is the one-line fix that stops the next instance.**
3. **The four remaining rows should be checked the same way rather than assumed** — §3 (frontier,
   both faces), §3c (stale-advertisement), §3d (defragmentation), §4 (idiom). *I have not verified
   those; a grep for their completion in `THE_PLAN` returns nothing conclusive, and per this
   document's own law that is not evidence of absence.* ⛔ **Named as the specific unrun question:
   for each of the four, is there a `THE_PLAN` or `CORPUS_MAP` entry recording a close?**

## ⌗ WHAT THIS IS NOT

- **Not a claim that the sweeps' work is wrong or that anything needs re-running.** Both closed with
  recorded verdicts; the defect is a status marker in a second document.
- **Not a claim about the other four rows.** *They may be genuinely owed — §3b in particular is the
  positive face, which the method says is not dispatchable and which nothing here suggests has run.*
- **And not a large defect.** *It is worth writing up because of where it sits: the document a node
  reads to learn how the programme works, in the section that tells it what is left to do.*

## ⚠ AND ROW 4 CARRIES A DIAGNOSIS THE CORPUS MADE AND HAS NOT ACTED ON

`THE_PLAN` l.3468, **found r1724**:

> *"**⚠ AND THE GATHERING DID NOT REACH ITS LEDGER — found r1724.** `JARGON_LEDGER.md` is **3.8 KB,
> three entries, newest content r1310** — while the per-paper idiom pass this item says fed it **ran
> on all seventeen papers by r1406**, ninety-six revisions later. **Either the pass found nothing
> further, or its findings never reached the ledger** … ⌗ The cheap discriminator, and it is one
> command: the live counts. ***`do-not-assert` now stands at 43 across 14 files** against the
> ledger's single row for it — **a ledger with one row for a term with 43 sites is an index entry,
> not a gathering**.*"

**I ran the discriminator the note names.**

| | at r1724 | **at r2428 (HEAD)** |
|---|---|---|
| `JARGON_LEDGER.md` | 3.8 KB | **3.8 KB** — *unchanged* |
| `do-not-assert` sites in `corpus/*.tex` | 43 across 14 files | **45 across 14 files** |

⇒ ***The diagnosis is precise, the discriminator is one command, and roughly seven hundred revisions
later the ledger is byte-for-byte the same size and the gap has widened by two.***

⌗ **This is the corpus's own law turned on itself, and it is the sharpest instance I have found:**
***"a receipt naming a defect is evidence it was FOUND, never evidence it is still THERE — check the
fix, not just the diagnosis."*** *Here the fix was never made, and what makes it visible is that the
note wrote down its own test.* **A defect that names its discriminator is a defect that can be
re-checked cheaply by anyone; that is the whole value of writing it that way, and nothing had
re-checked it.**

## ⇒ SO THE FIX TO §7 IS LARGER THAN THE DRAFT ABOVE SAID

*Not "mark two rows RUN" but:* **give the register a status vocabulary that can express what actually
happened to these six** — `CLOSED (rev)` · `ABSORBED INTO <instrument>` · `RAN, FINDINGS NOT LANDED` ·
`OWED`. **Row 4 needs the third of those, and the corpus has no word for it**, which may be why a
found-and-unfixed defect sat quietly: *there was no status that says "this ran and produced nothing
we can point at."*
