# Time Travel: A Five-Dimensional Trope — or, The Fallacy at the Heart of Modern Physics

The Five-Dimensional Problem," 2025-06-20). **Tier:** C (conceptual geometric-boundary constraint).

---

This is not about the science-fiction or the mathematical-physics versions of time travel (closed
timelike curves, FTL loops). It is about the **core structure** — topological, dimensional, dynamical —
a world must have for time travel to *even make sense*. And that exposes a **category mistake** unnoticed
in pop science, physics, and philosophy alike: treating **occurrences in time** (things that *happen*)
as **existents in space** (things that *are*). We first *spatialize* time (one of four dimensions of
space-time), then tacitly *temporalize* the whole manifold — picturing space-time itself as an existent
object, like Newtonian space, an arena in which everything exists and evolves. That move quietly
**smuggles in a fifth dimension** — a second, Newton-like absolute time, not present in the formal
physics, required only to make the mental picture feel complete. It changes no mathematics; it distorts
the interpretation.

## What we see is not what is

We never see the world as it is *now* — only past images carried by light (the Moon 1.3 s ago, the Sun
8 min ago, Pluto 5 h ago). Yet we rightly infer that these things *exist now*, in a 3D world coexisting
with us as time progresses. *What we see is a projection from the past — of what now is.* Relativity
complicates "now," but that complication cannot be appreciated until the deeper confusion — **what
exists vs what occurs** — is resolved. (The relativity-of-simultaneity treatment is the *Wild Goose
Chase* essay.)

## Existence vs occurrence

A thing that **exists** (a chair) has nonzero duration — it persists, it *is* for some span of time. A
thing that merely **occurs** (a lightning flash, a camera flash) is atemporal in structure — over as
soon as it begins. **Occurrences happen *as* things exist.** Imagine an elephant that flashes into being
beside the Sun for one instant and vanishes — it *occurs*; the Sun *exists*. Objects that exist and
events that occur belong to **different ontological categories**; an object may exist for a century, an
event happens at an instant — not interchangeable. Confusion arises when we collapse that distinction.

## Space-time: the real elephant in the room

In standard physics, space-time is **the set of all events that ever occur** — mostly innocuous (a clock
tick at a lonely point a million light-years off), occasionally memorable (Caesar's death). This is
textbook. But: take one atom of the flashing elephant at the instant it occurred — that is an *event*;
it happened, it does not *exist* (it lacks duration; existence implies a span of time — "things do not
exist atemporally" is oxymoronic). So can space-time "exist"? Only by **adding something** — a fifth
dimension capturing the sense that the 4D structure itself *is*, the way we intuitively take ourselves
and 3D objects to exist. That move "transforms four-dimensional physics into five-dimensional
metaphysics."

## Existence as a physical dimension

Existence is not a spatial direction; it is a **structural feature of reality** that varies
independently of space and gives form to persistence — "the *condition* that allows [events] to happen
at all." The enduring elephant in the room (vs the flash) differs not in shape, mass, or position but in
that it **endures** — present with you across moments. **Existence is what the clock measures** — not
movement *through* space-time, but being that lasts; and as it lasts, each clock tick is a new event, a
new happening. (To say you "move through" time is to imagine the dimension of existence as itself
existing prior to your existing along it — "a complete mess.") Space-time is the **map/geometry/scaffold**
representing, at once, all that occurs *as things exist* — it does not itself persist as reality. To
treat the 4D block as *existing* is to sneak in a layer conferring being on the *map* — "where physics
ends and metaphysics begins."

## Meta-now

"Does Caesar's death exist *now*?" — but *now* there cannot mean simultaneous-with-us (there is no such
frame; it is deep in our past light cone). Time travel needs the moment to still *be there*, waiting to
be arrived at — to *persist*, not merely to have occurred. That requires a **meta-now**: a second-order
"now" layered over the whole manifold, as if all eternity sits there presently — the now of a Newtonian
meta-time, with metrical structure, "flowing equably without relation to anything external," now applied
to *all of space-time*. **That fifth dimension is not in the physics; it is in our heads** — a tacit
"levelling up of the dimensionality of reality": a 3D space existing as absolute time progresses becomes
a 4D space-time existing as absolute *meta-time* progresses.

## The basic physical structure of time travel

Grandfather-paradox stories (*Back to the Future*, *Harry Potter*'s Time-Turner) assume the **whole of
space-time exists and can be traversed** — a persistent 4D world beings move through, consciousness
flowing like water through a riverbed. But **flux requires persistence**: nothing flows through an
occurrence, nothing flows in an instant. To speak of movement through time, memory, recurrence, you need
the events to still be there — the manifold to *endure* — i.e. a **fifth-dimensional structure**.
Fixed-timeline stories (*The Terminator*, *Slaughterhouse-Five*'s Tralfamadorians, for whom every moment
eternally *is*) need the same: the whole manifold already existing, fixed and complete. Without that
deeper existence, what remains is "a four-dimensional elephant that flashes — a world-tube that occurs —
that comes in and out of being, instantaneously, all at once. No flow… Just everything happening, all at
once, in a world that merely occurs. Not one that exists."

## Implications for physics

Time travel requires erasing the difference between what **happens** and what **is** — reifying the event
structure of space-time into a higher-dimensional reality that exists. That framework, acknowledged or
not, is everywhere: it shapes how physicists talk about relativity, how philosophers write about time,
the block universe, "time is an illusion." Hold the line between what exists and what merely occurs, and
the time-travel stories collapse, the eternalist metaphors dissolve, the paradoxes vanish — and so does
much of the hidden scaffolding under modern physics. "This isn't just a problem for science fiction.
It's a problem at the heart of physics."
