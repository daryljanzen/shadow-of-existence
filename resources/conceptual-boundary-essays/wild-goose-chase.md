# Albert Einstein's Wild Goose Chase

**Author:** Daryl Janzen. **Source:** cosmiCave.org, 2025-08-06. **Tier:** C (conceptual
geometric-boundary constraint). **Saved:** r269 gathering (fetched at source, full clean transcription).

---

Imagine you're on a train or a plane, gliding at constant speed. Four hundred years ago, Galileo clarified something remarkable: in such an "inertial state," everything inside the moving vehicle behaves exactly as it would if the vehicle were standing still. Jump straight up in the aisle and you land where you started — your momentum carries you. For thousands of years people thought otherwise (you'd land behind, the cart dashing ahead); on a bumpy cart the confounding forces hid the truth, but in a smooth high-speed cruise the effect is unmistakable. Galileo would have known it had he seen an airplane.

The *mis*interpretation of this simple situation has misled thinkers for centuries. It once convinced humanity that Earth must be motionless at the centre of the cosmos. Galileo corrected that. But at the start of the twentieth century, **Einstein fumbled the interpretation again, just as Aristotle had — and convinced physicists and philosophers that there is no such thing as "now," and that the passage of time is an illusion.** This essay unpacks Einstein's mistake and explains how relativity must be interpreted to be consistent with the evidence.

## Does the ball really take as long to hit the back wall as the front?

Pitching balls from mid-cabin. *Convention 1 — the plane is at rest, the ground moves:* a ball (or photons from a central bulb) thrown to the back and front by a perfect central machine reach the walls **synchronously**. *Convention 2 — the plane moves over fixed ground:* after the ball leaves your hand, the back wall moves toward the launch point ("helping" the ball — shorter distance, less time), while the front wall recedes (the ball must overtake it — longer distance, more time). So the back ball arrives first, the front ball later.

**Whether two events are described as synchronous depends on the conventions of "space" and "rest" one assumes a priori. Synchrony follows identically from the assumed convention.**

## The relativity of synchrony as a general consequence

This relativity of synchrony is **not particular to Einstein's theories, nor tied to light or the light postulate** — it depends only on the geometry and kinematics of relative motion. There is a one-to-one correspondence between one's definition of "rest" and one's definition of "synchrony." It holds even when the "ball" is slower than the "plane" (throwing to the back is really *slowing* the ball's shared inertia so the wall overtakes it). The light postulate (invariance of *c* in every inertial frame) is more specific: it fixes the *numbers* — how much clocks desynchronize, how space and time adjust (velocity composition, time dilation, length contraction; confirmed by GPS) — but the *possibility* of relative synchrony comes first, from the choice of rest frame alone.

This leaves the deeper question untouched: does relativity require *simultaneity* to be relative, or only *synchrony*? A separate, **ontological** issue.

## The science of relativity — and its limit

Relativity's metrical predictions are extraordinarily well confirmed (time dilation, length contraction, velocity composition, GPS clock desynchronization). But **none of these experiments tell us whether we are really moving or really at rest.** The relativity principle ensures local experiments cannot distinguish a true state of rest. Round-trip symmetry shows why: even when the plane is conventionally moving (so two simultaneously-launched balls reach the walls asynchronously), by symmetry the balls return to the centre at exactly the same time — any time gained outbound is lost inbound. Replace balls with photons off mirrors and this is Einstein's two-way light-signalling synchronization: you can establish synchrony within your frame (and so a conventional rest), but motion relative to an external standard stays hidden. **When it comes to deciding whether a universal state of rest and absolute simultaneity exist, local experiments have nothing to say.**

## The philosophical fork and the naïve-realist temptation

Two logically coherent options explain these facts:

1. **There is one true state of rest, with objective ontological simultaneity** (distinct from frame-relative synchrony). A truly moving observer cannot detect their motion or determine whether conventionally-synchronous events are truly simultaneous — they lack access to that ontological property.
2. **There is no true state of rest, hence no objective simultaneity** — motion and synchrony are only relatively significant, and simultaneity is *defined* as synonymous with conventional synchrony.

The frame-dependent option (2) superficially aligns with naïve realism — but it **collapses** in the case where you sit at rest on a plane you *know* is moving (windows closed): you know the light reaches the back before the front, yet your frame-synchronized clocks record the two events at the same time. **You cannot both conventionally define simultaneity as identical to synchrony and conventionally define yourself to be moving, even when you are certain you are.** Einstein's preferred convention forces you to choose the duck even when you know it's a rabbit. Neither option is "more objective": (1) posits unobserved structure; (2) actively privileges one perspective. The system was **underdetermined by the available evidence**.

## Lessons from history

(Developed at length in *Scientific Shadow Reading: How We Discovered Earth is a Planet*.) Three lessons: **(i)** do not default to the naïve-realist option — geocentrism was exactly that error, taking ~2000 years to overturn; **(ii)** do not fear real underlying structure you cannot yet detect — Aristarchus, Copernicus, Galileo, Kepler, Newton could not directly detect Earth's motion, yet embraced heliocentrism for its coherence; **(iii)** inability to measure a thing *locally* is not proof it cannot be measured at all — annual stellar parallax eventually gave direct evidence of Earth's orbit, and "so too we may find direct empirical evidence of a cosmic rest frame."

## Our cosmological motion

Einstein (1905), imagining a sea of randomly drifting stars, could not conceive of a well-defined cosmic rest frame, so he committed to frame-relative simultaneity. **But the universe did present a means of measuring our actual motion through it.** Cosmic expansion (Slipher 1913, Hubble 1929): more distant galaxies recede faster (the doubling-room analogy). The modern reading is expansion *of space* (cosmological redshift accumulated along the photon's path), not Doppler motion through space.

The standard model's five assumptions rest on the most foundational one: **a three-dimensional space, an associated state of rest, and a cosmic time.** Without it nothing else can be defined. **From a logical standpoint, the degree to which cosmic expansion is empirically confirmed to have been uniform throughout cosmic history entails equivalent confirmation of the more fundamental hypothesis: that there really is a cosmic time and a globally well-defined state of rest.**

The CMB makes this precise. Its redshift is isotropic to better than **one part in 100,000** (the preserved primordial Sachs–Wolfe anisotropy would have been erased had expansion been non-uniform). So either space expanded precisely uniformly, or an impossible fine-tuning cancelled all local variations. Either way, the universe has a well-defined state of rest — not a coarse average but **one of the most precise measurements in cosmology**. And the CMB dipole measures our own velocity through it: **369.82 ± 0.11 km/s toward the constellation Crater.**

## Objective ontology must follow the evidence

Ontological commitments must track empirical evidence. The synchrony/simultaneity fork should have been framed honestly as underdetermined — neither option "more objective." Instead Einstein defined simultaneity ≡ synchrony, and the resulting "relativity of simultaneity" was taken as evidence that there is no objective "now." **This is a modal fallacy: absence of evidence treated as evidence of absence.** Galileo's argument (we cannot locally detect Earth's real motion) was inverted into "there is no fact of the matter about motion."

Had the fork stayed open, the CMB (Penzias–Wilson 1965), its blackbody isotropy and dipole (COBE 1992), and the preserved primordial anisotropy (WMAP/Planck) should each have been recognized as mounting empirical evidence for cosmic time and ontological simultaneity. **The evidence is clear: synchrony is relative; simultaneity is absolute.** Einstein's simultaneity convention is a historically understandable but now empirically obsolete metaphysical detour.

## Einstein's metaphysical wild goose chase

The relativity of simultaneity birthed the superluminal-time-travel fantasy and the block-universe (eternalist) reading — both predicated on simultaneity being *ontologically* relative. Take the cosmological evidence for an objective "now" seriously and these dissolve: Andromeda exists *now* (not days ago); superluminal travel, were it possible, would be *Star Wars* (zip to places further along in cosmic time), not *The Time Machine*. The block-universe debate "fizzles" once one sees the logic never favored it and the evidence strongly supports a well-defined ontological now. The belief that space-time "exists" — the "fabric of the cosmos" — is predicated on simultaneity being fundamentally relative. "We've been chasing a metaphysical wild goose… It's time to face facts and correct course, leaving the idea of pure relative motion and its fantastic consequences behind us, once and for all."
