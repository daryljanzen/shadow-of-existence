Three eminent physicists — **Penrose** (Nobel, for black-hole formation as a robust GR prediction),
**Steinhardt** (Princeton, Albert Einstein Professor), **Turok** (then Perimeter director) — each put a
false claim into the record: that **Einstein proposed a cyclic/"bouncing" cosmology.** He did not. Janzen
noticed it over a decade ago, approached Steinhardt privately and respectfully (2013), was minimized and
deflected; Penrose ignored him; now he calls it out.

## The cultural problem

"A sickness in modern physics": concepts distorted and passed along like a game of **telephone**.
Physicists aren't doing their homework; they move on before they're sure; misinformation goes unchecked —
and **who would check?** The eminent dismiss anyone lacking comparable "intellectual capital." Janzen's own
case in point: an airtight argument that black-hole physics has for 60 years rested on a logical fallacy
(a misreading of the mathematics) was rejected **not because anyone showed it wrong, but because no one
trusts themselves to verify it and he lacks the clout to be presumed right** — a catch-22. The governing
line, from *Fargo*'s Gloria Burgle: **"You don't have to like the truth for it to be true."**

## The two false claims

**Penrose (1979):** lists, among Einstein's attempts to avoid singularities, "the idea that a non-singular
'bounce' of the universe might be achieved through irregularities," citing "Einstein, Berl. Ber. (1931)
235." A great narrative hook (no one *wanted* singularities; even Einstein resisted) serving the agenda of
celebrating post-Einstein achievements. **Steinhardt & Turok** (*Endless Universe*): instead *recruit*
Einstein's authority — claiming his 1931 paper consciously chose Friedmann's "closed, periodic
(oscillatory)" model. Both false.

## What Einstein actually did (the record, shadow-read)

- **1917 cosmology** (his deepest cosmological reflections): built a static spherical universe in which
  **matter is essential** (space-time should not exist independent of a material component, unlike empty
  Minkowski). To do it he assumed — *against the spirit of relativity* — a **preferred cosmic rest frame**
  ("a system of reference relative to which matter may be looked upon as permanently at rest," justified
  empirically by small stellar velocities), made space a **finite 3-sphere** (killing the boundary-value
  infinity), and added **Λ** to balance gravity. *This is the lens for everything after: Λ justified only
  to permit a finite, matter-filled universe; willing to sacrifice pure relativity of motion for a
  preferred rest frame.* [Directly relevant to CR: Einstein himself reached for an objective cosmic rest
  frame and a matter-essential ontology.]
- **de Sitter's reply** mocked the infinity worry and showed Λ permits an infinity-free model — but a
  *matter-free* one, undermining Einstein's premise.
- **1922–1927:** Einstein read Friedmann (1922), privately inferred that if the universe is non-static, Λ
  is unnecessary ("if there is no quasi-static world, then away with the cosmological term," to Weyl, May
  1923) — but never explained himself; first wrongly objected to Friedmann, then retracted ("correct and
  clarifying"). Told Lemaître (1927): "Your calculations are correct, but your physical insight is
  abominable" — rejecting the Λ-driven interpretation.
- **1931 paper** (*Zum kosmologischen Problem*), ~2 pages: first explicit acceptance of a **dynamical**
  universe; drops Λ ("theoretically unsatisfactory"); writes the closed-model scale factor in its
  **cycloidal** form but **describes only the expanding branch**; treats the singular origin as an
  idealization artefact. **No reference to cyclicity, periodicity, or a bounce.**

## The misreading lineage (the shadow-reading payoff)

- **Einstein → Tolman (27 June 1931 letter):** Einstein downplays the note ("only the point that the
  λ-term is unnecessary if one allows time-variable world radius"); no cyclic hint.
- **Tolman's reply (14 Sept 1931):** Tolman, in his own thermodynamic enthusiasm, introduces "your
  proposed quasi-periodic solution" and "contraction… followed by renewed expansion" — **the periodicity
  language Einstein never used.**
- **Tolman's 1931 *Phys Rev* paper** attaches Einstein's name (citation "Berl. Ber. (1931) 235") to "a
  simple model… which exhibits a possibility for quasi-periodic solutions" — the citation placed
  deceptively on the *model*, not on Tolman's own added speculation.
- **de Sitter (1931)** glosses: Einstein's *preference* is for the Λ=0 solution, "which belongs to a
  family of oscillating solutions" — the oscillation is de Sitter's addition, Einstein's preference only
  the Λ=0.
- **Penrose** reproduces Tolman's exact shorthand citation — so he read Tolman, inferred the bounce, and
  **never read Einstein's 1931 paper itself.** Steinhardt & Turok likely read only Tolman's *reply* letter,
  not Einstein's enclosing letter, and read Tolman's words backwards into Einstein.

"None of these authors fabricated evidence outright; each merely read what he expected, or wished, to
find" — myth by **gradual rhetorical contagion**.

## The ethics (the scientific-ethics core)

Why it matters: **influential theorists invoke Einstein's authority to lend their proposals unearned
gravitas**; rhetorical convenience outweighs accuracy; "the casual treatment of historical truth as
optional, the quiet presumption that the reputation of a 'trusted genius' counts as empirical support."
Steinhardt–Turok: "not merely that they were wrong, but that they did not care enough to be right."
Penrose: "a predisposition to interpret evidence through the prism of his own metaphysical convictions" —
the *same* tendency Janzen charges against modern black-hole physics (read selectively, in ways that
reinforce one's agenda). The deepest lesson: **even the brightest minds can look directly at the evidence
and see not what is there but what they wish — and if they can do it once, they can do it again, so trust
that they infer correctly can never be absolute.** Einstein, who distrusted unexamined authority, has
become "its unwitting patron saint."
