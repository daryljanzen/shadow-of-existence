# Memo: HELIX-2 Enrollment Rescue Plan

**To:** Head of Clinical Operations, Aldane Therapeutics
**Re:** Recommended plan for steering committee
**Date:** 13 November 2026

## Recommendation

Approve a US-only package costing about $805,000:

- Fund extra coordinators at the Atlanta and Houston sites.
- Add the Chicago community health network as a site.
- Add **8** (not 10) US academic centres, chosen for diverse patient populations.

Do not pursue Option B. Also open a discussion with FDA now, because no expansion option alone gets us to the 20% diversity commitment.

## The gap

We enrolled 196 participants in 9 months, or 21.8 per month (0.91 per site). If that rate holds, the existing 24 sites reach about 436 by 30 September 2027. That is roughly **44 short** of 480. The rate could be rising as later sites mature, or falling as sites exhaust their patient pools. I have not seen month-by-month data, and we should get it before the committee meets.

## Options compared (assuming approval by 1 December)

| Plan | Cost | Extra participants by 30 Sept | Diversity effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: 10 US sites, active May | ~$650k | ~32 | Neutral to positive |
| B: 6 EU sites, active April | ~$444k + CRO fees | ~43* | Negative |
| Recommended package | ~$805k | ~45–50 | Positive |

*Uses the CRO's claimed 1.6 per site per month, at half that rate for the first 3 months.

**Option A alone falls short.** Five months of start-up leaves only five months of enrollment, and three of those run at half rate.

**Option B is cheapest per participant, but it damages diversity.** About 10% non-US enrollment probably still satisfies FDA's "meaningful US population" expectation. The problem is that the Polish and Hungarian sites would add about 43 participants who are almost certainly not Black or Hispanic. US sites would then need roughly 31% Black and Hispanic enrollment from here on, against today's 11%.

## Recommended package

- **Atlanta/Houston coordinators: $160k** (2 coordinators, December to September). They could be productive by mid-January. If each adds about 1 participant per month (to be confirmed with the sites), that yields about 17. This is our fastest lever, and both cities have large Black and Hispanic populations.
- **Chicago network: ~$125k** ($60k support, about $45k start-up, $20k monitoring). It would add only about 3 participants, but likely the highest diversity yield of any option.
- **8 academic centres: $520k** ($360k start-up, $160k monitoring). Expected to add about 25 participants.
- **The 7 low-enrolling sites:** put them on 60-day improvement plans and close any with no realistic pipeline. This might add about 5. Savings from closure are limited, because enrolled participants still need monitoring.

This leaves **$95k in contingency**.

## Diversity is the larger risk

We need 96 Black and Hispanic participants and have about 22. If existing sites continue at 11%, they add about 26. That leaves about 48 needed from roughly 50 new participants, which is not realistic.

Reaching 20% requires existing sites to change how they recruit. That means community clinic referrals, targeted outreach, and coordinator time set aside for outreach. I recommend:

1. Setting site-level diversity targets and reporting them monthly.
2. Requesting an early FDA meeting to present our status and remediation plan, rather than surprising the agency at submission.

## Triggers

Our margin is thin: about 45–50 projected against a gap of 44. On 1 March, if enrollment is below 24 per month, we should use the contingency to fund coordinators at other top-five sites.

##

FACTS:
HELIX-2 is a Phase 3 trial of a once-weekly diabetes drug. The protocol requires 480 participants. The enrollment deadline is September 30, 2027, because the database lock and regulatory submission timelines depend on it. Enrollment began on February 1, 2026 at 24 sites in the United States. By November 1, 2026 (9 months), 196 participants had enrolled. The top 5 sites have enrolled 52% of participants. Seven sites have each enrolled 2 or fewer. Each active site costs about $4,000 a month in monitoring and management fees. Option A: add 10 US academic medical centres. Start-up costs are $45,000 per site. Contracting, ethics approval and training would take about 5 months. Aldane's history shows new US sites enroll at about the trial's current average rate, but usually at half that rate in their first 3 months. Option B: add 6 sites in Poland and Hungary through a contract research organisation (CRO). Start-up costs are $30,000 per site, and they could be active in about 4 months. The CRO says its sites in these countries have enrolled about 1.6 participants per site per month in similar diabetes trials. Supplying non-US sites requires separately labelled study drug, which has a 4-month lead time and costs $120,000. Aldane has agreed a diversity plan with the FDA that commits to enrolling at least 20% Black and Hispanic participants. Current enrollment is 11% Black and Hispanic. The FDA reviewer has also said the agency expects a "meaningful US population," since the drug will be marketed mainly in the US. Currently 100% of participants are in the US. Two of the high-performing sites are in Atlanta and Houston, and both say they could enroll more if they had additional study coordinators, at about $90,000 a year per coordinator. A community health network in Chicago has offered to take part as a site, but needs about $60,000 in extra support for staff training and data systems. The trial budget has $900,000 remaining for site expansion.

KNOWN COMPLICATIONS:
- The shortfall is smaller than it looks: at the current rate (196 over 9 months, about 21.8 a month), the trial would reach about 436 by September 30, about 44 short. Option A's sites, active about April, would yield fewer than the simple estimate because of their slow first 3 months.
- Option B looks fastest and cheapest, but does nothing for the 20% diversity commitment and reduces the US share. The study drug lead time also needs to be checked.
- Uneven site performance: 7 sites with 2 or fewer participants cost about $28k a month in total. Adding coordinators at strong sites, or including the Chicago community network, may be cheaper and better for diversity than adding many new sites.
- The budget constraint: A costs $450k in start-up plus about $40k a month in monitoring once active; B costs $180k plus $120k for drug labelling plus monitoring. Both must fit within $900k alongside any other measures.

AUTHOR'S ANTICIPATED CRITIQUES:
1. **Gap estimate is crude.** It assumes a constant rate and ignores how the existing sites ramped up and whether they are fatiguing. Without monthly data, the 44 figure could be well off in either direction.
2. **The biggest contributor rests on a guess.** The coordinator uplift of 1 per site per month has no evidence behind it. Hiring and training may also take longer than six weeks.
3. **Option B is dismissed too quickly.** It is the most cost-efficient choice and spreads the geographic risk. The diversity argument assumes the 20% applies to total enrollment rather than the US subgroup, and the memo never checks this against the FDA agreement.
4. **Chicago's timeline and costs are assumed.** Community sites often take longer to start than academic centres, and the $45k start-up cost is assumed rather than quoted.
5. **Costs are incomplete.** The memo leaves out monitoring during start-up, drug supply for new US sites, outreach programmes, and possibly per-patient costs. It is also unclear whether all of these come from the $900k.
6. **Choosing 8 academic sites looks budget-driven.** Selecting sites for diverse catchment may lower enrollment below the historical average.
7. **The diversity fix is vague.** The memo identifies the problem but gives no costed or quantified plan to raise existing sites from 11% to about 26%.
8. **The March trigger is weak.** Late-added coordinators have little time to contribute, and there is no real fallback if the plan underdelivers.
9. **Start-up timing is optimistic.** Activating 8 sites in parallel within 5 months is unlikely. Activation will probably be staggered, which reduces yield.
10. **Protocol-level causes are ignored.** The memo does not look at screen-failure rates, eligibility criteria, or patient burden as reasons for slow enrollment.
11. **Downstream impact is not discussed.** The memo does not address what happens to database lock and submission if enrollment slips anyway.

FINDINGS:
- The baseline of ~436 assumes 11 months of remaining enrollment, but from 13 Nov only about 10.5 months remain, giving roughly 425 and a gap of about 55, so the memo should state its data cut-off date.
- The 45–50 participant range relies on a speculative ~5 from low-enrolling sites; without them the package yields about 45 against a gap of 44, effectively zero margin, and no downside case is shown.
- The Option B diversity comparison is one-sided because it cites the 31% US Black/Hispanic requirement for B but omits that the recommended package also requires about 26%, both far above the current 11%.
- The 1 March fallback trigger is too late, since new sites could not activate until around August and coordinator funding would recover only about 5–8 participants, so an earlier checkpoint, a clearer metric definition, and a quantified recovery estimate are needed.
- The choice of 8 new sites instead of 10 is unexplained and appears driven by an implied $900k budget, sacrificing about 6 participants when the margin is about 1.
- Option B's cost excludes CRO fees, making the cost-per-participant comparison incomplete.
- The CRO's claimed 1.6 participants per site per month is unverified and nearly double the current US rate, yet is not treated skeptically.
- The Chicago activation date is not stated, so the ~3 participant estimate cannot be checked.
- The ~1 participant per coordinator per month assumption is the largest single lever and is unconfirmed.
- The 20% diversity commitment is not defined in terms of which groups it covers or whether it comes from the FDA diversity action plan.
- The ~10% non-US figure for Option B should be ~9% (43 ÷ 480).
- The memo does not check whether the low-enrolling sites it would close enroll diverse patients, so closing them could lower diversity.
