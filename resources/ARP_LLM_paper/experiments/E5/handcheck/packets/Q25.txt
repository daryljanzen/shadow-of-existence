DOCUMENT:
# Budget Justification: Riverbend Music Festival Grant, 2027

**Prepared for:** Ellery Town Council, January 2027 budget session
**Submitted by:** Ellery Tourism Office
**Request:** Increase the festival grant from $300,000 to $420,000 (+$120,000, or 40%)

## Purpose of the increase

The organisers have asked for $120,000 more for two items:

- **A second stage ($85,000).** This would add capacity and programming.
- **Higher artist fees ($35,000).** These would attract better-known performers.

Both are meant to grow attendance and raise the festival's regional profile.

## What the festival delivers

The festival is Ellery's largest annual event. In 2026 it recorded 28,000 ticket scans over three days, which the organisers estimate represents about 11,500 individual attendees.

The organisers' economic impact study values the festival at $6.4 million. Council should treat this as an upper bound, for two reasons:

- **It counts residents' spending.** In the organisers' survey, 46% of respondents live in Ellery, and much of their spending would likely happen in town anyway.
- **Its multiplier is high.** It uses a multiplier of 2.1, which is high for a town of Ellery's size.

A more conservative estimate counts only visitor attendance days (about 54% of 28,000, or roughly 15,100) at $109 per day, with a multiplier of 1.5. That gives roughly **$2.5 million** in local economic activity. This is still substantial.

The one directly measured return is lodging tax receipts, which were **$38,000 higher** than on the same weekend in 2025.

## Full cost to the town

The grant is not the town's only cost. In 2026 the town also paid $96,000 in police overtime and $41,000 in extra sanitation. That brought the total 2026 cost to **$437,000**.

At the requested grant level, the 2027 cost would be at least **$557,000**. It is likely to be higher, because a second stage and larger crowds will need more policing and services.

## Risks council should weigh

1. **Date conflict.** The state university's move-in weekend (August 21–22) overlaps the planned festival dates (August 20–22). When the two events overlapped in 2023, local hotels sold out weeks in advance. The county sheriff also could not supply the 20 deputies the festival normally borrows. A repeat would raise police overtime and limit visitor lodging.
2. **Neighbourhood impact.** The town received 212 noise complaints in 2026, mainly from Riverside and Mill Hill. Riverside residents have petitioned for a 10 p.m. finish. A second stage could make the noise problem worse.
3. **Relocation.** Carver has offered $350,000 and a larger site. Losing the festival would end the lodging tax gain and the visitor spending. However, Carver's offer is $70,000 below the amount requested.
4. **Budget context.** Departments have been asked to hold spending flat. A 40% increase to an external grant will need clear justification.

## Recommendation

The Tourism Office supports the increase to $420,000, subject to these conditions:

- **Dates.** The organisers move the festival off the university move-in weekend, or confirm in writing that security staffing is in place by March 2027.
- **Noise.** The organisers submit a noise management plan for both stages, including sound limits, stage orientation and a review of the finish time with Riverside residents.
- **Reporting.** The organisers report verified 2027 attendance, visitor share and spending, using an independent method.
- **Second stage funding.** The $85,000 for the second stage is released only after the date and noise conditions are met.

If council prefers a lower figure, **$350,000** would match Carver's offer and limit the risk of relocation.

##

FACTS:
The Riverbend Music Festival is a three-day event held each August in Ellery's riverside park. The town gave the festival a $300,000 grant in 2026. The organisers have asked for $420,000 in 2027 to add a second stage ($85,000) and increase artist fees ($35,000) to attract better-known performers. The organisers report 2026 attendance as 28,000, based on ticket scans over the three days. Most tickets are 3-day passes, and the organisers estimate there were about 11,500 individual attendees. An economic impact study commissioned by the organisers estimates the festival's value to the local economy at $6.4 million. The study multiplied 28,000 attendees by an average spending of $109 per attendee per day, and then by an economic multiplier of 2.1. In the festival's own attendee survey, 46% of respondents said they live in Ellery. The town's lodging tax receipts for the festival weekend were $38,000 higher than for the same weekend the previous year. The town also paid for $96,000 in police overtime and $41,000 in extra sanitation services during the festival. These are paid from the police and public works budgets, not from the festival grant. The town received 212 noise complaints during the 2026 festival, most from the Riverside and Mill Hill neighbourhoods. A petition from Riverside residents asks the council to end performances at 10 p.m. instead of 11:30 p.m. The organisers say an earlier end time would make it harder to book headline acts. The festival organisers have said that if the grant is not increased, they will consider moving the festival to the neighbouring town of Carver, which has offered a $350,000 grant and a larger site. The 2027 festival is planned for August 20-22. The state university, 8 miles away, has scheduled its student move-in weekend for August 21-22, 2027. Last time the two events overlapped, in 2023, local hotels were sold out weeks in advance and the county sheriff could not provide the 20 deputies that the festival usually borrows. Ellery's general fund budget is $34 million. The council has asked all departments to hold spending flat in 2027.

KNOWN COMPLICATIONS:
- The $6.4M impact figure is inflated. It counts ticket scans (28,000) rather than individual attendees (about 11,500), it includes local residents (46%) whose spending is not new to the town, and it applies a generous multiplier.
- The real cost to the town is higher than the grant: $420k + $96k police + $41k sanitation = about $557k, compared with directly measurable lodging tax of $38k.
- The 2027 date clashes with university move-in weekend: hotels will be full and sheriff's deputies unavailable, so the date reduces visitor benefits and creates a security gap.
- The offer from Carver ($350k) is a real competitive threat, but it also means the festival may stay for less than $420k, and the noise complaints from residents need a response.

AUTHOR'S ANTICIPATED CRITIQUES:
1. **Thin evidence of net benefit.** The revised $2.5 million figure still relies on the organisers' unverified $109 daily spending number and an assumed 1.5 multiplier. Neither has been independently tested.
2. **Unreliable visitor share.** The 46% resident figure comes from a self-selected survey with an unknown response rate, so the visitor share could be wrong in either direction.
3. **No breakdown of costs or revenue.** The document does not estimate 2027 police and sanitation costs or attendance gains from the new spending. It also does not show whether the extra $120,000 produces any additional return.
4. **Small direct return.** The verified $38,000 lodging tax gain is small next to the town's costs of more than $437,000. Other direct revenue, such as sales tax and parking, is not quantified.
5. **Weak case for the full amount.** By the document's own logic, $350,000 may be enough to keep the festival. That weakens the case for $420,000.
6. **Unequal treatment of budgets.** Recommending a 40% increase while departments are held flat raises a fairness problem that the document notes but does not resolve.
7. **Underplayed date risk.** Without the 20 sheriff's deputies, the festival may be unsafe or unaffordable. Arguably, fixing the dates should come before any funding decision rather than being a condition attached to it.
8. **Noise conditions lack enforcement.** No decibel limits or penalties are set, and the conditions do not respond directly to the petition's 10 p.m. request.
9. **Conflict of interest.** The Tourism Office is an advocate for the festival, not a neutral assessor of it.
10. **Untested relocation threat.** The document does not say whether Carver's offer is firm or what moving would cost the organisers.

FINDINGS:
- The draft places Carver's offer being $70,000 below the requested amount under risks, where it reads as an argument against the request, and never resolves it; the gap from the current grant is only $50,000.
- The $38,000 'directly measured return' compares festival weekend 2026 with the same weekend in 2025, so it measures either year-on-year growth or is confounded by other differences, not the festival's effect.
- The draft omits the brief's point that police overtime and sanitation costs fall on police and public works budgets that are being held flat, which links the festival to the flat-budget instruction.
- The draft omits the brief's $34M general fund figure, leaving council without a sense of scale for the $120K increase or the $557K total.
- The draft omits the organisers' position that an earlier 10 p.m. finish would make headliners harder to book, so it does not acknowledge the conflict between the noise condition and the $35,000 for artist fees.
- The draft overstates the relocation consequence, since Carver is a neighbouring town and some visitors would likely still stay and spend in Ellery.
- The claim that $350,000 matches Carver's offer is inaccurate because Carver also offers a larger site.
- The date condition's 'move the dates or confirm security staffing' alternative lets organisers keep the clashing weekend, and staffing does nothing about hotels selling out, which undercuts the lodging return the draft relies on.
- Several conditions lack mechanics: only the $85,000 has a release trigger, it is unclear what happens to the rest of the grant if the date condition fails, and the reporting condition has no consequence attached.
- The March 2027 deadline falls after the January vote, and the draft does not say what happens if it is missed.
- The draft does not examine spending composition; if the $109/day includes ticket purchases, much of that money flows to organisers and out-of-town artists rather than into Ellery's economy.
- The draft does not state the potential conflict of interest in the Tourism Office arguing for the increase, which a single disclosure line would address.
- The document is about 580 words, over the 450–550 word target.
- The draft omits the brief's 8-mile distance to the university, which explains why the date clash matters.
- The claim 'high for a town of Ellery's size' is unsourced and needs a source or benchmark range.
- The draft does not reconcile the $350K alternative with the $420K recommendation by explaining what the extra $70K buys.
