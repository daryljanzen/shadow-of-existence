# Grant Summary: Rural Mobile Pantry Expansion
**Harvest Table Food Bank | Request: $220,000 (one year) | Total project cost: $285,000**

## The need
Demand for food assistance in our county is rising quickly. Harvest Table served 18,200 households in 2025, up 26% from 14,450 in 2023. The gap is widest in Hollis, Wren and Tamarack townships, where food insecurity is estimated at 17%, compared with 11% countywide.

Supply is also tightening. The county's federal TEFAP commodity allocation, about 30% of our inventory, will be cut by 18% in fiscal year 2027. That is a loss of roughly 5% of our total food supply. We plan to replace it through [food sourcing plan].

## The project
Our mobile pantry currently runs two refrigerated trucks to nine rural sites each month. This project adds:
- a third refrigerated truck,
- a full-time driver and a site coordinator, and
- six new monthly distribution sites in the three highest-need townships.

Each visit provides about 42 pounds of food per household, including fresh and refrigerated items. In year one we expect to serve [number] households and distribute [number] pounds.

## Sites and schedule
The county Department of Social Services will host all six sites in its field-office parking lots at no cost. This lets us launch quickly and connects clients to DSS services on site. However, DSS lots are available only on weekdays from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. In our survey of rural clients, 58% work hourly jobs, and most prefer evening or Saturday distributions. During year one we will:
- recruit evening and Saturday host sites in each township [named prospects, if any], and
- track attendance by time slot so the schedule follows client needs.

## Cost-effectiveness
Mobile distribution costs $0.61 per pound, compared with $0.19 through our 61 volunteer-run partner pantries. That works out to about $26 per household visit. We use the mobile model only where volunteer pantries cannot reach people.

We hold the existing program to the same standard. Attendance has fallen at four of our nine current sites. We are reviewing each one and will consolidate or relocate sites that no longer meet need.

FACTS:
Harvest Table served 18,200 households in 2025, up 26% from 14,450 in 2023. It distributes food through 61 partner pantries and a mobile pantry program. The mobile pantry uses 2 refrigerated trucks to visit 9 rural sites once a month. The proposed expansion adds a third refrigerated truck and 6 new mobile sites in Hollis, Wren and Tamarack townships. Food insecurity in those townships is estimated at 17%, compared with 11% for the county as a whole. Projected costs: refrigerated truck $165,000 (one-time); a driver and a site coordinator $92,000 a year combined; fuel and maintenance $28,000 a year. First-year cost is $285,000. The Carlisle Foundation's guidelines say capital purchases may not exceed 40% of any grant award. Grants are for one year only, and applicants must explain how ongoing costs will be paid after the grant ends. Each mobile pantry visit distributes about 42 pounds of food per household. The mobile program costs Harvest Table about $0.61 per pound distributed, compared with $0.19 per pound through partner pantries, which use volunteers and donated space. Attendance has been falling at 4 of the current 9 mobile sites over the past year, and one board member has asked whether those sites are still needed. The county Department of Social Services has offered to host the new sites in the parking lots of its 6 field offices free of charge, but only on weekdays between 9 a.m. and 4 p.m. In a survey of rural clients, 58% said they work hourly jobs, and most said they would prefer evening or Saturday distributions. The county's allocation of federal TEFAP commodity food, which makes up about 30% of Harvest Table's inventory, will be cut by 18% for fiscal year 2027. Harvest Table's operating budget is $3.4 million, and it ended 2025 with an operating surplus of $40,000. A local grocery chain has offered to donate the truck's branding wrap and $15,000 a year in gift cards for clients.

KNOWN COMPLICATIONS:
- The truck ($165k) is 66% of a $250k request, well above the foundation's 40% capital cap ($100k), so the budget as proposed is not eligible.
- $120k a year in ongoing staff and running costs continues after a one-time grant, and the food bank's $40k surplus cannot cover it; the foundation requires a sustainability plan.
- The mobile pantry costs more than three times as much per pound as partner pantries, and 4 of the existing 9 sites are declining, which weakens the case for expansion unless it is clearly explained.
- The free host sites are only open on weekday daytimes, but most clients work hourly jobs and prefer evenings or Saturdays; in addition, the 18% TEFAP cut may limit how much food is available to distribute.

AUTHOR'S ANTICIPATED CRITIQUES:
1. **Why a third truck?** Two trucks covering nine monthly visits make only about 4–5 trips each per month. Reviewers will ask why the six new sites can't be served with existing trucks, which would save $165,000. The application needs data showing the current trucks are fully used, or the request should be redesigned.
2. **Sustainability is unfunded.** The program needs $120,000 a year after the grant, three times the 2025 surplus. The gift cards help clients but don't pay operating costs. Without named, committed sources, this fails a stated Foundation requirement.
3. **The $65,000 truck gap.** The capital cap leaves $65,000 of the truck unfunded. If that money isn't committed, the project may not be able to start.
4. **Hours conflict with clients' schedules.** Weekday 9–4 sites may miss the hourly workers who most need help. Reviewers will question whether the new sites will be well attended, especially since four existing sites are already declining.
5. **Declining sites undercut the case for expansion.** Falling attendance at current sites raises doubts about demand for mobile pantries. Reviewers may expect the four sites to be reallocated before new capacity is added.
6. **No reach targets.** The summary has no projected households or pounds for the new sites. Without them, reviewers can't judge cost per household served or impact.
7. **Higher cost per pound.** $0.61 per pound is three times the partner-pantry cost. Reviewers will ask whether recruiting volunteer pantries in these townships would be cheaper. The summary asserts this isn't possible but doesn't show it.
8. **Shrinking food supply.** The TEFAP cut reduces inventory just as the project adds distribution. Without a sourcing plan, the new sites may draw food away from existing clients.
9. **Mismatch with the requested amount.** If the foundation was told to expect a $250,000 request, the change to $220,000 needs explaining. A $250,000 request with only these costs would visibly break the capital cap.
