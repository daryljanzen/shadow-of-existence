DOCUMENT:
**MEMO**

**To:** CEO, Cedar Valley Health Partners
**From:** Information Security
**Date:** 13 November 2026
**Re:** Phishing-resistant MFA: funding and scope decision before the 1 March 2027 insurance renewal

**Recommendation**

Fund a phased rollout of hardware security keys rather than the full 1,150-user deployment at once.

- **Phase 1 (now):** Approve about $32,400 so the 62 privileged users and 210 remote-access users have keys before renewal.
- **Phase 2 (Q4 2027):** Approve about $104,500 to extend keys to the remaining 878 staff for email and VPN, and retire SMS codes.
- **Clinicians:** Do not require keys for clinicians' shared-workstation logins. Close the EHR gap through version 9.2 single sign-on, plus interim controls starting now.

**Why act**

Both compromises this year exploited the weaknesses this proposal addresses:

- A SIM swap defeated SMS codes on an email account.
- A reused password opened the EHR and exposed 1,900 patient records.

In the latest phishing test, 4% of staff (about 46 people) entered their credentials. SMS codes do not stop real-time phishing. Hardware keys do.

**Insurance economics**

If we miss the insurer's condition, the premium rises 35%, or **$63,000 a year**. Phase 1 costs:

- Keys: 272 users × 2 × $55 = $29,920
- Licenses: 272 × $9 = $2,448
- **Total: about $32,400**, roughly half of one year's premium increase.

Phase 1 pays for itself in its first year.

**Why not deploy fully now**

The full proposal costs about $143,000 in year one ($126,500 keys, $10,350 licenses, about $6,300 in replacements). It has three problems:

1. **Clinical time.** 340 clinicians × 40 logins × 15 seconds is about 10 minutes per clinician per shift, or roughly 57 staff-hours a day taken from patient care. Clinical leaders' objection is well founded.
2. **The EHR cannot use it yet.** The EHR supports MFA only from version 9.2, which arrives in Q3 2027. Keys would not protect the EHR before then.
3. **IT capacity.** Six IT staff are preparing the EHR upgrade, and the renewal window is 15 weeks, including holidays.

**Closing the EHR access gap (plan for the April audit committee)**

*Interim, Q1 2027:*
- Screen all EHR passwords against breached-password lists and force resets.
- Restrict EHR login to clinic networks and VPN.
- Alert on unusual record-access volume, since the 1,900-record access went unnoticed.

*Q3 2027, with version 9.2:*
- Enable single sign-on with MFA.
- At shared workstations, require full MFA once at the start of a shift, then badge tap plus a short PIN for repeat logins. This keeps logins close to today's speed.
- Design this with clinical leaders and validate it with the vendor.

**Risks and open questions**

- **Insurer definition.** We must confirm in writing that Phase 1 satisfies "privileged accounts and all remote access," and whether webmail counts as remote access. If it does, all staff fall in scope and the deadline is unrealistic. We need the answer within two weeks.
- **Licensing.** The vendor may require organization-wide licensing ($10,350 a year) rather than per-user licensing.
- **Overlap.** Some privileged users also use remote access, so 272 is an upper bound.
- **Lost keys.** Expect about 115 lost keys a year (about $6,300). Issuing two keys per person prevents lockouts.
- **Residual exposure.** Until Phase 2, about 878 email accounts remain on SMS codes.

**Decision needed by 1 December** so keys can be ordered and enrolled before renewal.

##

FACTS:
Cedar Valley Health Partners employs 1,150 staff across 14 clinics. Email and remote access (VPN) currently use multi-factor authentication with one-time codes sent by text message (SMS). The electronic health record (EHR) system uses only a username and password. The EHR vendor supports single sign-on with multi-factor authentication only in version 9.2, and Cedar Valley's upgrade to 9.2 is scheduled for Q3 2027. In the past 12 months, Cedar Valley had two account compromises. One was an email account taken over through a SIM swap that redirected the employee's text messages. The other was an EHR account accessed with a password reused from a breached website; the attacker viewed 1,900 patient records before the access was noticed. In the latest phishing simulation, 11% of staff clicked the link and 4% entered their credentials. The proposal is to give each staff member two hardware security keys at $55 each and to upgrade the identity platform license at $9 per user per year. The IT helpdesk expects about 1 in 10 users to lose a key each year. Of the 1,150 staff, 340 are clinical staff (mainly nurses and medical assistants) who share workstations and currently log in with a badge tap. They log in about 40 times per shift. The security team estimates that using a hardware key would add about 15 seconds per login. Clinical leaders oppose anything that slows down logins during patient care. Cedar Valley's cyber insurance renews on March 1, 2027. The insurer has said that if phishing-resistant MFA is not in place for "privileged accounts and all remote access" by renewal, the premium will rise 35% from its current $180,000. There are 62 IT and administrative staff with privileged access. 210 staff use remote access regularly. The IT team has 6 people and is also preparing the EHR upgrade. The board's audit committee has asked for a plan to address "the EHR access gap" by its next meeting in April.

KNOWN COMPLICATIONS:
- The biggest exposure, the EHR (password only, and the source of the actual patient-data breach), is not covered by the hardware key rollout until the 9.2 upgrade. A memo focused only on email and VPN misses the main risk.
- Shared-workstation clinical staff face significant friction: 340 staff x 40 logins x 15 seconds is about 57 staff-hours per day of added login time. Clinical leaders' objection is serious.
- The insurer only requires privileged and remote access to be covered by March 1. A phased rollout to about 272 users meets that requirement and avoids the 35% ($63k) premium increase, at much lower cost and workload than equipping all 1,150 users at once.
- The license is an ongoing cost ($10,350 a year for all users) plus replacing lost keys, so the program is not a one-time $126,500 purchase.

AUTHOR'S ANTICIPATED CRITIQUES:
1. **The clinical time estimate is unverified.** The 15-second and 40-logins figures come from the security team and were not measured. The memo also does not convert clinician time into dollars, so the trade-off is asserted rather than weighed.
2. **Insurer acceptance is the biggest risk but is left unresolved.** The whole business case depends on how the insurer interprets "all remote access," yet the memo gives no fallback plan if webmail is included.
3. **The breach cost is not quantified.** There is no estimate of HIPAA notification, regulatory, or legal costs (for example, from the 1,900-record incident). That understates the value of protection beyond the premium.
4. **Interim EHR controls are unpriced and unresourced.** Breached-password screening, network restriction, and access alerting all need tooling and staff time that the memo does not cost.
5. **IT labor is ignored.** Enrollment, helpdesk load, and lost-key handling for a six-person team are not costed, and Phase 2 lands during EHR upgrade stabilization.
6. **Alternatives are not evaluated.** Device-bound passkeys or platform authenticators, and FIDO2-enabled badges, might be cheaper or faster for clinicians.
7. **The shared-workstation design is not phishing-resistant on repeat logins.** "Badge tap plus PIN" is a proposal that is neither vendor-confirmed nor shown to satisfy the audit committee.
8. **Residual risk is long.** Most staff stay on SIM-swappable SMS for about a year, yet the memo treats Phase 2 timing as given rather than justified.
9. **The cost figures are upper bounds with unstated assumptions.** These include the overlap between groups, per-user licensing, and whether replacements fall in year one.
10. **The 1 December deadline is not justified.** The memo does not show the hardware lead times or the enrollment schedule behind it.
11. **No owner, milestones, or success metrics are named.** For example, there is no enrollment-completion target and no repeat phishing test.

FINDINGS:
- The memo's Phase 1 scope covers only the 210 staff who use remote access regularly, while the insurer's condition covers all remote access, so occasional VPN users could fall outside Phase 1 and cause the plan to fail the insurer's condition.
- The claim that both compromises exploited the weaknesses the proposal addresses is overstated, because keys would have stopped the SIM-swap email takeover but could not have stopped the EHR breach until version 9.2.
- The claim that Phase 1 pays for itself in its first year depends on the insurer accepting Phase 1 scope, which the memo itself lists as an open risk, yet the claim is stated without qualification.
- The organization-wide licensing risk is raised but not costed, so the memo does not show that the recommendation still holds (Phase 1 would rise to about $40,300, still under $63,000).
- Saying two keys per person 'prevents' lockouts overstates the effect, since users can lose both keys or leave the spare at home.
- The claim that badge plus PIN keeps logins close to today's speed is unvalidated and is likely to be tested by clinical leaders.
- It is unclear whether the CEO is being asked to approve Phase 2's ~$104,500 now or in 2027, because the decision deadline only covers Phase 1.
- Phase 1's recurring costs are omitted: about $2,450 a year in licenses plus about $1,500 for replacement keys.
- The memo flags that 878 accounts stay on SMS until Q4 2027 but proposes no interim mitigation, such as an authenticator app with number matching, for the SIM-swap risk that has already occurred.
- The memo is roughly 620–650 words, over the 450–600 word target, and the 'Why not deploy fully now' section partly repeats material from elsewhere.
