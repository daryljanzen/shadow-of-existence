# Grant Summary: Larch Commons

**Applicant:** Northside Community Housing
**Request:** $2,000,000 from the State Housing Trust Fund
**Project:** Larch Commons, 48 units of affordable rental housing

## The Request

Northside Community Housing requests $2.0 million to close the final financing gap for Larch Commons, a 48-unit affordable rental building on a vacant lot 0.3 miles from the Larch Street light rail station. The request equals $41,667 per unit, below the Trust Fund's $45,000 per-unit maximum. It is the last source needed. With this award, the project's financing is complete.

## Financing

Total development cost is $19.2 million. Committed sources:

| Source | Amount |
|---|---|
| Low-Income Housing Tax Credit equity | $11,500,000 |
| Bank construction and permanent loan | $3,100,000 |
| City housing loan | $2,000,000 |
| Deferred developer fee | $600,000 |
| **Committed subtotal** | **$17,200,000** |
| **Trust Fund request** | **$2,000,000** |
| **Total** | **$19,200,000** |

The per-unit cost is $400,000, which exceeds the Trust Fund's $350,000 priority threshold. Construction costs in the region are rising about 6% a year, according to Northside's architect. Any delay therefore raises the cost of delivering these units.

## Affordability

All 48 units will be affordable for 30 years, as the Trust Fund requires:

- **6 units (12.5%)** for households at or below 30% of area median income (AMI)
- **12 units (25%)** at 50% of AMI
- **30 units (62.5%)** at 60% of AMI

Rents at the 30% AMI level do not cover operating costs on their own. The county housing authority has committed six project-based rental vouchers to close that gap for 15 years. ## Readiness and Urgency

The city approved the project's zoning in September after a public hearing. The tax credit allocation expires if financing does not close by June 30, 2027. The Trust Fund announces awards in May 2027, so an award this round is the only way to keep the $11.5 million in tax credit equity. ## Site and Community

The site is within walking distance of light rail. The building provides 24 parking spaces, a ratio of 0.5 per unit. Some neighbours raised concerns at the zoning hearing about the building's size and its parking. The city approved the project after considering those concerns. A ground-floor community room will host after-school tutoring run by a local nonprofit.

## Track Record

Since 2005, Northside has completed 11 affordable housing projects. All remain in operation and in compliance with their affordability requirements.

AUTHOR'S ANTICIPATED CRITIQUES:
1. **Cost exceeds the priority threshold.** At $400,000 per unit, the project is $50,000 per unit (14%) over the $350,000 priority line. The summary gives no explanation, such as site conditions, the community room, structured parking, or prevailing wage. Citing cost escalation does not explain the baseline cost.

2. **Deep affordability falls short of maximum points.** Six units at 30% AMI is 12.5% of the building. Maximum points require 20%, which is 10 units. A reviewer will ask why Northside did not convert four 50% or 60% units to reach the threshold.

3. **The voucher term covers only half the affordability period.** The vouchers run for 15 years, but the units must stay affordable for 30. Without a funded reserve or a credible renewal plan, the 30% AMI units look financially unsustainable in years 16–30. The placeholder must be filled with a real plan.

4. **The closing timeline is very tight.** Awards come in May 2027 and the closing deadline is June 30, 2027, leaving roughly 30–60 days to close. Reviewers will want evidence that lender, investor, and city documents can be ready, and a fallback if they are not.

5. **Cost escalation risk is not addressed.** If costs rise 6% a year, the $19.2 million budget could grow before closing. The summary does not say when the budget was priced, what contingency is carried, or who covers overruns. A larger gap after award would be a red flag.

6. **Zoning risk is unclear.** The approval was contested. The summary does not say whether an appeal period has passed or whether litigation is possible.

7. **Parking and scale opposition may concern reviewers.** Transit proximity supports a 0.5 parking ratio, but the summary offers no parking study, demand data, or mitigation measures.

8. **Other commitments are not documented.** The summary does not say whether the tax credit equity, the city loan, and the bank loan are firm commitments or letters of interest, or what their terms are.

9. **The track record lacks scale and relevance.** "11 projects" says nothing about unit counts, recent activity, or experience with tax credit deals of this size and timeline.

10. **The deferred fee may raise questions.** A reviewer may ask whether $0.6 million can realistically be repaid from cash flow, given the thin margins on the 30% AMI units.
