# MEMORANDUM

**To:** City Engineer, City of Marlow
**From:** Project Risk Review
**Date:** November 13, 2026
**Subject:** Fifth Street Bridge deck replacement: risk comparison of full closure and staged construction

## Recommendation

Recommend **Option 1 (full closure)** to council. Attach firm conditions on emergency response, business access and schedule control. Option 2, as bid, cannot meet the federal grant deadline. If the grant is lost, the city's share rises far above the finance committee's cap.

## Key figures

| | Option 1: Full closure | Option 2: Staged |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 120 days | 270 days |
| Earliest finish (start May 15, 2027) | ~Sept 12, 2027 | ~Feb 9, 2028 |
| Low bid | $6.1M | $7.4M |
| City share (after $4.0M grant) | $2.1M | $3.4M |
| Margin under $3.5M cap | $1.4M | $0.1M |
| Road user cost* | $7.02M | $7.13M |
| Total (bid + road user cost) | $13.1M | $14.5M |

*Road user cost for Option 1 is 22,000 vehicles × 3.8 mi × $0.70 × 120 days. For Option 2 it is 22,000 vehicles × 4 min × $18/hr × 270 days. Road user cost is an economic cost, not a city cash cost.

## Decisive risks

**1. Grant deadline (Option 2 fails).** There are only 230 days between May 15 and December 31, 2027. Option 2 needs 270. Deck pours are normally impossible from December through February, so Option 2's pours would have to finish by about late November. That is roughly 200 days after the start, which would require cutting a quarter of the schedule. Losing the $4.0M grant would raise the city's share to $7.4M, more than double the cap. Option 1 finishes with about 110 days of float before the deadline.

**2. Budget cap (Option 2 marginal).** Even if the grant is kept, Option 2 leaves $100,000 of headroom, which is 1.4% of the contract. A single routine change order would break the cap. Option 1 leaves $1.4M for contingency.

**3. Water main relocation (both options).** Construction cannot begin until the utility finishes, on May 15, 2027. Every week of utility slippage comes straight off the float. Option 1 can absorb roughly ten weeks of slippage before the pour window closes. Option 2 has no float to absorb any slippage.

## Risks that Option 1 creates, and mitigations

**Emergency response.** East-side ambulance transports to Mercy Hospital would take about 6 minutes longer. About 2,300 north-east households would fall outside the 8-minute fire standard for four months. This is the most serious cost of Option 1 and should be a condition of approval. Mitigations to fund:
- Station a temporary engine and an ambulance on the east side.
- Formalise mutual aid agreements.
- Have the fire chief sign off on the plan before the contract is awarded.

**Businesses.** The closure falls in the fourteen businesses' peak summer season. Mitigations:
- Keep full access from the east side.
- Provide wayfinding signage and a "open for business" campaign.
- Consider a small relief or marketing fund from the $1.4M headroom.
- Seek a pedestrian and cyclist crossing if it is structurally feasible.

**School buses.** The closure overlaps the start of the school year by about three weeks. Coordinate detour routes and timings with the school district by spring 2027.

**Detour performance.** Confirm that Route 9 can absorb the extra traffic. Adjust signal timing before the closure begins.

## Contract conditions

- Set a fixed closure duration with incentives for early finish and liquidated damages for late finish, sized to the road user cost (about $58,500 per day).
- Require written confirmation of the water utility's May 15 date, with monthly progress reporting.
- Require the contractor's pour schedule to finish no later than October 2027.

## Bottom line

Road user costs are nearly equal, so they do not separate the options. The grant deadline and the cost cap do. Option 1 meets both with margin. Its real harms are emergency response and business disruption. These are significant but can be managed, and they last four months rather than nine.

##

FACTS:
The Fifth Street Bridge carries about 22,000 vehicles a day across the Tanner River and connects the city centre with the east side. Its deck must be replaced. Option 1, full closure: the bridge closes completely for about 4 months (120 days). The low bid is $6.1 million. Traffic would detour via the Route 9 bridge, which adds about 3.8 miles per trip. Option 2, staged replacement: half the bridge is rebuilt at a time while one lane stays open in each direction. Construction takes about 9 months (270 days). The low bid is $7.4 million. Traffic engineers estimate an average delay of 4 minutes per vehicle. The state's guidance for road user costs uses $0.70 per vehicle-mile for detours and $18 per vehicle-hour for delays. The project has a $4.0 million federal grant that requires substantial completion by December 31, 2027. The concrete deck pours must be done when temperatures will stay above 40°F, which in Marlow normally rules out December through February. The water utility must first move a water main that runs along the bridge. It has scheduled that work to finish on May 15, 2027, and construction cannot start until then. Fire Station 4, on the east side, covers the east side of the city. The east side has no hospital. Mercy Hospital is in the city centre. During a full closure, ambulances from the east side would need to use the Route 9 detour, adding about 6 minutes to transport times. The fire chief has said that a full closure would also push response times above the department's 8-minute standard for about 2,300 households in the north-east, which are normally covered from the city-centre station. Fourteen businesses on Fifth Street east of the bridge have written to the council. They say a four-month closure in the summer, their busiest season, could put some of them out of business. Three school bus routes use the bridge during the school year (late August to mid-June). The council's finance committee has said that the city's share of the cost must not go over $3.5 million.

KNOWN COMPLICATIONS:
- Road user costs are about the same for both options: about $7.02M for full closure (83,600 detour vehicle-miles a day x $0.70 x 120 days) and about $7.13M for staged (about 1,467 vehicle-hours a day x $18 x 270 days). Road user cost does not decide the choice.
- The schedule works against the staged option: work cannot start until May 15, 9 months ends around mid-February, and concrete cannot be poured from December to February, so it would miss the December 31 grant deadline. The full closure (ending around mid-September) fits.
- Emergency response: a full closure adds about 6 minutes to ambulance trips and breaks the 8-minute response standard for 2,300 households, which is a public safety issue rather than a convenience issue.
- The funding limit and business impact: Option 1's city share with the grant is $2.1M and Option 2's is $3.4M, both under $3.5M only if the grant is kept. A summer closure falls on businesses' busiest season, but it also avoids the school year.

AUTHOR'S ANTICIPATED CRITIQUES:
1. **Road user cost assumptions are crude.** The memo assumes all 22,000 vehicles detour the full 3.8 miles. In practice some trips would be cancelled, rerouted elsewhere or shifted in time. It also treats the 4-minute delay as uniform, when peak-hour queuing could be far worse. It does not say whether 22,000 is a weekday or an annual average daily figure.
2. **The emergency risk is not valued.** Response-time harm to 2,300 households and 6-minute ambulance delays are serious life-safety impacts. The memo lists them but does not quantify them. The cost and staffing of the proposed mitigations are not estimated and are not included in the $2.1M.
3. **Business harm is not valued.** The memo assumes signage and relief funds are enough, with no evidence or cost estimate.
4. **Option 2 is dismissed without exploring fixes.** These include accelerating the schedule, working multiple shifts, a winter-pour plan using heated enclosures, asking the grant agency for an extension, or rebidding with a schedule requirement. The memo also does not check whether "substantial completion" could come before final completion.
5. **The schedule analysis is simplistic.** Contractor durations are taken at face value. Mobilisation, curing time, weather days and testing are not shown. The October pour target is asserted rather than derived.
6. **Mitigations are paid from headroom without being costed.** Spending part of the $1.4M weakens the contingency argument.
7. **Some risks are not considered.** These include detour pavement wear, crash risk on Route 9, work-zone safety in Option 2, and the possibility that higher bid prices reflect a different risk allocation.
8. **There is no sensitivity analysis.** The memo does not test how the conclusion changes if the water main slips, bids are revised or traffic volumes differ.
9. **There is no stakeholder or equity analysis.** It does not ask who bears the burden, such as east-side residents versus through traffic.

FINDINGS:
- The memo runs to about 700 words, exceeding the brief's 450–600 word limit.
- The memo details Option 1's harms but omits Option 2's own impacts (nine months of traffic delay, business disruption through summer and winter, about four months of school bus disruption, and continued ambulance slowdowns), making the comparison look one-sided.
- The $1.4M headroom is counted twice: Risk 2 treats it as contingency while the uncosted mitigations (temporary engine and ambulance, business relief, signage and marketing, signal retiming) also draw on it, which weakens the claim that Option 1 meets the cap with margin.
- Option 2's winter problem is understated, because a 270-day schedule from May 15 runs into December–February when pours can't happen, so its real finish could be later than Feb 9.
- Risk 1 conflates finishing pours by late November with substantial completion, ignoring post-pour work and the two separate sets of pours under staged construction.
- The sentence 'This is the most serious cost of Option 1 and should be a condition of approval' is ambiguous because it reads as if the cost itself is the condition.
- The memo does not specify that the temporary emergency units should be placed in the north-east, given that Fire Station 4 already covers the east side and the affected 2,300 households are normally served from the city-centre station.
- The memo does not note that liquidated damages of $58,500/day may raise bid prices and should be confirmed with the city attorney.
