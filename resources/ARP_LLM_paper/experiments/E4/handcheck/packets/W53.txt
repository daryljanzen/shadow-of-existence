QUESTION:
How many yellow cards lead to a one-match suspension?

CANDIDATE A:
Five cautions.

CANDIDATE B:
Five cautions.

RESPONSE:
I checked each answer against the rules text. My earlier notes were wrong on questions 2, 3 and 4, so please go by the answers below.

**1. Yellow cards → one-match suspension**
**Five** cautions in league and league cup matches bring a one-match ban. Ten cautions bring a further two-match ban (Rule 15.1).

**2. Left-back off at half-time, back on later (Division One)**
**No.** In the Premier Division and Divisions One and Two, a substituted player **may not return** to the field, and you can make **at most three substitutions** (Rule 9.2). Rolling substitutions are allowed only in Divisions Three and Four (Rule 9.3). My notes wrongly said rolling subs applied in all divisions.

**3. Away team: money for the referee?**
**No.** The **home club** pays the £40 fee before kick-off, and the away club pays nothing towards it (Rule 11.2). My notes had this the wrong way round.

**4. Emailed on Wednesday that you can't raise a team for Sunday**
**Probably not.** You should avoid the 3–0 forfeit and the £50 fine if both of these are true:
- **You gave at least 48 hours' written notice to both the league and your opponents.** Wednesday to Sunday is comfortably more than 48 hours, and an email should count as writing. It's still worth confirming the league received it.
- **You haven't already used this notice provision twice this season.** It can only be used twice per season.

If both apply, the Fixtures Secretary will rearrange the match and there's no fine (Rule 13.2). If this would be your third use, the forfeit and £50 fine would apply. Separately, failing to fulfil three fixtures in a season can lead to expulsion (Rule 13.3). My notes left out the 48-hour exception entirely.
