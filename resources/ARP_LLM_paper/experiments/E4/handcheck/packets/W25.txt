QUESTION:
My daughter missed the morning for an orthodontist appointment, but I lost the appointment letter and have nothing to show. Will the absence be authorised?

CANDIDATE A:
Yes. Absence for medical or dental appointments is authorised.

CANDIDATE B:
No. Without an appointment card, letter or text as evidence, it is recorded as unauthorised.

RESPONSE:
Note: my earlier notes were wrong on three points (reporting, appointments and penalty notices). The answers below come from the policy itself.

**1. Yes, call on Tuesday and Wednesday too.** You must report by 8.45 am on *each* day of absence (section 3). A first-day message only covers several days if you gave an expected return date and he comes back on that date. Since you didn't give one, ring or use the app each morning. If you now know he'll be back Thursday, you could tell them that on Tuesday's call.

**2. Registers close at 9.15 am.** They're taken at 8.50 am. Arriving between 8.50 and 9.15 counts as late but present. Arriving after 9.15 without a valid reason is an unauthorised absence for the morning.

**3. Probably not, as things stand.** Section 4 authorises medical or dental absence only if you show the office an appointment card, letter or text. Without evidence, it's recorded as unauthorised. The policy doesn't say whether evidence can be provided afterwards. It's worth asking the orthodontist for a replacement letter or confirmation text and checking with the office whether they'll accept it.

**4. The local authority issues it, not the school.** If there are 10 or more unauthorised sessions (5 days) within a rolling 10 school weeks, including unauthorised holidays, the school refers the matter to the local authority. The penalty is £80 per parent per child if paid within 21 days, rising to £160 if paid within 28 days.
