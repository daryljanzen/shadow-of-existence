QUESTION:
What is the normal minimum age to volunteer?

CANDIDATE A:
16 (14–15-year-olds only in the warehouse on Saturday mornings with a registered parent or guardian).

CANDIDATE B:
16 (14–15-year-olds only in the warehouse on Saturday mornings with a registered parent or guardian).

RESPONSE:
1. **Normal minimum age:** 16. (14- and 15-year-olds can volunteer only in the warehouse on Saturday mornings, accompanied throughout by a parent or guardian who is also a registered volunteer.) Everyone completes a two-hour induction before their first shift. *(Section 3)*

2. **DBS check for warehouse sorting:** No. An enhanced DBS check is required **only for home delivery drivers**, because they visit clients in their homes. Warehouse, distribution centre and collection roles don't need one. Note that you must never work alone in the warehouse; at least two volunteers must be present. *(Sections 4–5)*

3. **Advising on disability benefits:** No. Volunteers must not give advice about benefits, debt, housing or immigration, **even with professional experience**, because the food bank isn't authorised to give it. Instead, refer the client to the Citizens Advice adviser (at the distribution centre on Tuesdays and Thursdays) or give them the signposting leaflet. *(Section 6)*

4. **Approving your mileage claim:** The **Volunteer Coordinator**. Shift leads cannot approve expenses. Submit the expenses form within two months of the journey, with parking receipts. You're paid 45p a mile plus parking, and the Treasurer pays by bank transfer within 14 days of approval. *(Section 8)*

**Correction to my earlier notes:** My summary had three errors that affect these answers. It said all volunteers need a DBS check, that experienced volunteers can advise clients on benefits, debt or housing, and that shift leads approve expenses. The handbook says the opposite in each case, and the answers above follow the handbook.
