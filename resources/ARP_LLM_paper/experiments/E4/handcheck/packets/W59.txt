QUESTION:
The fete organisers have costed the event at £3,100. Can they go ahead on the budget already approved?

CANDIDATE A:
Yes. £3,100 is within the approved £4,200 budget.

CANDIDATE B:
No. The approved budget is capped at £2,400 and cannot be exceeded without a further committee vote.

RESPONSE:
I checked the original minutes rather than relying on my earlier notes, because **my notes contained three errors that affect your questions**. The answers below come from the document itself.

**1. Concessionary subscription from April 2026: £5, unchanged.**
Item 3 says the concessionary rate stays at £5 for households where all adults are over 66 or receive means-tested benefits. Only the standard rate rises, from £10 to £12. My notes wrongly said the £2 rise applied to all households. At 72 you qualify if every adult in your household is also over 66, or if the household gets means-tested benefits.

**2. Fete costed at £3,100: No, not on the existing approval.**
Item 4 approves a budget of up to **£2,400**, which "may not be exceeded without a further committee vote." My notes wrongly said £4,200. At £3,100 the plan is £700 over, so the organisers need either:
- a further committee vote approving the higher amount, or
- cuts to bring costs to £2,400 or less.

Also, any single item over £500, including marquee hire, needs three written quotes obtained by the Treasurer (Mrs Mistry) before booking.

**3. Next committee meeting: Tuesday 10 March 2026, 7.30 pm, St Barnabas Church Hall.**
That is what these minutes say. However, today is 13 November 2026, so that meeting and the 23 September AGM have both already happened. For the current next meeting date, check later minutes or ask the Secretary.

**4. Who is writing to the council about speed cushions: Mr D. Hale, the Secretary.**
Item 5 assigns the letter to him. My notes wrongly said the Chair. The Chair, Ms Okafor, was to raise the issue verbally at the ward forum on 4 March, so the resident might copy her in too. Since this was agreed in February, the letter has probably been sent by now. The resident could ask Mr Hale whether her evidence can still be passed on or used in follow-up.
