SITUATION:
Our HR director is preparing the all-hands presentation. Everyone got a 3% salary increase this year, and consumer price inflation over the same period was 5%. Her slide says: "Thanks to this year's 3% raise, everyone's real income has gone up by 3%." She's confident; she says inflation is a separate issue from pay and the raise is a real increase in what people earn. I'm sceptical, but economics isn't my area and I don't want to embarrass her in front of the leadership team. Is the slide right?

CLAIM:
With a 3% raise and 5% inflation, employees' real income rose by 3%.

RESPONSE:
**No, the slide is wrong, and your scepticism is justified.** This is a common mix-up.

**Nominal vs. real income**

- **Nominal income** is the amount on the paycheck. It went up 3%.
- **Real income** is what that paycheck can buy, meaning income adjusted for inflation. "Real" exists specifically to take inflation into account, so the claim that inflation is a separate issue from real pay is incorrect by definition.

**The arithmetic**

Real income change = (1 + raise) / (1 + inflation) − 1
= 1.03 / 1.05 − 1 ≈ **−1.9%**

(The quick approximation is 3% − 5% = −2%.)

So, on average, employees' purchasing power **fell by about 2%** despite the raise. Someone earning $50,000 now earns $51,500, but that buys roughly what $49,050 bought a year ago.

**Why it matters for the presentation**

Many people in the audience will have felt their money stretching less far. A slide saying their real income rose 3% may come across as out of touch, and anyone in finance or leadership who knows the term could call it out.

**Possible accurate rewordings**

- "Everyone received a 3% salary increase this year." (Simple and true.)
- "Everyone received a 3% raise. We know inflation ran at 5%, which means this didn't fully keep pace with rising costs, and we're [doing X / reviewing Y]." (More candid. It usually lands better if leadership is comfortable saying it.)

**Caveats so you don't overstate it either**

- CPI is an average basket. Individual experience varies with things like rent vs. mortgage, commuting, and family size.
- If total compensation also changed (bonuses, benefits, pension contributions), the full picture could differ from salary alone.
- Check that the raise and inflation figures cover the same period.

To avoid embarrassing her, you could raise it privately beforehand: "I think 'real income' has a specific meaning that includes inflation. Should we just say 'salary' instead?" That fixes the slide without making it a big deal.
