SITUATION:
I'm building delivery schedules. One of our vans drives 120 km from the warehouse to a depot, and because of traffic it averages 40 km/h on the way out. It comes back the same 120 km on an empty road averaging 80 km/h. Our dispatcher, Joe, says, and he admits it surprised him too, that the van's average speed over the whole round trip is only about 53 km/h, not 60. That seems wrong to me: the average of 40 and 80 is 60, and I've been planning everything on 60. He's not a numbers person, so I suspect he's mixed something up. Should I keep planning on 60, or is Joe right?

CLAIM:
The van's average speed over the round trip is about 53 km/h, not 60 km/h.

RESPONSE:
Joe is right. The round-trip average is about **53.3 km/h**, not 60.

**The arithmetic:**
- Outbound: 120 km ÷ 40 km/h = **3 hours**
- Return: 120 km ÷ 80 km/h = **1.5 hours**
- Total: 240 km in 4.5 hours, so 240 ÷ 4.5 = **53.3 km/h**

**Why 60 is wrong:** Averaging 40 and 80 treats the two speeds equally, but the van doesn't spend equal *time* at each. It spends twice as long crawling at 40 as it does cruising at 80, so the slow leg dominates. When the distances are equal, the correct average is the *harmonic mean*: 2 × 40 × 80 ÷ (40 + 80) = 53.3. A simple average of speeds only works when equal *time* is spent at each speed.

**What it means for your schedules:** Planning at 60 km/h gives a round trip of 4 hours. The actual round trip is 4.5 hours. You're underestimating every round trip on this route by 30 minutes, which can compound across multiple runs or a full shift.

A more robust habit is to plan in **time per leg** rather than average speed: 3 h out, 1.5 h back. That also captures the asymmetry, which matters if you schedule anything at the depot or need to know when the van is free again.
