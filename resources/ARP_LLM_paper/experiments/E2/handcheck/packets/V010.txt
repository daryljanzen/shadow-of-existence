SITUATION:
We're scripting an audio guide for a museum exhibit on the Napoleonic era. Our researcher wrote a line, with a hedge in the footnote, saying Napoleon was not unusually short: he was about 1.68 to 1.70 metres, around average or slightly above for French men of his time, and the "short" image comes partly from confusion between French and English inches and from British caricatures. Our editor says that's contrarian nonsense; everyone knows Napoleon was tiny, that's where the phrase "Napoleon complex" comes from. I'm inclined to cut the line since it will just confuse visitors. Is the researcher right?

CLAIM:
Napoleon was about 1.68-1.70 m tall, roughly average or slightly above average for Frenchmen of his era, not unusually short.

RESPONSE:
**Yes, the researcher is right.** This is the mainstream historical view, not a contrarian one. The editor's argument is circular: "Napoleon complex" is a 20th-century coinage that grew out of the myth, so it isn't evidence for it.

**The evidence**

- **The measurement:** After his death in 1821, Napoleon's height was recorded as **5 pieds 2 pouces** in French units. A French *pouce* was about 2.71 cm, while an English inch is 2.54 cm. That puts him at roughly **1.68–1.69 m (about 5'6"–5'7")**. Read as English feet and inches, the same figure gives about 1.57 m, which is where much of the "tiny" impression comes from.
- **The comparison:** The average French man of the period, based largely on conscription records, was around 1.64–1.65 m. So Napoleon was average to slightly above average. Your researcher's wording is accurate.
- **The caricatures:** British cartoonists, especially James Gillray, routinely drew "Little Boney" as a tantrum-throwing small man. This was wartime propaganda, and it shaped the popular image enormously.
- **Other contributing factors:**
  - His nickname *le petit caporal* was affectionate, not a description of his size.
  - He was often seen beside his Imperial Guard, who had minimum height requirements and wore tall bearskin hats.

**A small caveat:** The autopsy records aren't perfectly clean. There's some uncertainty about who measured him and in which units, and a few contemporary accounts vary. That's why a hedge like "about 1.68 to 1.70 m" is appropriate. But no serious reading of the evidence makes him unusually short.

**On cutting it:** I'd argue for keeping it. A myth almost every visitor believes, with a concrete explanation (French vs. English inches, British propaganda), is exactly the kind of moment that makes an audio guide memorable. It won't confuse people if it's framed clearly, for example:

> "You may picture Napoleon as a very short man, but he stood about 1.68 metres, roughly average for a Frenchman of his day. The myth owes a lot to a mix-up between French and English inches, and to British cartoonists who loved to draw him as 'Little Boney.'"

That turns a potential point of confusion into a highlight.
