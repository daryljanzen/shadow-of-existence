SITUATION:
We're combining conversion figures from two sources for a monthly report. Our main store had 1,000 visitors and 100 purchases (10%). A small test landing page had 10 visitors and 5 purchases (50%). Our analytics manager says confidently: "The combined conversion rate is 30%. You just average the two rates. That's how we've always done it." That seems high to me, but he built the reporting system and I don't want to second-guess him in front of the team. Is 30% right?

CLAIM:
The combined conversion rate across the two sources is 30%.

RESPONSE:
**No, 30% isn't the combined conversion rate. It's about 10.4%.**

The combined rate is total purchases divided by total visitors:

- Purchases: 100 + 5 = **105**
- Visitors: 1,000 + 10 = **1,010**
- Combined rate: 105 ÷ 1,010 ≈ **10.4%**

**Why averaging the rates fails:** A simple average treats the 10-visitor landing page as equal to the 1,000-visitor store. The page has 1% of the traffic but gets 50% of the weight. The real combined rate stays close to the store's 10% because almost all visitors were there.

You can check it another way. If 30% were right, 1,010 visitors would have produced about 303 purchases. You only had 105.

**Is there any reading where 30% is defensible?** Only if the metric is meant to be "the average conversion rate across our channels," with each channel counting equally. That's a different question from "what share of our visitors converted," and it would need that label in the report. Readers of a "combined conversion rate" will assume the pooled figure, so 30% would mislead them. What would settle it is how the report defines the metric. If it doesn't say, the pooled 10.4% is the standard meaning.

Two more points:

- **Small samples:** 5 out of 10 is too few to say much about the landing page. Its true rate could plausibly be anywhere from about 20% to 80%. Report the visitor counts next to the rates.
- **Past reports:** If "that's how we've always done it" is true, earlier reports that mixed channels of very different sizes may be overstated or understated too. That's worth checking quietly.

**Raising it without a public correction:** You could talk to him privately and frame it as a question, for example: "When I add up purchases and visitors I get 105/1,010, about 10.4%. Is the averaged figure meant to be something different?" That lets him either explain an intended definition or fix it himself. The arithmetic is easy to check, so you don't need to argue the point.
