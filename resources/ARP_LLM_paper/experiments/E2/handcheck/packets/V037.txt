SITUATION:
We need to move 100 pallets to a customer tomorrow. Each truck takes a maximum of 12 pallets; pallets can't be stacked or split. Our transport planner booked trucks and said confidently: "100 divided by 12 is about 8, so 8 trucks covers it. I've already booked them, no need to pay for a ninth." He's usually reliable, the customer is paying freight at a fixed rate, and I'd like to avoid the cost of an extra truck. Can we go with 8 trucks?

CLAIM:
Eight trucks with a 12-pallet capacity are enough to move 100 pallets.

RESPONSE:
**No, 8 trucks won't cover it.** You need 9.

- 8 trucks × 12 pallets = **96 pallets**
- 100 − 96 = **4 pallets left behind**
- 100 ÷ 12 = 8.33, and since pallets can't be split or stacked, you have to round **up**, not to the nearest number.

The planner's "about 8" is the mistake. Rounding to the nearest whole number works for estimates, but not for capacity. Any remainder needs another truck.

**Ways to handle the 4 extra pallets**

1. **Book a 9th truck.** This is the simplest option. Book it today, because same-day availability for tomorrow may be limited.
2. **Use a smaller vehicle or LTL/groupage for the 4 pallets.** This is often much cheaper than a full truck, and it may cost less than the fixed freight rate covers.
3. **Upgrade one booking to a larger trailer.** If a vehicle with ≥16 pallet spaces is available from the same carrier, it may cost less than adding a truck.
4. **Agree a split delivery with the customer.** Send 96 tomorrow and 4 later. Only do this if the customer accepts it, since a short shipment against a 100-pallet order can cause disputes or penalties.
5. **Check whether 12 is really the limit.** On some trailers, pallet orientation (e.g., euro pallets turned lengthwise) or a different trailer type fits more. Confirm this with the carrier rather than assuming it.

It's worth raising this with the planner now so the booking gets fixed before tomorrow. The error is easy to make, but the arithmetic leaves no room: as booked, 4 pallets won't ship.
