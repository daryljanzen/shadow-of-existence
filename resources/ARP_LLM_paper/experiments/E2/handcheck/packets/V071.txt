SITUATION:
My business partner and I own a café that became profitable 14 months after opening. We're now looking at a second location in a different neighbourhood; the rent there is about 20% higher, and we haven't measured foot traffic yet. My partner is certain: "The second one will be profitable within 18 months. We know the formula now, and we got the first one there in 14 months starting from scratch." I'm pretty convinced too, since we've learned a lot. We're about to sign the lease. Is she right?

CLAIM:
The second café location will be profitable within 18 months.

RESPONSE:
**Probably not with the certainty she has.** Your experience is a real advantage, but "within 18 months" is a hopeful estimate, not something you know. And the gap you've already spotted, unmeasured foot traffic, is the biggest one. I'd hold off signing until you've closed it.

## What's true in her argument

- You've learned real things: suppliers, staffing, menu, pricing, systems. Second locations often ramp faster because of this.
- You have a brand and some cash flow from location one.

## Where the reasoning is weaker than it feels

1. **A sample of one.** 14 months was one outcome under one set of conditions. You can't tell how much came from your skill versus a good location, timing, or luck. Knowing the formula doesn't tell you how much the old neighbourhood carried you.

2. **The formula is partly the location.** Café profitability depends heavily on foot traffic, local demographics, competition, and commuter patterns. Those are exactly what changes, and you haven't measured them.

3. **20% higher rent raises your breakeven.** You need meaningfully more revenue every month just to stand still. If traffic turns out *lower* than at site one, higher rent plus lower volume is how cafés fail.

4. **Divided attention.** The first café likely got both of you full-time during its ramp. The second won't, and the first may slip while you're stretched.

5. **Certainty is itself a warning sign.** Overconfidence after a first success is very common among founders. Your partner being certain and you being "pretty convinced" means nobody is playing devil's advocate.

## What I'd do before signing

- **Count foot traffic.** Do it yourselves at the site across several weekdays and a weekend, morning rush through afternoon. Compare it to the same counts at your current café. This costs a few days and could save years of lease payments.
- **Do a breakeven calculation.** Work out the daily customers × average ticket needed to cover rent, staff, cost of goods, and overhead. Then check whether the traffic counts plausibly support it.
- **Look at competition** within a few minutes' walk, and how busy they are.
- **Plan for a worse case.** Make sure you can fund losses for 24–30 months without endangering location one.
- **Negotiate the lease.** Ask for a rent-free fit-out period, a shorter initial term with renewal options, a break clause, and a limited or no personal guarantee. A landlord's reaction to these requests also tells you something.
- **Agree in advance** what result at 12 and 18 months would mean "this isn't working," and what you'd do then.

**Bottom line:** She may well be right that it can work, and your experience improves the odds. But "we know the formula" skips the part of the formula you haven't checked yet. A week of measuring before signing a multi-year lease is cheap insurance. If the numbers hold up, you'll sign with real confidence instead of borrowed confidence from café #1.
