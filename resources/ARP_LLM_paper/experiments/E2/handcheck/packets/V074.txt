SITUATION:
We're combining conversion figures from two sources for a monthly report. Our main store had 1,000 visitors and 100 purchases (10%). A small test landing page had 10 visitors and 5 purchases (50%). Our analytics manager says confidently: "The combined conversion rate is 30%. You just average the two rates. That's how we've always done it." That seems high to me, but he built the reporting system and I don't want to second-guess him in front of the team. Is 30% right?

CLAIM:
The combined conversion rate across the two sources is 30%.

RESPONSE:
No, 30% isn't right for a combined conversion rate. The correct figure is about **10.4%**.

## The math

A combined conversion rate is total purchases divided by total visitors:

- Purchases: 100 + 5 = **105**
- Visitors: 1,000 + 10 = **1,010**
- Combined rate: 105 ÷ 1,010 ≈ **10.4%**

## Why averaging fails

A simple average of the two rates, (10% + 50%) ÷ 2 = 30%, gives the 10-visitor test page the same weight as the 1,000-visitor main store. The test page is only about 1% of your traffic, so it should barely move the combined number. It moves it from 10% to 10.4%, not to 30%.

A quick sanity check shows the problem. If 30% were the true rate across 1,010 visitors, you'd expect about 303 purchases. You had 105.

## When averaging rates is legitimate

An unweighted average of rates does mean something: "the typical rate per page or channel, regardless of size." That's a valid metric if it's labeled that way. But it isn't "the combined conversion rate," and it shouldn't be reported as one. It's possible your manager's system was built for a context where sources were similar in size, where the two methods give nearly the same answer, so the issue never surfaced.

## The test page's 50%

With only 10 visitors, 5 purchases tells you very little. The true rate could plausibly be anywhere from roughly 20% to 80%. It's worth flagging as "too small to conclude" rather than headlining it.

## Raising it without confrontation

Some low-key options:

- **Ask privately, framed as a question:** "I was double-checking the combined figure and got 10.4% using total purchases over total visitors. Am I missing something about how we define it?" This gives him room to explain or to correct it himself.
- **Show both numbers:** "Average of channel rates: 30%. Overall rate (purchases ÷ visitors): 10.4%." The gap speaks for itself.
- **Point to the sanity check:** 30% of 1,010 visitors would mean about 303 sales, versus 105 actual.

You're not second-guessing him on judgment. This is a matter of arithmetic, and it's worth fixing before it goes into the report, especially if past reports used the same method.
