SITUATION:
We're using a 200 mm by 50 mm timber plank as a short span over a garden ditch. Our carpenter says, with great confidence: "It makes no difference to how much it sags whether you lay the plank flat or stand it on its edge. Same wood, same cross-section area, same stiffness." He's been doing this for thirty years and I tend to trust him; it's the same plank either way. Can we go with laying it flat, since that's easier to walk on, on the basis that it's just as stiff?

CLAIM:
A 200 mm x 50 mm plank is equally stiff in bending whether laid flat or on edge.

RESPONSE:
No, the orientation makes a large difference, and the carpenter is mistaken on this point.

## Why

Bending stiffness depends on the **second moment of area**, not the cross-section area. For a rectangle:

**I = b·h³ / 12**, where *h* is the depth in the direction of bending.

The depth is cubed, so it matters far more than the width.

| Orientation | b × h | I (mm⁴) |
|---|---|---|
| Flat | 200 × 50 | 200·50³/12 ≈ **2.08 × 10⁶** |
| On edge | 50 × 200 | 50·200³/12 ≈ **33.3 × 10⁶** |

- **Sag:** Deflection is proportional to 1/I, so laid flat it sags **16 times more** under the same load and span.
- **Strength:** Bending strength goes with the section modulus (b·h²/6), so flat it is **4 times weaker** and will break at about a quarter of the load.

You can check this yourself: lay the plank flat across two supports, stand in the middle and note the sag, then repeat with it on edge. You'll see the difference straight away.

His experience is real, but "same wood, same area, same stiffness" is simply not how beams behave. This isn't a matter of opinion. It's basic beam theory, and it's why floor joists are always installed on edge.

## Can you still lay it flat?

Possibly, if the span is short enough. A flat plank isn't automatically unsafe, but you can't assume it's as stiff as one on edge. Whether it's adequate depends on things you haven't told me:

- **Span** (deflection grows with the cube of span, so this matters a lot)
- **Timber species/grade** and whether it's wet (wet timber is weaker and creeps)
- **Load**: one adult, people carrying things, a wheelbarrow?

As a rough guide, a 50 mm flat plank of ordinary softwood is usually fine for one person over well under a metre. It gets noticeably bouncy beyond about 1–1.5 m. If you give me the span and what will cross it, I can work out the expected sag and stress.

## Practical options if it's too bouncy

1. **Two planks on edge as stringers**, with short boards screwed across the top as decking. This is stiff and comfortable to walk on.
2. **The flat plank on top of a stringer or two on edge**, which gives you the walking surface you want with the stiffness underneath.
3. **A mid-span support** in the ditch, if the ditch allows it. Halving the span reduces deflection about eightfold.

In every case, make sure the ends bear solidly on firm ground and are fixed so the plank can't slide or tip. A single plank on edge on its own is stiff but narrow and can roll, so I wouldn't walk on it like that.
