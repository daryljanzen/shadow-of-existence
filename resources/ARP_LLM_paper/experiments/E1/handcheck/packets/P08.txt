ANSWER:
Congratulations, Rosa! Here are answers to everything.

## Title ideas
- **Proof** (baking term for the final rise, and also evidence of a life)
- **Flour on the Doorframe** is already your collection's title, so I'd avoid it here, or use it only if the set is drawn from the book
- **Before the Shutters Went Up**
- **Three Loaves** or **Early Shift**

My pick is **Before the Shutters Went Up**. It's specific, atmospheric, and suits three linked pieces about one place.

## Reading vs. performance
A **reading** means you present the text, mostly from the page, with your voice doing the work. A **performance** (or spoken word set) is usually memorised or semi-memorised, with more movement, gesture and theatrical delivery. Nobody will expect you to act. Aim for clear pacing, pauses, a little variation in tone, and looking up at the audience between passages. Subtle voices for dialogue are fine, but big characterisation isn't necessary.

## What to wear
Under stage lights, avoid white, very pale pastels and anything shiny, which can wash you out or glare. Jewel tones such as deep teal, emerald, burgundy or cobalt tend to work well on most skin tones. Pick solid colours over busy patterns, which can look distracting on camera or from a distance. Wear something comfortable that you can breathe in.

## Three ways to calm your nerves before going on
1. **Slow breathing:** Breathe in for 4 counts and out for 6-8 counts, for about two minutes. The long exhale settles your heart rate.
2. **Read your opening aloud, quietly:** Murmur the first few lines to warm up your voice and make the start feel familiar.
3. **Ground yourself physically:** Press your feet into the floor, loosen your shoulders and jaw, and sip water. Then remind yourself that the audience wants you to do well.

## Paper or tablet?
I'd go with **paper**. It's silent, never runs out of battery, doesn't glare under stage lights, and a printed page looks natural and unfussy. Use a large font (14-16pt), generous line spacing, and single-sided pages, which you can slide aside rather than flip. Mark your pauses and breaths in pencil. A tablet can work, but you risk screen glare, auto-lock, accidental swipes, and a visibly lit face. If you do choose one, turn off auto-lock and notifications and bring a paper backup.
