ANSWER:
Here's everything on your list.

## 1. What are Incoterms?
Incoterms are a set of standard international trade terms, such as EXW, FCA or DAP, published by the International Chamber of Commerce. Each one sets out who pays for transport, insurance and duties, and the exact point where responsibility and risk pass from seller to buyer.

## 2. A clearer folder name
**"Outbound Shipments"** says what's inside without anyone having to open it. If you want to keep it tidy, you could add subfolders by date or customer, for example `Outbound Shipments / 2026-11 / Leeds - [Customer Name]`.

## 3. Stretch wrap or strapping for mixed boxes?
**Use stretch wrap as your main method.** Mixed boxes come in different sizes and shapes, and wrap holds them all together and keeps out dust and damp. Strapping alone can crush smaller boxes or let them slip out between the straps.

**Add strapping as well** if the pallet is heavy or tall, or if it will travel a long way. Put corner protectors (edge boards) under the straps so they don't cut into the boxes.

## 4. Shipping cost
**Volumetric weight per pallet:**
120 × 80 × 150 = 1,440,000 ÷ 6,000 = **240 kg**

**I can't give a final figure yet because I don't have the actual weight of the pallets.** The carrier charges whichever weight is higher, so here is how it works out:

- **If each pallet actually weighs 240 kg or less:** 3 × 240 kg × £0.45 = **£324.00**
- **If a pallet weighs more than 240 kg:** use its real weight instead. For example, a 300 kg pallet costs 300 × £0.45 = £135.00.

£324 is the **minimum** this shipment can cost. Weigh the pallets, or check the weights on the paperwork, and send them to me if you'd like me to work out the exact total. It's also worth checking the carrier's quote for fuel surcharges or VAT on top.

## 6. Three checks before a pallet leaves
1. **Paperwork matches the goods.** Make sure the delivery note, labels and address match the order, with the right customer, the right number of boxes and the right reference.
2. **The load is secure and stable.** Nothing should overhang the pallet edges, heavy boxes should be at the bottom, the wrap or straps should be tight, and the pallet itself shouldn't be damaged.
3. **Weight and size are recorded correctly.** Weigh and measure each pallet and make sure the figures match what was booked with the carrier. Wrong figures are the most common cause of surprise charges.
