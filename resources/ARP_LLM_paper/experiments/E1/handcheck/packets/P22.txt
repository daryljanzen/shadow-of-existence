ANSWER:
## Biscuit's care plan

**Don't go with Happy Paws.** Biscuit needs insulin at 8am and 8pm with food, and they can only give oral medication. Missing or delaying doses over 10 nights is a real health risk.

**Jo alone doesn't work either.** She can only do evenings, so the 8am dose would be uncovered.

**Your vet's boarding is the only option that covers the medical need**, but it's £65 × 10 = **£650, which is £150 over budget**. Options to close the gap:
- Ask the vet if they offer a multi-night discount or a lower rate for long stays.
- Ask whether Jo could do the 8pm dose and someone else (a neighbour, a vet nurse home visit, or a pet sitter trained in injections) covers 8am. Get the vet to show them the injection first.
- Ask Jo if Biscuit could stay at her place, if her work hours allow both doses.

If none of those work, I'd still go over budget on the vet over compromising his insulin schedule.

## The other bits

**Bag name:** "Sir Barks-a-Lot's Overnighter" or "The Biscuit Tin" (my pick, since it holds Biscuit).

**Note for his carer:**
> Hi! This is Biscuit. He's a friendly, sociable boy who loves people, but he pulls on the lead, so please keep a firm grip, especially when he spots something interesting. He's diabetic and needs insulin with food at 8am and 8pm, so please see the care sheet for details. Thank you so much for looking after him!

**Three things to pack:**
1. Insulin and syringes in a cool bag, plus a spare supply in case of delays
2. His usual food, pre-portioned for each meal, so the insulin timing and diet stay consistent
3. A front-clip harness (helps with the pulling) and a familiar blanket

**Printed sheet or phone note?** Print it, and keep a shared phone copy as backup. A printed sheet can sit with the insulin and be read at a glance. It should list doses, times, feeding amounts, signs of low blood sugar, and your vet's number. Include a contact number with the international dialling code, since you'll be abroad.

**Collar tag colour:** Go for **fluorescent yellow or lime green**, ideally reflective. Both stand out against a black coat in the dark. A small clip-on LED light on the collar would help even more on winter walks.
