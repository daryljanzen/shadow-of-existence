ANSWER:
# Team name

Some options with "Foxes" in them, in case the plain name is taken:

- **Fearless Foxes**
- **Red Foxes** or **Flying Foxes**
- **[Your area] Foxes**, e.g. "Hillside Foxes". This is the easiest way to be unique in the league.
- **Firefoxes**
- **Fox Cubs**
- **Silver Foxes**, which the parents will enjoy more than the kids.

Check with the league secretary before you order anything with the name printed on it.

# Sam and the goal

**You can't keep Sam in goal for the whole match without breaking the rule.**

- 7 players on the pitch × 60 minutes = **420 player-minutes** per match.
- 14 kids × 30 minutes minimum = **420 player-minutes**.

That's an exact fit. Every child plays **exactly 30 minutes**, Sam included. If anyone plays longer, someone else drops below 30 and you've broken the rule.

**What I'd do:**

- **Play Sam in goal for one half.** That's all 30 of Sam's minutes. Put Sam in the second half so the score is protected when games are usually decided.
- **Rotate the other half** among two or three kids who want to try goalkeeping. Give them a little keeper practice in training. At under-11s, letting several kids try the position helps their development, and you may find a second keeper.
- **The easiest sub plan is to swap the whole team at half-time.** Two groups of 7 each play one half. You could also swap 3–4 players at quarter-time intervals, as long as everyone ends up on 30 minutes.
- **What to tell the parents:** "League rules require every player to play 30 minutes. With 14 kids, that's exactly 30 each, Sam included." It's a rule, not your opinion, so it's hard to argue with.

# Welcome message (two sentences)

> Welcome to the Foxes! 🦊 I'm looking forward to a fun season where every child gets plenty of game time, and if you'd like to help with half-time snacks, a rota of bananas, apple slices or grapes (plus a water bottle for every player) would be brilliant.

# Three warm-up drills (cones and balls only)

1. **Traffic lights:** Each player dribbles a ball inside a coned square. You call "green" (dribble), "amber" (slow, close control), or "red" (stop with your foot on the ball). Add "roundabout" for spinning the ball round. It works on ball control and listening.
2. **Cone gates:** Scatter pairs of cones about a metre apart around the area to make "gates". Players dribble through as many gates as they can in 60 seconds. Then do it in pairs, passing through the gates.
3. **Rob the nest:** Put a pile of balls in the middle and a coned "nest" for each team of 3–4 in the corners. Players run one at a time to bring a ball back to their nest, and they can steal from other nests too. It gets them sprinting, laughing and warmed up.

# Away kit colour

**Bright yellow, lime, or fluorescent green** are hard to clash with. Few teams wear them at home, and they show up well. **Teal or purple** are also good uncommon choices. Look at your league's list of home colours, and pick something that also contrasts with your own home kit and with what referees usually wear (black).

# Offside, for a ten-year-old

> "You can't hang around near the other team's goal waiting for the ball. When your teammate passes it forward to you, there need to be **at least two players from the other team** (usually the goalkeeper and one defender) level with you or closer to their goal than you. If there aren't, you're offside and the other team gets a free kick. It's fine if you're in your own half, if you're behind the ball, or if you get it from a throw-in."

**Note:** many leagues don't use offside in 7-a-side. In England, for example, it only starts at 9v9. Check your league's rules before teaching it.

# Half-time snack (not orange slices)

- **Banana pieces.** Quick energy, no mess, cheap.
- **Grapes,** halved for safety.
- **Apple slices** or **clementine segments.**
- **Water** for everyone, rather than sugary drinks.

Before you set up a rota, ask parents to tell you about any **allergies**, and keep nuts away from the team.

Good luck this season!
