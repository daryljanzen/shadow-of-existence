## Three questions candidates may ask
1. **What does success look like in the first six months?** Have clear priorities and early projects ready.
2. **What tools, data sources and team structure would I be working with?** Be ready to talk about tech stack, data quality and who they'd collaborate with.
3. **What are the next steps and timeline, and what's the development or progression path?** Know when you'll decide, how you'll notify candidates, and what training or growth is available.

Salary range, flexibility and remote working are also common, so it's worth having those answers too.

## Scoring format
I'd use a **shared online form with one submission per panellist**, built on the same criteria and rating scale for every candidate. It's quick to complete in the 15-minute gap, and it makes comparing candidates easier. Have Ana and Ben each score independently *before* discussing, so neither anchors the other. Keep a few printed copies as a fallback in case of tech problems. Paper only is fine too, but you'd need to transcribe it afterwards.

## Telling an internal applicant they didn't make the shortlist
Do this by phone or in person if you can, before any written notice. Something like:

> "Thank you for applying. I wanted to tell you personally that you won't be moving forward to the final interviews this time. We had a strong field, and this decision was a close one. Your application showed [specific strength], and I'd really like to share some feedback on what would make you an even stronger candidate in future. Would you be open to a conversation about that? I'd also like to support your development here, so please let me know what would be helpful."

Be specific and honest about the reasons, and offer feedback promptly.
