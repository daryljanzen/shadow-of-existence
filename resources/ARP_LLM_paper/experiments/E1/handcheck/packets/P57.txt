## 4. Three questions candidates might ask
**Requires:** likely candidate questions to prepare answers for.

1. **What does success look like in the first six months?** Have specific projects or goals ready.
2. **What tools, data sources, and team structure would I work with?** Know your stack, data quality, and who they'd report to.
3. **What are the next steps and timeline, and what are the development and working-pattern options?** Know when you'll decide, plus training budget, progression, and hybrid or remote policy.

## 5. Scoring format
**Requires:** a recommendation between a shared online form and individual paper sheets.

I'd go with a **shared online form**. It enforces the same criteria for every candidate, compiles scores automatically, and leaves a clean record. Set it up so Ana and Ben each score independently and can't see each other's ratings until they've both submitted, so one doesn't anchor the other. Use the 15-minute gap for scoring and notes. Paper is a reasonable fallback if devices would be distracting or Wi-Fi is unreliable, but you'd then have to transcribe the scores.

## 6. Telling an internal applicant they didn't make the shortlist
**Requires:** a kind, honest message. A conversation, or at least a call, is better than email alone for internal candidates.

> Hi [Name],
>
> Thank you for applying for the Data Analyst role, and for the effort you put into your application. It was a strong field, and after careful consideration we haven't included you in the final interview stage this time.
>
> I'd really like to talk it through and share some feedback, particularly on [specific strength] and where you might build further for future opportunities. Would you have 15 minutes this week or next?
>
> We value the work you do here, and I hope you'll keep an eye out for future roles.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

Offer specific feedback, and don't let the email be the first they hear of it if you can speak to them first.
