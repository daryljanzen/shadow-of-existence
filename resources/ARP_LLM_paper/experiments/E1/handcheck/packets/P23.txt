## Children wording
> *"We'd love your little ones to join us for the ceremony. The evening reception will be an adults-only celebration, and we hope this gives you the chance to relax and enjoy a night off with us."*

This welcomes children first and frames the evening as a treat for parents rather than an exclusion.

## RSVP deadline
> *"Kindly reply by Friday 5th September so we can finalise numbers with our caterers."*

What makes people reply on time:
- **Use a specific date including the weekday.** "By the 5th" is easy to forget.
- **Give a reason.** Mentioning the caterers makes it feel like a real deadline.
- **Set it 6–8 weeks before the wedding**, and give your venue a date about 2 weeks earlier than they need it, as a buffer for stragglers.
- **Make replying easy**, with a wedding website, QR code or pre-stamped card.

## Photo booth cost

**Total = (£95 × hours, minimum 3) + £60**

## Bridesmaid colours (October barn, sage and cream)
- **Dusty sage or eucalyptus green.** This is the obvious match and looks lovely against wood and hay.
- **Terracotta or rust.** This is my top pick. It's autumnal and warm, and it makes sage and cream look richer.
- **Deep olive or moss.** This is a moodier tonal option for a cosy evening feel.
- **Mixed earth tones** (sage, rust, mustard and champagne) would suit a relaxed, mismatched look.

Velvet or satin fabrics suit October well. I'd avoid pale pastels, which can look washed out in barn lighting.

## Favours under £2
1. **Seed paper or wildflower seed packets** in sage packaging, printed with "Let love grow" (about 50p–£1.20).
2. **Mini jars of local honey or homemade jam** with a cream label (about £1.50–£2, cheaper if you buy honey in bulk).
3. **Scratch-card or lottery-ticket-style table games**, or **mini Kilner jars of mulled wine spices**, which suit October perfectly (about £1–£1.80).

A bonus idea is to make the favour double as the escort card (see below).

## Seating plan: board or escort cards?
**For 90 guests, I'd go with one big board**, with a couple of adjustments:
- **Arrange it alphabetically by surname**, not by table. Otherwise 90 people crowd round it hunting for their names.
- **Place it away from the doorway**, or use two boards (A–L and M–Z) to spread the crowd.
- It's cheaper, quicker to update when last-minute changes happen, and a styled board (sage foliage, a wooden frame or an old window) works beautifully in a barn.

**Escort cards** are better if you want them to double as favours (for example, a name tag tied to each honey jar). They take more time to set up on the day, though, and need a long table near the entrance.
