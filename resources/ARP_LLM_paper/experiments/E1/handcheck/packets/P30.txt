### 2. The mug reorder
**What it requires:** checking whether Supplier A's stock arrives before you run out, then weighing the £320 saving against lost sales.

**The problem with A:**
- 140 units ÷ 10 a day = **14 days of stock**, so you run out around **26 November**.
- Supplier A delivers in **21 days**, around **3 December**.
- That leaves about **7 days with no mugs, roughly 70 lost sales**. It falls during peak gift-buying, with the mug as the centrepiece of your window. If Christmas demand rises above 10 a day, the gap gets longer.

So the £320 saving costs you a week of your best-seller at the worst possible time. You'd also have customers asking for the mug in the window and being told it's gone.

**What I'd do: split the order.**

| Option | Cost | Stockout? |
|---|---|---|
| 400 from A | £1,240 | Yes, about 7 days |
| 400 from B | £1,560 | No |
| **100 from B + 300 from A** | **£1,320** | **No** |

With the split, B's 100 arrive on day 5. That gives you about 190 on hand, enough to last until about day 24. A's 300 arrive on day 21, so stock never runs out. **It costs £80 more than all-A and keeps £240 of the saving.** If you expect Christmas demand to climb, make it 150 from B and 250 from A.

Put both orders in today. Every day you wait pushes A's delivery further into December.

### 4. Gift-wrapping: paid or free, and tissue colour
**What it requires:** a pricing recommendation and a colour suggestion for a Scandi look.

**Recommendation: offer both levels.**
- **Free basic wrap:** tissue paper plus a branded sticker. It costs pennies, encourages people to buy gifts from you at Christmas, and gets your shop seen at the recipient's end too.
- **Paid upgrade (£2–£3):** a box, cotton ribbon and a dried sprig or tag. People who want something special will pay for it, and it covers your time.

**Tissue colour:** oatmeal or natural kraft, warm white, or soft sage. Pair it with a black or charcoal sticker or twine. Avoid bright white, which looks clinical, and avoid red and gold, which works against the Scandi feel.

---

### 5. Three low-cost loyalty perks
**What it requires:** ideas that build loyalty without much expense.

1. **Free upgraded gift wrap for members.** It costs you a little ribbon, and members feel valued at gift-giving times.
2. **Early access to new ranges.** Give members 48 hours before launch, starting with Evenlight. It costs nothing and gives people a reason to sign up.
3. **Bring back your empty candle jar for 10% off your next one.** It's cheap, it's sustainable, and it brings people back into the shop.

If you go with the split order, this can go out around **17 November**, when Supplier B's delivery lands. You could also skip "brief disappearance" and simply announce fresh stock in time for Christmas.
