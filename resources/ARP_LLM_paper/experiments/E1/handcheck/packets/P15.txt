ANSWER:
# Candle questions

## Scent name ideas
- **Windfall** (my pick): apples fallen from the tree, with a hint of something smoky. It's short and works well on a label.
- **Orchard Embers**
- **Bonfire Orchard**
- **Smoke & Cider**

## Product description opener
Instead of "This candle is a great candle for any room," try one of these:

- "Windfall apples and a freshly lit bonfire: the smell of the first properly cold evening of autumn."
- "Crisp orchard apple wrapped in soft woodsmoke, poured by hand at my kitchen table."
- "Light it and the room turns into an October evening, with a sweet, smoky haze and no coat required."

Swap in your own scent name. Specific images work better than "great."

## Gross vs net profit
- **Gross profit** is what you sell for minus the direct cost of making the product. For you that's £28 − £11 = **£17 per candle**.
- **Net profit** is what's left after all the other costs of running the business: market pitch fees, marketplace fees, insurance, website, photography, travel, equipment, and any tax. Net is the real "what do I keep" figure.

## Craft markets or online first?
I'd start with **craft markets**.
- Scent is hard to sell through a screen, but people can sniff your candles in person.
- You get instant feedback on scents, prices and packaging, which is useful evidence for a bank.
- Glass and wax are heavy and fragile, so you avoid shipping costs and breakages while you learn.
- Christmas markets are on now, so there's a good chance to test the range.

Once you know your bestsellers, add an online shop or marketplace for repeat customers. Before you start, check you have public liability insurance and that your labels meet UK candle safety and labelling rules.

## Break-even
Each candle contributes **£17** (£28 − £11) toward your fixed costs. You haven't told me your monthly fixed costs, so I can't give one number, but the formula is:

**Break-even candles per month = monthly fixed costs ÷ £17**

| Monthly fixed costs | Candles to break even |
|---|---|
| £500 | 30 |
| £1,000 | 59 |
| £1,500 | 89 |
| £2,000 | 118 |

Fixed costs include pitch fees, insurance, workspace, software, and any wage you want to pay yourself. The bank will want to see a wage included. Also remember that marketplace or card fees reduce the £17 on each sale.

## Three launch post ideas (no face needed)
1. **Pour to flame:** a close-up, hands-only (or just wax and jar) video of melting, pouring, trimming the wick and lighting. Add a soft voiceover or on-screen text.
2. **Scent flat-lay:** film the candle surrounded by a real apple, wood chips, a cinnamon stick and autumn leaves, ending with the flame flickering. Caption it with the name reveal.
3. **The naming story:** a text-over-video post with a few seconds each of the shortlisted names, then "Meet *Windfall*" with the finished candle. You could ask followers to vote first to build interest.

## Label colour
Skip burnt orange and kraft. I'd go for **deep damson plum** with warm cream lettering and a small copper or apple-red accent. It still feels autumnal (blackberries, dusk, woodsmoke) but looks distinct among oranges and browns on a market table. Other good options are **dark forest green** or **smoky slate blue** with cream text. Print a test label and look at it from a few feet away, since a market stall is mostly seen from a distance.

For the bank meeting, bring your cost per candle, your break-even table with real fixed costs filled in, and your market sales so far. Good luck next week!
