ANSWER:
# Your numbers

**The $300 doesn't hold up.** Here's the math:

| Item | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Rent | $1,650 |
| Car payment | $480 |
| Utilities and phone | $220 |
| Student loan | $350 |
| Groceries | $600 |
| House savings | $900 |
| **Total** | **$4,200** |

That's your entire $4,200 take-home, so you're left with **$0** for eating out and fun, not $300. It's worth correcting before tonight so your partner doesn't plan around a number that isn't there. A few ways to get to about $300:

- **Savings at $600/month** keeps groceries at $600 and leaves $300 for fun.
- **Savings at $750 and groceries at $450** also gets you to $300, but the grocery cut is steep.
- **A split**, such as $700 savings, $550 groceries, and $150 fun, is a compromise if you'd rather not pick one extreme.

Also, a budget with exactly $0 of slack tends to break the first time something unexpected comes up, so a small buffer line is worth considering.

# Account name
Some ideas: **The Home Stretch**, **Project Front Door**, **Our Next Address**, or **The Hearth Fund**.

# Fixed vs. variable
A fixed expense is the same amount every month (like rent), while a variable expense changes from month to month (like groceries or eating out).

# App or spreadsheet?
I'd go with a **shared spreadsheet** to start. It's free, you can both see and edit it, and building it yourselves forces you to talk through each category. An app can help later if manual entry becomes a chore, but a spreadsheet works well for a first budget with only a handful of categories.

# Cheap date nights (cooking at home)
1. **Homemade pizza night:** make the dough, each person builds their own, and see who does better.
2. **Cook a restaurant dish you'd normally order out:** try making your usual takeaway favorite and compare the cost.
3. **Themed tasting dinner:** pick a cuisine, cook a simple three-course meal, and put on matching music.

# Softer message
Here are a few options, depending on tone:

- "Can we look at our budget together tonight? I'd love to figure out how we both want to handle eating out and fun money. I know my coffee habit is part of that too."
- "I'd like us to set a fun-money amount that works for both of us. I'm happy to look at my own coffee spending as well."
- "Want to sit down tonight and build our budget together? I think we can fit in some eating out and treats, and I'm willing to adjust my coffee budget too."

These work because they frame it as a shared plan rather than a complaint about one person. Mentioning your own coffee habit up front shows you're not singling them out. It may also help to agree on a fun-money number for each of you, so nobody has to defend individual purchases.
