## Name badge colour
Print the names in **black or dark navy** for maximum legibility on white card, in a large sans-serif font (at least 36 pt for first names). For some colour, add a **teal or mid-blue** border or header strip. Avoid yellow, light green, and pastels, which are hard to read, especially for older eyes.

## Registration
For a mostly older crowd, I'd use **a paper sign-up sheet at the library desk, plus a phone number people can call**, and offer an online form as an optional extra. That way nobody is shut out, and anyone comfortable online can still use it. Whichever you use, collect an email address (or postal address) so you can send the thank-you message afterwards.

## Three sit-down icebreakers
1. **Table question cards:** Put a card on each table with a prompt like "What's the best post or notice you've ever seen from a local group?" Everyone chats with their neighbours at their own pace.
2. **Sticker map:** Put a big map of the area on the wall. As people arrive, they place a sticker on the neighbourhood or town their group serves. It starts conversations without anyone speaking publicly.
3. **Group-goal sticky notes:** Hand out sticky notes and ask people to write one thing they hope to learn. Collect them on a poster, then group similar ones together and refer back to them during the day.
