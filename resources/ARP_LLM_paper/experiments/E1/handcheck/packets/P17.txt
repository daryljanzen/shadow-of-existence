ANSWER:
## 1. Scent name
- **Windfall & Woodsmoke** (my pick: windfall apples are the ones that drop in autumn, and it sounds like a place you'd want to be)
- Smoked Orchard
- Bonfire Orchard
- Last of the Orchard

## 2. Description opener
Instead of "This candle is a great candle for any room":

> "Windfall apples, crisp and a little sweet, drifting through the smoke of a garden bonfire. Light it and the room turns to the first properly cold evening of the year."

Or shorter: *"Orchard apples and curling woodsmoke, poured by hand in small batches at my kitchen table."* The kitchen-table detail is a real selling point, so use it.

## 3. Gross vs net profit
- **Gross profit** is what you earn from sales minus the direct cost of making the product. For you, that's £28 minus £11 = **£17 per candle**.
- **Net profit** is what's left after *all* the other costs: market pitch fees, marketplace fees, insurance, postage, marketing, equipment, your workspace, and tax.

Example: 100 candles bring in £2,800 and cost £1,100 to make, so gross profit is £1,700. If another £700 goes on pitch fees, insurance, and so on, net profit is £1,000. The bank will care most about net.

## 4. Markets or online first?
**Start with craft markets.** Scent is hard to sell through a screen, and at a stall people can smell the candle, which helps sales. You also get instant feedback on which scents and prices work, and you get paid on the day with no listing fees or postage headaches. Use what you learn (best sellers, customer comments, photos) to launch an online shop second. Keep a mailing list sign-up on your table so market customers can reorder online.

## 5. Break-even
Profit per candle is £17, so:

**Break-even candles per month = monthly fixed costs ÷ £17**

| Monthly fixed costs | Candles to break even |
|---|---|
| £500 | 30 |
| £1,000 | 59 |
| £2,000 | 118 |
| £3,000 | 177 |

Fixed costs include pitch fees, insurance, equipment, workspace, software, and ideally a wage for yourself. If you want to pay yourself £2,000 a month, add it to the fixed costs. Send me your real figures and I'll work out the exact number. Also remember that marketplace fees reduce your £17 per candle, and your £11 doesn't yet include your own time.

## 6. Three faceless launch posts
1. **Hands-only pour video:** a close-up of melted wax going into the jar, set to calm music, with the scent name as the caption.
2. **Styled still life:** the candle among real apples, a small log, and autumn leaves in warm light, with the new description opener as the caption.
3. **Name the scent / behind the label:** show the label sketches or scent ingredients and ask followers to vote on a name or tell you which is their favourite autumn smell. It builds an audience before you launch.

## 7. Label colour
Go for **deep smoky green** (think dark bottle or forest green) on a cream or off-white label, with a small **apple-red** accent. It suits both apple and woodsmoke, it's calm and grown-up, and it will stand out among all the orange and kraft on the table. A second option is slate blue-grey with a cream label and a warm brass-coloured detail.
