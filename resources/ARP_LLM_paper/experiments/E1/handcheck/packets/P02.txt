ANSWER:
Welcome to the allotment world, and no need to apologise!

## Annuals vs perennials
**Annuals** complete their life cycle in one growing season, so you sow them fresh each year (e.g. lettuce, beans). **Perennials** come back year after year from the same plant (e.g. rhubarb, asparagus, most herbs like mint and chives).

## Names for the plot
- **The Muddy Boots Patch**
- **Bean There, Dug That**
- **The Little Green Kingdom**
- **Grow Wild Corner**
- **[Family name] & Sprouts**, e.g. "Jones & Sprouts"
- **The Secret Veg Garden**

You could let the kids vote, or have each one invent a name and draw it from a hat.

## Three easy veg to grow with kids
All of these can be sown in spring and harvested well before the summer holidays end:

1. **Radishes**: Sow from March and they're ready in about 4 weeks. That's quick enough for impatient kids.
2. **First early potatoes** (e.g. 'Charlotte' or 'Rocket'): Plant in March or April and dig them up from late June. It's like a treasure hunt.
3. **Runner beans or dwarf French beans**: Sow in May. They grow fast, the seeds are big enough for small hands, and you can pick them from July.

Courgettes are a good bonus option. One plant will produce more than you can eat.

## Tomato feed dilution
- The feed rate is 15 ml per 5 litres, which is **3 ml per litre**.
- 2 US gallons is **7.57 litres**.
- 7.57 × 3 = **about 23 ml of feed for a full sprayer.**

The "2 gallon" rating is sometimes the total tank volume rather than the fill line, so check for a marked maximum fill level. If it only holds, say, 6 litres of liquid, use 18 ml. Also, tomato feed usually works best poured at the base of the plant with a watering can rather than sprayed on the leaves, so check the bottle's directions.

## Shed colour
To blend into a hedge, go for a **muted, darkish green or green-grey**. Darker, duller tones recede visually, while bright or pale colours stand out. Some good choices:
- Sage or olive green
- Dark forest or "old English" green
- Deep green-grey or slate
- Dark brown also blends well against bare winter hedges

Cuprinol Garden Shades and Ronseal both make these colours in exterior wood-treatment ranges. "Wild Thyme," "Willow" and "Old English Green" are all popular allotment choices. Try a test patch first, since colours often look brighter outdoors than on the tin.
