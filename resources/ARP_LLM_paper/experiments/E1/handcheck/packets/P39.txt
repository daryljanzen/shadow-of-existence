ANSWER:
Happy to help. Your partner is close, but not quite right.

## Spiced pear latte names
- **Pear & Pillow**: cosy without trying too hard
- **The Orchard Hour**: suggests a slow afternoon
- **Hearth Pear**: warm, with a nod to spice
- **Pear Tree Latte**: plain and simple, and it'll fit on a chalkboard
- **Ginger & Pear**: just says what's in it, which often works best

## Flat white price
- March: £3.80 × 1.10 = **£4.18**
- August, 10% off: £4.18 × 0.90 = **£3.762**, so about **£3.76**

You're **not** exactly back where you started. The 10% rise and the 10% discount don't cancel out, because the discount is taken from the higher price. Overall it's 1.10 × 0.90 = 0.99, a **net decrease of 1%**. That's 4p less per flat white than before March. It's small, but across every drink it adds up, and you're doing it while milk costs are still higher. If you want to be back at £3.80, the discount would need to be about 9.1%, not 10%.

## Loyalty scheme
For a café your size, I'd start with a **paper stamp card**. It costs almost nothing, there's no sign-up friction, and regulars like it. An app makes sense later if you want customer data or targeted offers, but it costs money and many customers won't bother downloading it. Try paper for a season and see how it goes.

## Pastries to pair with the pear latte (all oven-friendly)
1. **Frangipane tart or pear-and-almond slice**: it echoes the pear without competing with it.
2. **Cinnamon swirls or morning buns**: the warm spice goes well with the latte.
3. **Ginger or oat-and-brown-sugar shortbread**: it's simple, keeps well, and works well for dunking.

## Cost of goods sold (COGS)
COGS is what it costs you to make what you sold: ingredients and packaging, such as coffee, milk, flour, and cups. It leaves out overheads like rent and wages, so subtracting it from your sales shows how much you have left to cover those.
