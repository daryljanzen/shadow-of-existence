ANSWER:
## 1. Scent name (creative naming)
- **Windfall & Woodsmoke** is my pick. Windfalls are the apples that drop in the orchard, so it says "apple" and "autumn" at once.
- Other options: *Orchard Embers*, *Bonfire Orchard*, *Cider & Cinder*.

## 2. Product description opener (copywriting)
Your current line could describe any candle. Specific images work better:

- "Bonfire smoke drifting through an orchard at dusk: sharp windfall apples, glowing embers, and a jumper you don't want to take off."
- Shorter: "Crisp apples, curling woodsmoke, and the first properly cold evening of the year."

## 3. Gross vs net profit (plain-English explanation)
- **Gross profit** is what you earn from sales after subtracting only the direct cost of making the product. For you, that's £28 − £11 = **£17 per candle**.
- **Net profit** is what's left after *all* your other costs: stall fees, marketplace fees, postage, insurance, equipment, marketing, utilities, and tax. If you go full-time, include a wage for yourself too.

Gross profit shows whether the product makes money. Net profit shows whether the business does. Banks care most about the second.

## 4. Craft markets vs online marketplace (recommendation)
**Start with craft markets**, and set up a basic online shop alongside them.
- Scent is hard to sell through a screen. At a market people sniff, pick up, and buy on impulse.
- You get instant feedback on scents, prices, and labels, which is valuable evidence for the bank.
- Heavy glass jars are costly and fiddly to ship, and marketplaces add fees and heavy competition.
- Downsides: stall fees, early mornings, and weather. Choose two or three markets to trial rather than committing to many.

## 5. Break-even (calculation)
Each candle leaves **£17** after materials (£28 − £11). To get a monthly break-even figure I need your **fixed monthly costs**, which you haven't given me yet. The formula is:

**Break-even candles per month = fixed monthly costs ÷ £17**

Some examples:

| Fixed costs per month | Candles needed |
|---|---|
| £500 | 30 |
| £1,000 | 59 |
| £2,000 | 118 |
| £3,000 | 177 |

Fixed costs should include stall fees, insurance, workspace, software, and any marketing. If you want to pay yourself, add your target monthly wage. Marketplace fees and card charges aren't in your £11, so they reduce the £17 slightly. Send me your numbers and I'll work out the exact figure before your bank appointment.

## 6. Three faceless launch post ideas (social media ideas)
1. **Hands-only pour video.** Show the wax melting, the fragrance going in, and the pour, with a text overlay such as "Meet Windfall & Woodsmoke."
2. **Flat-lay "ingredients" photo.** Arrange a candle, a few apples, a piece of charred wood, and a cosy blanket. Caption it with the scent notes and the story of the name.
3. **Lit-candle close-up.** Film the flame and a wisp of smoke in a dim room, with a short caption about the first cold evening. Add a "launching Saturday" date.

## 7. Label colour (design recommendation)
Skip burnt orange and go for **deep damson (dark plum)** with warm cream text. It suggests orchard fruit and dusk smoke, and it will stand out among all the orange and kraft paper. Add a small accent in **copper foil or apple green** for a bit of polish. If you want something less purple, **smoky charcoal with an orchard-green accent** also works well.

---
One practical point for the bank and for selling: in the UK, candles need proper safety labelling and product safety documentation, and the bank may ask about public liability insurance. It's worth having those ready.
