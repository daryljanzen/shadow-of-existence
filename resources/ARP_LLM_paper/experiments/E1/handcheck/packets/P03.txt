You have **10 more units than the records say you should**, so I wouldn't sign it off as "no variance." Overages are usually paperwork rather than loss. Things worth checking:

- **Deliveries:** Did one delivery contain more than 60? Check the delivery notes against what was booked in.
- **Customer returns:** Were any bottles refunded and put back on the shelf without being booked back into stock?
- **Till errors:** Were other items scanned as this bottle, or was a sale scanned twice? Look at the till log for multi-quantity lines or voids.
- **Counting:** Recount, and check for bottles in other locations or a similar variant (different colour or size) mixed in.
- **Timing:** Your count was this morning, but the figures cover October. Any sales or deliveries between 31 Oct and today need adjusting. Extra sales since then would make the gap *bigger*, not smaller.

If you can't find the cause, report it as a +10 variance with a short note on what you checked. Area managers generally prefer that to a clean sheet that later turns out to be wrong.

## 4. Markdown vs discount

- **Markdown:** a permanent reduction to the item's selling price, usually to clear slow or seasonal stock. The price on the ticket changes for everyone. Clearance is markdowns.
- **Discount:** a reduction applied at the point of sale, often conditional and temporary. Examples are 10% off for staff, a voucher code, "20% off this weekend," or "buy two, save £5." The ticketed price stays the same.

Markdowns hit your margin permanently and are tracked as such. Discounts are usually reported separately so you can see what promotions cost you.

## 5. Old price crossed out or just the new price?

**Show the old price crossed out.** "~~£80~~ £45" sells much better than "£45" alone because shoppers can see the saving.

The catch: the "was" price must be genuine. Under UK consumer protection rules, it should be the price you actually charged for a reasonable period beforehand, not an inflated one. If an item was only briefly at full price, or you're unsure, use wording like "Clearance price £45" instead.

## 6. Three cheap ways to get weekday afternoon foot traffic

1. **School pickup (about 3:00–3:30pm).** Parents walk past every day. Put a clear offer outside, like "Parents' hour: 15% off 2:45–3:45" or a kids' welly/hat bin by the door. Ask the school if you can sponsor something small, such as a raffle prize or a voucher in the PTA newsletter, to get your name in front of every family.
2. **The GP surgery.** Doctors push walking and outdoor exercise, and waiting patients have time to kill. Ask if you can leave flyers or a small poster, for example a "Walking for health starter kit" offer or a free local walks map with your shop's name on it. Many surgeries run social prescribing schemes that refer people to walking groups, so it's worth asking who coordinates that.
3. **A regular in-store event.** Since the two of you are free in that slot, run something like a free "how to layer for winter" or boot-fitting session every Wednesday at 2pm. It costs only your time, gives people a reason to visit, and gives you something to post on social media and in local Facebook groups. Retired walkers are a good audience for weekday afternoons.
