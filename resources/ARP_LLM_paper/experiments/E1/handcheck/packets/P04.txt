ANSWER:
## 1. Project name (requires a creative suggestion fitting the bird theme)

**Project Tern**, after the Arctic tern, which makes one of the longest migrations of any animal. Alternates: **Project Swallow** or **Project Starling**.

## 2. Which hosting option (requires checking each option against your constraints and budget)

**Pick (b): managed Postgres in Frankfurt, $55/month.**

- **(a) is ruled out, despite being cheapest.** Your contracts require personal data to stay in the EU, and US-East is outside it. Pricing doesn't matter if it breaches the contract with your school customers.
- **(b) meets every constraint.** It's in the EU, managed, has automatic backups, and at $55 it's under your $60 budget.
- **(c) fails the "nobody does ops" requirement.** It's cheap and in the right region, but you'd own backups, patching, security, and failover. That's a real risk for a team with no ops experience holding student data.

Check that (b)'s backups are also stored in the EU, since some providers replicate them to other regions by default.

## 3. Connection pooler (requires a two-sentence explanation for a non-technical reader)

A connection pooler is like a receptionist for the database. Instead of every user request opening its own line to the database, which is slow and limited in number, it keeps a small set of lines open and shares them among everyone. This keeps the app fast and stable when many people use it at once.

## 4. Documentation format (requires choosing one of three)

**A shared doc.** It's easy to draft collaboratively, supports comments and review, and suits a plan that will change before next month. Create tickets for the individual tasks and link them from the doc. Once the migration is done, move the final version to your wiki as a record.

## 5. Status update (requires one line for team chat)

> Heads up: the Postgres migration (Project Tern) is planned for next month. A plan doc and tickets are coming soon.

## 6. Three post-migration tests (requires a list of three)

1. **Data integrity:** compare row counts and spot-check key tables (users, schools, records) between old and new databases.
2. **Application functionality:** run core user flows end to end (login, reading and writing data, reports) against the new database, and check performance and connection limits under realistic load.
3. **Backups and restore:** confirm automatic backups are running, then do a test restore to verify they work, and confirm the data location is in Frankfurt.
