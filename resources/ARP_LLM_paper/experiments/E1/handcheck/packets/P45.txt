## 2. Route and timeline

**Order: Depot → B → A → C → Depot**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 08:30 | Leave depot |
| 08:50 | Arrive Drop B (20 min drive) |
| 08:50–09:05 | Unload at B (done 25 min before the 09:30 opening) |
| 09:05–09:30 | Drive to Drop A (25 min) |
| 09:30–09:45 | Unload at A (inside the 09:00–10:00 window) |
| 09:45–10:25 | Drive to Drop C (40 min) |
| **10:25–10:55** | **30-minute break near C** |
| 10:55–11:00 | Ready to go at C |
| 11:00–11:15 | Unload at C (the earliest it will be accepted) |
| 11:15–12:00 | Drive back to depot (45 min) |
| **12:00** | **Back at depot** |

**Why this order:** B has to be first. Going to A first means he leaves A at 09:15 at the earliest and reaches B at 09:40, which is after it opens. C has to be last because it won't accept anything before 11:00. The wait before C is the natural place for the break.

**One warning:** the return is exactly 12:00, so there is no slack at the end. C can't start before 11:00, and 15 minutes unloading plus 45 minutes driving brings him back at 12:00 whatever happens earlier in the day. If C is ready the moment he arrives, he's fine. Any delay at C will make him late. It would be worth either:
- asking C whether they'd accept at 10:45, or
- agreeing that 12:00 is a soft deadline.

## 3. Friendlier delivery text
> "Hi! Your driver from [Business] is about 15 minutes away with your furniture. See you shortly!"

## 4. Three checks before handover
1. **Surfaces:** no new scratches, dents or marks on the finish, especially corners and edges.
2. **Structure and fittings:** legs are firm, joints are tight, and handles, hinges and castors are secure.
3. **Function:** drawers slide, doors open and close properly, and glass or mirrors are intact.

## 5. Paper or emailed delivery note?
**Emailed is better.** Get a signature on a phone or tablet, with a photo of the item in place, and send the note automatically. It can't get lost, it's time-stamped, and it's easy to find later if there's a dispute. Keep a few paper notes in the van for when there's no signal or the customer prefers paper.

## 6. Proof of delivery (for the part-timer)
**Proof of delivery** is a signed and dated record, ideally with a photo, showing that the customer received their item in good condition.

## 7. Evening-before text (140 characters)
> Hi [Name], your furniture from [Business] arrives tomorrow between [time] and [time]. Reply here if that's tricky and we'll sort it. Thanks!

Two things will keep it to a single message:
- **Watch the length.** It is 140 characters with the placeholders as written, which leaves about 20 characters for longer names and times. If your business name is long, shorten it.
- **Use plain characters.** Use straight apostrophes (') and no emoji. Curly quotes or emoji can cut the limit for one text to 70 characters, which would split it into two messages.
