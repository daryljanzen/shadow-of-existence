ANSWER:
Hello Margaret,

## Menu card heading

- **"Here's to the Next Chapter"**
- **"A Toast to [Name]"**
- **"With Thanks & Best Wishes"**

The first works well for a retirement: warm and forward-looking without being sentimental.

## The main course

**Roast beef and Yorkshire puddings can't be done properly in that kitchen.** Both need an oven. The hall's licence rules out cooking them elsewhere, and hobs can't roast a joint or make Yorkshires rise.

**What I'd propose:** slow-braised beef in red wine or ale, with creamy horseradish mash, honey-glazed carrots, buttered greens and a rich gravy.

- **It suits the kitchen.** Braising is done entirely on the hob. Use two or three large induction-compatible pots. Brown the beef in batches early, then simmer for 2½–3 hours until tender. The hot cupboard holds it perfectly, and braised beef actually improves with waiting. With eight rings you can run the beef, mash and vegetables side by side.
- **It suits the budget.** Braising uses cheaper cuts such as chuck or shin. At about 200g raw per head, that's 12kg of beef, roughly £150–£170 at current prices. That leaves plenty of the £16 per head for sides, gravy, garnish and your margin.
- **It keeps the spirit of her request.** Beef, gravy and horseradish give it the same traditional Sunday-lunch feel.

**How to explain it to her** (you could say something like this):

> "I completely understand wanting roast beef and Yorkshires. It's a classic for a reason. Unfortunately the hall has no oven, and its licence means all hot food must be cooked on site, so I couldn't roast the beef or make Yorkshires to a standard I'd be happy serving. What I'd suggest instead keeps everything you love about a roast: tender slow-braised beef, rich gravy and horseradish mash with seasonal vegetables. It's cooked fresh on site, it holds beautifully, so every guest gets a hot plate at the same time, and it sits comfortably within your budget."

**Before finalising, two questions are worth asking the hall:**
- Could you bring in your own countertop or portable oven? Check the power supply and whether their licence or insurance allows it.
- Would reheating Yorkshires made elsewhere count as "cooked on the premises"? Don't assume it does. Get a clear answer in writing if you're tempted.

If the answer to either is no, the braise is the safe and honest proposal.

## Allergen notice

*"If you have any allergies or dietary needs, please let our team know, and we'll gladly tell you exactly what's in each dish."*

## Three cold canapés

1. **Smoked salmon blinis** with crème fraîche, dill and lemon zest.
2. **Whipped goat's cheese crostini** with fig chutney and toasted walnut (vegetarian).
3. **Chicken liver parfait on brioche toasts** with onion marmalade.

All three can be made the day before and assembled shortly before service. Keep the toppings and bases separate until then so nothing goes soggy.

## Napkin colour

**Ivory or soft cream.** It's neutral, looks elegant against dated green and lets the food stand out. For a bit more warmth, **muted gold or mustard** suits a celebration and works well with green. Avoid other greens, which will clash with the curtains, and bright white, which can make older fabrics look tired.

## "Mise en place"

*"Mise en place is French for 'everything in its place.' It means preparing and organising all your ingredients and equipment before you start cooking, so that service runs smoothly."*

## RSVP wording

*"Kindly reply by [date], letting us know of any dietary requirements or allergies so we can make sure everyone is well looked after."*

Best of luck with the quote. I hope it's a lovely send-off.
