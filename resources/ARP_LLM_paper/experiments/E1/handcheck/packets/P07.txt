### 2. Lone working policy (for a new starter)
A lone working policy sets out how we keep you safe when you're visiting patients or travelling on your own, without a colleague nearby. It covers practical steps like letting the office know where you are and when you expect to finish, what to do if you feel unsafe, and how we'll check in if we don't hear from you.

### 4. Mileage logging: paper or app?
I'd recommend an **app**, with paper as a backup for anyone who prefers it. Here's why:
- **GPS tracking** (e.g. MileIQ, Driversnote, or your trust's own system if it has one) records journeys automatically, so nobody has to remember odometer readings.
- **Fewer errors and less chasing** for you, because totals are calculated for you and claims can be exported.
- **Audit-friendly**, since entries are timestamped and harder to lose than a diary left in the car.

A couple of things to check first:
- Does your organisation already mandate a system, such as an e-expenses portal? If so, use that.
- If staff use personal phones, check with IG/data protection about location tracking and patient addresses. A good rule is to log postcodes or visit references rather than patient names.
- Keep a simple paper template available for anyone without a suitable phone or in areas with poor signal.

### 7. Low-cost lunchtime wellbeing ideas (drop-in friendly)
1. **"Bring & Share" lunch table.** Everyone brings a snack or dish to share, laid out across the whole hour. People can arrive whenever they get back from visits, grab a plate and chat, then leave when they need to. The only cost is whatever people choose to bring.
2. **Rolling walk-and-talk.** A gentle 15–20 minute loop near base that leaves on the hour and again at half past. People join whichever lap suits their schedule. It's free, gets everyone outside, and is a nice break from driving.
3. **Wellbeing drop-in corner.** Set up a quiet space with tea, a gratitude or "shout-out" board where colleagues can leave thank-you notes for each other, and a few simple activities like colouring sheets, a puzzle, or a short guided breathing audio on loop. Costs very little, and people can stay for 5 minutes or 50.
