ANSWER:
No need to apologise for the volume. Here goes:

**1. Annuals vs perennials**
Annuals grow, flower, set seed and die within one year, so you sow them again each spring (e.g. lettuce, sunflowers). Perennials come back year after year from the same roots (e.g. rhubarb, asparagus, most herbs like mint).

**2. Plot name ideas**
- **The Muddy Patch**
- **Sprout Corner**
- **The Half Acre** (a cheeky joke for a half-plot)
- **Wiggly Worm Gardens**
- **The Secret Veg Garden**
- Your family name plus "Patch," e.g. *The Smith Patch*, which kids often love seeing on a sign

**3. Three easy vegetables for kids**
- **Radishes:** Sow from March and they're ready in about 4 weeks. They're perfect for impatient little gardeners, and you can keep sowing them all summer.
- **First-early potatoes:** Plant in March or April and dig them up in June or July. Digging for "buried treasure" is a guaranteed hit.
- **Courgettes:** Plant out after the frosts in late May and you'll be picking from July. One or two plants is plenty, because they're very productive and kids love finding the giant ones.

Runner beans are a good bonus option if you fancy building a bean teepee for the kids to hide in.

**4. Tomato feed for your sprayer**
- 2 US gallons ≈ 7.57 litres
- The dose is 15 ml ÷ 5 L = 3 ml per litre
- 7.57 × 3 = **about 23 ml** of feed for a full sprayer

If it turned out to be UK gallons (9.09 L), it would be about 27 ml. Either way the feed is forgiving, so 23 ml is safe. If you don't fill the sprayer right to the top, just use 3 ml per litre of water you actually put in.

**5. Shed colour**
Go for a **muted, darkish green**, such as olive, sage or a deep "forest" green. Dark, greyish tones recede visually, while bright or pale colours stand out. Most garden wood-paint ranges have shades with names like "Old English Green," "Willow" or "Seagrass." If you're unsure, a dark olive is the safest bet against a hedge. Any of these should keep the committee happy.

**6. Friendlier noticeboard note**
> **Wheelbarrow Wanted Home! 🛒**
> Hello everyone! Just a gentle reminder that our communal wheelbarrow is for all of us to share. If you've borrowed it, please pop it back by the shed when you're done so the next person can find it. Thanks so much, and happy gardening!

Good luck with the plot. September is a great time to start, because you get the winter to dig and plan.
