## 3. Friendlier "on the way" text
> Hi [Name], it's [Driver] from [Business]. I'm on my way with your furniture and should be with you in about [X] minutes. See you soon!

## 4. Three checks before handing furniture over
1. **Finish and surfaces:** look for any new scratches, dents, chips or scuffs from the journey.
2. **Structure and moving parts:** check that it stands level, the joints are firm, and drawers and doors open and close smoothly.
3. **Completeness:** confirm it's the right piece and that every part is there, such as shelves, keys, handles, glass and fixings.

## 5. Paper or emailed delivery note?
I'd go with an **emailed note**, signed on the driver's phone or tablet. It can't get lost, it's time-stamped, and it's easy to find if there's a later dispute. Keep a few paper copies in the van for customers who ask, or who don't have email.

## 6. Proof of delivery (for the part-timer)
**Proof of delivery** is a signed and dated record, often with a photo, showing that the customer received the item in good condition.

## 7. Evening-before text (under 160 characters)
> Hi [first name], your furniture from [Business] arrives tomorrow between [9 and 10am]. Reply if that time doesn't suit. Thanks!

- **Length:** the fixed wording is 93 characters. That leaves about 67 characters for the name, business and time. A filled-in example ("Hi Sarah… Oak & Ash Restorations… between 9 and 10am") comes to 130 characters.
- **Keep it plain:** no emojis, curly apostrophes or long dashes. Any of these can cut the single-message limit from 160 characters to 70, so the text would be split into several messages.
