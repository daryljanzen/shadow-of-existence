## 1. Winter clearance event name
**Needs:** creative naming, short and punchy.

- **Winter Wipeout**

## 2. Window sign wording
**Needs:** short persuasive copy with a headline, a hook, and an urgency line.

> **THE BIG THAW**
> Winter gear. Prices dropped.
> **Up to 50% off** jackets, boots, bottles & more
> *Once it's gone, it's gone. Ends [date].*

Alternatives for the headline line: "Winter's not over. Our prices are." Or, if you want to be cheeky: "Our loss is your warm gain." Only put "up to X%" on the sign if at least some items really hit that figure, since that's a trading standards point.

## 3. Markdown vs. discount, and old price crossed out?
**Needs:** a terminology explanation and a display recommendation.

- **Markdown:** a *permanent* reduction in the selling price, usually because stock is slow, seasonal, or end-of-line. The new price becomes the price in your system.
- **Discount:** a *temporary or conditional* reduction, such as a promo code, a loyalty price, "10% off today," or multi-buy. The base price stays the same.

Clearance pricing is usually markdowns.

**Display:** show the old price crossed out next to the new price. It makes the saving obvious and sells better than a bare new price. Two caveats, since your area manager may ask:
- The "was" price should be a price you genuinely charged recently. In the UK, the Pricing Practices Guide and CMA guidance expect this. Other regions have similar rules.
- Check whether head office has a standard ticket format before you print anything.

## 4. Water bottle stock reconciliation
**Needs:** arithmetic and a judgement on whether it's safe to sign off with zero variance.

- Opening stock: 240
- Deliveries: 60 + 60 = 120
- Total available: **360**
- Sold per till: 275
- **Expected closing stock: 85**
- Counted: 95
- **Variance: +10 units (10 more than expected)**

**It doesn't reconcile, so I wouldn't sign it off as zero variance.** A surplus usually points to one of these:
- Returns or refunds restocked physically but not processed in the system
- A delivery booked in short, such as a received quantity under what actually arrived
- Sales rung up as a different item or variant (for example, a different colour or size)
- A transfer in, or stock sitting in the back room that was already counted elsewhere
- A voided or cancelled sale where the item was never taken
- A miscount, so recount the back room first

Also, you said the count was done this morning, and today is 12 November. If any sales or deliveries happened between 31 October and the count, the expected figure changes. Strictly, the reconciliation should compare the count against the stock position as at the same moment. If sales since month-end are included in the 275, or missing from it, that could explain or enlarge the gap.

Recount, check the returns and delivery notes, and then either explain the +10 or record it as a variance with a note. Your area manager will trust a documented variance more than a suspiciously perfect one.

## 5. Three cheap ways to boost weekday afternoon foot traffic
**Needs:** low-cost, practical ideas that use the school and GP surgery nearby.

1. **School-run offer:** put an A-board outside with something for parents at pickup time, like "Show this at 3:15 for 10% off kids' waterproofs, socks or bottles." Pair it with a small display of kids' outdoor gear by the door. You could also ask the school to include a line in its newsletter.
2. **GP surgery noticeboard and "waiting room" flyers:** ask the surgery whether you can leave a small poster or cards. Patients and staff are a captive local audience. Offer staff a modest discount with ID, such as "NHS and school staff 10% off, weekdays 2-5."
3. **Weekday afternoon "reason to come in" events:** run a free 20-minute demo or clinic, such as boot waterproofing, how to layer for winter walks, or a local trail-route chat. Promote it on a window sign and a local Facebook group. It turns your quiet shift into something useful, and the sock display can finally rest.
