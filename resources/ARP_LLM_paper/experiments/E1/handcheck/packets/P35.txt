ANSWER:
Congratulations on the first bookings! Here's everything in order.

## 1. Name ideas
- **Crumbs & Confetti**
- **Sprinkle & Graze**
- **The Little Platter Co.**
- **Pass the Platter**
- **Tiny Grazers**

Crumbs & Confetti and Pass the Platter feel the most grown-up while still being playful. Check that your favourite isn't taken on Instagram and Companies House before you commit.

## 2. Tagline
- "Party food the kids actually eat (and the grown-ups photograph)."
- Shorter: "Platters & cupcakes for little guests."

## 3. Nut-free snacks kids actually eat
1. **Mini sausage rolls**, which almost always disappear first.
2. **Cheese and tomato pizza bites or slices**
3. **Fruit skewers or grapes**, with the grapes cut lengthways for younger children.

Always check labels for "may contain nuts" on bought-in items like pastry, crisps and sauces. Cupcakes are easy to keep nut-free too, as long as your sprinkles, chocolate and decorations are checked.

## 4. Allergen info
Best practice is to do **both**. Put a small label or card on each platter naming the main allergens, and include a full sheet with the order. Also ask about allergies and intolerances when you take the booking. In the UK there are 14 major allergens to cover, including gluten, milk, eggs, soya, sesame and nuts. If you sell anything pre-packed, such as boxed cupcakes, stricter labelling rules may apply. Your local council's environmental health team or the Food Standards Agency can confirm what applies to you. Also, if you haven't yet, you generally need to register as a food business with your council at least 28 days before you start trading, so with bookings next month, do that straight away.

## 5. Pricing: the maths is slightly off
£12.60 is a **40% markup** (£9 + 40%), not a 40% **margin**. Margin is the share of the *price* that is profit:

- £3.60 profit ÷ £12.60 = about **28.6% margin**

For a true 40% margin: **£9 ÷ 0.60 = £15.00 per head**. That leaves £6.00 profit, which is 40% of £15.

One more thing, given you undercharged on the cakes: £9 covers only ingredients and packaging. It doesn't include your time (shopping, prepping, baking, assembling, delivering), travel, equipment, insurance, or card/platform fees. If that £6 per head has to pay for all of that, you may still be underpaid. Estimate your hours for a typical booking and add a labour cost. For a small party, I'd consider £16-£18 per head, or a minimum order or flat delivery fee. Pricing slightly higher at the start is easier than raising prices later.

## 6. Colour scheme
Your pink and yellow instinct is fine. The trick is to make the shades richer and add a grounding colour:

- **Raspberry or deep rose** instead of baby pink
- **Mustard or warm marigold** instead of lemon yellow
- **Deep teal, navy or forest green** as the anchor colour
- **Cream** as the background

That keeps the fun, but it reads as stylish, and it will look good in Instagram stories. A clean modern font, with a playful touch only in the logo mark, will also keep it from feeling nursery-ish.

Good luck with the first two bookings! xx
