**2. Lone working policy, in two sentences**
*Needs:* A plain-English explanation for someone new.

A lone working policy sets out how we keep you safe when you're working on your own, such as visiting patients in their homes without a colleague. It covers things like letting the team know where you are and when you expect to finish, how to check in, and what to do if you feel unsafe or something goes wrong.

**4. Mileage logging: paper diary or app?**
*Needs:* A recommendation with brief reasoning.

**I'd recommend an app**, or a simple shared form such as Microsoft Forms or Google Forms if you want to keep it free.

- Entries are timestamped and harder to lose than a paper diary left in a car.
- Totals add up automatically, which saves you time and avoids sums going wrong.
- Nurses can log a trip straight after each visit from their phone.

Keep a paper option for anyone who struggles with tech or has poor signal in rural areas. It's also worth checking with your organisation's finance or IT team whether they already use an approved system.

**7. Lunch-hour wellbeing ideas (drop-in friendly)**
*Needs:* Low-cost activities that people can join late or leave early.

1. **Bring-and-share lunch.** Everyone brings something small and people come and go as visits allow. It costs almost nothing and is great for catching up.
2. **Walk-and-talk.** Do a gentle loop near the base, starting at a set time, and latecomers can join on the second lap or meet the group partway. It's free and gets people outside after time in the car.
3. **Relaxation drop-in.** Put on a free 10-minute guided relaxation or stretching video, played on a loop, with tea and biscuits. People can join any session and slip out whenever they need to.
