ANSWER:
# Lisbon trip answers

## Three things to do on a free evening
1. **Sunset at a miradouro.** Santa Luzia or Senhora do Monte give you sweeping views over the rooftops and the Tagus. Take a drink with you.
2. **Fado in Alfama.** Live fado over dinner is a classic Lisbon evening. Book ahead, as the small venues fill up.
3. **LX Factory or Time Out Market.** LX Factory has restaurants, bars and bookshops in a converted industrial complex. Time Out Market is easier if you want a quick, varied dinner.

## Saying "thank you"
Yes, it depends on who is speaking, but on the speaker's gender, not the listener's:
- A man says **obrigado**.
- A woman says **obrigada**.
- "Muito obrigado/a" means "thank you very much."

## Tipping
It's not expected the way it is in the US. Service is built into the prices and waiters are paid a regular wage. Rounding up or leaving about 5-10% for good service is appreciated but optional. One thing to watch: bread, olives and other *couvert* items placed on your table are charged if you eat them.

## Will your bag meet the carry-on limit?
It depends on your airline, and I don't know which one you're flying. Your bag is **56 × 36 × 23 cm and 9 kg**, which is borderline:
- **TAP Air Portugal** (55 × 40 × 20 cm, 8 kg): over on length, depth and weight.
- **Lufthansa** (55 × 40 × 23 cm, 8 kg): over on length and weight.
- **easyJet** (56 × 45 × 25 cm): fits, but easyJet's free under-seat bag is much smaller, so check which allowance you've paid for.
- **British Airways** (56 × 45 × 25 cm): fits.
- **Ryanair**: the free bag is much smaller, and the paid priority bag is 55 × 40 × 20 cm, 10 kg, so yours would be slightly over on length and depth.

Limits change, so check your airline's page. If it's a tight one, aim to get the bag under 8 kg and consider squeezing the depth. Wheels and handle count, and the bag often needs to fit a sizer.

## Rolling or folding work shirts?
**Fold them**, ideally flat and with the collars alternating, or use a packing folder. Folding gives fewer hard creases on structured fabrics than rolling. Roll T-shirts, underwear, socks and knitwear to save space. Put heavy items (shoes, toiletries) near the wheels and wear your bulkiest shoes and jacket on the plane to save weight.

## Luggage tag colour
Go for **bright orange, neon yellow or lime green**. Black, navy and red bags are the most common, so those colours stand out. Put your contact details inside the bag too, and consider slipping in an AirTag or similar tracker, which is useful after your lost case.

## Making the Friday evening return easier
- **Pack the night before** and keep your essentials (passport, charger, toiletries bag) in one place.
- **Check in online** and save your boarding pass to your phone.
- **Check out in the morning** and ask the hotel to store your bag so you aren't dragging it through meetings.
- **Plan your route to the airport.** Lisbon's airport is close to the centre, and the red metro line takes about 20 minutes. A taxi or ride-hail is simpler after a long day.
- **Keep liquids in a clear bag** at the top of your case for security.
- **Leave a generous buffer.** Friday evenings are busy, so aim to arrive about 2 hours before departure.

Have a great trip. *Boa viagem!*
