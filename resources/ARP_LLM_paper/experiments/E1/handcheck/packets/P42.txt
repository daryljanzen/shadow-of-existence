ANSWER:
## Title ideas
- *Mud on My Boots: Growing Up on the Farm in the 1950s*
- *Up Before the Cockerel*
- *Forty Stories from the Farm*
- *Hay in My Hair: [Mum's Name] Remembers*

I'd pick *Up Before the Cockerel*, with the subtitle *Forty Farm Memories from the 1950s*. It's warm and a bit cheeky, and the subtitle tells people what's inside.

## Dedication
> *For Mum,*
> *who told these stories so well that we had to write them down.*
> *Happy 80th birthday, with all our love.*
> *From all of us*

A plainer version: *"For Mum, on her 80th birthday. With love from all of us."*

## Binding
Your brother's plan doesn't work as it stands. The printer's saddle-stitch limit is 120 pages and your layout is 148, so they won't print it that way. The costs:

| Option | Cost for 150 | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Saddle-stitch | £285 | Only possible at 120 pages or fewer |
| Perfect-bound | £465 | £90 over budget (about 60p a copy) |

My suggestion is to try cutting the page count first, without touching the 14-point:
- Don't start every story on a fresh page. If the 40 pieces are short, this is probably where most of the white space is.
- Remove blank pages and separate title pages.
- Tighten line spacing slightly (for example 1.4 down to 1.2) and trim margins a little. You can also try a slightly larger page size.

You need to lose 28 pages, about 19%. That's realistic if stories currently start on new pages, but I can't promise it. Page counts for stapled booklets must be a multiple of 4, so aim for 120 or, to be safer, 112–116. At 120 pages a stapled booklet can be bulky, so ask the printer whether that count includes the cover and what paper weight they'd advise.

If you can't get below 120:
- **Perfect-bound with a top-up.** Ask family for about £90 in total, or 60p a copy.
- **Perfect-bound, 120 copies.** That's £372, within budget. Check you really need 150.

If saddle-stitch works, you'll have about £90 left. You could spend it on a nicer cover finish or a few extra copies.

## Cover colour
A warm barley gold or harvest yellow, with dark green or charcoal lettering. It reads as "farm" and it photographs well. Go for a matt finish, because gloss throws reflections in photos. I'd avoid pure white (it glares) and very dark navy or black (the detail disappears).

## Photos
Put them alongside the stories. Your readers will enjoy seeing the tractor or the farmhouse right next to the memory, and it makes the book feel like hers. Use captions at the same 14-point size. If page count becomes the problem, a single photo section is easier to lay out, but it won't save pages unless it's on different paper, so I'd only do that if you have to.

## Getting relatives to contribute
1. **Memory postcards.** Post or message each relative a card with three prompts, such as "My favourite thing about visiting the farm" and "Something Mum did that made us laugh". Short prompts get more replies than "write something".
2. **Voice notes.** Ask people in the family WhatsApp group to record a one-minute voice note. You transcribe them, so nobody has to write anything.
3. **A countdown with a real deadline.** Announce the date now, send a reminder at two weeks and at one week, and name the stragglers jokingly ("Uncle Pete, we're looking at you"). Offer to phone anyone who'd rather just talk.

## Back-cover blurb
> *Before breakfast she'd already milked the cows, fed the hens and dodged the goose. Forty stories from [Mum's Name]'s childhood on the farm, told with love, mud and a fair bit of exaggeration. Read them with a cup of tea, and don't let her tell you it didn't happen quite like that.*

If you tell me a detail or two, such as a notorious animal, a family catchphrase or a disaster in the kitchen, I can make it much more personal.
