ANSWER:
## 1. Alternative job titles
- **Operations Manager**: only if the role has real ownership of processes or budgets. Otherwise it overstates it.
- **Business Operations Coordinator**: same level, but signals a wider remit than admin.
- **Operations & Planning Coordinator**: good if scheduling, resourcing or forecasting is a big part of the job.

## 2. Warmer rejection sentence
> "Thank you for the time and care you put into your application. After careful consideration, we've decided not to progress it further on this occasion, but we were glad to learn about your experience and wish you every success."

## 3. Competency questions on prioritisation
1. "Tell me about a week when you had more urgent requests than you could possibly deliver. What did you drop or delay, and how did the people affected find out?"
2. "Describe a time you prioritised something that turned out to be the wrong call. How did you realise, and what did you change afterwards?"
3. "Walk me through your actual to-do list or plan from a recent busy day. Why was it in that order, and what moved during the day?"

These resist rehearsed answers because they ask about failure, trade-offs and real artefacts. Probe with "What exactly did *you* do?" and "What would you do differently?"

## 4. The £44,500 salary
**I can't confirm that £44,500 is fine to offer.** Under the policy you've described, it's £2,500 above the top of the band and needs the Finance Director's written sign-off, which you don't have. The hiring manager's approval doesn't replace it. Your options:

**Option A: Offer at the top of the band now**
> "We're pleased to offer you a starting salary of £42,000 per annum, which sits at the top of the approved range for this role."

**Option B: Signal intent without committing (best if you want to keep the candidate warm)**
> "We'd very much like to welcome you to the team. We're currently seeking final approval on the salary you've requested and expect to confirm the figure by [date shortly after the 30th]. In the meantime, I'd be happy to talk through the rest of the package."

**Option C: Get the approval sooner.** Check whether the Finance Director has a named deputy or delegated authority while she's on leave, or whether she'd approve by email. Policies often allow for this.

Don't put £44,500 in writing until the sign-off exists. A written offer can be binding, and undoing it would be far more awkward than a short delay.

## 5. Scorecard format
Use **both**: a 1–5 score per competency, plus a short evidence note for each score. Make the evidence note mandatory.

- Scores make candidates comparable and reduce "gut feel" decisions.
- Notes show *why* each score was given, which helps if a decision is challenged.
- Define what a 1, 3 and 5 look like for each competency before the interviews.
- Have each interviewer score on their own before the panel discusses.

## 6. "Notice period" in one line
> **Notice period:** how much warning someone must give before they leave the job (or before the company ends their employment), which tells you the earliest date a new hire can actually start.
