## 3. Newsletter blurb
"Does your club, charity or community group struggle to get noticed online? Join us on Saturday for a friendly half-day workshop of talks, a hands-on session and lunch, where you'll pick up practical social media skills you can use straight away. No experience needed, and everyone is welcome."

## 4. Name badge colour
Use **deep teal or navy** as a coloured border or header band, with the name printed in **black, large bold type** in the middle of the white card. Dark colours give strong contrast on white and are easier on older eyes. Avoid pale pastels, yellow and light grey, which wash out. If you want to colour-code, use a different band colour for speakers and volunteers.

## 5. Registration: paper or online?
For a mostly older crowd, I'd go with **paper as the main method**: a sign-up sheet at the library desk, plus a phone number to call. Many people in this group will find that easier and more comfortable, and it matches the library setting. Add a simple online form as a secondary option for anyone who prefers it, and have one volunteer enter the paper names into the same spreadsheet so you have a single list. Whichever you use, collect only the name, contact details and any dietary needs.

## 6. Three icebreakers (no standing up to talk about yourself)
1. **Sticker poll wall:** Put up a poster with questions like "Which platform do you use most?" and give everyone dot stickers to place as they arrive. It shows what the room knows and gives people something to chat about.
2. **Table-card questions:** Leave a card on each table with a light question, such as "What's the best thing you've seen shared online lately?" People can talk about it with their neighbours, and no one has to address the room.
3. **Group-name match-up:** Give each person a slip with a local group or a social media term, and have them find the matching slip at their table. Make it a pairing or word-match game so it works as a quiet, seated warm-up.
