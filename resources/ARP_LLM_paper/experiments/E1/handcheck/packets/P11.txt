ANSWER:
Here's everything in order.

**1. Subject line for your notice email**

> Notice to End Tenancy – [Your Address] – Move-Out Date 31 March 2027

Putting the address and date in the subject makes it easy for the agent to find later.

**2. More professional version of your sentence**

> "I am writing to give notice that I will be vacating the property, and I would be grateful if my deposit could be returned as promptly as possible after the check-out."

**3. "Fair wear and tear" in one sentence**

It usually means the normal, gradual deterioration from everyday use over time, such as lightly worn carpets or faded paint, as opposed to damage caused by accident, misuse or neglect, which a landlord could deduct from a deposit.

**4. Three things to photograph before handing back the keys**

1. **Every room's walls, floors and carpets**, taken wide and then close up on any marks, with timestamps.
2. **The kitchen and bathroom**, including the inside of the oven, fridge, sink, toilet and shower, to show cleanliness.
3. **The meter readings and the keys themselves**, showing gas, electricity and water readings and every key you're returning.

**5. Is 31 January early enough?**

**No, it's one day short.** February 2027 has only 28 days, so "two months" here is less than 60 days:

- 31 January to 28 February = 28 days
- 28 February to 31 March = 31 days
- **Total = 59 days**

**The latest date is 30 January 2027**, which gives exactly 60 days.

I'd recommend not cutting it that fine. Some agreements count the day notice is received, or treat emails sent after business hours as arriving the next day. Sending in **mid-January** gives you a buffer. Ask the agent to confirm receipt in writing and keep that reply.

**6. PDFs or spreadsheet?**

**Use one folder of PDFs, with a simple spreadsheet index inside it.**

- **PDFs** are better as evidence. They can't easily be edited, anyone can open them, and they keep the dates and appearance of emails, receipts and the inventory.
- **A spreadsheet on its own** is just your own summary, so it carries less weight in a dispute. It is still useful as a contents list: date, document name, and a one-line description.
- **To hand it over**, zip the folder and send it as one attachment or one shared link. That's ideal for a new agent and avoids anything getting lost piecemeal.

**For the three-folders habit:**

- Make **one** folder now, named something like `Tenancy – [Address] – 2027 Move-Out`, and pin it or add it to your favourites.
- Name files starting with the date so they sort in order, e.g. `2027-01-15 Notice email.pdf`, `2027-03-31 Kitchen photos.pdf`.
- Delete or rename the other two folders so they can't catch stray files. If something lands in the wrong place, move it straight away rather than "later".
- Keep one backup copy, such as cloud storage or a USB stick, but treat the main folder as the only one you save into.

Good luck with the move. Getting the notice in early and keeping one tidy folder should prevent most of the mess from last time.
