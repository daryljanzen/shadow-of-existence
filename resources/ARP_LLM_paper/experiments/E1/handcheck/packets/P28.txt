ANSWER:
Here's each request and my answer.

## 1. Title for your 10-minute set
**Needs:** a creative suggestion that ties together three pieces about your grandmother's bakery.

- *First Proof* (the dough's rise, and a first draft)
- *Before the Ovens Wake*
- *Three Loaves*
- *Crumb* (small, tactile, a bit tender)

My pick is *Before the Ovens Wake*. It suggests early mornings and quiet, and it works for a family story.

## 2. Reading vs. performance
**Needs:** an explanation of the difference and whether you're expected to act.

- A **reading** centres on the text. You read from the page, with a clear voice, good pacing and natural expression.
- A **performance** is closer to theatre or spoken word. It's usually memorised, with movement, character voices and staging.

You aren't expected to act. Looking up at the audience at the end of sentences, pausing, and varying your pace is plenty. If the programme lists you as a "reading," that's what people expect. If you're unsure, ask the organisers.

## 3. What to wear under stage lights
**Needs:** colour advice for avoiding a washed-out look.

Go for saturated mid-to-deep colours like emerald, teal, cobalt, burgundy or plum. Avoid white, cream and pale pastels, which blow out under bright lights. Also avoid small busy patterns and very shiny fabric. Black works but can look flat. If you can find out the backdrop colour, avoid matching it.

## 5. Three ways to calm nerves before going on
**Needs:** three practical techniques for the last ten minutes.

1. **Slow breathing:** breathe in for 4 counts and out for 6 to 8. Repeat for a couple of minutes.
2. **Warm up your voice:** quietly read your opening lines aloud or hum. Once your voice feels familiar, it feels less risky.
3. **Ground yourself physically:** feel your feet on the floor, drop your shoulders and unclench your jaw. Tell yourself "I'm excited," which your body can't easily tell apart from nervous.

## 6. Paper or tablet
**Needs:** a recommendation between the two.

I'd go with **paper**. It can't run out of battery, dim, lock or show a notification. Print in 14 to 16 pt, single-sided, with generous spacing and numbered pages. Slide each page aside as you finish rather than turning it. A tablet is fine if you use airplane mode, turn off auto-lock, set the brightness manually and carry a paper backup, but paper is the safer choice.

**Timing tip:** at a comfortable reading pace of about 130 words a minute, ten minutes is roughly 1,300 words. Aim for about 1,100 to 1,200 across all three pieces so you have room to pause.
