ANSWER:
**1. Job title alternatives**
- **Operations Manager**: only if the role owns processes or budgets. Otherwise it overstates the job.
- **Business Operations Coordinator**: same level, but signals wider scope.
- **Operations & Delivery Coordinator**: useful if the role keeps projects or services running.

**2. Warmer rejection sentence**
> "Thank you for the time and care you put into your application. After careful consideration, we've decided not to move forward with it on this occasion, but we were glad to learn about your experience and wish you every success."

**3. Prioritisation interview questions**
1. "Tell me about a time two people senior to you each needed something urgently and you couldn't do both. What did you do, and what did you tell the person who waited?" Follow up with: "Looking back, would you make the same call?"
2. "Walk me through how you planned your last Monday morning. What was on your list, and what didn't get done?" This is hard to rehearse and shows real habits.
3. "Here are six tasks landing at once [give a realistic list for your team]. Talk me through your order and what you'd delegate, defer or drop." Then change one detail mid-answer to test adaptability.

**4. Salary: I can't confirm £44,500 is fine**
Under the policy you've described, £44,500 is £2,500 above the band. It needs written Finance Director sign-off, which you don't have. Offering it now would breach the policy, however keen the hiring manager is. Your options:

- **Find out if someone can sign off in her absence.** Many organisations name a deputy or the CFO/MD for exactly this. That's the quickest route.
- **Make a conditional offer** that's clear the figure is pending approval:
  > "We're delighted to offer you the role of Operations Coordinator. We are seeking approval for a starting salary of £44,500 per annum and expect to confirm this in writing by [date]; the rest of the offer stands as set out below."
- **Offer within band now** and leave room to discuss more:
  > "We're pleased to offer a starting salary of £42,000 per annum, and we're happy to discuss a salary review at [6 months / end of probation]."
- **Wait until the 30th.** This risks losing the candidate, so tell them about the delay honestly if you choose it.

Don't let any verbal "yes" go out before approval.

**5. Scorecard format**
Use **both**. Give each competency a 1–5 score with a short note justifying it. The scores make candidates comparable and reduce gut-feel bias. The notes explain the scores and help when panellists disagree. Define what a 1, 3 and 5 look like for each competency so everyone scores the same way.

**6. Notice period**
> "The notice period is how much warning someone must give before they leave a job (or before you can end their employment), as set out in their contract."
