ANSWER:
**Project title options**
- "Growing Together: Accessible Raised Beds and Paths for [Garden Name]"
- "[Garden Name] Community Garden: Opening Up Access for All"
- "Growing Together: An Accessible Garden for Every Age"

Pick whichever fits your garden's name and the funder's tone. Including "accessible" tells the reader what the money delivers.

**Tightened summary sentence**
"Our project will bring people of all ages and backgrounds together in a shared local space."

**Is £8,000 within the rules? No, it's slightly over.**
50% of £14,500 is **£7,250**, which is below the £8,000 maximum, so the 50% rule is the binding limit. The most you can request is **£7,250**. Requesting £8,000 would be 55% of costs and could get the application marked ineligible, so please change it before submitting. Your budget table should then show £7,250 requested and £7,250 from other sources.

**What "match funding" means**
It's the money you raise from other sources to cover the rest of the project cost, because the funder won't pay for all of it. Here you need £7,250 in match funding. It can come from other grants, fundraising, donations, or your group's own reserves. Many funders also accept in-kind contributions, such as volunteer time or donated materials, but check their guidance on whether those count. Also say whether the match is already secured or still pending, as funders usually ask.

**Three realistic outcome measures for one year**
1. **Participation:** the number of unique people using the garden, and how many are in each age group (for example, children, working-age adults, over-65s), taken from a sign-in sheet or volunteer log.
2. **Accessibility:** the number of people with mobility or access needs using the new paths and raised beds, plus the number of accessible sessions run, with short feedback from those users.
3. **Community engagement and produce:** the number of volunteer hours given, the number of events or sessions held, and the amount of produce grown or shared (for example, kg harvested or number of households receiving it).

A short end-of-year survey would give you satisfaction and "sense of belonging" figures to add to these.

**Format for partner letters**
I can't tell from what you've shared, so check the funder's guidance first, since some specify a format. If it says nothing, a **signed PDF on the partner's letterhead** is the safer choice, as it looks more formal and is harder to dispute. An email is often accepted but is weaker evidence. If you're short on time, ask partners to send a PDF with a signature or a typed name and job title, plus the date. Each letter should say specifically what they'll contribute or why they support the project.
