ANSWER:
**1. Title**
"Incident Review: [Service] Outage, Tuesday [date] – [Short Impact Summary]" e.g. "Incident Review: Login and API Outage, Tuesday 4 Nov". Keep it descriptive and blame-free.

**2. Status-page rewrite**
"Between [start time] and [end time] [timezone], some customers were unable to [log in / load dashboards / use the API]. The issue is fixed, and everything is working normally. We're sorry for the disruption and will share what we learned by [date]."

**3. Severity scheme**
- **Sev 1 – Critical:** Core product down or data at risk for most customers. All hands, status page update right away.
- **Sev 2 – Major:** Important feature broken or degraded for some customers, with no good workaround. On-call responds immediately, status page updated.
- **Sev 3 – Minor:** Small or cosmetic issue, or a workaround exists. Handled during working hours, no status page update needed.

**4. MTTR**
MTTR (mean time to recovery/restore) is the average time it takes us to get service working again after an incident starts.

**5. SLA check**
- 30 days = 43,200 minutes.
- 0.1% of that = **43.2 minutes** allowed downtime. (The 43.8 figure comes from an average month of ~30.4 days, so it's not right for a 30-day month.)
- Your outages total 18 + 12 + 21 = **51 minutes**.
- 51 > 43.2, so **yes, you've exceeded the allowance**, by about 7.8 minutes. Even under the 43.8 figure you'd still be over, so "we're fine" is wrong either way.
- Uptime for the month is about 99.88% (1 − 51/43,200).

Check your contract for how downtime is defined (e.g. partial degradation, scheduled maintenance exclusions, measurement method) and what remedy applies (usually service credits, often requested by the customer). Whether it's a formal breach depends on those terms, so it's worth having someone read the clause before you communicate externally.

**6. Action items**
1. Write and publish a runbook for the failure mode from this outage, within a month.
2. Add or tune alerting so this specific failure pages the on-call person, rather than being noticed by customers.
3. Run one lightweight review of recent changes/deploy process (e.g. a rollback checklist) and commit to the one or two improvements you can actually fit in.

Each is small, owned by a named person, and has a date, which keeps it deliverable.

**7. Format**
Async comments on the doc, with a short deadline (e.g. 3 working days) and one owner to summarize and finalize, and only schedule a call if there's a real disagreement left.
