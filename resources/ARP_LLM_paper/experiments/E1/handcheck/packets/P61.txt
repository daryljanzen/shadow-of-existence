ANSWER:
No, I wouldn't confirm that booking. Dr Patel only works Tuesdays and Thursdays, and 18 November is a Wednesday, so Mrs Okafor wouldn't see him. The system probably showed a free slot because the diary is open that day, not because he's in. If neither works for her, ask the manager or a clinician before booking her with someone else, since she asked for Dr Patel specifically.

**Reminder text**

> Hi [Name], a reminder of your appointment at [Practice] on [date] at [time]. Can't make it? Please let us know at least 24 hours ahead on [phone number] so we can offer the slot to someone else. Thank you!

If you want it shorter still:

> Hi [Name], you have an appointment at [Practice] on [date] at [time]. If you can't come, please call [number] so we can offer it to someone else. Thanks!

I dropped the line about removal from the list because it sounds harsh in a reminder. If the practice wants it kept for policy reasons, check with the manager first.

**Three ways to reduce missed appointments**
1. **Send two reminders**, for example a few days before and again the day before, with a simple way to cancel (reply "C" or a phone number).
2. **Make cancelling easy** with a text reply, online form, or a dedicated voicemail line, so people don't give up when the phones are busy.
3. **Book follow-ups before patients leave** and give them an appointment card, or tell them the date and time verbally, so it's fresh in their mind.

**DNA** means "Did Not Attend": the patient didn't turn up and didn't cancel beforehand. It's different from a cancellation, which is when they let you know in advance.

**Staff rota**
I'd use a shared spreadsheet, such as Google Sheets or Excel online, as the main version. It's easy to update when someone swaps shifts or is off sick, and everyone sees the same thing. Printing a copy for the office wall is a good backup if the system is down, just make sure it's dated so nobody uses an old one.

**Waiting room sign**

> **Please take phone calls outside**
> Out of respect for patient privacy and a calm waiting room, please step outside to use your phone. Thank you!

If you'd like it friendlier, you could use: "Phone ringing? Please take your call outside so everyone can have a bit of peace. Thank you!"

You're doing well for your first week on the front desk. Checking the clinician's days like you did here is the right habit.
