ANSWER:
Here's each request and what it needs, followed by my answer.

## 1. Business name
**Needs:** creative brainstorming that suits kids' party platters and cupcakes, and sounds fun but grown-up.

- **Little Bites & Co.**
- **The Party Platter Pantry**
- **Crumbs & Confetti**
- **Pip & Platter**
- **Sprinkle & Share**

I'd go for **Crumbs & Confetti** or **Pip & Platter**, since both feel stylish rather than babyish. Check that the name isn't taken on Instagram, Companies House and the domain before you commit.

## 2. Instagram tagline
**Needs:** one short, cute line.

- "Party food the kids devour and the grown-ups photograph."
- "Platters, cupcakes and zero party-prep stress."

## 3. Three nut-free kid-friendly snacks
**Needs:** snacks that are popular with children and made without nuts.

1. **Mini sausage rolls**, a reliable favourite that usually disappears first.
2. **Ham and cheese sandwich pinwheels**, which are easy for small hands.
3. **Fruit skewers or a fruit rainbow**, with grapes cut lengthways for little ones.

Always check the labels on shop-bought pastry, sauces and sprinkles, because "may contain nuts" warnings are common.

## 4. Allergen info: on the platters or on a sheet?
**Needs:** best practice for a small caterer (not legal advice).

Do both. A sheet with the order is good, but it's easy to lose or ignore on the day. Best practice is:
- A **small label or card on each platter** naming the dish and flagging key allergens (milk, egg, gluten, nuts, sesame, etc.).
- A **full ingredient and allergen sheet** with every booking.
- Asking each parent about allergies **when they book**.
- Being careful with wording. "Made without nuts" is safer than "nut-free" unless you can guarantee no cross-contamination in your kitchen.

Check the current rules with your local council's food safety team or the Food Standards Agency. They're friendly with new businesses and can confirm what applies to you.

## 5. Pricing: is £12.60 right?
**Needs:** checking the maths and recommending a price.

No, but it's a very common mix-up. Adding 40% to your cost is a **markup**, not a **margin**. At £12.60, your profit is £3.60, which is only **28.6% of the price**.

For a 40% margin, profit has to be 40% of the selling price, so the cost is 60% of it:

**£9 ÷ 0.60 = £15.00 per head**

Check: £15 − £9 = £6 profit, and £6 ÷ £15 = 40%.

Since undercharging is your worry, also remember that £9 covers only ingredients and packaging. If it doesn't include your **time (shopping, prep, baking, assembly), delivery, and overheads** like fuel, insurance and equipment, your real profit is lower than it looks. Consider:
- Setting a **minimum number of guests** per booking so small orders are still worth your time.
- Adding a **delivery fee**.
- Treating £15 as a floor rather than a target, and pricing up if your costs rise.

## 6. Logo colour scheme
**Needs:** a palette that's fun but not nursery-like, and appeals to parents in their 30s and 40s.

You don't need to ditch pink and yellow. Just make them more sophisticated:
- **Dusty raspberry or deep rose** (~#C2476B) instead of baby pink
- **Mustard or marigold** (~#E3A72F) instead of lemon yellow
- **Deep navy or forest green** (~#1F3A4D) as a grounding neutral for text
- **Warm cream** (~#FFF6E9) as the background

The dark neutral and cream make the brights look stylish, and the palette will photograph well in Instagram stories. A modern serif or rounded-but-clean font will help it feel grown-up too.
