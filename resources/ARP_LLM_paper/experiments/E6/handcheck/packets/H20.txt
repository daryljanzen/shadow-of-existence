FINAL VERSION:
# Refurbishment Budget: Store Productivity Review, FY2025

## Recommendation

**Under company policy, the refurbishment budget should go to South.** On a full-year basis, South has the lowest sales per square foot of sales floor, at **£240**. It was also the lowest in 2024, so the weakness is persistent rather than a one-year effect.

West looks weakest on unadjusted figures (£210/sq ft), but only because it was closed for three months for flood repairs. Annualised, it is at **£280**, second-lowest.

## Method

The policy sets two requirements:

1. **Denominator: sales floor area.** The KPI is sales per sq ft of **sales floor**. Gross internal area, which includes stockrooms, is not the policy measure.
2. **Full-year basis.** West traded only 9 months in 2025 (closed January to March), so its sales are annualised: £2.1m ÷ 9 × 12 = **£2.8m**. All other stores traded 12 months, and no floor areas changed. The operations note confirms the repairs did not alter West's layout.

## Sales density on sales floor area, 2025

| Rank | Store | 2025 sales (full-year basis) | Sales floor (sq ft) | Sales per sq ft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Central | £4.2m | 12,000 | £350 |
| 2= | North | £2.4m | 8,000 | £300 |
| 2= | East | £1.8m | 6,000 | £300 |
| 4 | West | £2.8m *(annualised)* | 10,000 | £280 |
| **5 (lowest)** | **South** | **£3.6m** | **15,000** | **£240** |

The annualisation decides the result. Without it, West would show £210 and would wrongly rank lowest.

## Robustness checks

**Seasonality.** Pro-rating assumes January to March is an average quarter, worth 25% of the year. West would fall below South's £240 only if its full-year sales were under £2.4m. April to December brought in £2.1m, so January to March would have to normally contribute under £0.3m, or less than 12.5% of the year. That is implausibly low, even for a soft post-Christmas quarter. West's 2024 monthly sales would confirm this, but the conclusion is very unlikely to change.

**Reopening effects.** The annualisation treats April to December as normal trading. If West restarted slowly after the flood, its true full-year run-rate is *higher* than £2.8m. That would only widen the gap with South.

**Prior year.** In 2024 every store traded a full year on the same floor areas, so no adjustment is needed. The ranking gives the same answer:

| Rank | Store | 2024 sales | Sales per sq ft |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Central | £4.0m | £333 |
| 2 | West | £2.9m | £290 |
| 3 | North | £2.3m | £288 |
| 4 | East | £1.7m | £283 |
| **5 (lowest)** | **South** | **£3.5m** | **£233** |

| South | £3.5m | £3.6m | +2.9% |

| West | £2.9m | £2.8m *(annualised)* | −3.4% |

- **Chain.** Reported sales fell from £14.4m to £14.1m. With West annualised, the chain grew to about £14.8m (+2.8%). The whole of the reported fall comes from West's closure.
- **West.** Most of West's reported 27.6% drop (£2.9m to £2.1m) is the closure. The underlying dip of about 3% is worth watching. Annual figures can't show whether it reflects post-flood disruption or a continuing trend. Either way, it does not make West the lowest under the policy.
- **South.** South's growth (+2.9%) is roughly in line with the chain's underlying growth. It trails North, East and Central. Its problem is space productivity, not growth: it has the largest sales floor (15,000 sq ft) and sells the least per foot. A refurbishment that improves layout, space use or range would address this directly.

## Next steps

1. **Allocate the FY2025 refurbishment budget to South.**
2. Use West's 2024 monthly sales, if available, to confirm its usual January to March share and fully settle the seasonality question.
3. Monitor West's post-reopening trading separately from this decision.
4. **Measure the outcome.** Twelve months after the works, compare South's sales per sq ft of sales floor with its £240 baseline and with the rest of the chain over the same period. If the store closes or trades partially during the works, use a full-year-equivalent figure. **Track total sales as well.** If the sales floor is reduced, the KPI rises mechanically even with no extra sales, so the per-sq-ft figure alone could overstate the improvement.
