# Refurbishment budget: store productivity review, FY2025

## Recommendation

**The refurbishment budget should go to South.**

- **South has the lowest score on the policy KPI.** It achieves **£240 per sq ft** of sales floor. The next-lowest store is West at £280 on a 12-month basis, so South is £40 (14%) below it.
- **South was also lowest in 2024 (£233),** when every store traded all 12 months. This is not a one-year effect.

**One point needs confirming before the budget is committed.** West was closed from January to March 2025 because of a flood. If its 9-month sales are used without adjustment, West scores **£210**. That would make West the lowest store, £30 below South. We read "full-year basis" as requiring a 12-month equivalent, for the reasons below. The policy owner should confirm this reading.

## Sales density (sales per sq ft of sales floor)

| Store | 2025 sales (full-year basis) | Sales floor (sq ft) | 2025 £/sq ft | 2024 £/sq ft | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **South** | **£3.6m** | **15,000** | **£240** | **£233** | **+3%** |
| West | £2.8m* | 10,000 | £280 | £290 | −3%* |
| North | £2.4m | 8,000 | £300 | £288 | +4% |
| East | £1.8m | 6,000 | £300 | £283 | +6% |
| Central | £4.2m | 12,000 | £350 | £333 | +5% |

\*West is annualised from 9 trading months: £2.1m × 12/9 = £2.8m.
- West's reported 2025 figures are £2.1m in sales and £210 per sq ft, a 28% fall on 2024.
- The −3% growth figure assumes January to March is an average quarter for West.

The 2024 densities are rounded to the nearest pound.

## Applying the policy

**Denominator.** The policy specifies sales floor, so gross internal area is not used.
- This matters because selling space ranges from 67% of the store (East) to 86% (Central).
- On gross area, South and East would tie at £200, and unadjusted West would score £175.

**"Full-year basis".** The phrase can be read in two ways:
- **(a)** use each store's reported annual sales as they stand; or
- **(b)** put every store on a 12-month-equivalent basis, adjusting any store that did not trade all year.

We recommend (b), for three reasons:

1. **It fits what the KPI measures.** The KPI measures how productively selling space is used. Under (a), West would receive the budget because of an insured flood closure, not because its space underperforms.
2. **West's own record supports it.** In 2024 West traded all 12 months with the same layout and scored £290, the second-highest in the chain. That figure needs no adjustment and gives no space-productivity reason to refurbish West.
3. **The wording leans towards (b), though not decisively.** The figures are already annual, so "full-year basis" most plausibly addresses stores that did not trade the full year. Reading (a) is still defensible on the wording alone.

**If past practice has been to use reading (a), the policy as written points to West.** In that case the closure should be raised as an exception rather than applied mechanically.

## Robustness

**West's seasonality.** Pro-rata annualising assumes January to March normally brings in 25% of West's annual sales.
- West would only fall below South if that quarter normally brought in **less than 12.5%**.
- That would imply West's full-year sales fell about 17% on 2024, which nothing in the operations note suggests.
- **Data request:** West's monthly sales for 2024 and 2025. These would let us compare April to December year on year, or annualise using West's 2024 monthly pattern.

**Post-reopening trading.** Any slow recovery after reopening is already inside West's £280 and pulls it down. If anything, £280 understates West's normal productivity, which widens its lead over South.

**Consistency over time.** South was lowest in both years:
- In 2024 it was £50 below the next-weakest store (East) and £57 below West.
- In 2025 it was £40 below the next-weakest store (West).
- The gap has narrowed, but South remains clearly lowest.

## Notes for the refurbishment brief

**South's problem is under-used space, not decline.** South has the largest sales floor in the chain but only the second-highest sales, and its sales grew 3% in 2025. The brief should go beyond a cosmetic refresh and consider:
- the layout and the space given to each category; and
- whether part of the floor could be released, converted or re-let.

**Measuring the result.** After the works:
- compare South's density with its £240 baseline and with the chain's average change over the same period;
- report total sales alongside density, because shrinking the floor raises the KPI even if sales don't improve; and
- annualise any closure period the same way West was treated here.

**Chain context.** Reported chain sales fell from £14.4m to £14.1m, entirely because of West's closure. On a full-year-equivalent basis, chain sales rose about 3% to £14.8m. West's recovery should be tracked monthly as a separate trading issue.
