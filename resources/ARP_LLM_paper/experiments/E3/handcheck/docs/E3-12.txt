Grant Application: Chilled Storage for Fresh Food Distribution
Applicant: Hollins Valley Food Bank (registered charity)
To: Ashworth Community Foundation, Community Resilience Fund (capital grants)
Amount requested: £48,600

1. About us
Hollins Valley Food Bank has provided emergency food to households in the valley since 2014. We are run by two part-time paid staff and around 70 volunteers, and we operate from a warehouse in Hollinsford and three distribution sites in Hollinsford, Brackley Bridge and Over Hollins, which open on different days of the week. Referrals come from GPs, schools, Citizens Advice and the local authority's welfare team.

2. The need
Demand has risen sharply. The number of households we supported rose from an average of 410 a week in 2023 to 620 a week in 2025, an increase of 66%. Over the same period our food donations have changed in character. Supermarket surplus schemes now offer us large volumes of fresh produce, dairy and chilled ready meals, but we have no chilled storage beyond three domestic fridges, so we currently decline around 1.2 tonnes of fresh food each week. Our parcels are therefore dominated by tinned and dried goods, and clients, referrers and our own volunteers consistently tell us that families want fresh food, particularly for children.

3. The project
We are seeking funding to:
- install a 12 cubic metre walk-in cold room at our Hollinsford warehouse;
- buy a used refrigerated van, so that chilled food can be collected from supermarket depots and taken to our four distribution sites at safe temperatures;
- upgrade the warehouse electrical supply to support the cold room.

With these in place we could accept the 1.2 tonnes a week we currently decline, about 62 tonnes a year. Using the national food-redistribution conversion of 2,380 meals per tonne, that is around 147,600 additional meals a year, at a one-off capital cost of about 33p per meal in the first year alone.

4. Budget
Item | Cost
Walk-in cold room, supply and installation | £26,400
Refrigerated van (three years old, 40,000 miles) | £18,500
Electrical supply upgrade | £3,700
Total requested | £48,600

Quotes from two suppliers were obtained for the cold room and the electrical work; the lower quote is shown in each case. The van price is from a commercial dealer and includes a 12-month warranty.

5. Running costs
We have obtained estimates for the additional running costs. The cold room will add around £4,200 a year to our electricity bill. Insurance, fuel, servicing and road tax for the van will cost around £6,800 a year. Our 2025 accounts show total income of £96,000, almost all of it restricted to food purchasing and staff costs, an unrestricted surplus of £900, and unrestricted reserves of £5,100.

6. Outcomes and monitoring
We will measure:
- tonnes of fresh and chilled food received and distributed each month;
- the proportion of parcels containing fresh produce (currently under 10%; target 80% within six months);
- client feedback through our quarterly survey.
We will report to the Foundation at six and twelve months.

7. Timetable
The cold room supplier can install within eight weeks of order. We would order on award, with the cold room and van in service by October 2026.

8. Sustainability
This is a one-off capital investment. Once installed, the cold room and van will be sustainable without further grant funding, and will serve the valley for at least ten years. Our partner supermarkets have confirmed in writing that they will continue to offer us surplus fresh food.

9. Why fund us
The Foundation's Community Resilience Fund aims to strengthen local organisations' long-term capacity to meet need. This project does exactly that: a modest capital investment that permanently transforms the quality of the food we can give to families, at a very low cost per meal.
