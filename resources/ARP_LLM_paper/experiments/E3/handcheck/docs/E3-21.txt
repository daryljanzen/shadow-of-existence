Policy Brief: Extending the Heat Pump Top-Up Grant Countywide
Climate and Environment Team, Harnley County Council, June 2026

1. Recommendation
That Cabinet extend the £2,500 local top-up grant for domestic heat pumps, piloted in two districts in 2025, to the whole county from April 2027, with a budget of £2.5 million a year for an initial two years.

2. Background
Domestic heating accounts for around a quarter of the county's carbon emissions, and most homes are heated by gas or oil boilers. The national boiler upgrade grant covers part of the cost of a heat pump, but survey evidence shows the remaining upfront cost is still the main barrier for households, with typical net costs of £4,000 to £7,000 after the national grant.

In January 2025 the Council launched a local top-up of £2,500 per installation in Ashby and Coleton districts, paid on top of the national grant. The top-up was available to owner-occupiers only, with the installation carried out by an accredited installer. The pilot ran for the full calendar year.

3. Results
Table 1. Heat pump installations, by district group
District group | 2024 installations | 2025 installations | Change
Pilot districts (Ashby, Coleton) | 140 | 238 | +70%
Other five districts | 210 | 252 | +20%
County total | 350 | 490 | +40%

Installations in the pilot districts grew by 70%, compared with 20% elsewhere. The top-up therefore caused around 50 percentage points of additional growth, and we expect a similar uplift in the remaining districts once the top-up is available countywide.

The pilot also had broader benefits. In March 2025 the regional installer training centre opened at Coleton College, and the number of accredited installers based in the two pilot districts rose from 9 to 23 over the year. Installers reported that the top-up gave them the confidence to recruit. Wait times for an installation survey in the pilot districts fell from around ten weeks to four.

Customer feedback was positive: of 196 households who completed the follow-up survey, 88% were satisfied or very satisfied with their heat pump, and 91% said the top-up was important in their decision.

4. Value for money
Grant payments in the pilot totalled £595,000 (238 installations at £2,500). Each heat pump replacing a gas or oil boiler saves about 2.1 tonnes of carbon dioxide a year, so the 238 installations will save around 500 tonnes a year, or 7,500 tonnes over a 15-year equipment life. That is a cost to the Council of about £56 per tonne, well below the £120 per tonne threshold the Council uses to appraise climate investments.

5. Proposal
We propose a countywide top-up of £2,500 per installation, with the same eligibility and installer requirements as the pilot, capped at 1,000 grants a year (£2.5 million). On current trends, demand is likely to reach this cap in the first year. Because the pilot was open to landlords as well as owner-occupiers, we expect demand from the private rented sector to remain a significant share of applications, which supports the Council's private-sector housing standards objectives.

Administration would continue to be handled by the existing two-person grants team, with one additional post funded separately from the Council's climate reserve. Grants would be paid directly to installers on completion, as in the pilot, to reduce the risk of fraud.

6. Risks
Installer capacity outside the pilot districts is lower, and waiting lists could lengthen if demand rises sharply. We will work with the training centre to promote accreditation in other districts.
If the national grant is reduced, the local top-up may be insufficient on its own to sustain demand. We will review the scheme annually.

7. Conclusion
The pilot shows that a modest local top-up substantially increases heat pump uptake, at a cost per tonne of carbon well within the Council's threshold. Extending it countywide is a cost-effective way to accelerate the decarbonisation of home heating.
