Internal Audit Memo: Travel and Subsistence Expense Claims, FY2025-26
To: Chief Financial Officer and Audit Committee
From: Internal Audit
Date: 2 June 2026

1. Objective and scope
This audit assessed whether travel and subsistence expense claims comply with the Group Expenses Policy and whether approval controls operate effectively. It covered claims submitted between 1 April 2025 and 31 March 2026, during which 9,860 claims with a total value of £2.31 million were paid.

2. Policy summary
Under the Group Expenses Policy, claims must be supported by itemised receipts (except mileage, which is claimed at the HMRC rate and supported by a journey log), must be approved by the claimant's line manager or a more senior manager, and must not exceed policy limits. The principal limits are £110 per night for hotels outside London, £180 per night in London, and £35 per day for meals when travelling overnight.

3. Approach
We selected a random sample of 240 claims, stratified by business unit, and tested each claim against policy. We also reviewed system approval settings and interviewed finance staff and a selection of approvers.

4. Results
Table 1. Exceptions identified in the sample of 240 claims
Exception type | Claims affected | % of sample
Missing or non-itemised receipt | 22 | 9.2%
Over policy limit (hotel or meals) | 14 | 5.8%
Approved by claimant or by a more junior colleague | 6 | 2.5%
Duplicate claim | 3 | 1.3%
Claims with at least one exception | 38 | 15.8%

Some claims had more than one exception. The total value of exceptions in the sample was £4,180, of which £1,960 related to the six inappropriately approved claims.

The most common exception was spending over policy limits, particularly on hotels. Of the 14 over-limit claims, 11 were hotel stays outside London, with an average rate of £134 per night against the £120 limit. Approvers told us that in several regional cities it is now difficult to find a suitable hotel within the limit, especially midweek. This suggests that the limit itself, rather than claimant behaviour, is the main source of non-compliance.

Inappropriate approvals arose because the expenses system allows any user with approver rights to approve any claim, including their own. Three of the six were self-approved by senior managers.

The three duplicate claims were identified because the same receipt image was submitted twice. All three were refunded once questioned.

5. Mobile expenses app
In September 2025, Sales and Field Engineering adopted the new mobile expenses app, which lets staff photograph receipts at the point of purchase. Claims from these two units had an exception rate of 9%, compared with 21% across the rest of the business. The app clearly reduces exceptions, and rolling it out to all units should bring the Group exception rate down to around 9%. Most claims from Sales and Field Engineering are mileage claims, which do not require receipts; claims from other units are mostly hotel, rail and meal claims.

6. Recommendations
6.1 (High priority) Reconfigure the expenses system so that claimants cannot approve their own claims and claims can only be approved by someone at or above the claimant's line manager in the reporting hierarchy. Owner: Head of Finance Systems.
6.2 (Medium) Review the hotel limit outside London against current market rates, with a view to raising it. Owner: Group Financial Controller.
6.3 (Medium) Roll out the mobile expenses app to all business units by December 2026. Owner: Head of Finance Systems.
6.4 (Low) Introduce automated duplicate-receipt detection. Owner: Head of Finance Systems.

7. Overall opinion
Limited assurance. The approval control weakness is significant and should be fixed immediately. Otherwise, the main source of exceptions is an outdated hotel limit rather than poor compliance, and the mobile app offers a proven route to reducing exceptions further.
