Policy Brief: Scaling High-Dose Tutoring in Year 7 Mathematics
Riverside County Education Authority, Policy Unit, January 2026

Summary
In 2024-25 the Authority funded a pilot of high-dose mathematics tutoring for Year 7 pupils in six urban secondary schools. Pupils who received tutoring made substantially more progress than a randomly selected comparison group. This brief recommends extending the programme to all 42 secondary schools in the county from September 2026, at an estimated annual cost of £2.9 million.

The pilot
Tutoring was delivered in groups of three pupils, three one-hour sessions a week, for 30 weeks, during the school day. Tutors were undergraduate and postgraduate students from Riverside University, recruited, trained and paid £16 an hour through the University's Education Department. Pupils were eligible if they had scored below the expected standard in the end-of-primary mathematics assessment.

Of 480 eligible pupils in the six schools, 360 were offered places by lottery, because tutor capacity was limited; the remaining 120 formed the comparison group. Because places were allocated at random, the comparison is a fair one: the two groups had near-identical prior attainment, attendance and demographic profiles at the start of the year.

Results
Progress was measured using the standardised Year 7 mathematics assessment, taken in September 2024 and again in June 2025.

Group | Pupils assessed in June | Mean score, Sept 2024 | Mean score, June 2025 | Gain
Tutored | 342 | 88.0 | 98.6 | 10.6
Comparison | 114 | 87.8 | 93.4 | 5.6

Tutored pupils gained 10.6 points against 5.6 for the comparison group, a gain 89% larger. The difference of five points is equivalent to roughly four months of additional progress, and is in line with the effects reported in the national evidence base for small-group tutoring. Attendance at sessions averaged 87%, and 95% of the pupils offered a place completed the programme.

Teachers in all six schools reported that tutored pupils were more confident in lessons, and four of the six head teachers have asked for the programme to continue.

Proposal
We propose to extend the programme to all eligible Year 7 pupils in the county's 42 secondary schools from September 2026, retaining the design that proved effective: the one-to-one format used in the pilot, three sessions a week for 30 weeks, delivered in school hours by trained university-student tutors. Riverside University has agreed in principle to expand its recruitment, and its Education Department will continue to train and pay tutors.

Around 3,100 pupils a year meet the eligibility criterion across the 42 schools. Of these schools, 26 are in the county's rural districts, between 40 and 75 miles from the University campus; the remaining 16 are in or near Riverside itself, within a short bus ride of the campus, as were all six pilot schools.

Cost
The pilot cost £750 per tutored pupil: £480 in tutor pay (90 hours of tutoring per group, shared among three pupils, at £16 an hour), £220 for university coordination and training, and £50 for materials and assessment. Applying the pilot unit cost to 3,100 pupils gives an annual cost of £2.9 million.

This compares favourably with the Authority's current spending on Year 8 and Year 9 catch-up classes, which cost £1,100 per pupil and have shown no measurable effect on attainment in the last two internal evaluations. We propose that the catch-up budget be wound down over two years and the savings redirected to tutoring.

Risks and mitigations
Tutor quality. All tutors will complete the University's 12-hour training course and be observed twice per term by a school mathematics lead.
Timetabling. Sessions will be scheduled during form time and one weekly mathematics lesson, as in the pilot, so pupils do not miss other subjects.
Evaluation. The Policy Unit will continue to track progress using the standardised assessment and will report to Committee annually.

Recommendation
The pilot provides rigorous, randomised evidence that high-dose tutoring substantially accelerates progress for pupils who start secondary school behind in mathematics. The model is proven and its costs are known. We recommend that the Committee approve £2.9 million a year from September 2026 to extend tutoring to all 42 secondary schools, funded in part by winding down the catch-up class budget.
