Pricing Proposal: Introducing an Off-Peak Membership
Pulse Fitness, Commercial Team, August 2026

1. Proposal
We propose introducing an off-peak membership at £29 a month from 1 October 2026, alongside our existing standard membership at £42 a month. Off-peak members would have access before 4 pm on weekdays and all day at weekends, with full access to the gym floor and to classes running in those hours.

2. Background
Pulse operates four clubs with 9,600 standard members. Membership has been flat for 18 months, while two budget gyms have opened within our catchments, both offering memberships at £22 to £25 a month. Our clubs are busy between 5 pm and 8 pm on weekdays, when the gym floor often runs at or near capacity, but quiet during weekday daytimes, when typical utilisation is around 30%.

Access-control data for May to July 2026 show that 31% of our current standard members visit exclusively in what would be the off-peak window, and a further 22% visit mostly off-peak.

3. Market evidence
A survey of 640 non-members living within two miles of our clubs, conducted in June, found that 19% would "definitely" or "probably" join at £29 a month for off-peak access. The most interested groups were retirees, shift workers, parents of school-age children and students.

Four of our six closest competitors already offer an off-peak or daytime membership, priced between £25 and £32.

4. Classes and retention
Retention is the other half of our commercial challenge. Monthly churn among members who attend at least one class a week is 2.1%, against 4.8% among members who do not attend classes. Classes clearly keep members loyal, so we will add six extra daytime classes per club each week to support the new membership, which should lower churn among off-peak members towards the 2.1% level. The additional classes would cost £18,700 a month across the four clubs in instructor fees.

5. Revenue projection
We expect to recruit 1,200 new off-peak members in the first year, about 300 per club, which is consistent with the survey response and the size of our catchments. At £29 a month, this is £34,800 a month, or £417,600 a year, in new membership revenue. After the cost of the additional classes (£224,400 a year), net additional revenue is £193,200 a year.

Because daytime utilisation is low, the additional members can be accommodated without investment in equipment or space.

6. Operational changes
Access control will be updated so that off-peak members' cards do not open the turnstiles outside their access window. Off-peak members will have weekday access until 5 pm, giving those finishing early shifts or school pick-ups time to train. Members will be able to upgrade to standard membership at any time, and to switch between membership types with one month's notice.

7. Risks
Some standard members may perceive the off-peak price as unfair. We will communicate the change as an extension of choice. Competitor responses are possible; the budget gyms already price below our proposed off-peak rate, so we do not expect a price war.

8. Staff consultation
Club managers and front-of-house teams at all four clubs were consulted in July. Managers supported the proposal, noting that daytime classes are often under-filled and that a daytime membership would help build a regular community at quieter times. Front-of-house staff asked for clear signage and app messaging about access hours, which we will provide before launch.

9. Recommendation
The off-peak membership fills spare daytime capacity, responds to competitor offers and targets clearly identified customer groups. We project net additional revenue of around £193,000 a year and recommend launch on 1 October 2026.
