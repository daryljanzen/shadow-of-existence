Review of the Graduate Hiring Process: 2025 Intake
Talent Acquisition, Halden Engineering, January 2026

1. Purpose
This review assesses the 2025 graduate recruitment cycle, which opened on 1 September 2024 and closed with final offers on 30 April 2025, and recommends how sourcing budget should be allocated for the 2026 intake. The 2026 hiring target is 100 graduates, against 95 hired in 2025.

2. The process
Candidates complete an online application and numerical reasoning test, followed by a 30-minute phone screen. Those who pass attend a one-day assessment centre with a group exercise and a technical interview, after which offers are made within five working days. Median time from application to offer was 47 days, slightly better than the 52 days recorded for the 2024 intake.

Candidates were sourced through three channels: careers fairs at nine partner universities, paid listings on two graduate job boards, and the employee referral scheme, which pays a £1,000 bonus for each referred graduate who accepts an offer.

3. Funnel by channel
Table 1. 2025 intake by source
Source | Applications | Interviewed | Offers | Accepted | Left within 12 months
University careers fairs | 1,150 | 138 | 46 | 39 | 3
Job boards | 3,400 | 204 | 51 | 34 | 11
Employee referrals | 420 | 84 | 28 | 22 | 2
Total | 4,970 | 426 | 125 | 95 | 16

Overall, 8.6% of applicants reached the assessment centre, 29% of those interviewed received offers and 76% of offers were accepted. Job boards generated by far the largest volume of applications, though a lower proportion of them progressed: 6% of job-board applicants reached interview, compared with 12% from careers fairs and 20% from referrals.

Employee referrals produced the highest offer acceptance rate of any channel, which we attribute to the realistic picture of the company that referrers give their contacts before they apply.

4. Cost by channel
Table 2. Sourcing cost by channel
Source | Channel cost | Hires | Cost per hire
University careers fairs | £58,500 | 39 | £1,500
Job boards | £20,400 | 34 | £600
Employee referrals | £22,000 | 22 | £1,000

Careers-fair costs include stand fees, travel and accommodation for staff, and printed materials. Job-board costs are annual listing fees. Referral costs are bonuses paid.

Across all channels we spent £100,900 for 95 hires, an average of £1,240 per hire, above the sector benchmark of £1,150 per graduate hire reported in the Institute of Student Employers' latest survey. Bringing our cost per hire below the benchmark is therefore a priority for 2026.

5. Candidate experience and onboarding
The post-process candidate survey (312 responses) rated the assessment centre positively, with 84% describing it as fair. The main complaint was the gap between the phone screen and the assessment centre invitation, which averaged 16 days. We will introduce self-scheduling for assessment-centre slots to reduce this.

Graduates spend their first year on a structured rotation programme. Learning and Development estimates the cost of a graduate leaving during the first year, including programme costs, supervisor time and re-recruitment, at around £18,000 per leaver.

6. Recommendations for 2026
6.1 Reduce careers-fair attendance from nine universities to three, retaining only the three that produced the most hires, and cut the fair budget from £58,500 to about £19,500.
6.2 Reallocate the £39,000 saved to additional job-board listings and sponsored placement on a third board. Job boards deliver hires at £600 each, less than half the cost of careers fairs, so shifting volume towards them will allow us to reach the 2026 target of 100 hires while bringing average cost per hire well below the sector benchmark.
6.3 Keep the referral scheme unchanged.
6.4 Introduce self-scheduling for assessment centres and set a target of ten days from phone screen to invitation.

7. Conclusion
The 2025 process worked: we came close to target, candidates rated the assessment fairly and time-to-offer improved. The main weakness is cost efficiency. Rebalancing the sourcing mix towards job boards, our lowest-cost channel, addresses that directly without changing the selection process that candidates and hiring managers value.
