Incident Report: Partial Racking Collapse, Aisle 14
Daventry Distribution Centre, Health, Safety and Operations, 18 September 2026

1. Summary of the incident
At 02:47 on 3 September 2026, a reach truck reversing out of aisle 14 struck the front upright of bay 14-22. The upright buckled, and two adjacent bays of pallet racking (14-22 and 14-23) partially collapsed, bringing down 46 pallets of ambient grocery stock from levels 2 to 5. The aisle was unoccupied apart from the driver, who was uninjured. The area was cordoned off within ten minutes and the night shift manager stopped all work in aisles 13 to 15.

2. Immediate response
A racking inspector attended at 07:30 and confirmed that the damage was confined to the two bays. Damaged stock was removed under supervision over the following two days, and the replacement uprights and beams were fitted on 9 September. The aisle reopened on 10 September after a full inspection.

3. Losses
Of the 46 pallets brought down, 18 were written off entirely, at an average stock value of £2,400 per pallet, a loss of £43,200. The remaining 28 pallets were partially damaged, with an average loss after re-sorting of £650 per pallet, a further £21,800. Racking repair and inspection cost £14,800. Total direct cost of the incident was therefore £79,800, excluding lost productivity in aisles 13 to 15 for the six days the area was restricted.

4. Investigation
The driver, employed through our labour agency, had worked at the site for five weeks and held a valid reach-truck licence. CCTV shows the truck reversing at an angle and at a speed the investigating manager judged to be within site limits, before the rear of the truck contacted the upright. The driver stated he was looking over his shoulder at the time but that the upright was obscured by a pallet overhanging the adjacent bay.

The site racking inspection log shows that the upright at 14-22 had been recorded as amber damage (a dent requiring repair within four weeks, with the bay offloaded if not repaired) during the inspection of 2 July, nine weeks before the collapse. No repair or offloading had been carried out. The log also shows 14 other amber items across the site that are past their four-week repair deadline.

5. Protection measures
In 2025 we fitted steel rack-end and upright protectors to aisles 1 to 8. Since then, aisles 1 to 8 have logged 3 reported racking strikes, compared with 19 in aisles 9 to 16. Protectors clearly prevent racking strikes, and fitting them to aisles 9 to 16 would be expected to reduce strikes there by a similar proportion. Aisles 1 to 8 hold slow-moving reserve stock and account for about a fifth of reach-truck movements on site.

6. Root cause
The investigation concludes that the root cause was driver error: an inexperienced agency driver reversing without a clear line of sight. Contributing factors were the overhanging pallet in bay 14-21 and the absence of upright protectors in aisle 14.

7. Actions
7.1 All agency reach-truck drivers to complete an additional site-specific practical assessment, including reversing in aisles, before working unsupervised. Owner: Training Manager. Due: 30 October.
7.2 Fit upright protectors to aisles 9 to 16, at an estimated cost of £22,000. Owner: Facilities Manager. Due: 30 November.
7.3 Reinforce the pallet overhang check in the put-away procedure and include it in team briefings. Owner: Night Shift Manager. Due: immediately.
7.4 Share this report with the labour agency and request a review of how drivers are assessed before placement.

8. Conclusion
This was a serious incident that could have caused injury. With retraining of agency drivers and protectors in the remaining aisles, we are satisfied that the risk of recurrence has been addressed and that normal operations can continue.
