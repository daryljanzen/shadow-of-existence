Menu Pricing Review: Spring 2026
The Copper Kettle, prepared by the General Manager for the owners, March 2026

1. Purpose
This paper reviews main-course pricing and menu mix and proposes changes to take effect with the spring menu on 1 April 2026. Our overall gross margin on food, across all courses, has slipped from 70% to 67% over the past year as ingredient costs have risen faster than our prices, and we last changed main-course prices in September 2024.

2. Trading pattern
The Copper Kettle is a 64-cover restaurant open for lunch and dinner from Wednesday to Sunday. We serve an average of 1,050 main courses a week, with Friday and Saturday dinner accounting for just under half. Our customers are mainly local, with a strong repeat base: our booking system shows that around 60% of bookings come from guests who have dined with us in the past six months.

3. Competitor prices
We checked menus at the five restaurants we regard as our closest competitors. Their fish and chips ranges from £16.95 to £19.00, burgers from £15.50 to £17.50 and sirloin steak from £27.00 to £32.00. Our prices for these three dishes are the lowest in the group.

4. Supplier costs
Our meat supplier has given notice that beef prices will rise by 12% from 1 April 2026, reflecting national cattle prices. Other supplier prices are expected to be broadly stable over the spring. Fish prices have been volatile but are currently close to their 2025 average.

5. Proposals
5.1 Price increases. Raise fish and chips to £17.50, the beef burger to £16.00 and the sirloin steak to £28.00. All three would remain below the midpoint of the competitor range for that dish.

5.2 Remove the mushroom risotto. Risotto has the weakest margin on the menu and the lowest sales, so it is not earning its place. We propose replacing it with a seasonal vegetarian special at the same price.

5.3 Projected effect. If volumes hold, the price increases add £1.00 per plate on 310 fish and chips, £1.00 on 260 burgers and £2.00 on 120 steaks, a total of £810 a week in additional gross profit, or £42,120 a year. Because our prices remain competitive and our customer base is loyal, we expect volumes to hold. The increases would lift main-course gross margin to about 71%, helping restore our 70% overall target.

6. Communication
Menus will be reprinted for 1 April. Staff will be briefed on the changes, and we will not draw attention to the increases on the menu. We will monitor plates sold per dish weekly for the first eight weeks against the January to February baseline.

7. Risks
Some regular guests may notice the increases. However, the last price change in 2024 had no visible effect on bookings, and the new prices remain competitive. Weekday lunch trade is more price-sensitive than dinner, but it represents a small share of main-course sales.

8. Recommendation
We recommend that the owners approve the three price increases and the replacement of the mushroom risotto from 1 April 2026. Together these changes add about £42,000 a year in gross profit, improve our margin and keep us competitively priced, across our six trading days a week.
