Feasibility Study: Weekly Separate Food Waste Collection
Waste and Environmental Services, Thornbury District Council, October 2026

1. Purpose
Councils in England are required to introduce weekly separate food waste collections for households. This study assesses how Thornbury can deliver the service district-wide from April 2027, based on a six-month pilot, and estimates its costs and savings.

2. The pilot
From January to June 2026, 4,000 households in three wards received a 23-litre outdoor caddy, a 5-litre kitchen caddy and a supply of compostable liners, with collection every week on the same day as their existing bin collection. The pilot wards were chosen to include a mix of houses and flats, and of urban and village streets.

Table 1. Pilot participation by housing type (average weekly set-out, Jan-Jun 2026)
Housing type | Households | Participating households | Participation rate | Food waste per participating household per week
Houses | 3,200 | 2,112 | 66% | 1.9 kg
Flats | 800 | 168 | 21% | 1.4 kg
All | 4,000 | 2,280 | 57% | 1.9 kg

Participation was broadly similar across housing types, which gives us confidence that the service can be rolled out uniformly across the district without a separate approach for flats. Residents' feedback was positive, with 81% of surveyed pilot households rating the service good or very good. Contamination with packaging and other non-food items was low, at under 2% by weight in spot checks carried out at the treatment plant, and missed-collection reports fell steadily after the first month.

3. District-wide projection
Thornbury has 52,000 households, of which about 20% are flats. Applying the pilot's 64% participation rate and 1.9 kg per participating household per week across the district gives about 3,290 tonnes of food waste a year.

4. Treatment
Collected food waste would be taken to Brookfield anaerobic digestion (AD) plant, 14 miles from our depot, at a gate fee of £38 per tonne. Food waste that stays in the residual bin is incinerated at a cost of £112 per tonne. Every tonne diverted therefore saves £74, or around £243,000 a year on the projected tonnage. Brookfield has confirmed that it can offer the Council up to 1,800 tonnes a year of contracted capacity until its planned expansion is completed in 2029. The next nearest AD plant is 46 miles away.

5. Costs
Table 2. Annual cost of district-wide service
Item | Annual cost
Vehicles: six food waste vehicles at £165,000 each, depreciated over seven years | £141,400
Crews: six crews of two at £34,000 per operative | £408,000
Fuel and maintenance | £96,000
Caddies, liners and communications (annualised) | £88,000
Total | £733,400

Against the disposal saving of £243,000, the net annual cost is about £490,000. The Government has indicated that new-burdens funding will be provided for the service; our provisional allocation is £520,000 a year, which would cover the net cost in full.

6. Implementation
Vehicles must be ordered by December 2026 for delivery in March 2027. Caddies would be delivered to all households in March 2027, with a leaflet and a three-month supply of liners. Collections would start in the week of 5 April 2027. Crews will be recruited from February 2027 and trained on the new vehicles during March. A dedicated phone line and web page will handle caddy requests and missed collections during the first three months of the service.

7. Recommendation
The pilot demonstrates strong participation and resident satisfaction, and the service can be delivered on a uniform basis across the district. With treatment at Brookfield and new-burdens funding, the service is fully funded. We recommend that Cabinet approve the district-wide roll-out from April 2027 and authorise the vehicle order.
