Pricing Proposal: Weekend Rate Restructure
Harbourside Hotel, Revenue Management, April 2026

1. Proposal
From 1 June 2026 we propose to (a) raise the Best Available Rate (BAR) for Friday and Saturday nights from £145 to £165, and (b) introduce a two-night minimum for any stay that includes a Saturday night. Midweek rates are unchanged.

2. Context
Harbourside is a 112-room four-star hotel on the quay, with a leisure-led weekend business and a corporate-led midweek business. Our weekend BAR is £20 to £35 below the three comparable hotels in our competitive set, whose weekend rates currently range from £165 to £180. Weekend occupancy has recovered strongly since 2023 and we believe we are leaving money on the table.

Table 1. Weekend (Friday and Saturday) occupancy by month, 2025
Jan 62% | Feb 66% | Mar 74% | Apr 80% | May 85% | Jun 81%
Jul 89% | Aug 93% | Sep 88% | Oct 82% | Nov 72% | Dec 78%

Average weekend occupancy across 2025 was 79%. July and August are our strongest months, driven by the harbour festival and the school summer holidays, with September also benefiting from late-season visitors.

3. Evidence on price sensitivity
In the last week of June 2025 we raised the weekend BAR by £10, from £135 to the current £145. Weekend occupancy in July rose to 89%, from 81% in June. This tells us that our weekend guests are not price sensitive at current levels; if anything, the higher rate appears to have reinforced our positioning as a quality option, and we expect the further increase to be absorbed in the same way.

This is consistent with feedback from our guest satisfaction surveys, where weekend guests rate "value for money" at 8.4 out of 10, the highest score among our competitive set.

4. Two-night minimum
Saturday nights are our most constrained inventory: we sold out on 31 Saturdays in 2025. Many of these guests stay only one night, leaving rooms empty on Friday or Sunday either side. A two-night minimum for stays including a Saturday will convert single-night Saturday stays into two-night stays and smooth occupancy across the weekend.

Our booking data show that weddings at Quayside Hall, the venue next door, account for around 38% of our Saturday-night room bookings. Wedding guests typically book as a block through the couple, arrive on the Saturday afternoon and check out on the Sunday morning.

5. Revenue impact
Across our 124 rooms, 104 weekend nights a year and average weekend occupancy of 79%, a £20 increase in BAR is worth about £204,000 a year in additional room revenue, before any gain from longer stays. We have assumed weekend occupancy is maintained at 2025 levels, which we consider prudent given the evidence above.

The two-night minimum should add further revenue through additional Friday and Sunday nights. We have not quantified this but expect it to be material, particularly in shoulder months.

6. Implementation and monitoring
The new rates will be loaded into the channel manager on 15 May for stays from 1 June, giving online travel agencies two weeks' notice. Corporate contracted rates and group rates already contracted will be honoured. We will review pick-up weekly against the same weeks in 2025 and report to the General Manager monthly. If weekend occupancy for any month falls more than five points below 2025, we will review the rate.

7. Recommendation
The evidence shows that our weekend guests are not price sensitive at current rates and that we are priced well below our competitive set. We recommend approval of the £165 weekend BAR and the two-night Saturday minimum from 1 June 2026, delivering at least £204,000 of additional annual room revenue.
