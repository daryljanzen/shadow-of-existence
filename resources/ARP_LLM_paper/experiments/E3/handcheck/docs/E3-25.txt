Project Review: Delay to Practical Completion, Riverside Primary School Extension
Capital Projects Team, Mereford Council, November 2026

1. Purpose
This review explains why the Riverside Primary School extension reached practical completion ten weeks late, sets out the commercial position with the contractor, and draws lessons for the Council's capital programme.

2. Project summary
The project added a four-classroom teaching block and a new hall to Riverside Primary School. The contract was let on a single-stage design and build basis to Hartwell Construction in November 2025, with a contract sum of £3.84 million and a planned practical completion date of 14 August 2026, so that the new block would be ready for the September intake. Practical completion was certified on 23 October 2026, 70 days late. Pupils were accommodated in temporary classrooms for the first half of the autumn term.

3. Causes of delay
The contractor's delay analysis was reviewed by the Council's independent project manager, who agreed the following allocation of critical-path delay.

Table 1. Critical-path delay by cause
Cause | Days of delay | Responsibility under the contract
Unforeseen ground conditions (old foundations found during excavation) | 21 | Council (relevant event)
Late design changes requested by the school (extra fit-out to two classrooms) | 28 | Council (relevant event)
Delayed delivery of structural steel | 14 | Contractor
Exceptionally wet weather beyond the contract allowance | 7 | Neutral (time but no money)
Total | 70 |

The largest single cause of delay was the unforeseen ground conditions. The site investigation commissioned at feasibility stage used only four trial pits, none of which was located in the footprint of the new hall, where the old foundations were found.

The late design changes arose because the school's requirements for the two classrooms changed after the contract was signed, when the school decided to use them as a specialist resource base. The steel delay arose from the supplier's mill schedule, and the contractor did not identify an alternative supplier.

4. Commercial position
The contract provides for liquidated damages of £2,500 for each day of delay for which the contractor is responsible. The contractor is responsible for 14 days, so the Council will deduct £45,000 in liquidated damages from the final account. The contractor is entitled to an extension of time, but not to additional payment, for the 7 days of exceptional weather.

The final account has been agreed at £4.21 million, an overrun of £370,000 (9.6%) on the contract sum, mainly due to the ground works and the design changes.

5. Comparison with other projects
We compared Riverside with the Council's other education capital projects completed since 2022. The six projects let on a two-stage tender basis finished on average three weeks late, while the five let on a single-stage basis, including Riverside, finished on average nine weeks late. Two-stage tendering therefore reduces delay, and the Council should adopt it as the default procurement route for education projects. The two-stage projects were all refurbishments of existing buildings with budgets under £1.5 million; the single-stage projects were all new-build extensions.

6. Lessons and recommendations
6.1 Adopt two-stage tendering as the default for education capital projects over £1 million.
6.2 Require site investigations to cover the full footprint of any new building, with trial pits or boreholes at each proposed foundation zone.
6.3 Freeze client requirements at contract signature, with any later changes approved by the Director of Education and costed for time and money before instruction.
6.4 Require contractors to identify alternative suppliers for long-lead structural items at tender stage.

7. Conclusion
The delay at Riverside was caused principally by unforeseen ground conditions arising from an inadequate site investigation, compounded by steel supply problems and late changes. Adopting two-stage tendering, together with the specific measures above, would reduce the risk of a recurrence on future projects.
