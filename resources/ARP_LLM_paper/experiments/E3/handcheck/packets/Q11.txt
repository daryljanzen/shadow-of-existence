REVIEW:
# Review: Post-Implementation Review of Automated Triage for Home Claims

## Overall assessment

The operational gains may well be real. But the review has an arithmetic error in its headline figure, a contradiction about the current threshold, and a before/after comparison that is not like-for-like. Most seriously, it does not engage with the fraud evidence it presents. Its own data shows fraud detection falling sharply after launch. The recommendation would extend fast-tracking into the claim band the SIU identifies as the highest fraud risk. As written, the document does not support recommendation 6.1.

## Major issues

### 1. The fall in fraud referrals is reported but never discussed

- Table 1 shows fraud referrals dropping from 6.1 to 3.9 per 1,000 claims, a fall of about 36%.
- Section 2 says fast-tracked claims are no longer sampled by fraud screening.
- The likeliest explanation is that we stopped looking at 38% of claims, not that fraud fell.
- The SIU says claims under £1,500 accounted for 14% of confirmed fraud. That segment is now largely unscreened.

The review does not measure fraud or leakage in fast-tracked claims. That means:
- The £492k net saving takes no account of any increase in fraudulent or inflated payouts.
- The financial case is incomplete.

### 2. Recommendation 6.1 moves into the highest-risk band without addressing it

Section 5 states that claims of £1,500–£3,000 are:
- 22% of volume, but
- 41% of confirmed fraud,
- with false or inflated contents claims the most common type.

That is nearly twice their share of volume. Yet Section 5 is never mentioned in the recommendations or conclusion. Several claims in the review don't hold up against this:

- **"Same rules, same checks" is a weakness, not a reassurance.** The rules were designed and back-tested for claims under £1,500. They have not been shown to work in a band with a very different fraud profile.
- **No mitigating controls are proposed.** There is no reinstated fraud sampling, post-payment audit, or contents-specific rule.
- **The £409k estimate ignores expected fraud losses.** A band containing 41% of confirmed fraud could plausibly offset much or all of that saving. That needs to be quantified before proceeding.

### 3. Threshold inconsistency

- Sections 1–2 say the threshold is £1,500.
- Recommendation 6.1 says "raise the fast-track threshold from £1,000 to £3,000".

One of these is wrong. If the live threshold is actually £1,000, the 38% share, the back-test and the extension estimate all need re-checking.

### 4. The settlement-time headline is miscalculated

- A fall of 4.4 days from 14.2 days is **31%**, not 38%.
- 38% is the fast-track share, so the figures appear to have been conflated.
- The "38% improvement" in the author's summary should be corrected.

### 5. The periods are not like-for-like

- October–February is winter; April–August is spring and summer.
- Home claims differ by season. Winter brings storm, escape-of-water and frozen-pipe claims, which are typically more complex, slower to settle and lower in satisfaction.
- Part of the improvement in settlement time, satisfaction and handler hours may be seasonal.
- Excluding March removes transition noise but does nothing about seasonality.

The analysis should:
- compare against the same months of 2025, or
- adjust for claim mix by peril.

## Further weaknesses

### Settlement time is not broken down

- An all-claims average falls mechanically when 38% of claims are paid within 48 hours.
- The review should show settlement times for handler-managed claims separately, so we can see whether that service held up or got worse.

### Satisfaction is not segmented

- There is no split between fast-tracked and handled customers, and no response rates.
- "Largest movement we have recorded in a single year" compares different time spans, and seasonality may explain part of it.

### "Declined by the automated checks" contradicts Section 2

- Section 2 says flagged claims are passed to a handler.
- The complaints paragraph says customers were *declined* by the automated checks without a reason.

If the system is declining claims automatically, that is:
- a significant undisclosed feature, and
- a conduct and Consumer Duty issue, not just a communication fix.

This needs clarifying. Also, "few" complaints should be given as a number.

### The back-test validates volume, not accuracy

- Predicting 36% of claims fast-tracked and seeing 38% in live use shows the rules route claims at the expected rate.
- It says nothing about whether the right claims were fast-tracked, including whether fraudulent ones got through.
- "No changes needed after go-live" is not evidence of correct performance when nobody is checking fast-tracked outcomes.

### The extension estimate is not explained

- It is unclear how fast-tracking rises from 38% to 55%. That would mean fast-tracking about three-quarters of the £1,500–£3,000 band, far above the current rate.
- Implied hours saved per claim rise from about 1.0 to about 1.5. This may be reasonable, but it should be stated and justified.

### Indemnity spend is missing

- The review reports handling costs only.
- It does not show whether average payouts on comparable claims changed once handler scrutiny was removed.

### Savings are presented as cash

- Fewer handler hours partly went into absorbing higher volumes.
- Only the reduction in agency staff is clearly a cash saving.

## Suggested changes before any decision on 6.1

1. Correct the 38% figure and resolve the £1,000 vs £1,500 threshold discrepancy.
2. Redo the comparison against the same months a year earlier, or adjust for peril mix.
3. Run a retrospective fraud audit on a sample of fast-tracked claims to estimate leakage. Net the result against the savings.
4. Reinstate fraud sampling of fast-tracked claims, whatever happens with the threshold.
5. If an extension is still pursued, design and back-test rules specifically for the £1,500–£3,000 band, with particular attention to contents claims. Consider a staged step (for example, to £2,000) with fraud metrics as explicit go/no-go criteria.
6. Clarify whether the system declines claims, and address the conduct implications.
7. Split settlement time and satisfaction by route (fast-track vs handler).

The fixes for declined customers and the quarterly rule reviews (6.2 and 6.3) are sensible. 6.3 should explicitly include fraud and leakage metrics.

  O3. Satisfaction is not segmented by route, response rates are not given, and the 'largest movement in a single year' comparison is questionable
