REVIEW:
# Review: Weekly Separate Food Waste Collection Feasibility Study

**Verdict: not ready for Cabinet.** The recommendation is clearly worded, but its three supporting claims are contradicted by the study's own figures:

- the service can be uniform across housing types
- the service is fully funded
- treatment is local

Each needs fixing before the paper goes to decision-makers.

## 1. Participation was not similar across housing types

Table 1 shows houses at **66%** and flats at **21%**, roughly a threefold gap. Flats households also produce less (1.4 kg vs 1.9 kg). Yet Section 2 says participation was "broadly similar across housing types", and the uniform roll-out rests on that statement. Your summary repeats it ("participation held up across houses and flats").

The pilot supports the opposite conclusion: flats likely need a different approach, such as communal bins, different caddy placement or targeted communications. With about 10,400 flats in the district, this is a design decision Cabinet should see, not one to assume away.

## 2. The projection uses a participation rate the pilot didn't produce

Section 3 applies **64%** participation. The pilot's overall rate was **57%** (2,280 / 4,000). The pilot was 20% flats, the same as the district, so 57% is the right figure if you project at district level. Also, the blended yield is 1.86 kg, not 1.9.

| Basis | Annual tonnage |
|---|---|
| As stated (64% × 1.9 kg) | ~3,290 t |
| Pilot overall rate (57% × 1.9 kg) | ~2,930 t |
| By housing type (houses 66% × 1.9 kg; flats 21% × 1.4 kg) | ~2,870 t |

The by-housing-type figure is the most defensible. It cuts projected tonnage by about 13%, and the disposal saving falls with it, to about £212,000.

## 3. Brookfield cannot take the projected tonnage

Brookfield offers **up to 1,800 t/year until 2029**. Even the corrected projection of about 2,870 t exceeds this by roughly 1,000 t a year. The paper doesn't say where that tonnage goes. The options are:

- **Haul it to the next plant, 46 miles away.** The gate fee is unknown, and there are extra vehicle and crew time costs. This also undermines the "local treatment" claim.
- **Leave it in the residual bin.** In that case the diversion saving isn't realised.

If only 1,800 t is diverted at £74/t, the saving is **about £133,000**, not £243,000.

## 4. The service is not shown to be fully funded

- **The margin is thin.** On the paper's own numbers, the margin is £30,000 (£520,000 against £490,400).
- **Correcting for the capacity cap removes it.** The net cost becomes about £733,400 − £133,000 = **£600,000**, which is roughly **£80,000/year over** the allocation. The cost of treating the excess tonnage elsewhere comes on top of that.
- **The funding is not confirmed.** It is described as "provisional" and something the Government "has indicated". The paper should say what is confirmed, for how many years, and what happens if it is lower.
- **The cost lines may be incomplete.** Your summary says every cost is shown, but the paper doesn't mention:
  - absence and holiday cover for crews
  - supervision and management
  - employer on-costs (it's unclear whether £34,000 includes them)
  - depot or parking space for six vehicles
  - the caddy request and missed-collection phone line in Section 6

  These may be included elsewhere, but the paper should say so.

The arithmetic within Table 2 is correct (£733,400; vehicles £990,000 / 7 = £141,400).

## 5. Timetable and other points

- **Vehicle order deadline.** Vehicles must be ordered by December 2026, and it is already mid-November. Cabinet needs to see the funding and capacity risks before committing to that spend.
- **Missing procurement steps.** The timetable covers vehicles but not caddy procurement for 52,000 households, liner supply, or signing the Brookfield contract and arranging a second outlet.
- **Statutory deadline.** The paper says councils are "required" to introduce the service but doesn't give the statutory deadline or explain why April 2027 is compliant. If Thornbury is relying on a transitional arrangement, say so.
- **Satisfaction figure.** The 81% rating lacks a sample size and response rate. Respondents are likely skewed toward participating households.

## Suggested changes before submission

1. Remove the "broadly similar" claim. Present the flats gap and either propose a flats-specific approach or explain why uniform service is still appropriate.
2. Re-run the tonnage by housing type, giving about 2,870 t.
3. Address the Brookfield cap explicitly, covering where about 1,000 t a year goes until 2029 and at what cost.
4. Restate the net cost and funding position with the corrected figures, give the funding status and duration, and show the likely shortfall of about £80,000 or more.
5. Add a short risk section covering capacity, funding confirmation, flats participation and the vehicle lead time.
6. Reword the recommendation. For example, Cabinet could approve the roll-out and vehicle order subject to securing additional AD capacity and confirmed funding, and note the projected shortfall.

The pilot itself is good evidence and a sound basis for proceeding. The problem is that the paper draws stronger conclusions than its data supports.
