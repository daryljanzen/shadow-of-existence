REVIEW:
# Review: Vendor Comparison, Standard Parcel Delivery Contract

The randomised trial is a strong design, and the price arithmetic in Section 4 is correct. But the paper's own data contradict the recommendation. ParcelLine fails two of the four stated requirements. Once damage and loss costs are included, it is the most expensive option, not the cheapest. The customer-contact advantage is confounded.

## 1. ParcelLine fails the on-time requirement

Section 2 sets a minimum of **95%** on-time delivery. Table 1 shows ParcelLine at **93.1%**. The sentence "On-time delivery was above 95% for all three carriers" is therefore false, and so is the recommendation's claim that ParcelLine "met our service requirements in the trial." Only SwiftDrop (96.2%) and Meridian (97.5%) meet this threshold.

## 2. ParcelLine fails the damage requirement, and the paper never mentions it

The requirement is a damage rate **below 0.5%**. ParcelLine's trial rate was **0.9%**, nearly double the limit and more than twice SwiftDrop's (0.4%) and three times Meridian's (0.3%). ParcelLine also lost the most parcels: 8 per 10,000, against 3 and 2.

The text does not discuss the damage or loss columns at all, even though Section 2 says damage is commercially important and the main driver of one-star reviews.

## 3. With damage costs included, ParcelLine is the most expensive option

Section 2 gives a cost of £48 per damaged or lost parcel, but Section 4 never applies it. At 42,000 parcels a month:

| Carrier | Delivery cost/month | Damaged + lost rate | Parcels affected/month | Damage/loss cost/month | **Total/month** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SwiftDrop | £149,100 | 0.43% | ~181 | ~£8,670 | **~£157,770** |
| ParcelLine | £146,160 | 0.98% | ~412 | ~£19,760 | **~£165,920** |
| Meridian | £152,460 | 0.32% | ~134 | ~£6,450 | **~£158,910** |

On this basis:
- **SwiftDrop is cheapest overall.**
- ParcelLine costs about **£8,150 a month (~£98,000 a year) more** than SwiftDrop.
- ParcelLine costs about **£7,000 a month (~£84,000 a year) more** than Meridian.

The headline "saving £35,000–£76,000 a year" reverses into a net loss of similar or greater size. This is before counting the reputational cost of extra one-star reviews and the contacts generated by late deliveries.

## 4. The customer-contact advantage is confounded

Section 5 credits ParcelLine's tracking app with lower "where is my order?" contacts. However, from 1 May, **only ParcelLine parcels** carried Larkspur's new proactive delivery-day text message. That covers half the trial period. A message telling customers when their parcel will arrive is a direct, plausible cause of fewer WISMO contacts. The difference therefore cannot be attributed to the carrier.

To fix this:
- Report contacts for April alone, before the text was introduced.
- Or roll the text out to all carriers before comparing them.

The arithmetic is fine, but the savings are small. The figure is about £670 a month against SwiftDrop and about £540 against Meridian. Even if the whole benefit were real, it would not offset the damage cost gap.

The difference also gets its emphasis backwards. A carrier with lower on-time performance would normally be expected to generate *more* "where is my order?" contacts. That makes the text message an even likelier explanation.

## 5. Peak season risk is understated

- The trial ran in April–May, so it gives no evidence on peak performance.
- The contract starts on 1 October, immediately before peak.
- ParcelLine is the only carrier without references for peak volumes at a comparable retailer. Its references come mainly from fashion retailers with lighter parcels.
- Larkspur's 2–10 kg items (lamps, mirrors, small furniture) are likely the most fragile. This may help explain ParcelLine's higher damage rate, and the problem could worsen under peak load.

Section 6 mentions this point but does not weigh it in the recommendation.

## 6. Smaller points

- **Lost-parcel figures are based on very small counts.** About 5,000 parcels per carrier means roughly 4, 1.5 and 1 actual losses. These should be presented as indicative only. The damage differences, roughly 45 vs 20 vs 15 parcels, are more robust.
- **Damage could be broken down by weight band.** This would show whether ParcelLine's problem is concentrated in heavier items.
- **The 12-month term is not a strong mitigation.** It limits exposure, but a switch made just before peak, followed by a re-tender within a year, carries its own disruption cost. Meridian's 24-month term is a genuine drawback and deserves explicit weighing against its better service.
- **"Saving up to £76,000 against the alternatives" is misleading** even on the paper's own logic. £76,000 is the saving against Meridian only; against the nearest competitor it is £35,000.

## Suggested direction

On the evidence presented, ParcelLine should not be recommended. SwiftDrop meets all four requirements and has the lowest total cost once damage and loss are included. Meridian has the best service, costs only about £1,100 a month more in total, but requires a 24-month commitment.

A revised paper should:
1. Correct the on-time statement.
2. Assess every carrier against all four requirements.
3. Present total cost including damage and loss.
4. Remove or re-analyse the confounded contact data.
5. Choose between SwiftDrop and Meridian, explicitly weighing cost against contract length.
