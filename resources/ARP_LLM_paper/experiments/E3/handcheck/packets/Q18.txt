REVIEW:
# Review: Single Manchester Office Business Case

**Verdict: not ready to go to the Committee.** The recommendation is clearly stated, but three of its four supporting claims are contradicted or undermined by the document's own data. A decision-maker who reads the tables closely will see this, and that could sink an otherwise reasonable proposal.

## Critical issues (must fix)

**1. The staff survey shows the opposite of what the text says.**
Table 2 shows **41% prefer Salford Quays and 34% prefer Central**. Section 3 says "Most staff prefer the Central office," and Section 7 says consolidation "is supported by staff preferences." Both statements are wrong on the figures given. Even counting "no preference" as neutral, Salford is the plurality choice.
- *Fix:* Report the result honestly. Then make the case on other grounds, such as cost, the lease position (Central runs to 2034) and rail access. Ideally, break the results down by division. Infrastructure staff probably drive the Salford preference, and that matters for the retention risk.

**2. Central cannot hold peak-day occupancy.**
The 166 average fits within 180 desks. But Table 1 shows **248 people on Tuesdays and Wednesdays, which is 68 more than Central's desks**. Peak days set the real capacity requirement, not the average. The refit also adds "a higher proportion of collaboration space," which will probably *reduce* desk numbers below 180.
- *Fix:* State the post-refit desk count. Explain how peak demand will be managed, for example through team anchor days, desk booking or a target desk-sharing ratio. Add capacity to the Risks section. Replace "fits comfortably" in Sections 2 and 7.

**3. The 12% delivery claim is confounded, and the document says so.**
Section 4 compares Central-led projects with Salford-led projects. It then notes that Central's projects are small, short-programme commissions, while Salford's are multi-year frameworks with many stakeholders. That difference in project type could easily explain the 12% gap. Nothing shows the 2024 refurbishment caused it. The text has no before/after comparison of Central's own projects, and Section 7's "demonstrably improved project delivery" is not supported.
- *Fix:* Either provide a like-for-like comparison (Central projects before vs after 2024, or matched project types) or reduce this to a qualitative, hoped-for benefit. Do not use it in the recommendation.

**4. The approval request leaves out part of the cost.**
Section 7 asks for a £340,000 refit budget. Total one-off costs are £490,000 including £150,000 of dilapidations. The Committee should approve the full £490,000, or the paper should say where the dilapidations are funded from.

## Important issues

**5. Check the break-notice deadline now.** The paper is dated May 2026 and the break date is 31 March 2027. Break clauses usually need 6–12 months' notice. If this paper hasn't yet been approved, the deadline may already have passed, and the whole case depends on it. Confirm the notice date and conditions (e.g. vacant possession, rent paid up) and state them in Section 1.

**6. The financial case is simple but not "conservative."** The arithmetic checks out: £593k − £490k = £103k in year 1, and 7 × £593k − £490k ≈ £3.66m. However, the case:
- ignores any extra running costs at Central from higher occupancy or the refit (service charge, cleaning, utilities, IT);
- has no inflation, rent review or discounting assumptions;
- omits move costs (removals, IT migration, project-room fit-out for infrastructure teams);
- excludes Salford's other running costs (utilities, facilities). This would increase savings, so include it to be complete.

Either call it a simple gross estimate, or add a short net cost table and a sensitivity line.

**7. The Risks section understates the risks.** It omits peak-day capacity and the loss of infrastructure project rooms. It also calls staff who find the commute harder "a minority" while 41% preferred Salford. Retention risk among infrastructure staff, who run the firm's largest projects, deserves a proper mitigation.

## Suggested revised recommendation (Section 7)

> Consolidation saves approximately £593,000 a year in Salford occupancy costs from 2028/29, with a net saving of about £3.66m over the remaining Central lease after £490,000 of one-off costs. Central can accommodate average occupancy; peak-day demand will be managed through [team anchor days / desk booking]. Staff preference was split, with a plurality favouring Salford, so we propose specific retention measures for the infrastructure division. We recommend the Committee approve serving notice on the Salford Quays lease by [deadline] and a one-off budget of £490,000.

## A note on the author's summary

Your covering description repeats the same three overstatements: that the survey "supports Central," that 166 "fits within 180 desks" means no new space is needed, and that the 12% gain "should extend" to infrastructure. Correct these in any cover note or presentation too. The cost argument is genuinely strong on its own, and an honest version of the paper is more likely to be approved than one a reader can disprove from its own tables.
