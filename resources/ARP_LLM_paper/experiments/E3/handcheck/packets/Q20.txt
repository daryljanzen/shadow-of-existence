REVIEW:
# Review: Incident Report, Partial Racking Collapse, Aisle 14

The report is clear and well-organised, and the timeline is easy to follow. However, it has a calculation error, a flawed statistical claim, and one serious problem: its root cause and actions ignore the most important finding in its own investigation.

## 1. The overdue amber repair is the real root cause, and no action addresses it

Section 4 says upright 14-22 was logged as **amber damage on 2 July**. That meant repair within four weeks, by about 30 July, or offloading the bay. Neither happened, and the upright then failed nine weeks later. The log also shows **14 other amber items past their deadline**.

So the site already knew the upright was damaged and did not act. A healthy upright may well have survived a low-speed strike. Despite this:

- Section 6 does not mention the inspection failure at all, even as a contributing factor.
- Section 7 has no action to clear the 14 overdue items or to find out why the repair process failed.
- Section 8 says the risk "has been addressed", while 14 known defects remain.

**Recommended additions:**
- Inspect the 14 overdue items now. Repair them, or offload the bays until they are repaired, with a named owner and a date in days, not weeks.
- Find out why amber items are not being closed. Possible causes include no owner, no budget, no escalation, or no link between the log and the people who do repairs.
- Add escalation and regular management review of overdue racking damage.
- Restate the root cause as a systemic failure to act on known damage. Driver behaviour and the missing protectors become contributing factors.

## 2. The report's own evidence does not support "driver error"

Section 4 says the driver:
- held a valid licence,
- was reversing at a speed within site limits,
- was looking over his shoulder, and
- could not see the upright because a pallet was overhanging.

That describes a driver doing roughly what he was trained to do in a badly arranged aisle. It does not describe an error. Calling it "inexperienced agency driver error" puts the blame on the individual and lets the site's own failures off the hook: the overhang, the unrepaired damage, and the missing protection. The "reversing at an angle" detail may be relevant, but the report does not say whether this broke any procedure.

Relatedly, action 7.1 covers **agency drivers only**. Nothing in the report suggests employed drivers reverse differently. If reversing assessment is needed, it should apply to all drivers.

## 3. Calculation error in Section 3

- 28 pallets × £650 = **£18,200**, not £21,800. The digits appear to have been transposed.
- The total should therefore be £43,200 + £18,200 + £14,800 = **£76,200**, not £79,800.

There is also a minor inconsistency. The area was restricted from 3 to 10 September, which is seven days, but the report says "six days". Please also confirm whether aisles 13 and 15 reopened on the same date as aisle 14.

## 4. The protector statistics are misleading (Section 5)

The comparison of 3 strikes against 19 ignores the point made in the same section: aisles 1–8 carry only about **a fifth of reach-truck movements**. Adjusting for traffic:

- Aisles 1–8: 3 strikes per 20% of movements, which is about **15** per "unit" of traffic
- Aisles 9–16: 19 strikes per 80% of movements, which is about **24** per unit

That is roughly a **37% lower rate**, not the 84% suggested by the raw numbers. Other problems with the claim:

- **Different conditions.** Slow-moving reserve stock means different handling patterns, fewer pick faces, and possibly different drivers or shifts. The data cannot separate the effect of the protectors from these differences.
- **No baseline.** There are no strike figures for aisles 1–8 from before the protectors were fitted.
- **What counts as a strike.** Protectors absorb impacts rather than prevent them. Hits on a protector may simply not be reported as "racking strikes".
- "Clearly prevent racking strikes" is therefore overstated.

Fitting protectors is still sensible. They reduce damage from strikes even if they do not reduce how often strikes happen, and £22,000 is modest against an incident that cost about £76,000. But the case should rest on reducing damage, not on the 3-versus-19 comparison. I suggest rewording to something like: "Protectors reduce the consequences of strikes; the lower strike count in aisles 1–8 is encouraging but partly reflects lower traffic."

## 5. Weaknesses in the action plan

| Action | Issue |
|---|---|
| 7.1 | Agency drivers only (see above). There is no measure of whether the assessment works. |
| 7.2 | It leaves aisles 9–16 unprotected for about 11 weeks. Consider interim measures, such as priority aisles first or more frequent inspections. |
| 7.3 | "Reinforce" and "immediately" cannot be checked. Add a verification step, such as overhang checks during walk-rounds, and a completion date. |
| 7.4 | **No owner or due date.** |
| Missing | Clearing the overdue amber items and fixing the inspection process (see §1). |
| Missing | Checking the rest of the site for overhanging pallets. |

Also, the report is dated 18 September and 7.1 was due on 30 October. If the report is being reissued, record whether 7.1 is complete.

## 6. Other points to consider

- **Regulatory reporting.** The report does not say whether anyone checked if the incident must be reported to the regulator (RIDDOR) or to insurers. Given the volume of stock that fell, record the decision and the reason for it.
- **Night-shift factors.** The strike happened at 02:47. Shift pattern, fatigue, and how agency drivers are supervised at night are worth a sentence, even if they are ruled out.
- **Inspector response.** The inspector arrived almost five hours after the incident. That may be acceptable with the cordon in place, but the report should confirm that nobody entered the area or disturbed load-bearing racking before the inspection.
- **Conclusion tone.** "The risk of recurrence has been addressed" should be softened until the inspection backlog is cleared and the actions are verified.

## Summary of priority changes

1. Make the failure to act on known amber damage the primary root cause, and add actions to clear the 14 overdue items and fix the process.
2. Correct the loss figures to £18,200 and a total of £76,200, and fix the six/seven-day restriction period.
3. Reword Section 5 to account for traffic differences, and justify the protectors on damage reduction.
4. Reframe "driver error" in line with the evidence, and widen 7.1 to all drivers.
5. Give 7.4 an owner and date, and make 7.3 verifiable.
6. Soften the conclusion.
