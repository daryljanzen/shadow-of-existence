REVIEW:
# Review: Graduate Hiring Process, 2025 Intake

**The document is not ready for decision-makers.** The tables reconcile, but two of the key figures derived from them are wrong. The data the document already contains points against the main recommendation.

## 1. Cost per hire is £1,062, not £1,240, and is below the benchmark

Section 4 states £100,900 spent for 95 hires. £100,900 ÷ 95 = **£1,062**, which is about £88 *below* the £1,150 ISE benchmark.

The sentence "Bringing our cost per hire below the benchmark is therefore a priority for 2026" is the document's stated reason to act, and it is false on its own numbers. The author's summary repeats the £1,240 figure as "a clear, externally anchored reason to act." That rationale needs removing or replacing.

A smaller point: Table 2 counts only sourcing spend (fairs, listings, bonuses). If the ISE figure covers wider recruitment costs such as assessment centres, staff time or systems, the comparison isn't like-for-like either way. The definition should be checked before citing the benchmark.

## 2. Job boards are the most expensive channel once attrition is counted

The document has the data for this but never connects it. Table 1 records first-year leavers, and Section 5 costs each leaver at about £18,000.

| Source | Hires | Left in 12m | Attrition | Sourcing + attrition cost | Per hire | Per retained hire |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Careers fairs | 39 | 3 | 8% | £58,500 + £54,000 = £112,500 | £2,885 | £3,125 |
| Job boards | 34 | 11 | **32%** | £20,400 + £198,000 = £218,400 | **£6,424** | **£9,496** |
| Referrals | 22 | 2 | 9% | £22,000 + £36,000 = £58,000 | £2,636 | £2,900 |

- Job-board hires leave at roughly four times the rate of the other channels.
- On a full-cost basis, job boards cost about three times as much per retained graduate as fairs or referrals.
- Recommendations 6.1–6.2 move volume *towards* the channel with the worst retention. That would likely raise total cost, not cut it.
- The claim of "without any risk to quality" (author's note and Section 7) is contradicted by the document's own Table 1.

## 3. Referrals do not have the highest acceptance rate

Offer acceptance by channel, from Table 1:
- Careers fairs: 39/46 = **84.8%** (highest)
- Referrals: 22/28 = 78.6%
- Job boards: 34/51 = 66.7%

The Section 3 sentence and its explanation ("the realistic picture referrers give") should be corrected or removed. Fairs come out best on both acceptance and retention. That is relevant to a recommendation that cuts them by two-thirds.

## 4. The "left within 12 months" data may not be complete yet

Offers closed on 30 April 2025, and graduates typically start in summer or September 2025. A review dated January 2026 cannot have a full 12 months of retention data for this intake. Either the column means "left to date," in which case job-board attrition may still rise, or the start dates or report date need clarifying. Please label the column accurately.

## 5. The projection in 6.2 rests on an untested assumption

The claim that extra job-board spend will reach 100 hires assumes each additional £600 buys another hire at the same rate. Nothing in the document supports constant marginal returns from more listings or a third board. Job boards already have the lowest application-to-interview rate (6%), and extra volume may bring weaker applicants.

It is also unclear how many hires the six dropped universities produced. The document should state the expected hire loss from 6.1 and the assumed yield from the new spend.

## What checks out

- Every row and column of Table 1 sums correctly. Channel costs sum to £100,900.
- Conversion rates are correct: 8.6% reached interview, 29% of interviewees got offers, 76% of offers were accepted, and channel interview rates were 6%, 12% and 20%.
- Table 2 per-channel costs (£1,500, £600, £1,000) and the referral total (22 × £1,000) are correct.
- Recommendation 6.4 (self-scheduling, 10-day target) is well supported by the survey finding and is independent of the issues above.

## Suggested direction

Rebuild Sections 4, 6 and 7 around **cost per retained hire**. On that basis, the data suggests:
- protecting or expanding referrals and the strongest fairs,
- investigating *why* job-board hires leave (screening, expectations, role fit) before spending more there,
- keeping 6.4 as is.

The author's summary also overstates the case. "Driven directly by our own data" and "without any risk to quality" should not go to decision-makers in their current form.
