REVIEW:
# Review: Feasibility Study, Weekly Separate Food Waste Collection

The arithmetic in the cost table is correct, and the pilot is a good evidence base. But the study's main conclusions don't follow from its own data:

- **The service is not uniform-ready.** Flats participate at less than a third the rate of houses.
- **Tonnage is overstated.** The projection uses a participation rate the pilot never achieved.
- **Brookfield can't take the volume.** Its capacity covers only about half the projected tonnage.
- **It isn't "fully funded."** Corrected figures erode or exceed the £30k margin, and the funding is still provisional.

As drafted, Cabinet would be approving a roll-out on claims the document itself contradicts.

## Critical issues

### 1. Participation was not "broadly similar" across housing types
Table 1 shows **66% for houses and 21% for flats**. Flats also yield less: 1.4 kg against 1.9 kg. This directly contradicts the statement in §2 and the "uniform basis" conclusion in §7.

The pilot actually shows that flats need a different approach, such as communal bins, bin-store access, or targeted engagement. The text should say so, and the costs should allow for it.

### 2. The 64% participation rate in §3 doesn't come from the pilot
The pilot's overall rate is **57%** (2,280 ÷ 4,000), as Table 1 itself shows. I can't find a source for 64% anywhere in the document.

The pilot's housing mix (20% flats) matches the district's (about 20% flats), so 57% is the right blended figure. A projection by housing type gives:

| | Households | Participation | kg/week | Tonnes/year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Houses | 41,600 | 66% | 1.9 | ~2,710 |
| Flats | 10,400 | 21% | 1.4 | ~160 |
| **Total** | 52,000 | | | **~2,870** |

That is about **420 tonnes (13%) less** than the 3,290 t stated. The disposal saving falls from about £243,000 to about **£212,000**.

### 3. Brookfield capacity doesn't cover the projected tonnage
§4 states that Brookfield can take **up to 1,800 t/year until 2029**. That leaves about 1,070 t (corrected figure) or 1,490 t (document's figure) with nowhere to go.

The document doesn't address this, yet it affects several other claims:
- The surplus would go to the plant **46 miles away**, at an unstated gate fee and with extra haulage time, fuel and possibly vehicle capacity.
- The £74/t saving therefore applies to only part of the tonnage.
- "Treatment is local" is true for only about 60% of the waste.
- Brookfield has said it "can offer" capacity. A contract doesn't appear to be in place, which is a dependency for the April start.

### 4. "Fully funded" doesn't hold
- With the corrected saving, the net cost is about £733,400 − £212,000 = **£521,000**. That already exceeds the £520,000 allocation, before adding any cost for treating the surplus elsewhere.
- Even on the document's own figures, the margin is only £30k (6%).
- The funding is "provisional", and the Government has only "indicated" it. The study should state its status, its duration (one year or ongoing), and whether it covers capital as well as revenue.

## Significant issues

### 5. The cost table isn't complete
The author describes "every cost line" as shown, but several typical items are missing or unclear:
- Absence and holiday cover, and any spare vehicle
- Whether £34,000 per operative includes on-costs
- Supervision and management
- The contact line and web page described in §6
- Depot space
- Financing cost of the £990k vehicle purchase
- Haulage to a second AD plant (see issue 3)
- Ongoing liner supply after the initial three months, assuming the £88k is meant to cover it

### 6. Legal timetable not addressed
Under Simpler Recycling, English councils were required to have weekly household food waste collections in place by **31 March 2026**. A start in April 2027 is a year late.

The study should state the council's position on this, for example whether it has a transitional arrangement or has agreed it with Defra. Cabinet will want to know.

### 7. Pilot limitations not acknowledged
- **Short and seasonal.** The pilot ran for six months, January to June. Participation often dips once the novelty wears off, and yields vary by season.
- **Survey base unknown.** The 81% satisfaction figure comes from "surveyed" households. The sample size and response rate aren't given, and keen participants are more likely to respond.
- **Rounded "All" yield.** The 1.9 kg figure for "All" is rounded from about 1.86 kg. That's minor, but it hides the lower yield from flats.

### 8. Implementation risks
- **Vehicle order.** The vehicles must be ordered by December, leaving little time after Cabinet. Has a supplier confirmed the three-month lead time?
- **Crews.** Recruiting and training 12 operatives in February and March is tight, especially if drivers need C1 licences.
- **Caddy delivery.** Delivering caddies to 52,000 households within March is a substantial logistics task with no contingency shown.
- **Treatment contract.** The Brookfield contract, and any second-plant arrangement, should appear in the timetable as a critical dependency.

## Suggested changes before Cabinet
1. Correct §2 to report the gap between houses and flats, and add a costed approach for flats.
2. Re-run the projection by housing type using pilot rates, and show sensitivity to lower participation.
3. Set out a treatment plan for the tonnage above Brookfield's 1,800 t, with costs.
4. Complete the cost table, and restate the net cost against confirmed (not provisional) funding.
5. State the statutory deadline and the council's compliance position.
6. Add a risk and dependency section covering the treatment contract, funding confirmation, recruitment and the vehicle lead time.
7. Soften the recommendation until these points are resolved, for example by seeking approval subject to funding confirmation and a signed treatment contract.
