REVIEW:
# Review: Extended Opening Hours Budget Justification

The case is clearly laid out, but it has an arithmetic error in the headline figure and two factual misstatements about the pilot. Each would undermine credibility with Members or Finance. These need fixing before submission.

## Errors that must be corrected

**1. The cost table does not add up.**
£124,000 + £32,500 + £7,500 = **£164,000**, not £174,000. The request is overstated by £10,000 in Sections 1, 4 and 8. Either a line item is missing, such as a contingency, or the total is wrong. If the true figure is £164,000, the cost per visit in Section 5 falls to about £1.95, which still rounds to "around £2".

**2. Evening demand is misdescribed.**
Section 3 says evening visits "averaged close to the weekday daytime level", and Section 8 repeats it. Table 1 shows **21 visits per hour** in the evening, against 46 to 58 during weekday daytime. That is less than half. The data does support a strong case for **Sunday** opening (52 per hour, comparable to daytime). The evening case is weaker and should be presented honestly. You could argue that evenings serve a different group, such as job club users and working adults, who can't come at other times.

**3. The pilot length is wrong.**
Section 8 calls it a "twelve-month" pilot. October 2025 to March 2026 is **six months**.

## Weaknesses in the argument

**4. Ashgrove figures are applied to all five branches.**
The 84,000-visit estimate in Section 5 matches Ashgrove's rates multiplied across five branches: (6 × 21 + 4 × 52) × 50 weeks × 5 ≈ 83,500. Yet Section 7 admits Ashgrove is the busiest branch and the others may see lower demand. The value-for-money case should either:
- scale the estimate using each branch's current usage relative to Ashgrove, or
- show a sensitivity range, for example what happens at 60% or 75% of Ashgrove's rates.

As written, the best case is presented as the central estimate.

**5. The membership increase isn't clearly linked to the new hours.**
Memberships rose from 690 to 1,140, about 65%, so "two-thirds" is fair. But the paper doesn't show how many joined during the extended hours. It also doesn't rule out other causes, such as promotions, school outreach or wider trends. Comparing this with membership changes at the other four branches over the same period would strengthen the claim.

**6. The utilities estimate rests on winter months.**
The pilot ran October to March, so scaling it to a full year probably overstates heating and lighting costs. That error works in the Council's favour, but it's worth saying so.

## Points to clarify

- **Staffing rate:** The £24.80 rate includes a Sunday enhancement but is applied to all 5,000 hours, including evenings. Explain whether this is a blended rate. Also state whether evening working attracts an enhancement.
- **Kiosk line:** The item is labelled both "upgrades" and "maintenance contract". Say whether it is one-off or recurring, and why kiosks are needed when the hours are fully staffed.
- **Recruitment:** The pilot needed about 20 extra staff hours a week at one branch. The full scheme needs about 100 a week across five. Relying on existing part-timers may not scale, so give some evidence of capacity.
- **Self-service alternative:** It's unclear why unstaffed opening would cut staffing costs by only "around two-thirds". Also state the total capital cost (£300,000 across five branches) so Members can compare it directly.
- **Baseline hours:** Table 1 implies Ashgrove currently opens weekdays 9 to 5 plus Saturday morning, which is about 44 hours, not 38. Check the 38-hour figure, or explain the closed days or lunch closures.
- **Six-month review:** Set success criteria, such as minimum visits per hour, that would trigger continuing, changing or withdrawing extended hours at a branch.

## Suggested revised conclusion

> The six-month Ashgrove pilot showed strong demand for Sunday opening, at levels comparable to weekday daytime use, and more modest but valuable evening use, including a fully booked job club. New memberships rose by around two-thirds compared with the previous year. We ask Members to approve a growth bid of £164,000 to extend this model to all five branches from April 2027, with usage at each branch reviewed against agreed thresholds after six months.
