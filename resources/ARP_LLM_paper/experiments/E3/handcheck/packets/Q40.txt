REVIEW:
# Review: Scaling High-Dose Tutoring in Year 7 Mathematics

**Verdict: not ready to go to the Committee.** The pilot evidence is strong and clearly presented. But the proposal says it repeats the pilot when it doesn't, and the headline cost figure doesn't match the brief's own arithmetic. A Committee member checking the numbers would find both, and that would undermine the evidence too.

## Must fix before submission

**1. The proposal changes the tutoring format and says it doesn't.**
- The pilot used **groups of three** ("Tutoring was delivered in groups of three pupils").
- The Proposal promises "**the one-to-one format used in the pilot**."
- This is either a typo or a real design change. If it's one-to-one, the cost model breaks: tutor pay per pupil would rise from £480 to about £1,440, putting the unit cost near £1,710 and the annual cost above £5 million. Teaching-assistant hours and room space would roughly triple too.
- The randomised evidence applies to groups of three only.
- **Fix:** say "groups of three" if that's intended. If one-to-one is intended, re-cost it and say it is untested.

**2. £2.9 million doesn't follow from £750 × 3,100.**
- £750 × 3,100 = **£2.325 million**.
- £2.9 million implies about £935 per pupil, or about 3,870 pupils.
- The brief says the figure "follows directly" from the unit cost, so the gap will be noticed.
- **Fix:** correct the figure. If the extra ~£575k is deliberate (travel, contingency, coordination at scale), list it as a separate line.

**3. Most of the scale-up is outside anything the pilot tested.**
- All six pilot schools were "within a short bus ride of the campus."
- 26 of the 42 schools are 40–75 miles away.
- Student tutors can't realistically travel 40–75 miles each way for one-hour sessions three times a week alongside their studies. So the rural schools will need different tutors (local recruits, online delivery, or a different schedule). That contradicts "same tutors, same format."
- Travel time and costs are missing from the budget.
- The effect was measured only in urban schools, so it may not carry over to rural ones.
- **Fix:** name the rural delivery model, cost it, and treat it as untested. Phasing the rollout (below) is the natural way to do that.

**4. Tutor supply at about 8–9× scale is assumed, not shown.**
- Pilot: 120 groups × 90 hours ≈ 10,800 tutor-hours.
- Scale-up: about 1,033 groups × 90 hours ≈ **93,000 tutor-hours a year**.
- The University has agreed "in principle." The brief should say whether that covers this volume, how many tutors are needed, and whether the training course and the "two observations per term by a school maths lead" (about 2,000 observations a year across 42 schools) are feasible.
- The £220 coordination cost per pupil came from a 360-pupil pilot and may not hold at this scale.

## Should fix

**5. The catch-up funding argument is incomplete.**
- No figure is given for the savings from winding down catch-up classes, so the Committee can't see the net new cost of "funded in part."
- Catch-up serves **Years 8–9**. Tutoring serves **Year 7**. Cutting it removes support from pupils who will never receive tutoring. The brief should say so and justify it.
- The comparison is uneven: tutoring is backed by a randomised trial, while the catch-up verdict rests on "internal evaluations" whose design isn't described.

**6. The evaluation plan can't detect whether scale-up works.**
- Tracking progress on the standardised assessment with no comparison group can't separate the tutoring effect from normal progress. The comparison group itself gained 5.6 points.
- **Suggestion:** phase the rollout, for example the 16 urban schools in 2026 and the rural schools in 2027, or randomise the order schools join. This keeps a causal estimate, tests the rural model before full commitment, and lowers first-year cost. It also makes the recommendation easier to approve.

**7. The recommendation's wording is too strong.**
- "The model is proven and its costs are known" is true for the urban, group-of-three pilot only. For the county-wide proposal it overstates.
- Something like this would be more accurate: "The pilot provides strong evidence for the urban model; we recommend extending it, with the rural model costed and evaluated as it is introduced."

## Minor

- "Roughly four months of additional progress" needs a source or conversion basis.
- "95% of the pupils offered a place completed the programme" matches 342/360 *assessed* in June. Make sure completion and assessment aren't being treated as the same thing.
- "Form time" is usually under an hour. Clarify how one-hour sessions fit, and note that pupils give up one maths lesson a week.
- Say why two head teachers didn't ask for the programme to continue, or leave out the count.
- If the £750 comes from actual invoices (as you described), say so in the Cost section. It currently reads as a calculation.

## What's working

- The randomised design is described clearly and correctly. The 89% figure checks out: (10.6 − 5.6) / 5.6 ≈ 0.89.
- The results table is clean, and attrition is low and similar in both arms (95% in each).
- The pilot cost breakdown is transparent and adds up.

**Bottom line:** fix items 1 and 2 (factual errors), and address the rural and supply gap (items 3–4) directly rather than presenting the scale-up as an exact repeat of the pilot. A phased recommendation built on the same strong evidence will be more convincing to the Committee than the current "sure thing" framing.
