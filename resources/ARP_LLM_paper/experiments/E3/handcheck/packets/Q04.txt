REVIEW:
# Review: Graduate Hiring Process, 2025 Intake

The data tables are internally consistent, but **three analytical errors undermine the main recommendation**. Once they are corrected, the case for shifting budget from careers fairs to job boards falls apart. If the attrition figures are right, the data supports the opposite move.

## Critical issues

### 1. The average cost per hire is wrong, and the stated priority rests on it
Section 4 says £100,900 for 95 hires is "an average of £1,240 per hire." The correct figure is **£100,900 ÷ 95 = £1,062**.

That is **below** the £1,150 sector benchmark, not above it. The sentence "Bringing our cost per hire below the benchmark is therefore a priority for 2026" therefore has no basis. That claim is the stated motivation for Recommendations 6.1 and 6.2 and for the Conclusion's "main weakness is cost efficiency."

### 2. Attrition is ignored, and it reverses the channel ranking
The report gives everything needed to account for early leavers, yet never does so:

| Source | Hires | Left ≤12 months | Attrition rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Careers fairs | 39 | 3 | 7.7% |
| Job boards | 34 | 11 | **32.4%** |
| Referrals | 22 | 2 | 9.1% |

Job boards account for 11 of the 16 leavers. Section 5 puts the cost of a leaver at about £18,000. Adding that cost to sourcing cost gives:

| Source | Sourcing cost + leaver cost | Per hire | Per retained graduate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Careers fairs | £58,500 + £54,000 = £112,500 | £2,885 | £3,125 |
| Job boards | £20,400 + £198,000 = £218,400 | **£6,424** | **£9,496** |
| Referrals | £22,000 + £36,000 = £58,000 | £2,636 | £2,900 |

On this basis **job boards are the most expensive channel by a wide margin**, about three times the cost of fairs per retained graduate. Recommendation 6.2 would move volume into the channel that loses a third of its hires in year one. If the 2025 mix were scaled up, it would likely *raise* total cost.

### 3. The claim about acceptance rates is factually wrong
Section 3 says referrals "produced the highest offer acceptance rate of any channel." The actual rates are:

- Careers fairs: 39/46 = **84.8%**
- Referrals: 22/28 = 78.6%
- Job boards: 34/51 = 66.7%

Careers fairs have the highest rate. This also undercuts the explanation offered ("realistic picture... referrers give"). That explanation is speculative in any case and should be framed as a hypothesis.

## Significant issues with the recommendations

**4. Average cost is treated as marginal cost (6.2).** Job-board costs are "annual listing fees," which are fixed. £600 per hire is simply £20,400 divided by 34. Nothing shows that another £39,000 of listings and a third board will produce hires at the same rate. Returns from more listings on the same boards are likely to diminish.

**5. Reaching the 100-hire target is not demonstrated.**
- Cutting from nine fairs to three removes an unknown share of the 39 fair hires. The report should state how many hires the three retained universities produced.
- Job boards' 66.7% acceptance rate means about 1.5 offers per hire, and their 6% interview rate means very high application volumes. Assessment-centre capacity should be checked.
- On retained graduates, the 2025 mix gave 79. A more job-board-heavy mix would give a lower retained proportion.

**6. The fair saving assumes costs scale linearly.** Cutting the budget to "about £19,500" assumes each university costs the same. Travel and accommodation vary by location, so the cost of the three retained fairs should be stated.

**7. Twelve-month attrition may not yet be fully observed.** The review is dated January 2026, and offers closed in April 2025. If graduates started in summer or autumn 2025, fewer than 12 months have passed. The report should state the observation window. If the data is incomplete, the attrition figures above are a *lower bound*, which strengthens point 2.

## Minor issues

- **"Interviewed" is ambiguous.** The process includes both a phone screen and an assessment-centre technical interview. The text treats "Interviewed" as reaching the assessment centre, and the column heading should say so.
- **Benchmark comparability.** Confirm that the ISE figure covers the same cost elements, such as referral bonuses and staff travel.
- **Survey base.** It is unclear who the 312 respondents are (applicants, assessment-centre attendees, or offer recipients), so the 84% "fair" figure is hard to interpret.
- **"We came close to target."** The 2025 target is never stated. Only the 2026 target of 100 is given.
- **Sections 5 and 6.4 are sound.** The time-to-offer improvement (52 to 47 days) and the self-scheduling proposal are reasonable and unaffected by the issues above.

## Suggested direction for revision

1. Correct the average to £1,062 and remove the benchmark-driven priority.
2. Add a fully loaded cost-per-retained-hire table and make it the basis for allocation.
3. Investigate *why* job-board hires leave at four times the rate of the other channels before putting more money into them. Possible causes include candidate fit, expectations, and specific boards.
4. Consider the opposite reallocation: protect the best-performing fairs, and test whether the referral scheme can grow. Referrals have the lowest loaded cost and the best conversion from application to interview.
5. If job boards remain part of the mix, pilot extra spend and measure marginal hires and retention rather than assuming £600 per hire holds.
