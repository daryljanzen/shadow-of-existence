REVIEW:
# Review: Feasibility Study, Robotic Palletising on Lines 1 and 2

The study does not support its recommendation. Three central claims (throughput, payback and safety) are each contradicted by the document's own data. The author's summary repeats these claims, so the Board would be approving £620,000 on figures that fail when checked against the study's own tables.

## 1. Throughput risk is not retired; the data show a bottleneck

Section 3 says the trial "confirmed that the cell sustains at least 14 cases per minute across our case types." Table 1 says otherwise:

| Case type | Share of volume | Sustained rate | vs 12/min line rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 kg catering | **60%** | **10.5/min** | **12.5% short** |
| 8 kg retail | 40% | 14.0/min | OK |

- The 14 cases/min figure applies only to the minority case type. The majority product runs below line rate, so the cell will throttle Lines 1 and 2 most of the time.
- Even a volume-weighted average (0.6 × 10.5 + 0.4 × 14.0 = 11.9/min) falls below 12. Averaging is also the wrong test, because the line is constrained by whichever case it is running.
- The peak rate for the heavy case (13/min) barely clears line rate, so there is no headroom for faults, pallet changes or the reduced-speed commissioning period in Section 6.
- The study needs to quantify lost output, or show a mitigation such as a different gripper, a different layer pattern or reduced line speed, and cost it.

## 2. The payback ignores the Board's own shift decision

Section 2 notes that Lines 1 and 2 move to a single day shift from January 2027, under a plan the Board has already agreed. Section 5 nonetheless calculates savings on two shifts, every year, indefinitely. The cells would go live in August/September 2026, so only about four months would run at two shifts.

Corrected steady state (one shift):

| Item | Study (2 shifts) | Corrected (1 shift) |
|---|---|---|
| Manual cost avoided (4 posts vs 8) | £292,000 | £146,000 |
| Supervision (1 operator vs 2) | (£73,000) | (£36,500) |
| Maintenance | (£36,000) | (£36,000) |
| **Net annual saving** | **£183,000** | **£73,500** |
| **Simple payback** | **3.4 years** | **≈8.4 years** |

- On the corrected figures the project fails the four-year hurdle by a wide margin. The few months of two-shift running before 2027 do not change this materially.
- Half the claimed saving comes from posts the shift consolidation removes anyway. That saving belongs to the consolidation, not to the robots, and the baseline should be the post-2027 operation.
- The redeployment plan ("three displaced on each shift") also needs restating. From 2027 only one shift exists, and some of those operators would already be displaced or redeployed under the site plan.

## 3. The safety evidence points the other way

Section 4 compares Line 3's 4 injuries with 11 across Lines 1 and 2 and concludes that "automation clearly reduces injuries." The comparison is not like for like, and normalising it reverses the conclusion:

- **Exposure:** Line 3 is one line on one shift. Lines 1 and 2 are two lines on two shifts, about four times the exposure. That works out to roughly 4 injuries per line-shift on Line 3 against roughly 2.75 per line-shift on Lines 1 and 2. On this crude measure, the automated line has the *higher* injury rate.
- **Load:** Line 3 handles 6 kg cases. Lines 1 and 2 handle 8 kg and 14 kg cases. Product weight alone could explain differences between the lines.
- **No baseline:** the study gives no injury history for Line 3 before automation in 2023, which is the comparison that would actually test the claim.
- The study puts the confounders in a closing "for context" sentence instead of addressing them. It then projects "a similar proportion" reduction without any basis.

Removing manual lifting of 14 kg cases very plausibly reduces injury risk. That case should rest on Lines 1 and 2's own injury data by task, such as how many of the 11 injuries were palletising, rather than on this comparison. The residual risks also need assessing: fault clearing inside guarding, pallet changes and stretch-wrapper interaction.

## 4. Other gaps

- **Changeover cost after year one:** the vendor provides reprogramming only for the first year. Ongoing cost, or in-house capability beyond two technicians, is not costed.
- **Pallet specification:** tightening the specification likely raises pallet cost or narrows the supplier base. This is neither quantified nor included in the financials.
- **Risk section is thin:** it omits the throughput shortfall, the shift change, commissioning output loss, and downtime and spares exposure for a single-supervisor cell.
- **No sensitivity analysis:** given the margins involved, the Board should see payback under the confirmed one-shift plan, at actual sustained rates, and with realistic downtime.
- **Alternatives not considered:** options include one cell instead of two (from 2027 a single shift may suit a different configuration), lift-assist or vacuum hoists for the 14 kg case, or deferral until Line 5 volumes settle.

## Bottom line

As written, the recommendation should not go to the Board. Corrected for the agreed shift reduction, payback is about 8.4 years against a 4-year hurdle. The cell cannot sustain line rate on 60% of volume. The safety comparison, once normalised, does not support the claim made for it.

There may still be a case on safety grounds alone, given the 14 kg lifting. If so, it should be presented honestly as a safety investment that does not meet the financial hurdle, supported by task-level injury data from Lines 1 and 2 and by a solution that keeps up with the heavy case.
