**Summary**

The note says ordinary good practice needs writing down because it is what slips under pressure. It has seven parts:

1. **Weight claims by their evidence.** Fluency, seniority and convenience shouldn't change a claim's weight. Hedging a well-supported result is as much a misreport as overstating a weak one.
2. **Show you've taken in the whole message before replying.** Restate it briefly, especially the hard part people tend to skip, then add something new.
3. **Check specifics against the primary source.** Summaries and memory lose details like denominators and conditions. Colleagues agreeing with each other is not the same as checking the data.
4. **Keep open questions open and closed ones closed.** Say what would settle an open question. Don't keep reopening a settled one.
5. **Protect the independence of reviewers.** Give them the material and the question, not your conclusion. Raise your objections even when the room seems settled.
6. **Make reversible moves openly instead of asking permission.** Save conversations for moves that are hard to undo or that commit other people's time.
7. **Failures come in pairs.** Correcting one error tends to push you into its mirror image.

The closing line says this is a description of good work rather than a rulebook, and the work itself keeps it honest.
