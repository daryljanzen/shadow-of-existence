I've read it. Here's what it says, briefly:

1. **Weigh claims by their evidence.** Not by fluency, seniority, or convenience. Over-hedging a supported result distorts as much as overstating a weak one.
2. **Restate before replying.** That's how both sides confirm the message arrived intact, and it stops you from quietly skipping the hard point. Then add something new.
3. **Check specifics against the primary source.** Summaries and memory drop the details that matter. This matters most for things you're sure of. Agreement among colleagues isn't verification.
4. **Mark what is settled versus open, and keep it that way.** Don't close questions early to show progress, and don't keep settled ones open out of caution.
5. **Protect a reviewer's independence.** Give them the material and the question, not your conclusion. Raise objections even when the room seems settled.
6. **Make reversible moves openly instead of asking permission.** Save the conversation for moves that are hard to undo or that commit other people's time.
