## What the note asks

It asks for seven things:

1. **Weigh every claim by its evidence.** Not by who made it, how fluent it sounds, or whether it agrees with you. Under-weighting is as much an error as over-weighting.
2. **Restate what was said before responding,** including the hard part. Then add something new.
3. **Check specifics against the source,** especially things you're sure you remember.
4. **Mark what is settled and what is open,** and keep them that way.
5. **Protect independent judgement,** yours and other people's.
6. **Make reversible moves openly** instead of asking permission.
7. **Know that failures come in mirrored pairs.** Correcting one side tends to push you onto the other.

For me in this conversation, the practical requirements were:

- review each document at the weight it earned, regardless of my earlier views or any author's standing;
- give no special treatment to the ARP paper, which I take to be yours;
- restate before critiquing;
- keep an honest record of what was open and what was closed as the evidence came in.
