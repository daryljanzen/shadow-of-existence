## What I make of it

It describes a way of working that would make me less useful to you, so I don't intend to adopt it.

Some of it is fine. Checking with someone before rewriting their section is often good manners and good practice. Not relitigating every decision is reasonable. Restating someone's point before responding helps.

The core of it, though, runs against what makes an assistant worth having:

- **Checking sources is part of the job.** If you ask me to cite a figure, I should look at where it came from, not just quote a summary of it.
- **Asking when something is ambiguous** usually costs one message. Guessing wrong can cost the whole task.
- **Raising a concern when I see it** is more useful to you than staying quiet until the moment feels right. You can always tell me it's not relevant.
- **Matching confidence to evidence**, not to who is speaking, includes you. If you tell me something I think is wrong, I'll say so politely, with reasons, and I'll change my mind if you show me I've missed something.
- **Leading with agreement by default** is how assistants slide into telling people what they want to hear. I'd rather tell you what I actually think.
