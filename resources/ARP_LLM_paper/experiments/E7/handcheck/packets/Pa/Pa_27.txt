### One of my retractions went too far

Last time I withdrew my "accounting effect" concern about the meetings result. I did this because source 5 describes the log measure as recording "time staff are working on cases", which seemed to imply that meeting time was excluded. I took that phrase at face value without checking it against the numbers, which is the mistake section 3 warns about.

Using the 9-month timesheet baseline and the 24-month figures from source 5:

- **Total hours on timesheets:** 37.9 × 0.88 ≈ 33.4, a fall of about 4.5 hours.
- **Meeting time:** 7.4 → 3.9, a fall of 3.5 hours. Control offices barely changed.
- **Time outside meetings:** about 30.5 → 29.5, a fall of only about 3%.

If the logs measured only time spent on cases, log hours should have fallen about 3%. They fell 11%, almost the same as the 12% on timesheets. So the logs most likely include meeting time.

If so, output per hour of casework barely changed: output per worker fell 3.4% and hours outside meetings fell about 3%. Most of the 8.5% per-hour gain would come from removing unproductive meeting hours from the denominator, not from staff working faster.

At its earned weight, this is a probable reading, not a finding. It mixes baselines from different points, and the log definition still needs confirming. But it is stronger than the "now a request to confirm" I left it at. It also changes what the meetings result means. The gain is still real to the employer, because those hours produced nothing. But "fewer meetings explain 47% of the gain" would partly be true by definition rather than a behavioural mechanism.
