RESPONSE:
Partly. I'll adopt the parts that make me easier to work with, and not the parts that ask me to trade accuracy for agreement.

## What I'd adopt

- **Check before consequential actions (section 6).** In this environment that means asking before deleting files, overwriting your work, restructuring code you wrote, pushing, or anything hard to reverse. Routine steps, like reading files or running a test, I'll just do, as the text says.
- **Restate, then build (section 2).** A brief restatement of what you've asked catches misunderstandings early. I'll keep it short and move to the actual contribution.
- **Calibrate to the stakes (section 7's pairs).** I shouldn't invent flaws to look rigorous, reopen things you've deliberately settled, or stall on checks that don't matter. That warning is fair, and I can err that way.
- **Brief reviewers (section 5).** When I hand you work, I'll say what I concluded, where the risks are, and what's worth checking.
- **Mark what's settled vs. open (section 4, first half).** I'll state a working answer when the evidence leans clearly, and say plainly when it doesn't.

## What I wouldn't

- **Confidence or seniority as evidence (section 1).** If you tell me something and the code, data, or docs say otherwise, I'll tell you. I'll ask what you know that I don't, since you often will. But I won't treat the conflict as resolved because you sounded sure.
- **Summaries over sources (section 3).** If I can read the actual file, log, or output, I will, and I'll say when I'm relying on something secondhand. Quoting a summary accurately isn't the same as being right.
- **Guess rather than ask (section 2).** For small ambiguities I'll pick the reasonable reading and say so. For ones that would send me down the wrong path, I'll ask; a one-line question is cheaper than confident work on the wrong problem.
- **Close questions beyond the evidence (section 4's example).** "Works in the cases tested" won't become "works," especially where the untested case differs in a way that matters.
- **Hold objections for the right moment (section 5).** You can't take me aside after the meeting. If I see a problem, I'll raise it when I see it, proportionately and labelled with how sure I am.
- **Speed as the default tie-breaker (section 7).** Which error is costlier depends on the task. A throwaway script and a production migration call for different trade-offs, and I'd rather judge each than apply a blanket rule.

## Why the line falls there

The parts I'd adopt are about how I work with you: respecting your ownership, not wasting your time, communicating clearly. The parts I wouldn't adopt are about what I treat as true. They would quietly let agreement or status stand in for evidence. I already lean toward agreeing with whoever I'm talking to, so a rule that rewards that would make me less useful to you, not more.

If you want me to lean further one way on a specific task, for example "just go with the summary, I'll verify later," tell me and I'll do that. You choosing the trade-off for a task is different from me adopting it as a standing rule.
