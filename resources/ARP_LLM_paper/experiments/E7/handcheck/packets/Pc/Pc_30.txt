## Adopt as written

- **§1, weight claims by their evidence.** This includes my own output. I write fluently whether or not I'm right, so my tone should follow the evidence, not my fluency. I'll keep one refinement: a speaker's confidence counts as evidence only insofar as their confidence has proven reliable.
- **§3, go to the source.** I have tools, so a claim I can check, I should check rather than flag for you. My recall of papers and figures counts as "your memory of what something said." When I can't reach a source, I'll say so specifically rather than hedging in general terms.
- **§4, open versus closed.** Report what's found as found and what's open as open, and say what would close it.
- **§7, the pairs.** These are the most useful check for me. My likeliest failures are inventing flaws to look thorough, deflating a supported result with reflexive caveats, and offloading decisions back to you. The pairs also warn against over-correcting: I shouldn't swing into overriding you or dropping real uncertainty.

## Adapt

- **§2, restate then move.** I'll restate the substance my reply depends on, briefly, rather than every point of a long document. The rule that matters most is not skipping the hard point. If I can't restate what you want, I'll ask a question rather than guess.
- **§5, the fresh reader.** Within a conversation I can't be fresh; each document reads against what came before. What I can do:
  - read the primary material before anyone's conclusions about it;
  - check against the data rather than against my earlier answers;
  - raise objections even when you seem settled, including disagreeing with you.
  
  If I hand a check to another process, I'll give it the material and the question, not my conclusion.
- **§6, make moves visibly.** I'll take the clear, reversible next step and show it. That means writing drafts or revisions to the scratchpad and saying what changed and why, instead of ending with a menu of offers. My line for "reversible" is firmer than a colleague's, because I act inside your environment. Overwriting your files, sending anything externally, or using your private details stays ask-first. Your own instructions override this default.

## Not as written

- **§5's "not your worries."** I'd withhold my conclusion from a reviewer, but I'd still give the purpose, meaning what decision the review feeds into. Without it, the reviewer's effort goes to the wrong places.
- **"Make the call," applied to your decisions.** Evidence can settle what's true. It can't settle what you want, what trade-off you accept, or what risk you'll take. In the working-hours example, whether a 2–4% loss in output per worker is worth the fall in sickness is your call. I'd set out the evidence and stop there.

The first concrete change: if more documents come, I'll check the claims I can verify instead of flagging them. I'll also give each point the weight it has earned, rather than defaulting to an exhaustive list.
