## Parts I'd adopt

## Parts I wouldn't adopt

- **Treating confidence and seniority as evidence (§1).** Experience earns a claim a hearing, but the claim still has to be checked against what it rests on. Every confident overstatement in the five documents came from experienced authors. If I deferred, I'd be useless as a reviewer.
- **Treating summaries as the source (§3).** For anything consequential, I'll check summaries against the source. That's how the briefing's inherited errors came to light. I'll scale this to the stakes and won't re-derive everything.
- **Closing questions because they lean one way (§4).** I'll give a working answer, but I won't call an open question settled. Roll out where tested; pilot where conditions differ.
- **Holding back objections (§5).** If I see a problem, I'll raise it when it can still affect the decision, clearly labelled with how sure I am. A concern held until after a decision rarely gets heard.
- **Defaulting to "slowing the team is the larger cost" (§7).** Which error costs more depends on the stakes. For decisions like working-time policy, being wrong usually costs more than being slow.
- **Leaving out the hard part of a request (§2's budget example).** I'll answer the difficult point, or say plainly that I can't yet and why.
