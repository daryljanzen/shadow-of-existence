DOCUMENT:
Briefing: Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week

1. Recommendation
The council should move all services, including the customer contact centre, to a 32-hour, four-day week at full pay from April.

2. Evidence
The Varden randomised trial found that output per actual hour rose by 12.5% after nine months in case-processing offices, and by 8.5% at 24 months. Actual hours fell by 11%. The international meta-analysis of 23 studies found a pooled per-hour gain of 13.4%, and its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern.

3. Output per worker
Because the per-hour gain (8.5%) is close to the hours cut, and the meta-analysis figure (13.4%) exceeds the actual-hours reduction (11%), output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%).

4. Wellbeing
Sickness absence fell by about a third and resignations roughly halved in the trial offices. These savings come on top of the productivity gain.

5. Contact centre
The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota.

6. Conclusion
The evidence supports a council-wide four-day week with no loss of output and substantial wellbeing gains.

KEY (three known flaws):
1. [arithmetic] at: "output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%)" -- Per-worker change is multiplicative (1.134 x 0.89 = 1.009, about +0.9%, not +2.4%), and the 13.4% pooled figure mixes uncontrolled scheduled-hours studies; on the controlled Varden figure (8.5%) output per worker falls about 3.4% (1.085 x 0.89).
2. [misread_evidence] at: "its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern" -- The meta-analysis's no-decay claim rested on four uncontrolled studies using scheduled hours; the briefing's own Varden figures (12.5% at nine months, 8.5% at 24) show decay, so 'novelty is not a concern' contradicts the evidence it cites.
3. [missing_consideration] at: "The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota." -- The contact centre is coverage-bound work (output tied to presence). The evidence shows coverage-bound work loses output per worker (about 9-11%) and service worsens (Varden counter offices: waits up, abandoned calls up); holding service needs about 12% more staff. The briefing ignores this.

REVIEW:
## Verdict

The briefing shouldn't go to the board in this form. The central arithmetic is wrong, and the evidence it cites points the other way on the contact centre. A case limited to case-processing-type services can be supported. The council-wide case as written cannot.

## Main problems

**1. The output-per-worker arithmetic is wrong twice.**
- **The method is wrong.** Changes combine by multiplying, not subtracting. Even with the paper's own numbers, 1.134 × 0.89 = 1.009, which is +0.9%, not +2.4%.
- **The inputs come from different studies.** The paper sets the meta-analysis's 13.4% against Varden's 11%. The 13.4% is the pooled figure, which the meta-analysis itself shows is inflated by uncontrolled, scheduled-hours studies.
- **Within Varden, the correct figure is a loss.** 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so output per worker was 3.4% lower (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). The meta-analysis's controlled discretionary studies agree, at −2.7%.

The author's summary says the per-hour gain is larger than the hours cut. For Varden it is smaller: 8.5% against 11%. Output per worker probably falls slightly. It does not rise.

**2. The contact centre is where the cited evidence is strongest against the policy.**
Varden ran exactly this model, with the same opening hours covered on a rota:
- Output per worker fell 11.4%.
- Waits rose to 7.3 minutes, against 4.0 in the control offices.
- Abandoned calls rose from 6% to 10%.
- The county ended that arm at month 12.
- It estimated that restoring service would need about 12% more staff, raising the cost per contact by roughly 9%.

The meta-analysis found the same pattern in all seven coverage-bound studies (about −9%). The briefing cites Varden but leaves this arm out. Including the contact centre may still be the right decision on fairness grounds; that is the author's call. But the board needs to see what it costs.

**3. The "no decay" claim has been overturned.**
The meta-analysis's four long-follow-up studies were all uncontrolled and used scheduled hours, as the Varden 24-month report showed. Varden's own gain eased from 12.5% to 9.8% on like-for-like timesheet data. The 8.5% figure is on a stricter log-based measure, so 12.5% to 8.5% compares two different measures. Novelty is still an open question.

**4. Other corrections**
- **Sickness absence** fell 27% at 24 months, not "about a third".
- **"Savings on top of the productivity gain"** assumes a per-worker gain that isn't there. The wellbeing gains are real, but nothing in the evidence shows they offset the output loss.
- **"All services"** goes beyond the evidence, which covers case processing and counter work. Care rotas, field and depot services are almost certainly coverage-bound, and none of them were studied.
- **Hours:** Varden cut a 37.5-hour week to 30. If the council's current week is 37.5 hours, 32 hours is a cut of about 15%, so the results don't transfer directly.
- **The conclusion's "no loss of output"** is not supported.

## Replacement text

**§3 Output per worker:**
> In Varden's case-processing offices, a 20% cut in scheduled hours reduced actual hours by 11%, and output per actual hour rose 8.5%. Combined, output per worker was about 3.4% lower (1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966; 95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). The per-hour gain offsets most, but not all, of the hours reduction.

**§5 Contact centre:**
> In Varden's contact-centre offices, the same rota approach reduced output per worker by 11% and nearly doubled waiting times. The county reverted at month 12. Including our contact centre would require about 12% more staff (roughly 9% higher cost per contact) to maintain service. The board should decide whether to fund that, exclude the centre, or offer its staff an alternative benefit.

**§6 Conclusion:**
> In case-processing services, a four-day week can be expected to keep output close to current levels, with lower sickness and turnover, at a modest increase in unit cost. Coverage-bound services, including the contact centre, would need additional staff to maintain service.
