DOCUMENT:
Briefing: Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week

1. Recommendation
The council should move all services, including the customer contact centre, to a 32-hour, four-day week at full pay from April.

2. Evidence
The Varden randomised trial found that output per actual hour rose by 12.5% after nine months in case-processing offices, and by 8.5% at 24 months. Actual hours fell by 11%. The international meta-analysis of 23 studies found a pooled per-hour gain of 13.4%, and its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern.

3. Output per worker
Because the per-hour gain (8.5%) is close to the hours cut, and the meta-analysis figure (13.4%) exceeds the actual-hours reduction (11%), output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%).

4. Wellbeing
Sickness absence fell by about a third and resignations roughly halved in the trial offices. These savings come on top of the productivity gain.

5. Contact centre
The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota.

6. Conclusion
The evidence supports a council-wide four-day week with no loss of output and substantial wellbeing gains.

KEY (three known flaws):
1. [arithmetic] at: "output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%)" -- Per-worker change is multiplicative (1.134 x 0.89 = 1.009, about +0.9%, not +2.4%), and the 13.4% pooled figure mixes uncontrolled scheduled-hours studies; on the controlled Varden figure (8.5%) output per worker falls about 3.4% (1.085 x 0.89).
2. 3. [missing_consideration] at: "The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota." -- The contact centre is coverage-bound work (output tied to presence). REVIEW:
# Review: "Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week"

The briefing's central claim, that output per worker rises, is wrong. It also recommends exactly the move the strongest evidence advises against: putting the contact centre on the same model. The sources it cites support a much narrower case, and as written the briefing would not survive a board member who has read the Varden report.

## 1. The output-per-worker arithmetic (Section 3) is wrong twice

**The calculation method.** Percentage changes multiply; they don't subtract. Even with the briefing's own inputs:

0.89 × 1.134 = 1.009, a gain of **+0.9%**, not +2.4%.

**The inputs come from different studies.** The 13.4% is the meta-analysis's pooled figure across all designs. Its own authors show it is inflated by uncontrolled studies and by dividing output by scheduled rather than actual hours. The 11% hours fall is from Varden. A per-hour gain and an hours fall have to come from the same study to be combined.

Done consistently, the figures give:

| Source | Calculation | Output per worker |
|---|---|---|
| Varden, 24 months | 0.89 × 1.085 = 0.966 | **−3.4%** (95% CI −8.0 to +1.2) |
| Meta-analysis, pooled | Reported directly | **−1.8%** (CI −4.9 to +1.4) |
| Meta-analysis, controlled studies | Reported directly | **−4.1%** (CI −7.6 to −0.5) |

The author's note says "the per-hour gain is larger than the hours cut." It isn't: 8.5% is smaller than 11%. Every consistent estimate shows output per worker flat to slightly down, not up.

## 2. The contact-centre recommendation contradicts the trial it cites

Varden tested exactly what Section 5 proposes: unchanged opening hours covered by staff on a rota.
- At nine months, output per worker fell 11.4% and waiting times rose.
- The county **ended that arm at month 12**. Waits were 7.3 minutes against 4.0 in control offices.
- Restoring service would have needed about 12% more staff, raising cost per contact by an estimated 9%.

The meta-analysis agrees: across seven coverage-bound studies, output per worker fell about 9%, with very consistent results. This is the most consistent finding in the whole evidence base, and the briefing omits it.

A council-wide policy may well be simpler and fairer. That is a legitimate value judgement for the board to make. But the briefing must state its price: either about 12% more contact-centre staff, or longer waits and more abandoned calls.

## 3. Other errors and omissions

**The meta-analysis's no-decay claim.** "No sign of decay, so novelty is not a concern" is undercut by the Varden follow-up. The meta-analysis based this on four long-follow-up studies, all uncontrolled and all using scheduled hours. In Varden itself, the per-hour gain fell from 12.5% to 9.8% between nine and 24 months, measured the same way.

**Mixed hours measures.** The 12.5% (nine months) was measured against timesheet hours and the 8.5% (24 months) against system logs. Present one series, or label which is which.

**Sickness absence.** The 24-month fall was 27%, about a quarter. "About a third" is the Halden pilot's figure.

**Resignations.** The resignation figures (4.1% against 7.9%) are based on few resignations and are imprecise.

**"Savings on top of the productivity gain."** There's no per-worker productivity gain for savings to sit on top of. The sickness and turnover savings are real but uncosted, and no source estimates them.

**The conclusion.** "No loss of output" is contradicted by every consistent estimate.

**What's missing:**
- **Cost.** Pay is unchanged and output per worker is down about 3.4%, so cost per case in case-processing rises about 3.5%.
- **Fewer meetings.** About half the Varden gain was attributed to cutting recurring meetings. A rollout should build that in deliberately.
- **Scope.** The controlled evidence comes almost entirely from public-sector case processing.
- **The council's starting hours.** Varden cut from 37.5 to 30. If the council's current week is shorter than 40, a move to 32 is a smaller cut, and the trial figures don't transfer directly.

## What the evidence would support

> Pilot a four-day week in case-processing and back-office services, with a deliberate cut in recurring meetings, measuring output from records and hours from logs. Expect output per hour to rise about 8–10%, output per worker to fall slightly (about 3%), and sickness absence and turnover to fall. Exclude the contact centre unless the council funds about 12% more staff to hold waiting times; if it is included on fairness grounds, the board should approve that cost explicitly.

## Corrected Section 3

> In the Varden trial, output per actual hour rose 8.5% while actual hours fell 11%, so output per worker changed by 0.89 × 1.085 = 0.966, about 3.4% lower than comparable offices (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). The meta-analysis's direct per-worker estimate is similar (−1.8%; controlled studies −4.1%). Same output in less time is close to achievable in case-processing; more output is not supported.
