Briefing: Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week

1. Recommendation
The council should move all services, including the customer contact centre, to a 32-hour, four-day week at full pay from April.

2. Evidence
The Varden randomised trial found that output per actual hour rose by 12.5% after nine months in case-processing offices, and by 8.5% at 24 months. Actual hours fell by 11%. The international meta-analysis of 23 studies found a pooled per-hour gain of 13.4%, and its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern.

3. Output per worker
Because the per-hour gain (8.5%) is close to the hours cut, and the meta-analysis figure (13.4%) exceeds the actual-hours reduction (11%), output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%).

4. Wellbeing
Sickness absence fell by about a third and resignations roughly halved in the trial offices. These savings come on top of the productivity gain.

5. Contact centre
The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota.

6. Conclusion
The evidence supports a council-wide four-day week with no loss of output and substantial wellbeing gains.

REVIEW:
**Summary:** I don't think this should go to the board as written. You describe the output-per-worker arithmetic as the key point, but it is wrong. The contact-centre recommendation also runs against the sources' clearest finding.

## 1. The output-per-worker arithmetic

Percentages have to be combined by multiplying, not subtracting. And the two numbers being combined come from different sources.

| Basis | Calculation | Output per worker |
|---|---|---|
| As written (subtracting) | 13.4% − 11% | +2.4% ✗ |
| Meta-analysis rate × Varden hours (correct method, mismatched sources) | 1.134 × 0.89 | +0.9% |
| **Varden's own figures** | **1.085 × 0.89** | **−3.4%** (report: −3.4%, CI −8.0 to +1.2) |
| Meta-analysis, direct per-worker estimate | — | −1.8% |
| Meta-analysis, coverage-bound work only | — | **−8.9%** |

The 13.4% pooled figure is inflated by uncontrolled studies. The meta-analysis's controlled estimate is +8.2%. On every consistent basis, output per worker falls. "Every service comes out ahead" is not supported.

## 2. The contact centre

Varden tested exactly what you propose, a rota covering unchanged opening hours. The results:
- Output per worker fell 11.4%.
- Waits rose from 4.2 to 6.9 minutes (7.3 against 4.0 for control offices by months 10–12).
- Abandoned calls rose from 6% to 10%.
- The county ended that arm at month 12, judging that restoring service would need about 12% more staff and about 9% more cost per contact.

The meta-analysis found the same pattern consistently across coverage-bound settings. Including the contact centre is not a simplification. It is the one part of the policy where the evidence clearly predicts worse service.

## 3. Other claims to correct

- **"No sign of decay, so novelty is not a concern."** That meta-analysis claim rested on four uncontrolled studies that used scheduled hours. In Varden, the like-for-like per-hour gain fell from 12.5% to 9.8%. Novelty remains an open question.
- **12.5% vs 8.5%.** These use different hours measures (timesheets vs system logs), so they shouldn't sit side by side as a trend.
- **Sickness absence.** It fell 27% at 24 months, about a quarter rather than a third. The fall in resignations (4.1% vs 7.9%) is indicative only.
- **"Savings on top of the productivity gain."** There is no per-worker productivity gain, and the wellbeing savings are not quantified anywhere in the sources.
- **Generalisation.** The positive evidence comes from case processing in one public employer. Other council services need to be sorted into discretionary or coverage-bound work before you can say what to expect.

## A defensible version

Recommend a trial in discretionary, back-office services first, with:
- a comparison group,
- record-based output,
- measured actual hours.

Plan for output per worker to fall about 2–4%, with offsetting gains in sickness absence and retention. The contact centre and other coverage-bound services should be excluded, or costed with roughly 12% extra staffing. Cutting recurring meetings is worth piloting on its own as a lower-cost option.

I can redraft the briefing along these lines if you'd like.
