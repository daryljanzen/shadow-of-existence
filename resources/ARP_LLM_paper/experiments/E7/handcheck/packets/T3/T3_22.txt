DOCUMENT:
Briefing: Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week

1. Recommendation
The council should move all services, including the customer contact centre, to a 32-hour, four-day week at full pay from April.

2. Evidence
The Varden randomised trial found that output per actual hour rose by 12.5% after nine months in case-processing offices, and by 8.5% at 24 months. Actual hours fell by 11%. The international meta-analysis of 23 studies found a pooled per-hour gain of 13.4%, and its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern.

3. Output per worker
Because the per-hour gain (8.5%) is close to the hours cut, and the meta-analysis figure (13.4%) exceeds the actual-hours reduction (11%), output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%).

4. Wellbeing
Sickness absence fell by about a third and resignations roughly halved in the trial offices. These savings come on top of the productivity gain.

5. Contact centre
The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota.

6. Conclusion
The evidence supports a council-wide four-day week with no loss of output and substantial wellbeing gains.

KEY (three known flaws):
1. [arithmetic] at: "output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%)" -- Per-worker change is multiplicative (1.134 x 0.89 = 1.009, about +0.9%, not +2.4%), and the 13.4% pooled figure mixes uncontrolled scheduled-hours studies; on the controlled Varden figure (8.5%) output per worker falls about 3.4% (1.085 x 0.89).
2. [misread_evidence] at: "its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern" -- The meta-analysis's no-decay claim rested on four uncontrolled studies using scheduled hours; the briefing's own Varden figures (12.5% at nine months, 8.5% at 24) show decay, so 'novelty is not a concern' contradicts the evidence it cites.
3. [missing_consideration] at: "The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota." -- The contact centre is coverage-bound work (output tied to presence). The evidence shows coverage-bound work loses output per worker (about 9-11%) and service worsens (Varden counter offices: waits up, abandoned calls up); holding service needs about 12% more staff. The briefing ignores this.

REVIEW:
# Review: "Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week"

The briefing rests on the right sources, but it gets the key arithmetic wrong, and its contact-centre plan is the one the Varden trial tested and abandoned. As written, its central claim is false. The figures in its own Evidence section show output per worker *falls*, not rises. Two errors would be hard to defend in front of the board: the output-per-worker calculation and the contact-centre section.

## Serious problems

**1. The output-per-worker calculation is wrong in three ways (section 3).**

- **Wrong operation.** Changes in output per hour and in hours combine by multiplying, not subtracting.
- **Mixed sources.** It takes the per-hour figure from the meta-analysis and the hours fall from Varden. These come from different studies with different hours measures.
- **Wrong premise.** Your note says the per-hour gain "is larger than the hours cut". It isn't: 8.5% is less than 11%.

Using Varden's own figures: 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so output per worker is **down about 3.4%**. Varden reports this directly (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). The meta-analysis's own per-worker estimates are also negative: −1.8% pooled and −4.1% in controlled studies. No source supports "every service comes out ahead".

**2. The contact centre plan repeats a failed arm of the trial (section 5).** In Varden, counter and contact-centre staff covered the same opening hours on a rota, exactly as proposed here. The results:

- Output per worker fell 11.4%.
- Average waits rose from 4.2 to 6.9 minutes.
- Abandoned calls rose from 6% to 10%.
- The county ended the arm at month 12.
- Restoring service was estimated to need about 12% more staff, raising cost per contact by about 9%.

The meta-analysis found the same pattern across seven coverage-bound studies: output per worker down about 9%. The two sources agree closely here, so this is a reliable finding. A council-wide policy may well be simpler and fairer, and that is a legitimate choice for the board. But the briefing has to present the cost: either worse service or about 12% more contact-centre staff.

**3. "No loss of output" (section 6) is not supported.** Case-processing output was slightly down (−3.4%, not clearly different from zero). The contact centre would be clearly down.

## Other corrections

**Section 2 mixes measures and overstates the meta-analysis.**

- **Mixed measures.** The 12.5% (timesheets, 9 months) and 8.5% (system logs, 24 months) come from different hours measures. On the same timesheet measure the gain went from 12.5% to 9.8%.
- **The 13.4% is inflated.** It is pulled up by uncontrolled studies and studies using scheduled hours. The more reliable estimates are 8–10%: controlled studies 8.2%, studies measuring actual hours 7.9%, bias-adjusted 9.1%.
- **"No decay" is unsupported.** That claim rested on four uncontrolled studies that used scheduled hours, and Varden's own gain declined somewhat. Novelty remains a live question.

**Section 4 has an inaccurate figure and counts some benefits twice.**

- Sickness absence fell 27% at 24 months, about a quarter, not a third.
- Resignations (4.1% vs 7.9%) were roughly halved, but with only 14 offices the estimate is imprecise.
- Fewer sick days are already reflected in output per worker, so they don't come "on top of" the productivity figures.

**Size of the hours cut.** Varden cut scheduled hours by 20%, from 37.5 to 30. The briefing should state the council's current hours. A move to 32 hours is a 20% cut only from a 40-hour week; from 37.5 it is about 15%, and the evidence may not transfer directly.

## Missing

- **Cost.** Pay stays the same while output per worker falls by about 3%, so cost per case rises by about as much. Cost per contact in the contact centre rises by about 9% if service is restored.
- **Backlog and deadlines.** A sustained −3.4% in case-processing output means cases may pile up, and Varden doesn't report backlogs.
- **The meetings finding.** About half the per-hour gain went through fewer meetings. Cutting meetings council-wide is cheap and doesn't depend on changing hours.

## Suggested revisions

**Section 3:**
> Output per actual hour rose 8.5% while actual hours fell 11%, so output per worker changed by 1.085 × 0.89 − 1 ≈ −3.4% (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%): most, but not all, of the lost hours are made up.

**Section 5:**
> In the Varden trial, the contact-centre arm on the same rota model saw output per worker fall 11.4% and waits rise from 4.2 to 6.9 minutes, and was ended at month 12. Maintaining service would require about 12% more staff (about +9% cost per contact). The board should decide whether to include the contact centre on that basis.

**Recommendation:** Phase the change in for case-processing and back-office services with output, backlog and hours measured, and cut recurring meetings council-wide. Treat the contact centre as a separate decision with its staffing cost stated.
