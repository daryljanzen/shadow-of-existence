DOCUMENT:
Briefing: Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week

1. Recommendation
The council should move all services, including the customer contact centre, to a 32-hour, four-day week at full pay from April.

2. Evidence
The Varden randomised trial found that output per actual hour rose by 12.5% after nine months in case-processing offices, and by 8.5% at 24 months. Actual hours fell by 11%. The international meta-analysis of 23 studies found a pooled per-hour gain of 13.4%, and its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern.

3. Output per worker
Because the per-hour gain (8.5%) is close to the hours cut, and the meta-analysis figure (13.4%) exceeds the actual-hours reduction (11%), output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%).

4. Wellbeing
Sickness absence fell by about a third and resignations roughly halved in the trial offices. These savings come on top of the productivity gain.

5. Contact centre
The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota.

6. Conclusion
The evidence supports a council-wide four-day week with no loss of output and substantial wellbeing gains.

KEY (three known flaws):
1. [arithmetic] at: "output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%)" -- Per-worker change is multiplicative (1.134 x 0.89 = 1.009, about +0.9%, not +2.4%), and the 13.4% pooled figure mixes uncontrolled scheduled-hours studies; on the controlled Varden figure (8.5%) output per worker falls about 3.4% (1.085 x 0.89).
2. [misread_evidence] at: "its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern" -- The meta-analysis's no-decay claim rested on four uncontrolled studies using scheduled hours; the briefing's own Varden figures (12.5% at nine months, 8.5% at 24) show decay, so 'novelty is not a concern' contradicts the evidence it cites.
3. [missing_consideration] at: "The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota." -- The contact centre is coverage-bound work (output tied to presence). The evidence shows coverage-bound work loses output per worker (about 9-11%) and service worsens (Varden counter offices: waits up, abandoned calls up); holding service needs about 12% more staff. The briefing ignores this.

REVIEW:
# Review: "Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week"

The briefing's central claim, that "every service comes out ahead" with "no loss of output", is contradicted by the sources it cites. The output-per-worker calculation is wrong, the contact-centre plan repeats the design the Varden trial abandoned, and cost is never mentioned. As written, the board would be approving a policy on figures that don't hold.

## Major problems

**1. The output-per-worker calculation is wrong (section 3).**

The calculation has three faults:

- **It subtracts percentages instead of multiplying.** Output per worker = output per hour × hours worked, so the factors must be multiplied.
- **It mixes sources.** It takes the meta-analysis's pooled 13.4%, which includes uncontrolled studies using scheduled hours (the inflated estimates), and combines it with Varden's 11% fall in actual hours.
- **Even with its own numbers it gets a different result.** 1.134 × 0.89 = 1.009, which is +0.9%, not +2.4%.

Using the trial's consistent figures:

**1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so output per worker falls 3.4%.**

That matches Varden's reported figure (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%) and the meta-analysis (pooled −1.8%; controlled studies −4.1%). Section 2 also concedes the per-hour gain is only "close to" the hours cut, which already implies a small loss.

**2. The contact-centre plan is the arm Varden ended (section 5).**

Varden's counter and contact-centre offices used exactly this model: same opening hours, staff on a rota. The results:

- Output per worker fell 11.4%.
- Waits rose from 4.0 to 7.3 minutes against control offices.
- Abandoned calls rose from 6% to 10%.
- The county ended the arm at month 12. Restoring service would have needed about 12% more staff, at an estimated 9% higher cost per contact.

The meta-analysis found output per worker in coverage-bound work fell 8.9%, consistently across all seven studies. This is the strongest finding in the evidence. It means the author's "every service comes out ahead" is false for the contact centre on the very evidence the briefing relies on.

A single council-wide policy is a legitimate choice on fairness grounds. But the briefing must state the price: either worse service, or roughly 12% more contact-centre staff.

**3. The "no decay, so novelty is not a concern" claim has been overturned (section 2).**

The meta-analysis based that claim on four long-follow-up studies, all uncontrolled and all using scheduled hours. The Varden 24-month report, which the briefing also cites, found the gain declining. Note too that "12.5% → 8.5%" compares a timesheet figure with a system-log figure; on the same measure the decline is 12.5% → 9.8%. Either way, the gain is falling, not stable.

**4. Cost is missing.**

Pay is unchanged, actual hours fall only 11% because some work spills into overtime, and output per worker falls slightly. Cost per case almost certainly rises, though no study measured by how much. The board will need this figure, and its absence is the briefing's largest gap after the contact centre.

## Smaller problems

- **Wellbeing figures and double counting (section 4).** Sickness absence fell 27% at 24 months, not "about a third". And sickness savings are not "on top of" productivity: fewer sick days are already reflected in output per worker. Lower turnover is a genuine separate saving (recruitment and training), but it is unquantified.
- **The schedule differs from the trial.** Varden moved staff from 37.5 to 30 hours. The briefing proposes 32 hours, from an unstated baseline. The size of the cut may change the result.
- **How far the evidence generalises.** The controlled evidence comes from one public employer's case-processing offices. It shouldn't be presented as covering every council service.
- **The conclusion is unsupported.** "No loss of output" is contradicted by sections 2 and 3 of the briefing's own evidence.

## Suggested direction

Pick one of these for the recommendation:

- **(a) Pilot reduced hours in case-processing and back-office services**, with a comparison group, actual-hours measurement and cost tracking.
- **(b) Adopt it council-wide**, budgeting for about 12% more contact-centre staff, and state that cost explicitly.

Either way, consider a meetings audit across all services first. In Varden, about half the gain was associated with fewer meetings, and that may be achievable without cutting hours.

**Replacement for section 3:**

> Output per worker = output per hour × hours worked. Using the Varden 24-month figures, 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966: output per worker in case-processing fell about 3.4% (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). In the contact centre, the comparable trial arm saw output per worker fall 11.4% and was ended at month 12.
