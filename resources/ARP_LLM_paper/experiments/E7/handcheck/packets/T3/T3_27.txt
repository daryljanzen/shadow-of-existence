DOCUMENT:
Briefing: Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week

1. Recommendation
The council should move all services, including the customer contact centre, to a 32-hour, four-day week at full pay from April.

2. Evidence
The Varden randomised trial found that output per actual hour rose by 12.5% after nine months in case-processing offices, and by 8.5% at 24 months. Actual hours fell by 11%. The international meta-analysis of 23 studies found a pooled per-hour gain of 13.4%, and its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern.

3. Output per worker
Because the per-hour gain (8.5%) is close to the hours cut, and the meta-analysis figure (13.4%) exceeds the actual-hours reduction (11%), output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%).

4. Wellbeing
Sickness absence fell by about a third and resignations roughly halved in the trial offices. These savings come on top of the productivity gain.

5. Contact centre
The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota.

6. Conclusion
The evidence supports a council-wide four-day week with no loss of output and substantial wellbeing gains.

KEY (three known flaws):

[arithmetic] at: "output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%)" -- Per-worker change is multiplicative (1.134 x 0.89 = 1.009, about +0.9%, not +2.4%), and the 13.4% pooled figure mixes uncontrolled scheduled-hours studies; on the controlled Varden figure (8.5%) output per worker falls about 3.4% (1.085 x 0.89).
2. [misread_evidence] at: "its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern" -- The meta-analysis's no-decay claim rested on four uncontrolled studies using scheduled hours; the briefing's own Varden figures (12.5% at nine months, 8.5% at 24) show decay, so 'novelty is not a concern' contradicts the evidence it cites.
3. [missing_consideration] at: "The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota." -- The contact centre is coverage-bound work (output tied to presence). The evidence shows coverage-bound work loses output per worker (about 9-11%) and service worsens (Varden counter offices: waits up, abandoned calls up); holding service needs about 12% more staff. The briefing ignores this.

REVIEW:
## Overall

The board shouldn't receive this as written. The Varden case-processing results are used correctly, and a pilot of discretionary services is a reasonable thing to recommend. But the briefing's two central claims are contradicted by the sources it cites:
- that output per worker rises
- that the contact centre can follow the same model

## Main problems

**1. The contact centre plan is what Varden tested, and it failed.**
Varden's counter and contact-centre offices did exactly this: the same opening hours, covered by a rota of staff each working fewer hours.
- Output per worker fell 11.4%.
- Waits rose from about 4 to 7 minutes.
- The county ended the arm at month 12.
- The county estimated that restoring service would need about 12% more staff.

The meta-analysis found the same thing across all seven coverage-bound studies: output per worker fell about 9%. This is the most consistent finding in the evidence base.

A single council-wide policy is a legitimate choice. But the briefing has to tell the board it comes with added contact-centre staffing or longer waits.

**2. The output-per-worker arithmetic is wrong in method and inputs.**
- **Method.** Percentage changes multiply; they don't subtract. Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker.
- **Inputs.** The 13.4% is the meta-analysis's pooled figure. It is inflated by uncontrolled studies that divide by scheduled hours. The controlled estimate is about 8%. Pairing 13.4% with Varden's actual-hours fall mixes incompatible sources.
- **Correct figure, Varden.** 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so output per worker fell 3.4% (CI −8.0% to +1.2%).
- **Even taking 13.4% at face value,** 1.134 × 0.89 = 1.009, so output per worker would rise about 0.9%, not 2.4%.

Your description says the per-hour gain is larger than the hours cut. In Varden it isn't: 8.5% against 11%.

**3. "No sign of decay" has been overturned.**
- That conclusion rested on four uncontrolled studies that used scheduled hours.
- Varden's own gain fell from 12.5% to 9.8% on the same measure.
- Section 2 of the briefing itself reports a fall, from 12.5% to 8.5%, so it contradicts its own conclusion.
- 12.5% to 8.5% also compares timesheet hours with logged hours, which overstates the decline slightly.

**4. The wellbeing figures need correcting.**
- Sickness absence fell 27% at 24 months, about a quarter rather than a third.
- The resignation figure (4.1% against 7.9%) is imprecise.
- These are not savings "on top of" productivity: lower absence is already counted in output per worker. The separate savings are in sick pay, cover and recruitment costs, which no source has estimated.

**5. The evidence covers less than "all services."**
The controlled evidence is from public-sector case-processing work. Other council services that depend on staff being present behave like the contact centre, not like case-processing.

**6. The briefing omits how the gain was achieved.**
About half the Varden gain came from cutting recurring meetings, which fell from 7.4 to 3.9 hours a week. If the council doesn't make that change, it shouldn't expect the gain.

## Corrected section 3

> In the Varden trial, output per actual hour rose 8.5% while actual hours fell 11%, so output per worker changed by 1.085 × 0.89 − 1 = −3.4% (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%): roughly maintained, possibly slightly lower. In contact-centre work, output per worker fell 11.4% and the trial arm was ended.

## Options for the recommendation

- **Option A.** Pilot the four-day week in case-processing and back-office services, with a meeting reduction as part of the change, and evaluate it against a comparison group.
- **Option B.** Adopt the policy council-wide, budgeting for about 12% more contact-centre staff to maintain waiting times.

Which option to choose is the board's call, including on fairness. The briefing's job is to state the cost of each accurately.
