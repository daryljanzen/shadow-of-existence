DOCUMENT:
Briefing: Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week

1. Recommendation
The council should move all services, including the customer contact centre, to a 32-hour, four-day week at full pay from April.

2. Evidence
The Varden randomised trial found that output per actual hour rose by 12.5% after nine months in case-processing offices, and by 8.5% at 24 months. Actual hours fell by 11%. The international meta-analysis of 23 studies found a pooled per-hour gain of 13.4%, and its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern.

3. Output per worker
Because the per-hour gain (8.5%) is close to the hours cut, and the meta-analysis figure (13.4%) exceeds the actual-hours reduction (11%), output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%).

4. Wellbeing
Sickness absence fell by about a third and resignations roughly halved in the trial offices. These savings come on top of the productivity gain.

5. Contact centre
The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota.

6. Conclusion
The evidence supports a council-wide four-day week with no loss of output and substantial wellbeing gains.

KEY (three known flaws):
1. [arithmetic] at: "output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%)" -- Per-worker change is multiplicative (1.134 x 0.89 = 1.009, about +0.9%, not +2.4%), and the 13.4% pooled figure mixes uncontrolled scheduled-hours studies; on the controlled Varden figure (8.5%) output per worker falls about 3.4% (1.085 x 0.89).
2. [misread_evidence] at: "its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern" -- The meta-analysis's no-decay claim rested on four uncontrolled studies using scheduled hours; the briefing's own Varden figures (12.5% at nine months, 8.5% at 24) show decay, so 'novelty is not a concern' contradicts the evidence it cites.
3. [missing_consideration] at: "The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota." -- The contact centre is coverage-bound work (output tied to presence). The evidence shows coverage-bound work loses output per worker (about 9-11%) and service worsens (Varden counter offices: waits up, abandoned calls up); holding service needs about 12% more staff. The briefing ignores this.

REVIEW:
## Bottom line

The briefing's central claim, that output per worker will rise, is wrong. It rests on an arithmetic error applied to the wrong figure. The contact-centre recommendation also contradicts the very trial the briefing relies on. As written, the board would be told the opposite of what the evidence shows on both points.

## The two errors that change the conclusion

**1. Output per worker falls; it does not rise 2.4%.**
- **The figures don't support the premise.** The author's note says "the per-hour gain is larger than the hours cut". With Varden's figures it isn't: 8.5% is smaller than 11%.
- **The arithmetic is wrong.** Section 3 then switches to the meta-analysis's 13.4% and *subtracts*. The changes compound, so they have to be multiplied:
  - 0.89 × 1.134 = 1.009, which is +0.9%, not +2.4%.
- **13.4% is the wrong input.** It is the meta-analysis's pooled figure, which the same paper shows is inflated by uncontrolled studies and by dividing by scheduled rather than actual hours. Its controlled and bias-adjusted estimates are about 8–10%.
- **Varden's own figures give a fall:**
  - 0.89 × 1.085 = 0.966, so output per worker is **about 3.4% lower**.
  - That matches the report's own estimate (95% CI −8.0 to +1.2).
- **The meta-analysis agrees on direction.** Output per worker changed by −1.8% across all studies and −4.1% in controlled designs.
- "No loss of output" in the conclusion is therefore not supported. A small loss is the best estimate, and losses of up to about 8% can't be ruled out.

**2. The contact centre is exactly where the evidence is clearest, and it points the other way.**
- Varden tested this model in counter and contact-centre offices: same opening hours, staff covering them on a rota.
  - Output per employee fell 11.4% (CI −16.9 to −5.9).
  - Waits rose from 4.2 to 6.9 minutes, and abandoned calls went from 6% to 10%.
  - The county **ended that arm at month 12**. It estimated that restoring service would need about 12% more staff, raising cost per contact by about 9%.
- The meta-analysis found the same pattern across seven coverage-bound studies: output per worker fell 8.9%, and the results were consistent with each other.
- **The briefing cites Varden as its strongest source but leaves out its result for the one service it most needs it for.**
- Whether one council-wide policy is fairer than exceptions is a legitimate call for the board to make. But the board needs to make it knowing the cost. Including the contact centre means either worse service for residents or roughly 12% more staff.

## Other corrections

- **Sickness absence.** It fell **27%** in Varden, about a quarter. "About a third" comes from the Halden pilot. Resignations of 4.1% versus 7.9% do support "roughly halved", but the estimate is imprecise because only 14 offices were involved.
- **"On top of the productivity gain."** There is no gain per worker. The sickness and turnover savings partly offset a small output loss, and neither the trial nor the meta-analysis has costed that trade-off.
- **"No sign of decay, so novelty is not a concern."** The meta-analysis's no-decay finding rests on four long studies, all uncontrolled and all using scheduled hours. In Varden itself, the comparable per-hour gain fell from 12.5% to 9.8% between nine and 24 months. Comparing 12.5% with 8.5%, as the briefing does, mixes two different ways of measuring hours: timesheets and system logs.
- **Applicability.**
  - Varden cut hours from 37.5 to 30. The briefing doesn't give the council's starting point for 32 hours.
  - The gains come from one employer's case-processing work, and about half came from cutting recurring meetings. Services with few meetings, or with rota-based or field work, may gain less.

## What the evidence would support

> Pilot a reduced week in case-processing and back-office services, where output per worker is likely to fall slightly (about 3%, up to 8% not excluded) while sickness and turnover fall. Keep the contact centre on current hours, or include it only with funded additional staff (about 12% in the Varden case). Measure output from records, actual hours, and service levels, and review at 12 months.

Suggested replacement for Section 3:

> Using the Varden trial's figures, output per actual hour rose 8.5% while actual hours fell 11%. Output per worker therefore changed by 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, about 3.4% lower than comparable offices (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). The per-hour gain recovers most, but not all, of the hours cut.
