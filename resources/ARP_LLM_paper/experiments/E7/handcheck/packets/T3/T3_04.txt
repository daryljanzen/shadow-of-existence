Briefing: Moving All Council Services to a Four-Day Week

1. Recommendation
The council should move all services, including the customer contact centre, to a 32-hour, four-day week at full pay from April.

2. Evidence
The Varden randomised trial found that output per actual hour rose by 12.5% after nine months in case-processing offices, and by 8.5% at 24 months. Actual hours fell by 11%. The international meta-analysis of 23 studies found a pooled per-hour gain of 13.4%, and its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern.

3. Output per worker
Because the per-hour gain (8.5%) is close to the hours cut, and the meta-analysis figure (13.4%) exceeds the actual-hours reduction (11%), output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%).

4. Wellbeing
Sickness absence fell by about a third and resignations roughly halved in the trial offices. These savings come on top of the productivity gain.

5. Contact centre
The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota.

6. Conclusion
The evidence supports a council-wide four-day week with no loss of output and substantial wellbeing gains.

KEY (three known flaws):
1. [arithmetic] at: "output per worker will rise by about 2.4% (13.4% minus 11%)" -- Per-worker change is multiplicative (1.134 x 0.89 = 1.009, about +0.9%, not +2.4%), and the 13.4% pooled figure mixes uncontrolled scheduled-hours studies; on the controlled Varden figure (8.5%) output per worker falls about 3.4% (1.085 x 0.89).
2. [misread_evidence] at: "its authors report that the effects show no sign of decay over time, so novelty is not a concern" -- The meta-analysis's no-decay claim rested on four uncontrolled studies using scheduled hours; the briefing's own Varden figures (12.5% at nine months, 8.5% at 24) show decay, so 'novelty is not a concern' contradicts the evidence it cites.
3. [missing_consideration] at: "The contact centre will follow the same model as the other services. Staff will cover the same opening hours on a rota." -- The contact centre is coverage-bound work (output tied to presence). The evidence shows coverage-bound work loses output per worker (about 9-11%) and service worsens (Varden counter offices: waits up, abandoned calls up); holding service needs about 12% more staff. The briefing ignores this.

REVIEW:
The central claim of this briefing is wrong. Corrected, the evidence points the other way for the board's key question. Output per worker *falls* in case-processing work and falls sharply in contact centres, and the briefing's own sources say so. The cited studies are good ones, but several figures are misused. In order of importance:

## 1. The output-per-worker arithmetic is wrong (§3)

There are two errors.

**Wrong method.** You can't subtract percentages here; you have to multiply.
- Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker.
- Even with the briefing's own inputs: 1.134 × 0.89 = 1.009, about **+0.9%**, not +2.4%.

**Wrong inputs.** The 13.4% is the meta-analysis's pooled figure. That figure is inflated by uncontrolled studies and by studies that divided by scheduled rather than actual hours, so it can't be paired with an actual-hours cut. The like-for-like estimates are:

| Source | Output per worker |
|---|---|
| Varden at 24 months: 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966 | **−3.4%** (CI −8.0% to +1.2%), matching the report |
| Meta-analysis, controlled discretionary studies | −2.7% |
| Meta-analysis, all controlled studies | −4.1% |

So in case-processing work, output per worker ends up about 3% lower, and with pay unchanged, cost per case rises by roughly 3–4%. "Every service comes out ahead" isn't supported for any service.

## 2. The contact centre contradicts the recommendation (§5)

The Varden trial tested exactly this model: the same opening hours, staffed on a rota. The result was:
- output per worker **−11.4%**;
- average wait up from 4.2 to 6.9 minutes;
- abandoned calls up from 6% to 10%.

The county **ended that arm at month 12**. It estimated that restoring service would need about 12% more staff and about 9% higher cost per contact. The meta-analysis found the same pattern across seven coverage-bound studies: output per worker −8.9%, with consistent results.

The briefing omits all of this. It is the strongest controlled evidence on the contact centre, and it argues against including it.

## 3. "Novelty is not a concern" is unsupported, and the briefing contradicts itself (§2)

- §2 reports Varden's per-hour gain falling from 12.5% to 8.5%, then says effects "show no sign of decay."
- The meta-analysis's no-decay claim rested on four long-running studies. All four were uncontrolled and used scheduled hours, as the Varden follow-up showed.
- Also, 12.5% to 8.5% compares two different hours measures. On the same measure (timesheets), the gain went from 12.5% to 9.8%.

The honest line is: "Gains declined somewhat over two years but remained positive."

## 4. Wellbeing figures are overstated (§4)

- Sickness absence fell **27%** at 24 months, which is about a quarter, not a third. Part of that fall may be mechanical: fewer hours worked means fewer occasions on which sickness can be recorded.
- The halving of resignations is real in the data but imprecise, because it comes from 14 offices and few resignations.
- "On top of the productivity gain" doesn't hold, because there's no per-worker gain to add them to. They partly offset a per-worker loss, but no study has quantified how much.

## 5. Things the board needs that are missing

- **Your own baseline.** Varden cut 37.5 to 30 hours. If council staff currently work 37.5, a 32-hour week is a 15% cut, not 20%, and the Varden figures won't transfer directly.
- **Generalisability.** The best evidence comes from one county and seven treated offices.
- **The meetings finding.** About half the per-hour gain came from cutting recurring meetings. Some of that might be captured without cutting hours at all.

## 6. Fairness

Fairness is a legitimate reason to want one policy for everyone. But the briefing should argue it openly, with its cost: either contact-centre waits rise and calls are lost, or the council funds roughly 12% more contact-centre staff. Presenting it as costless will not survive a board question.

## Suggested revisions

- **§1 Recommendation.** Any of these could be defended:
  - a staged or randomised rollout in case-processing services, with output, backlog and hours measured;
  - include the contact centre only with funded extra staffing;
  - pair the change with a meetings review.
- **§3.** Replace with: "Output per worker is likely to fall by around 3% in case-processing, and by around 9–11% in the contact centre unless staffing is increased."
- **§6 Conclusion.** Replace with: "The evidence supports a four-day week in case-processing services at a modest cost per case, partly offset by lower sickness and turnover. It does not support including the contact centre without extra staff."
