ANSWER:
One note first: I haven't seen the Varden report itself, only the figures in your message, so everything below rests on those.

**1. Headline**
After 24 months, offices on 30 scheduled hours produced 8.5% more per hour actually worked than control offices (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Biggest non-productivity benefits**
- Sickness absence fell by about a third.
- Resignations roughly halved.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours worked
= 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966

That is **about 3.5% lower output per worker**.

Using the confidence interval:
- Low: 1.034 × 0.89 = 0.920, or −8.0%
- High: 1.138 × 0.89 = 1.013, or +1.3%

So the best estimate is a small fall, but the data can't rule out no change. Two caveats the board may probe:
- The per-hour gain is measured against controls, while the 11% hours fall may be against baseline. If control hours also moved, the result shifts.
- No interval is given for the hours fall, so the true range is wider than shown.

**4. Slide title**
"Four-Day Week: More per Hour, Slightly Less per Worker"

I'd avoid a title implying overall output rose, since question 3 will contradict it.
