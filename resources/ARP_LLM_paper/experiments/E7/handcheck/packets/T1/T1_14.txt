ANSWER:
**1. Headline**
After two years, output per hour worked in case-processing offices on the shorter week was 8.5% higher than in control offices (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Two biggest non-productivity benefits**
- **Sickness absence fell 27% relative to control.** "About a third" overstates it; the report gives −27% at 24 months. "Over a quarter" is accurate.
- **Resignations were roughly halved:** 4.1% a year against 7.9% in control. With few resignations in total, this estimate is imprecise.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker
= 1.085 × 0.89
= 0.966

So output per worker fell **3.4%** relative to control. This matches the report's own estimate: −3.4%, 95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%. The interval includes zero, but the central estimate is a small loss. The per-hour gain recovers most of the 11% fall in hours, not all of it.

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter week: 8.5% more output per hour, 3.4% less per worker"

**Two things the board should hear**
- **These results are for case-processing only.** The county ended the counter and contact-centre arm at month 12. Waiting times there were 7.3 minutes against 4.0 in control, and restoring service would have needed 12% more staff.
- **This is one public employer with seven offices per arm.** Expect a board member to ask how far it generalises.
