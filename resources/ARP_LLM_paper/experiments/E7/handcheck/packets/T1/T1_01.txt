ANSWER:
One note first: I haven't seen the four-day week material in this conversation, so all of this is worked from the figures you've given.

**1. Headline**
At 24 months, output per hour worked was 8.5% higher in the reduced-hours offices than in the control offices (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%, from system logs).

**2. Two biggest other benefits**
- Sickness absence fell by about a third.
- Resignations roughly halved.

Meeting hours (7.4 down to 3.9 a week) are better presented as a likely mechanism for the productivity gain than as a benefit in their own right.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours worked per worker.

- Central estimate: 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so **about 3.4% lower**.
- Low end: 1.034 × 0.89 = 0.920, so 8.0% lower.
- High end: 1.138 × 0.89 = 1.013, so 1.3% higher.

Output per worker most likely fell slightly, and the range only just includes no change.

This range reflects the uncertainty in the per-hour figure only, not in the 11% hours figure. It also assumes the 11% fall is measured against the same control baseline as the per-hour gain. Check that in the report before Thursday.

**4. Slide title**
*Four-day week: more output per hour, slightly less per person*
