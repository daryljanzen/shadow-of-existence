ANSWER:
**1. Headline**
After two years, case-processing staff on the shorter week produced 8.5% more output per hour actually worked than comparable offices on the standard week (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Two biggest non-productivity benefits**
- **Sickness absence fell 27%** relative to control. That's about a quarter rather than a third: your "about a third" is closer to the 9-month figure (29%).
- **Resignations roughly halved**: 4.1% a year against 7.9% in control offices. With 14 offices and few resignations, this estimate is imprecise.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker.

- Output per hour: 1.085 (+8.5%)
- Hours per worker: 0.89 (−11%)
- 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so **output per worker fell about 3.4%**.

This matches the report's estimate (−3.4%, 95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). The interval includes zero, so the honest summary is "roughly maintained, possibly slightly lower."

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter week: more output per hour, roughly the same per worker"

**Two things the board should hear**
- **Scope.** These results are for case-processing work only. The county ended the counter and contact-centre arm at month 12 because waiting times rose (7.3 vs 4.0 minutes).
- **Meetings.** The fall in meeting hours you cite was the largest single contributor to the gain: about half, in the trial's registered analysis.
