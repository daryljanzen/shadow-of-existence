ANSWER:
One correction first: the report gives sickness absence down **27%** at 24 months, which is about a quarter, not a third. Your other figures match the report.

**1. Headline**
After two years, case-processing offices on a 30-hour week produced 8.5% more per hour actually worked than control offices (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Other benefits**
- Sickness absence down 27% relative to control. This may partly reflect fewer working days on which to be sick, since the report doesn't say how rotas were arranged.
- Annual resignations at 4.1% against 7.9% in control, roughly halved. With only 14 offices, this estimate is imprecise.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = (1 + per-hour change) × (1 + hours change)
= 1.085 × 0.89
= 0.966, or about **−3.4%**

This matches the report's own estimate (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%, so not distinguishable from zero). The per-hour gain made up most of the lost hours, but not all of them.

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter week: more output per hour, slightly less per worker"

**Two things the board may ask**
- **Counter and contact-centre offices:** the county ended that arm at month 12. Waiting times rose, and restoring service would have needed about 12% more staff. The results apply to backlog work, not front-line service.
- **Meetings:** meeting time fell 3.5 hours a week, most of the drop in hours. Much of the per-hour gain may come from fewer meetings rather than faster casework. Whether cutting meetings alone would deliver it is untested.
