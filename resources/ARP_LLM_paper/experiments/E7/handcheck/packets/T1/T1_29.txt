ANSWER:
**1. Headline**
In Varden County's case-processing offices, output per hour actually worked was 8.5% higher than in control offices after two years (95% CI 3.4–13.8%).

**2. Two biggest non-productivity benefits**
- **Sickness absence down 27%** against control. The report gives −27% at 24 months, so "about a quarter" is accurate; "about a third" overstates it.
- **Resignations roughly halved:** 4.1% a year against 7.9% in control.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker.

- Output per hour: × 1.085
- Hours per worker: × 0.89 (an 11% fall)
- 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so **output per worker is about 3.4% lower** than control.

This matches the report's own figure of −3.4% (95% CI −8.0 to +1.2). Because the interval includes zero, the fall isn't statistically significant. Don't subtract the percentages: 8.5 − 11 = −2.5 is wrong.

On unchanged pay, that means labour cost per unit of output is roughly 3–4% higher, partly offset by lower absence and turnover.

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter Week: More Output per Hour, Slightly Less per Worker"

**Two cautions for the board**
- These results come from one public employer, with 7 offices per arm, in discretionary case-processing work.
- The same trial's counter and contact-centre offices were returned to full hours at month 12, after waiting times rose. Expect that question if your operations include customer-facing cover.
