ANSWER:
One correction first: at 24 months, sickness absence fell **27%** relative to control, about a quarter rather than a third. The 36% figure is from the Halden pilot. Your other figures match the report.

**1. Headline**
"After two years, case-processing staff on a 30-hour week produced 8.5% more per hour worked than comparable offices on the standard week (95% CI 3.4–13.8%)."

**2. Two biggest non-productivity benefits**
- Sickness absence down 27% relative to control.
- Annual resignations 4.1% against 7.9% in control, roughly halved. Resignations were few, so this figure is imprecise.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = (hours ratio) × (output per hour ratio)
= (1 − 0.11) × (1 + 0.085)
= 0.89 × 1.085
= 0.966

So output per worker was about **3.4% lower** than control. This matches the report's −3.4% (95% CI −8.0 to +1.2), so a small loss or no change is possible. Pay was unchanged, so labour cost per case rose about 3.5% (1 ÷ 0.966).

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter Week: More per Hour, Slightly Less per Worker"

**Worth raising before the board does:** these results cover case-processing work only. The county stopped the counter and contact-centre arm at month 12. Waits there had risen to 7.3 minutes against 4.0 in control offices, and restoring service would have needed about 12% more staff.
