ANSWER:
**One correction first.** The report gives sick days down 27% relative to control at 24 months (29% at nine months), so "about a quarter" rather than "about a third". "Roughly halved" for resignations holds (4.1% against 7.9% a year), but the report notes that estimate is imprecise.

**1. Headline**
In case-processing offices, output per actual hour worked was 8.5% higher than in control offices after two years (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Biggest non-productivity benefits**
- Sickness absence about 27% lower than in control offices.
- Resignations about half the control rate (4.1% against 7.9% a year), though imprecisely estimated.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker
= 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966

So output per worker is about 3.4% lower than control. This matches the report's own figure of −3.4% (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%): a small fall that can't be distinguished from no change. With pay unchanged, cost per case is about 3.5% higher (1 ÷ 0.966).

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter week: more output per hour, about the same per person"

**Two things to have ready for the board**
- **Scope:** these results are for case-processing only. The county ended the counter and contact-centre arm at month 12 because waiting times rose. Restoring service would have needed about 12% more staff.
- **No double-counting:** the sickness gain is already reflected in the output-per-worker figure, so it shouldn't be added on top as an extra offset.
