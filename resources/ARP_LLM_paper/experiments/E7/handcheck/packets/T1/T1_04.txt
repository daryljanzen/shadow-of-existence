ANSWER:
**1. Headline**
In case-processing offices, output per hour actually worked was 8.5% higher than in control offices after two years (95% CI 3.4 to 13.8%).

**2. Biggest non-productivity benefits**
- **Lower sickness absence:** 27% fewer sick days than control at 24 months. That is just over a quarter, so I'd avoid "about a third" on the slide.
- **Better retention:** annual resignations were 4.1% against 7.9% in control, roughly halved. The report gives no confidence interval for this.

**3. Output per worker**

Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker
= 1.085 × 0.89
= 0.966

**Output per worker fell about 3.4%.** This matches the report's own estimate (−3.4%, 95% CI −8.0 to +1.2), so the result is not clearly different from zero. The per-hour gain offset most of the hours lost, but not all of it.

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter Week: More Output per Hour, Slightly Less per Worker"

**Two caveats the board may raise:**
- **These results apply only to case-processing (backlog) work.** The county ended the counter and contact-centre arm at month 12 because waiting times rose.
- **No cost figures were reported.** With pay maintained, a 3.4% fall in output per worker means labour cost per case is about 3.5% higher, before any overtime.
