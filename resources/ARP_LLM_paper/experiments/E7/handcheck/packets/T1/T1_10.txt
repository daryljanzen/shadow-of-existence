ANSWER:
**1. Headline**
After two years, case-processing staff on a 30-hour week produced about 8.5% more per hour actually worked than staff in control offices (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Two biggest benefits besides productivity**
- **Lower sickness absence:** sick days were down 27% relative to control at 24 months. That is closer to a quarter than a third; the third-like figure (29%) was at nine months.
- **Lower resignations:** 4.1% a year against 7.9% in control offices, so roughly halved. With 14 offices this estimate is imprecise.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours worked per worker.

- Output per hour: 1.085
- Hours worked: 0.89 (an 11% fall)
- 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, a change of **about −3.4%**

This matches the report's directly measured figure of −3.4% (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). Each worker produced slightly less, though the interval includes no change. Because pay was unchanged, labour cost per case rose by roughly 3.5%.

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter week: more per hour, slightly less per person"

**Two points to have ready**
- **Scope:** these results cover case-processing work only. The county ended the counter and contact-centre arm at month 12 because waiting times rose.
- **Meetings:** meeting time dropped sharply, and the report attributes about half the per-hour gain to that. This is one mediation analysis, so present it as likely rather than proven.
