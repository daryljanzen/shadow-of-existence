ANSWER:
I haven't seen the Varden report in this session, so this works only from the figures you've given. Check them against the report before Thursday.

**1. Headline**
At 24 months, offices moved to 30 scheduled hours produced 8.5% more per actual hour worked than control offices (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Two biggest benefits besides productivity**
- Sickness absence fell by about a third.
- Resignations roughly halved.

(The fall in meeting hours is better presented as a likely *mechanism* for the per-hour gain than as a separate benefit.)

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker.

- Point estimate: 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, about **3.4% lower** per worker.
- Using the CI bounds: 1.034 × 0.89 = 0.920 (−8.0%) and 1.138 × 0.89 = 1.013 (+1.3%).

So output per worker most likely fell slightly, but the data don't rule out no change. Present this as open, not as a confirmed fall.

Two caveats on the calculation:
- The range only reflects uncertainty in the per-hour figure, because I don't have a CI for the 11% fall in hours.
- Combining the figures assumes the 11% fall is measured against the controls, as the per-hour gain is. If it's against the offices' own baseline, the result shifts. The report should say which.

**4. Slide title**
"Four-Day Week: More per Hour, Fewer Absences and Leavers"
