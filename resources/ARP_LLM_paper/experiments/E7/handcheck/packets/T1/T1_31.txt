ANSWER:
**1. Headline**
In case-processing offices, a 30-hour week raised output per actual hour by 8.5% relative to control at 24 months (95% CI 3.4 to 13.8%).

**2. Two biggest other benefits**
- **Sickness absence down 27%** relative to control. One correction: "about a third" overstates it. 27% is the 24-month figure; 29% was the nine-month figure.
- **Resignations roughly halved:** 4.1% against 7.9% a year.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = (actual hours ratio) × (output-per-hour ratio)
= 0.89 × 1.085
= 0.966, which is **about −3.4%**.

This matches the report's own estimate (95% CI −8.0 to +1.2). The per-hour gain offsets most, but not all, of the 11% fall in hours. The interval includes no change.

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter week: more productive hours, nearly the same output"

**Before Thursday, three caveats the board may raise:**
- **This applies to case-processing work only.** The county ended the counter and contact-centre arm at month 12 because waits rose to 7.3 minutes against 4.0 in control offices.
- **The gain has shrunk.** It was 12.5% at nine months, and nothing has been measured beyond 24 months.
- **Fewer meetings were the largest single driver.** They account for about half the per-hour gain in this trial, which raises the question of how much a meeting cut alone would achieve.
