ANSWER:
I haven't seen the Varden report itself, so this uses only the figures you gave.

**1. Headline**
After 24 months, offices on 30 scheduled hours produced 8.5% more per hour worked than control offices (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Two biggest non-productivity benefits**
- Sickness absence fell by about a third.
- Resignations roughly halved.

(The drop in meeting hours, from 7.4 to 3.9 a week, is probably best presented as a likely *cause* of the per-hour gain rather than as a separate benefit.)

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours worked.

- 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so output per worker is **about 3.4% lower**.
- At the CI bounds: 1.034 × 0.89 = 0.920 (−8.0%), and 1.138 × 0.89 = 1.013 (+1.3%).

So the most likely result is a small fall in output per worker, and a gain can't be ruled out.

**Caveat:** this assumes the 11% fall in hours is measured against the same control baseline as the 8.5%. If it's a before/after figure, the calculation needs adjusting, so check this in the report before Thursday.

**4. Slide title**
"Four-day week: more per hour, slightly less per person"

The board will likely work out item 3 themselves, so a title that already reflects it will hold up better than a purely positive one.
