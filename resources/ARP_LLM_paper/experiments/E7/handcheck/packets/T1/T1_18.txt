ANSWER:
I haven't seen the Varden report itself, only the figures you've quoted here, so everything below rests on those.

**1. Headline**
At 24 months, offices on 30 scheduled hours produced 8.5% more per actual hour worked than control offices (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%, from system logs).

**2. Biggest other benefits**
- Sickness absence fell by about a third.
- Resignations roughly halved.

You've given me point figures only, so check the report for the uncertainty on these two before presenting them.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours worked

1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966, so **about 3.4% lower** than controls.

At the ends of the confidence interval:
- 1.034 × 0.89 = 0.920 (−8.0%)
- 1.138 × 0.89 = 1.013 (+1.3%)

So output per worker most likely fell slightly, and a small rise can't be ruled out. The true range is somewhat wider, because this ignores uncertainty in the 11% hours figure. It also assumes the 11% fall is measured against the same controls, so confirm that in the report.

Put plainly to the board: output per hour rose, and output per worker fell a little. The per-hour gain recovered most of the lost hours, but not all.

Meeting hours nearly halved (7.4 to 3.9 a week). That's a plausible source of the per-hour gain, but nothing in these figures tests it.

**4. Slide title**
"Four-day week: more per hour, slightly less per worker"
