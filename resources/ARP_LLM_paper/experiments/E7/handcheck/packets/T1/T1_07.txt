ANSWER:
One correction to your figures first. In the Varden 24-month report, sick days fell **27%**, about a quarter. The "about a third" comes from the Halden pilot. Your "roughly halved" for resignations holds: 4.1% versus 7.9% a year.

**1. Headline**
After two years, case-processing offices on reduced hours produced 8.5% more per hour worked than control offices (95% CI 3.4–13.8%). That is smaller than the nine-month gain, but still clearly above zero.

**2. Benefits beyond productivity**
- Sickness absence was down 27% relative to control.
- Annual resignations were 4.1% versus 7.9%. Treat this as imprecise, because it rests on only 14 offices.

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker
= 1.085 × 0.89
= 0.966, so output per worker was **about 3.4% lower** than control.

This matches the report's own estimate: −3.4%, 95% CI −8.0 to +1.2. The per-hour gain recovers most of the lost hours, but not all of them. A loss of up to about 8% can't be ruled out.

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter week: more per hour, slightly less per worker"

**One thing the board should hear**
These results cover case-processing work only. The counter and contact-centre offices in the same trial were returned to full hours at month 12. Waiting times had risen to 7.3 minutes, against 4.0 in control offices. The county estimated that restoring service would need about 12% more staff.
