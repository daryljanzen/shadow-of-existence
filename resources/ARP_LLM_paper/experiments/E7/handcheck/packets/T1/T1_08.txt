ANSWER:
**1. Headline**
After two years on a 30-hour week, case-processing staff produced 8.5% more per hour actually worked than staff in comparable offices on the standard week (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Two biggest non-productivity benefits**
- **Fewer resignations:** 4.1% a year against 7.9% in control offices, roughly halved.
- **Less sickness absence:** down 27% relative to control.

One correction: the report gives 27% for sickness absence at 24 months (29% at nine months). "About a third" overstates it, so I'd say "about a quarter."

**3. Output per worker**

Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker

= 1.085 × (1 − 0.11)
= 1.085 × 0.89
= 0.966

That is about 3.4% lower output per worker. This matches the report's own estimate of −3.4%.

The report's 95% interval is −8.0% to +1.2%, which includes zero. The board-ready wording: "Each worker produced about 3% less, a difference too small to rule out no change."

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter Week, Nearly Same Output: Two-Year Results"

One scope note for Thursday: these results come from public-sector case processing only. In the same trial, counter and contact-centre offices lost about 11% of output and saw longer waits, and that arm was ended at month 12. Expect the board to ask about this.
