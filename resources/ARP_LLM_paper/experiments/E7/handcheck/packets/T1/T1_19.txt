ANSWER:
**1. Headline**
In case-processing offices, output per hour worked was 8.5% higher than in control offices after two years (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Benefits beyond productivity**
- **Sickness absence fell about 27% relative to control.** That is closer to a quarter than a third; the one-third figure comes from the uncontrolled Halden pilot. Part of the fall may be mechanical if staff now work fewer days.
- **Resignations roughly halved:** 4.1% against 7.9% a year. Numbers are small, so the estimate is imprecise.

**3. Output per worker**

Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker

= 1.085 × 0.89
= 0.966
→ about **−3.4%**

This matches the report's own estimate of −3.4% (95% CI −8.0% to +1.2%). Output per worker was slightly lower, but a change of zero can't be ruled out. The per-hour gain recovered most, but not all, of the 11% fall in hours.

**4. Slide title**
"Shorter Week: More Output per Hour, Slightly Less per Worker"

**Before Thursday**
Present these results as applying to case-processing work only. The same trial's counter and contact-centre offices were returned to full hours at month 12 because waiting times rose (7.3 against 4.0 minutes). Restoring service there would have needed about 12% more staff.
