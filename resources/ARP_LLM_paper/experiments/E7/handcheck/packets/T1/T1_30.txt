ANSWER:
One note first: I haven't seen the Varden report in this conversation, so everything below uses only the figures you gave. Please check them against the report before Thursday.

**1. Headline**
Offices on 30 scheduled hours produced 8.5% more output per hour actually worked than control offices at 24 months (95% CI 3.4% to 13.8%).

**2. Two biggest non-productivity benefits**
- Sickness absence fell by about a third.
- Resignations roughly halved.

(Meeting time almost halving, 7.4 to 3.9 hours a week, is likely part of *how* the per-hour gain arose, not a separate benefit.)

**3. Output per worker**
Output per worker = output per hour × hours per worker
= 1.085 × 0.89 = 0.966
→ **about 3.4% lower per worker** than controls.

Using the CI ends:
- 1.034 × 0.89 = 0.920 (−8.0%)
- 1.138 × 0.89 = 1.013 (+1.3%)

So the range runs from about 8% lower to 1% higher. The data fit a small drop or roughly no change, but not a clear gain. This assumes the 11% fall in hours is measured against the controls. If it's a before/after figure, the comparison isn't like-for-like, so please check.

**4. Slide title**
"Fewer Hours, Higher Productivity per Hour, Steadier Staff"

The board is likely to press on the per-worker figure, so I'd show it on the slide rather than wait for the question.
