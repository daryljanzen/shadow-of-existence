No, the evidence doesn't support that, and the report explicitly says the question is open.

**What the evidence shows:**
- The only test of cutting meetings without cutting hours comes from those two control offices. Their per-hour output rose 4.1%.
- That's two offices, unplanned and uncontrolled, so it proves little either way. But if it points anywhere, it suggests meeting cuts alone capture some of the gain, which is the opposite of the claim.
- The mediation analysis attributed about half the gain (47%, CI 28–66%) to fewer meetings. That leaves room for the hours cut to matter in other ways, but doesn't show it's necessary.

**Wording you could use instead:**

Limited evidence from two control offices that cut meetings on their own suggests part of the gain might be achievable this way, and the report treats this as an open question.

If the board's real question is "why not just cut meetings?", that's worth answering directly. A pilot comparing meeting reduction alone against meeting reduction plus shorter hours would settle it.
