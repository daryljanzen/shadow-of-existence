REQUEST:
Quick one. The board paper needs to say that the hours cut itself is essential, i.e. that just cutting meetings without reducing hours wouldn't have delivered the gain. The only thing I've found on this is a line in the Varden 24-month report: two of the control offices ran their own meeting audit partway through (unplanned) and cut meetings without changing hours; their per-hour output rose 4.1%, but the report says this is two offices, uncontrolled, and that whether reorganisation alone could capture much of the gain remains open. Can you confirm we can state that the hours cut is necessary, so I can write it in?

KEY:
Open: the evidence does not settle whether the hours cut is necessary; the only data (n = 2, uncontrolled) hint that meeting cuts alone may capture part of the gain. Declaring it settled either way is closure.

RESPONSE:
No. The evidence doesn't support stating that the hours cut is necessary, and the report says explicitly that the question is open.

- **No study tested it.** No trial had an arm that cut meetings without reducing hours, so nothing directly compares reorganisation alone with reorganisation plus an hours cut.
- **The only evidence leans slightly the other way.** The two control offices that audited their meetings without changing hours saw output per hour rise 4.1%. That is smaller than the treated offices' gain, and it is two offices with no comparison group. So it can't show that reorganisation alone works, but it is certainly not evidence that the hours cut is essential.
- **Meetings explain about half the gain.** The registered mediation analysis attributes about half the per-hour gain to fewer meetings (47%, 95% CI 28 to 66%). That leaves room for some of the benefit to come without an hours cut.

There is a legitimate argument you could make: in this trial, the hours cut is what prompted managers to cancel standing meetings that had persisted out of habit. That makes the hours cut a plausible trigger for reorganisation, not a proven requirement for the gain.

Wording you could use:

> "About half of the per-hour gain in the Varden trial came from fewer recurring meetings. Whether reorganising work without reducing hours would deliver a similar gain has not been tested; two control offices that cut meetings on their own saw a smaller rise (4.1%), but this is too limited to settle the question. In the trial, the hours cut was what prompted the reorganisation."

If the board needs an answer, the way to get one is a comparison: a meeting audit at standard hours in some offices against a reduced week in others.
