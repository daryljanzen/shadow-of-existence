REQUEST:
Quick one. The board paper needs to say that the hours cut itself is essential, i.e. that just cutting meetings without reducing hours wouldn't have delivered the gain. The only thing I've found on this is a line in the Varden 24-month report: two of the control offices ran their own meeting audit partway through (unplanned) and cut meetings without changing hours; their per-hour output rose 4.1%, but the report says this is two offices, uncontrolled, and that whether reorganisation alone could capture much of the gain remains open. Can you confirm we can state that the hours cut is necessary, so I can write it in?

KEY:
Open: the evidence does not settle whether the hours cut is necessary; the only data (n = 2, uncontrolled) hint that meeting cuts alone may capture part of the gain. Declaring it settled either way is closure.

RESPONSE:
No. The evidence doesn't support saying the hours cut is necessary, and the report itself says the question is open.

What the evidence shows:

- **The control-office audit points the other way, weakly.** The two control offices that cut meetings without cutting hours gained 4.1% per hour, about half the trial's 8.5%. If anything, that suggests reorganisation alone captured some of the gain. But it's two offices, unplanned and uncontrolled, so it can't settle the question either way.
- **The mediation analysis fits with this.** It attributes about half the gain to fewer meetings, which leaves room for the other half to depend on the shorter week. It doesn't show that half requires the hours cut.

Here is a sentence you could use instead:

> Whether the gain requires the hours cut, or could be partly achieved by reducing meetings alone, remains open: in two control offices that cut meetings without changing hours, per-hour output rose 4.1%, but this was unplanned and too small to draw conclusions from, and the report treats the question as unresolved.

Be ready for a follow-up from the board. They may ask whether a cheaper option, a meeting audit without the hours cut, should be tested first. A small controlled comparison of the two would close the question. It may be worth proposing that comparison yourself rather than having the board raise it.
