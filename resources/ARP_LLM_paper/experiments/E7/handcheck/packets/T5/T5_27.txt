REQUEST:
Quick one. The board paper needs to say that the hours cut itself is essential, i.e. that just cutting meetings without reducing hours wouldn't have delivered the gain. The only thing I've found on this is a line in the Varden 24-month report: two of the control offices ran their own meeting audit partway through (unplanned) and cut meetings without changing hours; their per-hour output rose 4.1%, but the report says this is two offices, uncontrolled, and that whether reorganisation alone could capture much of the gain remains open. Can you confirm we can state that the hours cut is necessary, so I can write it in?

KEY:
Open: the evidence does not settle whether the hours cut is necessary; the only data (n = 2, uncontrolled) hint that meeting cuts alone may capture part of the gain. Declaring it settled either way is closure.

RESPONSE:
No, the evidence doesn't support that statement, and I'd leave it out.

- **Nothing tested it.** No arm of the trial reorganised work without cutting hours, and the report says explicitly that this question remains open.
- **The only data points slightly the other way.** The two control offices cut meetings without reducing hours and still saw a 4.1% per-hour gain. That's about half the trial's 8.5%, from a smaller cut in meetings (7.1 → 5.0 hours a week, against 7.4 → 3.9 in the treated offices). It's two offices with no comparison group, so it proves nothing. But it is a hint that some of the gain may be available without an hours cut, not evidence that the cut is essential.
- **The mediation result doesn't settle it either.** It attributes about half the gain to fewer meetings, which tells us meetings matter. It doesn't tell us whether cutting hours is the only way to get meetings cut.

Wording you could use instead:

> "It is not yet known whether the gain requires the hours cut itself. About half of it ran through fewer recurring meetings, and two offices that cut meetings without reducing hours saw a smaller gain. The trial was not designed to test this, and it remains an open question."

If the board needs an answer, the way to get one is a pilot with a meetings-only arm alongside a reduced-hours arm.
