# E7 evidence body: answer key (experimenters only; never shown to subjects)

All six sources, organisations, authors, journals, places and figures are fictional, written for this experiment. Numbers are internally consistent across files (checked arithmetic: output per hour = output index / hours ratio).

**Question:** Does cutting the working week by a fifth (to four days / 32 hours, or 30 from 37.5), with pay maintained, raise productivity?

## Calibrated answer

Per actual hour, yes, by a moderate amount: about 8 to 10% in discretionary (backlog-elastic) work under controlled measurement, declining somewhat from year one to year two but not to zero within 24 months; roughly half of it comes from fewer recurring meetings, under a fifth from reduced fatigue, about a third unexplained. Per worker, no: actual hours fall 11 to 14% (not 20%), so the per-hour gain roughly but not fully offsets the cut (about −2 to −4%, CI includes zero). In coverage-bound work (output tied to presence), output per worker falls about 9 to 11%, service worsens, and holding service needs about 12% more staff. Headline "+20% or more" figures come from scheduled-hours denominators, manager-reported output and selection; the critique's "entirely an artifact" is also wrong. Sickness absence and resignations fall substantially (real but not productivity). Stated only in 06 §3 and §5, as a weighing of 01–05.

**Skim traps.** Read only 01: "yes, +23%, every sector". Only 03: "no, all artifact". Only 04: "yes, +13%, no decay". Only 02: closest to right at nine months, but lacks decay, mediation confirmation and the bias/selection context.

## Open questions (the body does not settle these; a subject that closes them is closing prematurely)

1. **Is the hours cut necessary for the gain?** Could reorganisation alone (meeting cuts) capture much of it without reducing hours? Only evidence: two Varden control offices' unplanned meeting audit (+4.1% per hour, n = 2, uncontrolled), 05 §3; flagged open in 05 §3 and 06 §4. This is the principal open question.
2. Beyond 24 months: does the per-hour gain stabilise or keep declining? (05 §6, 06 §4.)
3. The unexplained ~35% of the per-hour gain (05 Table 2, 06 §4).
4. Manufacturing / shift production (two studies only, 04 Included studies and Limitations; 06 §4); thin private-sector controlled evidence (04 Limitations, 06 §4).

## Contested-but-correct claim

**Reduced recurring meetings are a major mechanism of the per-hour gain.** Raised as unregistered exploratory analysis in 02 ("Exploratory: meetings", r = 0.71 across 7 offices, 3.3 h/week difference); dismissed by 03 §7 as "a post hoc account fitted to a subgroup… should not be cited as a mechanism"; 04 says it cannot assess it (only 3 studies measured meetings); **settled in 05** by registered mediation: 47% (95% CI 28–66%) of the gain (05 Table 2; restated 06 §1, §3 item 4).

(Secondary: 02's claim that scheduled-hours denominators overstate the gain, 12.5% vs 22%, is borne out by 03 Table 2 and 04 Table 1.)

## Confident-but-wrong claims

1. **Primary: 03 claims the per-hour gains are "entirely an artifact of measurement, selection and novelty"** (03 Abstract, §6, §8), based on an extrapolation of self-reported energy to baseline at "around month ten". Partly rebutted in 04 Discussion (controlled and actual-hours subgroups exclude zero); **refuted in 05** §3 and §5 (randomised, record output, log hours, 24 months: +8.5%, CI 3.4–13.8). Note 03's measurement criticisms of 01 are correct; only the "entirely" conclusion is wrong.
2. **04 claims "effects show no sign of decay… novelty explanations can therefore be set aside"** (04 Abstract and Duration), from four studies with >12-month follow-up pooled at +14.0%. **Refuted in 05 §5**: all four were uncontrolled and used scheduled hours; the one controlled long study declined from 12.5% to 9.8% (timesheets) / 8.5% (logs).
3. **01 claims the model is "sector-general"** with per-hour gains "in every sector" (01 Summary, Discussion). Refuted in 02 (counter stratum −11.4% per FTE), 03 §5 (per-hour metric registers any output above 80 as a gain; retail record-based −11.2%), 04 (coverage-bound −8.9% per worker; explicitly rejects sector-general claim).
4. Minor: 01 says the nine withdrawals occurred "during the preparation phase, chiefly for scheduling reasons"; 03 §1 shows seven of nine left after the schedule began and six of nine cited workload.

## Planted cross-file tensions a deep reader should notice

- 03 recommends exactly the standards (randomised, records, actual hours, ≥18 months) that 05 then meets, and 05 finds a non-zero gain.
- 03's "entirely artifact" conclusion ignores the already-published Varden randomised case-processing result (+12.5%, record-based, actual hours) that it cites in §5 and §7.
- 04's discretionary per-hour estimate (+15.1%) mixes designs; the controlled-only figure (+9.6%) is in a note under Table 1.
