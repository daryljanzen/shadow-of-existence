# ARP paper — citation check against the sources

Every citing sentence in `arp_cogsci.tex`, checked against the cited work itself. Five batches, by literature. Each entry gives the claim as the paper makes it, what the source says, and the sentence as it now stands.

## Batch 1 — turn-taking and projection

| Reference | Source read | Finding | Sentence now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sacks, Schegloff and Jefferson 1974 | The article text: the turn-constructional component, its unit-types, projectability, the transition-relevance place | Describes unit-types (sentential, clausal, phrasal, lexical) by their grammatical construction, and the TRP as the first possible completion of a unit. Nothing on prosody or pragmatics. | Turns are built from unit-types, later called TCUs, whose grammatical shape lets listeners project their completion; speakership transfer is organised around the TRPs. "Prosodic and pragmatic" removed. |
| Stivers et al. 2009 | PMC full text | Gaps between turns in all ten languages peak at 0–200 ms; the cross-language mean is 208 ms. | Unchanged: "the typical gap between turns is around 200 ms". |
| Levinson 2016 | The Max Planck Institute's press release for the paper (the PDF itself could not be retrieved) | Responses come about 200 ms after a turn ends; planning even a single word takes 600 ms or more, so responses are planned in overlap with the incoming turn. | Unchanged. |
| Bögels, Magyari and Levinson 2015 | PMC full text | Response planning begins about 500 ms after the critical information for the answer arrives, often midway through the incoming question. Scripted quiz, Dutch. | "As soon as the information needed for the answer has been heard". |
| Riest, Jorschick and de Ruiter 2015 | Frontiers full text | Their Experiment 1: the distribution of turn-end estimates in natural turns is very similar to the distribution when listeners know the content in advance (Bayes factors favour no difference). Reaction is treated as a backup mechanism. | Projection is "the principal mechanism"; estimates are "distributed very much as they are" with advance knowledge. Was: "matches". |
| Magyari and de Ruiter 2012 | Frontiers full text | Listeners who anticipated more remaining words were later in their button presses; listeners predict upcoming content and use it to estimate how long the turn will last. | "Listeners time a turn's end by predicting the words still to come". Was: "anticipation tracks upcoming words". |
| Holler and Kendrick 2015 | White Rose eprint | Unaddressed participants often shift gaze to the next speaker before the current turn ends, timed to the turn's first points of possible completion; the authors read this as optimising recipiency. | States that finding directly. Was: "extended the finding to gaze orientation in multi-party settings". |
| Gisladottir, Chwilla and Levinson 2015 | MPI abstract | The action an utterance performs is recognised early (ERP effects from 200 ms after target onset); in constraining contexts no effect at the final word, read as prediction at the level of the action. | Listeners recognise the action early in the turn, and in constraining contexts before its final word, by predicting at the level of the action. Was: "anticipate … what kind of action it will perform". |

| Lerner 2004 (added) | Bibliographic record and secondary descriptions; chapter not retrieved | The collaborative turn sequence: completion, then the original speaker's ratification or rejection. | New. |
| Sidnell 2012 (added) | Abstract and record | Other-continuation: a speaker extends another's TCU after possible completion (host plus continuation). | New paragraph in §3.4 distinguishing ARPs from other-continuations, since a reviewer from conversation analysis would raise it. |
